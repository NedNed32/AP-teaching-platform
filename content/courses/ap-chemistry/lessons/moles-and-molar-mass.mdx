The mole connects the atomic scale to measurable masses.

$$n = \frac{m}{M} \qquad N = n \times N_A, \quad N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$$

The molar mass of a compound is the sum of its atoms' average atomic masses: $$\ce{H2O}$$ is $$2(1.008) + 16.00 = 18.02$$ g/mol.

<Note title="Units guide you">
Write units on every step: g ÷ (g/mol) = mol, and mol × particles/mol = particles.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Mass to particles">
How many molecules are in 11.0 g of $$\ce{CO2}$$ (44.0 g/mol)?

1. $$11.0/44.0 = 0.250$$ mol.
2. $$0.250 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}$$.

**Answer:** $$1.51 \times 10^{23}$$ molecules.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

How many formula units or molecules are in 123 g of $$\ce{MgCl2}$$?

**Answer:** $$7.78 \times 10^{23}\ \mathrm{particles}$$

**Why:** Convert grams to moles, then multiply by Avogadro's number.

**Working:** 1.29 mol × 6.022 × 10²³ = 7.78 \times 10^\{23\}.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$7.41 \times 10^{25}\ \mathrm{particles}$$: Convert grams to moles first.
- $$4.66 \times 10^{23}\ \mathrm{particles}$$: Moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
- $$1.29\ \mathrm{particles}$$: Multiply moles by 6.022 × 10²³.

### Worked exam question 2

How many moles are in 128 g of $$\ce{KCl}$$?

**Answer:** $$1.72\ \mathrm{mol}$$

**Why:** n = m/M with M = 74.55 g/mol.

**Working:** 128 g ÷ 74.55 g/mol = 1.72 mol.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$9540\ \mathrm{mol}$$: Divide mass by molar mass.
- $$0.582\ \mathrm{mol}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$3.43\ \mathrm{mol}$$: Recheck the molar mass.
