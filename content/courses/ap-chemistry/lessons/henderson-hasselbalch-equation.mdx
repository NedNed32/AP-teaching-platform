$$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\ce{A-}]}{[\ce{HA}]}$$

After adding strong acid or base, update moles of HA and A⁻ by stoichiometry, then apply the equation (moles can replace concentrations since volume is shared).

<Note title="Moles work too">
Because both species are in the same volume, the ratio of moles equals the ratio of concentrations.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Buffer pH">
A buffer contains 0.30 mol CH₃COO⁻ and 0.20 mol CH₃COOH (pKa 4.74). Find pH.

1. $$4.74 + \log(0.30/0.20) = 4.74 + 0.18$$.

**Answer:** 4.92.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A buffer contains 0.4 mol of a weak acid HA (pKa 3.75) and 0.33 mol of its conjugate base A⁻. Then 0.13 mol of NaOH is added. What is the pH?

**Answer:** 3.98

**Why:** First react: OH⁻ converts HA to A⁻. Then pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).

**Working:** ratio = 0.46/0.27; pH = 3.75 + log(1.7) = 3.98.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 3.52: The ratio is [A⁻]/[HA].
- 3.67: Update moles after the added acid or base reacts.
- 3.75: Only true when [A⁻] = [HA].

### Worked exam question 2

A buffer contains 0.21 mol of a weak acid HA (pKa 6.35) and 0.05 mol of A⁻. What is its pH?

**Answer:** 5.73

**Why:** pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).

**Working:** ratio = 0.05/0.21; pH = 6.35 + log(0.238) = 5.73.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 6.97: The ratio is [A⁻]/[HA].
- 6.35: Only true when [A⁻] = [HA].
- 6.03: Recompute the ratio.
