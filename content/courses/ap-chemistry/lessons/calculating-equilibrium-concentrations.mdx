1. Write initial concentrations.
2. Express changes in terms of x, using coefficients.
3. Write equilibrium concentrations.
4. Substitute into K and solve.

If K is small, assume $$[\ce{A}]_0 - x \approx [\ce{A}]_0$$, then check the assumption.

<Note title="Check the 5% rule">
After solving, compare x with the initial concentration to confirm the approximation.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Small K">
A ⇌ 2B with K = 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ and [A]₀ = 0.25 M. Find [B].

1. $$K = (2x)^2/0.25 \approx 4x^2/0.25$$.
2. $$x^2 = 2.5 \times 10^{-7}$$; x = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴.

**Answer:** $$[\ce{B}] = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}$$ M.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

For $$\ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2HI(g)}$$, initial [H₂] = 0.5 M and [I₂] = 1 M, with no HI. At equilibrium, [HI] = 0.48 M. What is K?

**Answer:** $$1.17\ \mathrm{}$$

**Why:** x = 0.24; [H₂] = 0.26, [I₂] = 0.76.

**Working:** K = (0.48)²/((0.26)(0.76)) = 1.17.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.43\ \mathrm{}$$: Square [HI] (coefficient 2).
- $$0.461\ \mathrm{}$$: Use equilibrium, not initial, concentrations.
- $$0.291\ \mathrm{}$$: [HI] = 2x.
