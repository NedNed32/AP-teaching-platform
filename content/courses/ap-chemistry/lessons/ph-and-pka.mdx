| Condition | Predominant species |
| --- | --- |
| pH less than pKa | HA (protonated) |
| pH = pKa | equal HA and A⁻ |
| pH greater than pKa | A⁻ (deprotonated) |

This is useful for indicators: an indicator changes color when the pH passes its pKa.

<Note title="Biological relevance">
Amino acid side chains are protonated or deprotonated depending on pH relative to their pKa.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Predict">
An acid has pKa 4.8. At pH 7.4, which form dominates?

1. pH is greater than pKa.

**Answer:** the conjugate base A⁻.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

48 mL of 0.11 M weak acid HA is titrated with 0.27 M NaOH. What volume of NaOH reaches the equivalence point?

**Answer:** $$19.6\ \mathrm{mL}$$

**Why:** Moles of base = moles of acid.

**Working:** 48(0.11)/0.27 = 19.6 mL.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$118\ \mathrm{mL}$$: V_b = M_aV_a/M_b.
- $$9.78\ \mathrm{mL}$$: That is the half-equivalence point.
- $$48\ \mathrm{mL}$$: The concentrations differ.

### Worked exam question 2

A weak acid (pKa 4.87) is titrated with NaOH; the equivalence point is reached at 22 mL. What is the pH after 11 mL of NaOH has been added?

**Answer:** 4.87

**Why:** At the half-equivalence point, [HA] = [A⁻], so pH = pKa.

**Working:** Half-equivalence: pH = pKa = 4.87.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 7.00: That is only for strong acid–strong base equivalence.
- 9.13: pH = pKa, not pKb.
- 2.44: pH equals pKa at half-equivalence.

### Worked exam question 3

A weak acid HA has pKa = 7.1. In a solution at pH 4.5, which statement is correct?

**Answer:** [HA] is greater than [A⁻]

**Why:** Below the pKa, the protonated form dominates.

**Working:** pH 4.5 &lt; pKa 7.1.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- [A⁻] is greater than [HA]: Compare pH with pKa.
- [HA] equals [A⁻]: Only at pH = pKa.
- Only HA is present: Both forms are present.
