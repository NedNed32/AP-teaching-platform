Catalysts provide an alternative mechanism with lower activation energy. They speed up forward and reverse reactions equally, so they do not change ΔH or K.

- **Homogeneous**: same phase as reactants.
- **Heterogeneous**: different phase, often a solid surface where reactants adsorb.
- **Enzymes**: proteins with active sites.

<Note title="Catalysts and equilibrium">
A catalyst helps a system reach equilibrium faster but does not shift it.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Explain">
How does a catalytic converter speed up the conversion of CO to CO₂?

1. Gases adsorb on a metal surface, weakening bonds and orienting molecules.

**Answer:** it provides a lower-activation-energy pathway on its surface.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

On a reaction energy profile, the reactants are at 71 kJ/mol, the transition state at 133 kJ/mol and the products at 68 kJ/mol. What is the activation energy of the reverse reaction?

**Answer:** $$65\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$

**Why:** Reverse Ea = transition state − products.

**Working:** Forward Ea = 62, ΔH = -3, reverse Ea = 65 kJ/mol.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$62\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: Measure from the correct starting point.
- $$-3\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: Check the sign and direction.
- $$133\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: Subtract the starting energy.

### Worked exam question 2

On a reaction energy profile, the reactants are at 68 kJ/mol, the transition state at 166 kJ/mol and the products at 3 kJ/mol. What is ΔH for the reaction?

**Answer:** $$-65\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$

**Why:** ΔH = products − reactants.

**Working:** Forward Ea = 98, ΔH = -65, reverse Ea = 163 kJ/mol.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$98\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: Measure from the correct starting point.
- $$65\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: Check the sign and direction.
- $$166\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: Subtract the starting energy.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** For the elementary step 2NO + Cl₂ → 2NOCl, what is the rate law?

**A.** $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{Cl2}]$$. Elementary steps use their coefficients as exponents.

**Q.** How does a catalyst increase the rate of a reaction?

**A.** It provides a pathway with a lower activation energy. The reaction goes by a different mechanism.

**Q.** In a mechanism, a species that is produced in one step and consumed in a later step is a(n):

**A.** Intermediate. It does not appear in the overall equation.

**Q.** Why does increasing temperature increase reaction rate?

**A.** A larger fraction of collisions have energy at or above the activation energy. The Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution shifts.

**Q.** Mechanism: (1) NO₂ + NO₂ → NO₃ + NO (slow); (2) NO₃ + CO → NO₂ + CO₂ (fast). Which rate law is consistent with it?

**A.** $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{NO2}]^2$$. The slow first step determines the rate.
