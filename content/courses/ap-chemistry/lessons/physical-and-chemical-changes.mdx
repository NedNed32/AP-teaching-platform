- **Physical**: intermolecular forces change; chemical identity stays the same (melting, boiling, dissolving molecular solids).
- **Chemical**: covalent or ionic bonds break and form, producing new substances.

Some processes, such as dissolving an ionic solid, break ionic bonds and can be argued either way; explain using bonds and interactions.

<Note title="Justify with bonds">
Explain your classification by what is broken and formed: intermolecular forces or chemical bonds.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classify">
Water boils at 100 °C. Physical or chemical? Why?

1. Hydrogen bonds between molecules break; H₂O molecules remain intact.

**Answer:** physical.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which is a physical change?

**A.** Water boiling at 100 °C. Only intermolecular forces are overcome.

**Q.** How should acetic acid be written in a net ionic equation?

**A.** $$\ce{CH3COOH(aq)}$$, as a whole molecule. It is a weak acid and mostly un-ionized.

**Q.** In HCO₃⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CO₃²⁻ + H₃O⁺, which species is the Brønsted–Lowry base?

**A.** $$\ce{H2O}$$. Water accepts a proton.
