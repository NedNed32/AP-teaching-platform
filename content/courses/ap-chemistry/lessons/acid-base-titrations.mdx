| Titration | Equivalence pH |
| --- | --- |
| strong acid + strong base | 7 |
| weak acid + strong base | above 7 |
| weak base + strong acid | below 7 |

At the half-equivalence point of a weak acid, $$[\ce{HA}] = [\ce{A-}]$$ so $$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a$$. Polyprotic acids show multiple equivalence points.

Choose an indicator whose color change (pKa) is near the equivalence pH.

<Note title="Buffer region">
The flat region before equivalence is where the solution acts as a buffer.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Read a curve">
A weak acid titration curve reaches equivalence at 24.0 mL; at 12.0 mL the pH is 4.20. Find Ka.

1. 12.0 mL is half-equivalence: pKa = 4.20.

**Answer:** $$K_a = 10^{-4.20} = 6.3 \times 10^{-5}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

48 mL of 0.11 M weak acid HA is titrated with 0.27 M NaOH. What volume of NaOH reaches the equivalence point?

**Answer:** $$19.6\ \mathrm{mL}$$

**Why:** Moles of base = moles of acid.

**Working:** 48(0.11)/0.27 = 19.6 mL.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$118\ \mathrm{mL}$$: V_b = M_aV_a/M_b.
- $$9.78\ \mathrm{mL}$$: That is the half-equivalence point.
- $$48\ \mathrm{mL}$$: The concentrations differ.

### Worked exam question 2

A weak acid (pKa 4.87) is titrated with NaOH; the equivalence point is reached at 22 mL. What is the pH after 11 mL of NaOH has been added?

**Answer:** 4.87

**Why:** At the half-equivalence point, [HA] = [A⁻], so pH = pKa.

**Working:** Half-equivalence: pH = pKa = 4.87.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 7.00: That is only for strong acid–strong base equivalence.
- 9.13: pH = pKa, not pKb.
- 2.44: pH equals pKa at half-equivalence.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which is the strongest acid?

**A.** $$\ce{HClO4}$$. More oxygen atoms stabilize the conjugate base.

**Q.** Why is HI a stronger acid than HF?

**A.** The H–I bond is weaker and I⁻ is larger, so H⁺ is released more easily. Bond strength decreases down the group.

**Q.** Two buffers have the same pH, but buffer X has ten times the concentration of each component. Which is true?

**A.** Buffer X has a greater buffer capacity. More acid and base are available to react.

**Q.** Which indicator is best for titrating acetic acid with NaOH (equivalence pH ≈ 8.7)?

**A.** Phenolphthalein (color change around pH 8–10). Its range includes the equivalence pH.

### Free-response practice: Titration of a weak acid

A student titrates 28.0 mL of 0.13 M weak monoprotic acid HA (Ka = 1.4 \times 10^\{-4\}) with 0.11 M NaOH.

**(a)** Write the net ionic equation for the titration reaction.

To earn the point: Correct equation. Model answer: HA(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → A⁻(aq) + H₂O(l)

**(b)** Calculate the initial pH of the acid solution.

To earn the point: Ka expression set up with x = [H₃O⁺]. Model answer: 1.4 \times 10^\{-4\} = x²/0.13

To earn the point: Correct pH. Model answer: x = 4.27 \times 10^\{-3\} M; pH = 2.37

**(c)** Calculate the volume of NaOH needed to reach the equivalence point.

To earn the point: Correct volume. Model answer: 28(0.13)/0.11 = 33.1 mL

**(d)** What is the pH at the half-equivalence point? Justify.

To earn the point: pH = pKa because [HA] = [A⁻]. Model answer: pH = 3.85

**(e)** Calculate the pH at the equivalence point.

To earn the point: Uses Kb = Kw/Ka and the diluted [A⁻]. Model answer: [A⁻] = 0.0596 M; Kb = 7.14 \times 10^\{-11\}

To earn the point: Correct pH (greater than 7). Model answer: pH ≈ 8.31

**(f)** Choose an appropriate indicator from: methyl orange (pKa 3.7), bromothymol blue (pKa 7.0), phenolphthalein (pKa 9.3). Justify.

To earn the point: Indicator whose pKa is closest to the equivalence pH. Model answer: Phenolphthalein.

**(g)** Explain why the solution at the half-equivalence point resists pH change when a small amount of HCl is added.

To earn the point: A⁻ reacts with added H⁺ to form HA (buffer). Model answer: A⁻ + H₃O⁺ → HA + H₂O, so the ratio [A⁻]/[HA] changes only slightly.
