$$\Delta S^\circ = \sum n S^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum n S^\circ(\text{reactants})$$

Units: J/(mol·K). Elements have nonzero S°.

<Note title="Unit mismatch">
S° is in J, ΔH° in kJ. Convert before combining them in ΔG.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Calculate">
Find ΔS° for N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ (S°: N₂ 191.5, H₂ 130.6, NH₃ 192.5 J/(mol·K)).

1. Products: 2(192.5) = 385.
2. Reactants: 191.5 + 3(130.6) = 583.3.

**Answer:** −198.3 J/(mol·K).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** How can an unfavorable reaction (ΔG positive) be made to occur?

**A.** By coupling it to a favorable reaction so the total ΔG is negative. Coupled reactions.

**Q.** A reaction has ΔG° = −50 kJ/mol but does not occur noticeably at room temperature. The best explanation is:

**A.** It has a high activation energy, so it is under kinetic control. Favorable is not the same as fast.

**Q.** Which process has a negative ΔS?

**A.** $$\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) -> 2SO3(g)}$$. Fewer moles of gas.

**Q.** When does a battery's cell potential reach zero?

**A.** When the reaction reaches equilibrium (Q = K). No net driving force remains.

**Q.** In a Zn/Cu galvanic cell, [Zn²⁺] is increased. What happens to the cell potential?

**A.** It decreases. A product's concentration rises, so Q increases.
