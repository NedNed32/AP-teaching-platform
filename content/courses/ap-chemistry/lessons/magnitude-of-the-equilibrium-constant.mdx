| K | At equilibrium |
| --- | --- |
| much greater than 1 | mostly products |
| about 1 | significant amounts of both |
| much less than 1 | mostly reactants |

K describes extent, not speed.

<Note title="Extent, not rate">
A reaction can have a huge K and still be extremely slow.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Interpret">
For a reaction, K = 4.5 × 10⁵. Describe the equilibrium mixture.

1. K is very large.

**Answer:** mostly products.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Adding an inert gas to a reaction mixture at constant volume does what to the equilibrium?

**A.** Nothing; partial pressures of the reacting gases are unchanged. Concentrations are unchanged.

**Q.** For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH negative, which change shifts the equilibrium toward NH₃?

**A.** Decreasing the volume. The product side has fewer moles of gas.

**Q.** A reaction has K = 2 × 10⁻¹². At equilibrium:

**A.** Reactants are greatly favored. Very small K.

**Q.** At equilibrium, which statement is true?

**A.** Forward and reverse reaction rates are equal. Dynamic equilibrium.

**Q.** Which change alters the value of K?

**A.** Changing the temperature. Only temperature changes K.
