If step 1 is fast and reversible and step 2 is slow:

1. $$\ce{2NO <=> N2O2}$$ (fast): $$[\ce{N2O2}] = K[\ce{NO}]^2$$
2. $$\ce{N2O2 + O2 -> 2NO2}$$ (slow): rate = $$k_2[\ce{N2O2}][\ce{O2}]$$

Substitute: rate = $$k[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}]$$.

<Note title="Eliminate intermediates">
Use the fast equilibrium to replace the intermediate with reactant concentrations.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Derive">
Fast equilibrium A ⇌ I; slow step I + B → P. Write the rate law.

1. $$[\ce{I}] = K[\ce{A}]$$.
2. rate = $$k_2K[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]$$.

**Answer:** rate = $$k[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

Initial-rate data for A + B → products:

*Initial rates*

| Trial | [A] (M) | [B] (M) | Initial rate (M/s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.1 | 0.1 | 0.08 |
| 2 | 0.2 | 0.1 | 0.16 |
| 3 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.08 |

What is the rate law?

**Answer:** $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]$$

**Why:** Doubling [A] multiplies the rate by 2; doubling [B] multiplies it by 1.

**Working:** Trials 1→2: rate ×2, so order 1 in A. Trials 1→3: rate ×1, so order 0 in B.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{B}]$$: Compare trials where only one concentration changes.
- $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]$$: Orders are found from the data, not from coefficients.
- $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]^2$$: Recheck how the rate changes when [A] doubles.

### Worked exam question 2

A first-order reaction has a half-life of 36 min. Starting at 4.1 M, what is the concentration after 180 min?

**Answer:** $$0.128\ \mathrm{M}$$

**Why:** 5 half-lives: divide by 2^5.

**Working:** 4.1 × (1/2)^5 = 0.128 M.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.41\ \mathrm{M}$$: Each half-life halves the concentration; it is not divided by 2n.
- $$-6.15\ \mathrm{M}$$: First-order decay is exponential.
- $$0.0641\ \mathrm{M}$$: Count the half-lives.

### Worked exam question 3

A first-order reaction has a rate constant of 0.0169 min⁻¹. What is its half-life?

**Answer:** $$41\ \mathrm{min}$$

**Why:** t½ = 0.693/k.

**Working:** t½ = 0.693/0.0169 = 41 min.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.0244\ \mathrm{min}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$59.2\ \mathrm{min}$$: Use ln 2 = 0.693.
- $$0.0117\ \mathrm{min}$$: Divide by k.

### Worked exam question 4

For $$\ce{N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3}$$, $$\ce{H2}$$ is consumed at 0.71 M/s. At what rate does $$\ce{NH3}$$ form?

**Answer:** $$0.473\ \mathrm{M/s}$$

**Why:** Multiply by the coefficient ratio 2/3.

**Working:** 0.71 × 2/3 = 0.473 M/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.71\ \mathrm{M/s}$$: Rates scale with the coefficients.
- $$1.06\ \mathrm{M/s}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$1.42\ \mathrm{M/s}$$: Divide by the reactant's coefficient too.
