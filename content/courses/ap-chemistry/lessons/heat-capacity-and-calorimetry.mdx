$$q = mc\Delta T$$

In a coffee-cup calorimeter: $$q_{rxn} = -q_{solution}$$. Then $$\Delta H = \frac{q_{rxn}}{n}$$ per mole of the limiting reactant.

Heat lost to the surroundings makes the measured $$\Delta T$$ smaller, so the calculated magnitude of ΔH is too small.

<Note title="Sign conventions">
The solution gains the heat the reaction releases, so the two q values have opposite signs.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Calorimetry">
Mixing 0.050 mol HCl with NaOH in 100. g of solution raises the temperature by 6.8 °C. Find ΔH (c = 4.18).

1. $$q = 100(4.18)(6.8) = 2842$$ J.
2. $$\Delta H = -2.842\ \text{kJ}/0.050\ \text{mol}$$.

**Answer:** about −57 kJ/mol.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

When a reaction involving 0.017 mol of limiting reactant occurs in 214 g of solution (c = 4.18 J/(g·°C)), the temperature falls by 8.8 °C. What is ΔH for the reaction?

**Answer:** $$463\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$

**Why:** q(solution) = mcΔT = 7870 J; q(reaction) = −q(solution); divide by moles.

**Working:** q = 214(4.18)(8.8) = 7870 J; ΔH = 463 kJ/mol.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$-463\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: A temperature rise means the reaction is exothermic (negative ΔH).
- $$7.87\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: Divide by the moles of reactant.
- $$111\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$$: Include the specific heat.

### Worked exam question 2

39 g of water at 73 °C is mixed with 163 g of water at 27 °C in an insulated container. What is the final temperature?

**Answer:** $$35.9\ \mathrm{{}^\circ C}$$

**Why:** Heat lost = heat gained; for water alone, a mass-weighted average.

**Working:** (39·73 + 163·27)/(202) = 35.9 °C.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$50\ \mathrm{{}^\circ C}$$: The masses are different.
- $$64.1\ \mathrm{{}^\circ C}$$: Pair each mass with its own temperature.
- $$46\ \mathrm{{}^\circ C}$$: Compute the weighted average.

### Free-response practice: Dissolving a salt

6.9 g of $$\ce{KNO3}$$ dissolves in 103 g of water in a coffee-cup calorimeter. The temperature falls by 10.3 °C. Assume the solution's specific heat is 4.18 J/(g·°C).

**(a)** Is the dissolution endothermic or exothermic? Justify.

To earn the point: Correct with reasoning. Model answer: Endothermic: the solution cooled, so energy was absorbed.

**(b)** Calculate the heat absorbed or released by the solution.

To earn the point: q = mcΔT with total mass. Model answer: (109.9)(4.18)(10.3) = 4730 J

**(c)** Calculate ΔH of dissolution in kJ/mol.

To earn the point: Correct value and sign. Model answer: 0.0682 mol; ΔH = 69.3 kJ/mol

**(d)** If some heat escaped to the surroundings, would the magnitude of the calculated ΔH be too large or too small? Explain.

To earn the point: Too small, because the measured temperature change is smaller than the true change. Model answer: Less ΔT → smaller q.
