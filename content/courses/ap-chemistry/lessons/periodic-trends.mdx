| Trend | Across a period (left → right) | Down a group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| atomic radius | decreases | increases |
| ionization energy | increases | decreases |
| electronegativity | increases | decreases |

Explain with Coulomb's law: across a period, more protons with similar shielding pull electrons closer; down a group, electrons occupy higher shells farther from the nucleus.

Cations are smaller than their atoms; anions are larger.

<Note title="Explain, do not just state">
AP answers need the reason: number of protons, shielding by core electrons, and distance from the nucleus.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Successive ionization energies">
An element's first three ionization energies are 738, 1451 and 7733 kJ/mol. Which group is it in?

1. The big jump is after the second electron.
2. So it has 2 valence electrons.

**Answer:** Group 2 (it is magnesium).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The first four ionization energies of an element are 579, 4053, 7052, 12129 kJ/mol. In which group of the periodic table is the element most likely found?

**Answer:** Group 1

**Why:** The large jump comes after removing 1 electron, so it has 1 valence electron.

**Working:** The big jump marks the start of removing core electrons.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Group 2: Locate the largest jump between successive ionization energies.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which formula is most likely for the compound of element X (Group 2) and element Y (Group 16)?

**A.** $$\ce{XY}$$. X²⁺ and Y²⁻ combine 1 : 1.

**Q.** Why is the radius of F⁻ larger than that of F?

**A.** The added electron increases electron–electron repulsion with the same nuclear charge. Anions are larger than their atoms.

**Q.** Which element has the highest electronegativity?

**A.** Fluorine. Electronegativity increases up and to the right.

**Q.** Which best explains why the atomic radius of Cl is smaller than that of Na?

**A.** Cl has more protons with similar shielding, so its valence electrons are pulled closer. Greater effective nuclear charge across a period.

**Q.** Which has a larger first ionization energy, Mg or Ba, and why?

**A.** Mg, because its valence electrons are closer to the nucleus. Down a group, electrons are farther away and more shielded.

### Free-response practice: Photoelectron spectra

The photoelectron spectra of Mg (Z = 12) and Al (Z = 13) are compared.

**(a)** Write the electron configuration of Al.

To earn the point: Correct configuration. Model answer: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p¹

**(b)** Explain why the 1s peak of Al has a higher binding energy than the 1s peak of Mg.

To earn the point: Al has more protons, so 1s electrons are attracted more strongly (Coulomb's law). Model answer: 13 vs 12 protons.

**(c)** Describe one difference in the number or relative height of peaks between the two spectra.

To earn the point: A valid difference based on configurations. Model answer: Al has one more electron, so its outermost peak is taller or an extra peak appears.

**(d)** Which element has the larger atomic radius? Justify using Coulomb's law.

To earn the point: Mg: fewer protons with similar shielding, so valence electrons are held less tightly. Model answer: Same period; effective nuclear charge increases across.
