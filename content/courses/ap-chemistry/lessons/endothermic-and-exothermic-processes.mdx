Energy flows between the **system** and **surroundings**.

| Process | Sign of ΔH | Surroundings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| exothermic | negative | warm up |
| endothermic | positive | cool down |

Bond breaking absorbs energy; bond forming releases energy.

<Note title="Temperature tells you">
A temperature drop in the solution means the process absorbed energy from it: endothermic.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classify">
When NH₄NO₃ dissolves, the solution temperature falls. Classify the process.

1. Energy flows from the water into the dissolving process.

**Answer:** endothermic.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Why does temperature remain constant while ice melts?

**A.** Added energy overcomes intermolecular forces rather than increasing kinetic energy. Phase changes occur at constant temperature.

**Q.** If ΔH = −286 kJ for H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O(l), what is ΔH for 2H₂O(l) → 2H₂ + O₂?

**A.** +572 kJ. Reverse (change sign) and double.

**Q.** Why is the enthalpy of vaporization of water much larger than its enthalpy of fusion?

**A.** Vaporization separates molecules completely, overcoming all intermolecular forces. Melting only loosens them.

**Q.** When a salt dissolves in water, the temperature of the solution decreases. Which is true?

**A.** The process is endothermic; energy flows from the water into the dissolving process. The surroundings lose energy.

### Free-response practice: Dissolving a salt

6.9 g of $$\ce{KNO3}$$ dissolves in 103 g of water in a coffee-cup calorimeter. The temperature falls by 10.3 °C. Assume the solution's specific heat is 4.18 J/(g·°C).

**(a)** Is the dissolution endothermic or exothermic? Justify.

To earn the point: Correct with reasoning. Model answer: Endothermic: the solution cooled, so energy was absorbed.

**(b)** Calculate the heat absorbed or released by the solution.

To earn the point: q = mcΔT with total mass. Model answer: (109.9)(4.18)(10.3) = 4730 J

**(c)** Calculate ΔH of dissolution in kJ/mol.

To earn the point: Correct value and sign. Model answer: 0.0682 mol; ΔH = 69.3 kJ/mol

**(d)** If some heat escaped to the surroundings, would the magnitude of the calculated ΔH be too large or too small? Explain.

To earn the point: Too small, because the measured temperature change is smaller than the true change. Model answer: Less ΔT → smaller q.
