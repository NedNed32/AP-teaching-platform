Manipulate given equations so they add up to the target:

- reverse an equation → change the sign of ΔH;
- multiply by a factor → multiply ΔH by that factor.

Then add the equations and their ΔH values.

<Note title="Match the target">
Arrange each given equation so its substances appear on the same side as in the target equation.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Combine">
C + O₂ → CO₂ (−394 kJ); CO + ½O₂ → CO₂ (−283 kJ). Find ΔH for C + ½O₂ → CO.

1. Keep the first; reverse the second (+283).

**Answer:** −394 + 283 = −111 kJ.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Reaction 1 has ΔH₁ = -282 kJ and reaction 2 has ΔH₂ = 183 kJ. A target reaction is obtained by doubling reaction 1 and adding reaction 2. What is ΔH for the target reaction?

**Answer:** $$-381\ \mathrm{kJ}$$

**Why:** Reverse → change sign; multiply → multiply ΔH; then add.

**Working:** 2(-282) + (183) = -381 kJ.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$-99\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: Doubling reaction 1 doubles ΔH₁.
- $$-747\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: Reversing changes the sign of ΔH₂.
- $$-1.03 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: Enthalpies add; they do not multiply.
