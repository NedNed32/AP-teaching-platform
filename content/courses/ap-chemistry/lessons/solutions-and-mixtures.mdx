$$M = \frac{n_{solute}}{V_{solution}\ (\text{L})} \qquad M_1V_1 = M_2V_2$$

Solutions are homogeneous mixtures; suspensions and colloids are heterogeneous.

To prepare a solution: weigh the solute, dissolve in less solvent, then dilute to the mark in a volumetric flask.

<Note title="Ions in solution">
1.0 M CaCl₂ contains 1.0 M Ca²⁺ and 2.0 M Cl⁻.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Dilution">
25.0 mL of 2.00 M NaOH is diluted to 100.0 mL. What is the new concentration?

1. $$(2.00)(25.0) = M_2(100.0)$$.

**Answer:** 0.500 M.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

22 mL of 6.6 M solution is diluted to 290 mL. What is the new concentration?

**Answer:** $$0.501\ \mathrm{M}$$

**Why:** M₁V₁ = M₂V₂.

**Working:** M₂ = (6.6)(22)/290 = 0.501 M.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$87\ \mathrm{M}$$: Dilution decreases concentration.
- $$4.83\ \mathrm{M}$$: Use M₁V₁ = M₂V₂.
- $$0.542\ \mathrm{M}$$: V₂ is the total final volume.

### Worked exam question 2

What is the molarity of a solution made by dissolving 0.09 mol of solute in enough water to make 522 mL of solution?

**Answer:** $$0.172\ \mathrm{M}$$

**Why:** M = mol ÷ liters.

**Working:** 0.09 ÷ 0.522 L = 0.172 M.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.72 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{M}$$: Convert mL to L.
- $$0.047\ \mathrm{M}$$: Divide by volume.
- $$5.8\ \mathrm{M}$$: The ratio is inverted.
