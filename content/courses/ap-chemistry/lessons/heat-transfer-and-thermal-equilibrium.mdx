Heat flows from higher to lower temperature until thermal equilibrium. In an isolated system:

$$q_{\text{lost}} = -q_{\text{gained}}$$

Particle view: faster-moving particles collide with slower ones, transferring kinetic energy.

<Note title="Conservation of energy">
Heat lost by the hot object equals heat gained by the cold object (if no heat escapes).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Final temperature">
50.0 g of water at 80.0 °C mixes with 50.0 g of water at 20.0 °C. Find the final temperature.

1. Equal masses of the same substance.

**Answer:** 50.0 °C.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

39 g of water at 73 °C is mixed with 163 g of water at 27 °C in an insulated container. What is the final temperature?

**Answer:** $$35.9\ \mathrm{{}^\circ C}$$

**Why:** Heat lost = heat gained; for water alone, a mass-weighted average.

**Working:** (39·73 + 163·27)/(202) = 35.9 °C.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$50\ \mathrm{{}^\circ C}$$: The masses are different.
- $$64.1\ \mathrm{{}^\circ C}$$: Pair each mass with its own temperature.
- $$46\ \mathrm{{}^\circ C}$$: Compute the weighted average.
