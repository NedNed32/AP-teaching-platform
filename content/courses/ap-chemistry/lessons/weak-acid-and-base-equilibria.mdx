$$\ce{HA + H2O <=> H3O+ + A-} \qquad K_a = \frac{[\ce{H3O+}][\ce{A-}]}{[\ce{HA}]}$$

With $$x = [\ce{H3O+}]$$: $$K_a \approx \frac{x^2}{C}$$, so $$x = \sqrt{K_aC}$$.

For weak bases: $$K_b$$, $$[\ce{OH-}] = \sqrt{K_bC}$$. For a conjugate pair, $$K_aK_b = K_w$$.

<Note title="Strength versus concentration">
A strong acid ionizes completely; a concentrated acid has lots of solute. These are different ideas.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Weak acid pH">
Find the pH of 0.10 M acetic acid (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵).

1. $$x = \sqrt{1.8 \times 10^{-6}} = 1.34 \times 10^{-3}$$.

**Answer:** pH = 2.87.
</WorkedExample>

### Free-response practice: Titration of a weak acid

A student titrates 28.0 mL of 0.13 M weak monoprotic acid HA (Ka = 1.4 \times 10^\{-4\}) with 0.11 M NaOH.

**(a)** Write the net ionic equation for the titration reaction.

To earn the point: Correct equation. Model answer: HA(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → A⁻(aq) + H₂O(l)

**(b)** Calculate the initial pH of the acid solution.

To earn the point: Ka expression set up with x = [H₃O⁺]. Model answer: 1.4 \times 10^\{-4\} = x²/0.13

To earn the point: Correct pH. Model answer: x = 4.27 \times 10^\{-3\} M; pH = 2.37

**(c)** Calculate the volume of NaOH needed to reach the equivalence point.

To earn the point: Correct volume. Model answer: 28(0.13)/0.11 = 33.1 mL

**(d)** What is the pH at the half-equivalence point? Justify.

To earn the point: pH = pKa because [HA] = [A⁻]. Model answer: pH = 3.85

**(e)** Calculate the pH at the equivalence point.

To earn the point: Uses Kb = Kw/Ka and the diluted [A⁻]. Model answer: [A⁻] = 0.0596 M; Kb = 7.14 \times 10^\{-11\}

To earn the point: Correct pH (greater than 7). Model answer: pH ≈ 8.31

**(f)** Choose an appropriate indicator from: methyl orange (pKa 3.7), bromothymol blue (pKa 7.0), phenolphthalein (pKa 9.3). Justify.

To earn the point: Indicator whose pKa is closest to the equivalence pH. Model answer: Phenolphthalein.

**(g)** Explain why the solution at the half-equivalence point resists pH change when a small amount of HCl is added.

To earn the point: A⁻ reacts with added H⁺ to form HA (buffer). Model answer: A⁻ + H₃O⁺ → HA + H₂O, so the ratio [A⁻]/[HA] changes only slightly.
