$$\Delta H_{soln} = (\text{energy to separate solute and solvent particles}) - (\text{energy released forming solute–solvent interactions})$$

Many salts dissolve endothermically because entropy increases enough to make ΔG negative.

<Note title="Both terms">
Explain favorability with both ΔH (interactions) and ΔS (dispersal).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Explain">
Ammonium nitrate dissolves spontaneously although the solution gets cold. Explain.

1. ΔH is positive.
2. ΔS is positive and TΔS outweighs ΔH.

**Answer:** the entropy increase makes ΔG negative.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

For a reaction, ΔH° = -92 kJ/mol and ΔS° = -176 J/(mol·K). What is ΔG° at 809 K, and is the reaction favorable?

**Answer:** 50.4 kJ/mol; not favorable

**Why:** ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, with ΔS in kJ/(mol·K).

**Working:** ΔG = -92 − 809(-0.176) = 50.4 kJ/mol.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1.42 \times 10^\{5\} kJ/mol; not favorable: Convert ΔS from J to kJ.
- -234 kJ/mol; favorable: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS (subtract).
- 50.4 kJ/mol; favorable: Negative ΔG means favorable.

### Worked exam question 2

A reaction has ΔH° = 25 kJ/mol and ΔS° = 206 J/(mol·K). Above what temperature is it thermodynamically favorable?

**Answer:** $$121\ \mathrm{K}$$

**Why:** Set ΔG = 0: T = ΔH/ΔS (with ΔH in J).

**Working:** T = 25000/206 = 121 K.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.121\ \mathrm{K}$$: Convert ΔH to J (or ΔS to kJ).
- $$8240\ \mathrm{K}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$-152\ \mathrm{K}$$: The answer is in kelvins.

### Worked exam question 3

*Standard reduction potentials*

| Half-reaction | E° (V) |
| --- | --- |
| $$\ce{Zn^{2+} + 2e- -> Zn}$$ | -0.76 |
| $$\ce{Pb^{2+} + 2e- -> Pb}$$ | -0.13 |

A galvanic cell is built from Zn/$$\ce{Zn^{2+}}$$ and Pb/$$\ce{Pb^{2+}}$$ half-cells. Which electrode is the anode?

**Answer:** Zn, where oxidation occurs

**Why:** The half-reaction with the lower reduction potential runs as oxidation at the anode.

**Working:** Zn (E° -0.76 V) is oxidized at the anode; Pb is reduced at the cathode.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Pb, where oxidation occurs: The higher potential is reduced at the cathode.
- Zn, where reduction occurs: Reduction happens at the cathode.
- Pb, where reduction occurs at the anode: Reduction occurs at the cathode, not the anode.

### Worked exam question 4

*Standard reduction potentials*

| Half-reaction | E° (V) |
| --- | --- |
| $$\ce{Al^{3+} + 3e- -> Al}$$ | -1.66 |
| $$\ce{Cu^{2+} + 2e- -> Cu}$$ | 0.34 |

For the galvanic cell made from these half-cells, what is ΔG°? (F = 96,485 C/mol)

**Answer:** $$-1160\ \mathrm{kJ}$$

**Why:** ΔG° = −nFE° with n = 6 electrons transferred and E° = 2 V.

**Working:** ΔG° = −(6)(96485)(2) = -1160 kJ.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1160\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: ΔG° is negative for a positive E°.
- $$-193\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: n = 6 electrons are transferred.
- $$-1.16 \times 10^{6}\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: Convert J to kJ.

### Worked exam question 5

*Standard reduction potentials*

| Half-reaction | E° (V) |
| --- | --- |
| $$\ce{Cu^{2+} + 2e- -> Cu}$$ | 0.34 |
| $$\ce{Ag+ + e- -> Ag}$$ | 0.8 |

What is E°cell for a galvanic cell made from these half-cells?

**Answer:** $$0.46\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode.

**Working:** 0.8 − (0.34) = 0.46 V.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.14\ \mathrm{V}$$: Subtract the anode's potential.
- $$-0.46\ \mathrm{V}$$: Cathode minus anode; a galvanic cell is positive.
- $$0.92\ \mathrm{V}$$: E° does not change when half-reactions are multiplied.
