As two atoms approach, potential energy falls (attraction) until a minimum at the **bond length**, then rises sharply (repulsion).

- Deeper wells mean **stronger** bonds.
- Multiple bonds are shorter and stronger than single bonds.
- Larger atoms form longer bonds.

For ionic solids, lattice energy increases with larger ion charges and smaller ion sizes (Coulomb's law).

<Note title="Charge beats size">
Ion charge usually affects lattice energy more than ion size: MgO has a much larger lattice energy than NaCl.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Compare lattice energies">
Which has the greater lattice energy, NaF or MgO?

1. MgO ions have charges ±2 versus ±1.
2. Ions are similar in size.

**Answer:** MgO.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** Which compound has the greater lattice energy, $$\ce{NaCl}$$ or $$\ce{MgF2}$$?

**A.** $$\ce{MgF2}$$, because its ions have larger charges. By Coulomb's law, attraction increases with the product of the ion charges. MgF2 has ion charges 2 and 1; NaCl has 1 and 1.
