Strong acids and bases ionize completely:

- $$[\ce{H3O+}] = [\text{acid}]$$ for monoprotic strong acids.
- $$[\ce{OH-}] = n \times [\text{base}]$$ for bases like $$\ce{Ba(OH)2}$$ (n = 2).

A tenfold dilution raises the pH of a strong acid by 1.

<Note title="Count hydroxides">
Group 2 hydroxides release two OH⁻ per formula unit.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Strong base">
Find the pH of 0.020 M NaOH.

1. pOH = −log(0.020) = 1.70.

**Answer:** pH = 12.30.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

What is the pH of a 0.05 M solution of $$\ce{Ca(OH)2}$$ at 25 °C?

**Answer:** 13.00

**Why:** Strong base: [OH⁻] = 2 × 0.05 M; pH = 14 − pOH.

**Working:** pH = 13.00.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1.00: That is the pOH; subtract from 14.
- 12.70: Each formula unit releases two OH⁻.
- 11.00: Strong acids and bases ionize completely; no equilibrium is needed.
