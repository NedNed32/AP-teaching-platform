Ionic solids are lattices of alternating cations and anions.

| Property | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| high melting points | strong electrostatic attractions |
| brittle | shifted layers put like charges side by side |
| conduct when molten or dissolved | ions are free to move |
| do not conduct as solids | ions are fixed in place |

<Note title="Particle diagrams">
Draw cations and anions alternating, with smaller cations for small ions like Na⁺ and larger anions like Cl⁻.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Explain a property">
Why does NaCl have a much higher melting point than ice?

1. NaCl: strong ionic attractions throughout the lattice.
2. Ice: weaker hydrogen bonds between molecules.

**Answer:** ionic bonds in NaCl are much stronger than ice's intermolecular forces.
</WorkedExample>

**A.** $$\ce{MgF2}$$, because its ions have larger charges. By Coulomb's law, attraction increases with the product of the ion charges. MgF2 has ion charges 2 and 1; NaCl has 1 and 1.

**Q.** Steel is harder than pure iron because:

**A.** Small carbon atoms in the gaps (interstitial alloy) hinder layers of iron atoms from sliding. Interstitial atoms disrupt slipping.

**Q.** Why is solid NaCl a poor conductor while molten NaCl conducts?

**A.** Ions are fixed in the solid but mobile in the liquid. Conduction needs mobile charges.

**Q.** Which Lewis structure is preferred when formal charges are considered?

**A.** The one with formal charges closest to zero and any negative charge on the most electronegative atom. That is the formal charge criterion.

**Q.** Why are metals good conductors of electricity?

**A.** Their valence electrons are delocalized and free to move. The electron-sea model.

**Q.** The nitrate ion has three resonance structures. What is true of its N–O bonds?

**A.** All three are identical, intermediate between single and double bonds. Resonance averages the bonds.
