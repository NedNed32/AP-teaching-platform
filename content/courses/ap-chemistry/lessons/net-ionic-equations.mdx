1. Write the balanced molecular equation.
2. Split strong electrolytes in solution into ions.
3. Cancel spectator ions.

Example: $$\ce{Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2KI(aq) -> PbI2(s) + 2KNO3(aq)}$$

Net ionic: $$\ce{Pb^{2+}(aq) + 2I^-(aq) -> PbI2(s)}$$

Solids, liquids, gases and weak electrolytes are written as whole formulas. All sodium, potassium, ammonium and nitrate salts are soluble.

<Note title="Weak acids stay together">
Write acetic acid as CH₃COOH, not as separate ions, because it is a weak acid.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Net ionic">
Write the net ionic equation for HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq).

1. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are spectators.

**Answer:** $$\ce{H+(aq) + OH-(aq) -> H2O(l)}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which is a physical change?

**A.** Water boiling at 100 °C. Only intermolecular forces are overcome.

**Q.** How should acetic acid be written in a net ionic equation?

**A.** $$\ce{CH3COOH(aq)}$$, as a whole molecule. It is a weak acid and mostly un-ionized.

**Q.** In HCO₃⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CO₃²⁻ + H₃O⁺, which species is the Brønsted–Lowry base?

**A.** $$\ce{H2O}$$. Water accepts a proton.
