$$\text{pH} = -\log[\ce{H3O+}] \qquad \text{pOH} = -\log[\ce{OH-}] \qquad \text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14 \ (25\,^\circ\text{C})$$

Water autoionizes: $$\ce{2H2O <=> H3O+ + OH-}$$, $$K_w = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$$. Kw increases with temperature, so neutral pH is below 7 at higher temperatures.

<Note title="Neutral is not always 7">
Neutral means [H₃O⁺] = [OH⁻]; that is pH 7 only at 25 °C.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Find pH">
[OH⁻] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ M. Find the pH.

1. pOH = −log(2.0 × 10⁻⁴) = 3.70.

**Answer:** pH = 10.30.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

What is the pH of a 0.05 M solution of $$\ce{Ca(OH)2}$$ at 25 °C?

**Answer:** 13.00

**Why:** Strong base: [OH⁻] = 2 × 0.05 M; pH = 14 − pOH.

**Working:** pH = 13.00.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1.00: That is the pOH; subtract from 14.
- 12.70: Each formula unit releases two OH⁻.
- 11.00: Strong acids and bases ionize completely; no equilibrium is needed.
