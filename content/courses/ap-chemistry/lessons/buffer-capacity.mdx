Capacity increases with the **amounts** of HA and A⁻. Two buffers with the same ratio have the same pH, but the more concentrated one resists change longer.

A buffer works best within about ±1 pH unit of its pKa.

<Note title="pH versus capacity">
The ratio sets pH; the amounts set capacity.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Choose">
To make a buffer at pH 9.2, should you use acetic acid (pKa 4.7) or NH₄⁺ (pKa 9.2)?

1. Choose pKa close to the target pH.

**Answer:** NH₄⁺/NH₃.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which is the strongest acid?

**A.** $$\ce{HClO4}$$. More oxygen atoms stabilize the conjugate base.

**Q.** Why is HI a stronger acid than HF?

**A.** The H–I bond is weaker and I⁻ is larger, so H⁺ is released more easily. Bond strength decreases down the group.

**Q.** Two buffers have the same pH, but buffer X has ten times the concentration of each component. Which is true?

**A.** Buffer X has a greater buffer capacity. More acid and base are available to react.

**Q.** Which indicator is best for titrating acetic acid with NaOH (equivalence pH ≈ 8.7)?

**A.** Phenolphthalein (color change around pH 8–10). Its range includes the equivalence pH.
