$$E = h\nu \qquad c = \lambda\nu \qquad E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

An electron transition between energy levels absorbs or emits a photon whose energy equals the energy difference.

<Note title="Units">
Convert nm to m (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m) before using c = λν.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Photon energy">
Find the energy of a photon with a wavelength of 500 nm.

1. $$E = \frac{(6.626 \times 10^{-34})(3.00 \times 10^8)}{500 \times 10^{-9}}$$.

**Answer:** $$3.98 \times 10^{-19}$$ J.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

What is the energy of a photon with a wavelength of 609 nm?

**Answer:** $$3.26 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}$$

**Why:** E = hc/λ, with λ in meters.

**Working:** E = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)(3.00 × 10⁸)/(609 × 10⁻⁹) = 3.26 \times 10^\{-19\} J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3.26 \times 10^{-28}\ \mathrm{J}$$: Convert nm to m.
- $$4.04 \times 10^{-40}\ \mathrm{J}$$: E = hc/λ.
- $$1.35 \times 10^{-48}\ \mathrm{J}$$: E = hc/λ.
