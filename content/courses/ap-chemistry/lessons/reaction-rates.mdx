$$\text{rate} = -\frac{1}{a}\frac{\Delta[\ce{A}]}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{b}\frac{\Delta[\ce{B}]}{\Delta t}$$

Rates increase with concentration, temperature, surface area and catalysts. Rates are measured by tracking concentration, pressure, color (absorbance) or volume of gas over time.

<Note title="Stoichiometry of rates">
Divide each species' rate by its coefficient to get a single reaction rate.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Relate rates">
In N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, H₂ is consumed at 0.30 M/s. How fast does NH₃ form?

1. $$0.30 \times \tfrac{2}{3}$$.

**Answer:** 0.20 M/s.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

For $$\ce{N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3}$$, $$\ce{H2}$$ is consumed at 0.71 M/s. At what rate does $$\ce{NH3}$$ form?

**Answer:** $$0.473\ \mathrm{M/s}$$

**Why:** Multiply by the coefficient ratio 2/3.

**Working:** 0.71 × 2/3 = 0.473 M/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.71\ \mathrm{M/s}$$: Rates scale with the coefficients.
- $$1.06\ \mathrm{M/s}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$1.42\ \mathrm{M/s}$$: Divide by the reactant's coefficient too.
