Chemical reactions rearrange atoms by breaking and forming bonds; new substances form. Evidence includes:

- formation of a gas or precipitate;
- color change;
- temperature change or light emission.

No single observation proves a chemical change; boiling also produces gas, for example.

<Note title="Conservation">
Atoms are conserved in every reaction, so equations must be balanced.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classify">
Mixing two clear solutions produces a yellow solid. What does this suggest?

1. A new insoluble substance (precipitate) formed.

**Answer:** a chemical reaction (precipitation).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

For $$\ce{N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3}$$, 87 g of $$\ce{N2}$$ reacts with 11 g of $$\ce{H2}$$. What mass of $$\ce{NH3}$$ can form?

**Answer:** $$62\ \mathrm{g}$$

**Why:** H2 is the limiting reactant.

**Working:** N2: 3.1 mol → 6.21 mol NH3; H2: 5.46 mol → 3.64 mol. 3.64 mol × 17.03 = 62 g.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$106\ \mathrm{g}$$: Use the limiting reactant, which gives less product.
- $$98\ \mathrm{g}$$: Not all of both reactants reacts.
- $$52.9\ \mathrm{g}$$: Apply the mole ratio from the equation.

### Worked exam question 2

A 48.9 mL sample of $$\ce{H2SO4}$$ is titrated to the equivalence point with 18.1 mL of 0.262 M NaOH. What is the concentration of the acid?

**Answer:** $$0.0485\ \mathrm{M}$$

**Why:** Each H₂SO₄ reacts with two OH⁻, so divide the moles of NaOH by 2.

**Working:** mol OH⁻ = 4.74 \times 10^\{-3\}; mol acid = 2.37 \times 10^\{-3\}; ÷ 0.0489 L = 0.0485 M.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.097\ \mathrm{M}$$: H₂SO₄ is diprotic.
- $$0.708\ \mathrm{M}$$: M_a = M_b V_b / V_a.
- $$0.194\ \mathrm{M}$$: Check the mole ratio.
