| Change to equation | New K |
| --- | --- |
| reverse | $$1/K$$ |
| multiply by n | $$K^n$$ |
| add reactions | $$K_1 \times K_2$$ |

<Note title="Different from ΔH">
ΔH adds and multiplies by n; K multiplies and is raised to the power n.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Combine">
K₁ = 2.0 for A ⇌ B, K₂ = 5.0 for B ⇌ C. Find K for 2A ⇌ 2C.

1. A ⇌ C: 2.0 × 5.0 = 10.
2. Doubled: $$10^2$$.

**Answer:** 100.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A reaction has K = 5. What is K for the reaction reversed and multiplied by 2?

**Answer:** $$0.04\ \mathrm{}$$

**Why:** Reverse → 1/K; multiply by n → Kⁿ.

**Working:** K' = 0.04.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$10\ \mathrm{}$$: K is raised to a power, not multiplied.
- $$0.2\ \mathrm{}$$: Reversing gives the reciprocal.
- $$125\ \mathrm{}$$: Multiplying by n raises K to the nth power.

### Worked exam question 2

A reaction has K = 26. What is K for the reaction multiplied by 2?

**Answer:** $$676\ \mathrm{}$$

**Why:** Reverse → 1/K; multiply by n → Kⁿ.

**Working:** K' = 676.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$52\ \mathrm{}$$: K is raised to a power, not multiplied.
- $$0.0385\ \mathrm{}$$: Reversing gives the reciprocal.
- $$1.76 \times 10^{4}\ \mathrm{}$$: Multiplying by n raises K to the nth power.

### Worked exam question 3

A reaction has K = 9. What is K for the reaction multiplied by ½?

**Why:** Reverse → 1/K; multiply by n → Kⁿ.

**Working:** K' = 3.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.111\ \mathrm{}$$: Reversing gives the reciprocal.
- $$4.5\ \mathrm{}$$: Multiplying by n raises K to the nth power.
