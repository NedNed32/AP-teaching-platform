## Steps

1. Count total valence electrons (add for negative charge, subtract for positive).
2. Place the least electronegative atom in the center (never H).
3. Connect atoms with single bonds.
4. Complete octets on outer atoms, then the central atom.
5. Use multiple bonds if the central atom lacks an octet.

Example: $$\ce{CO2}$$ has 16 valence electrons and two double bonds: O=C=O.

Exceptions: incomplete octets (B, Be), expanded octets (period 3 and below), odd-electron species (NO).

<Note title="Check your count">
The number of electrons drawn must equal the total valence electrons.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Draw">
Draw the Lewis diagram of NH₃.

1. 5 + 3(1) = 8 electrons.
2. N central with three single bonds to H.
3. One lone pair on N.

**Answer:** N with three N–H bonds and one lone pair.
</WorkedExample>

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** How many valence electrons are shown in the Lewis structure of $$\ce{PCl3}$$?

**A.** 26. Sum the valence electrons of all atoms, then add one per negative charge (subtract one per positive charge). PCl3 has 26 valence electrons.
