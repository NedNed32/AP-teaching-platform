Starting from reactants only, the forward rate is initially large and falls; the reverse rate starts at zero and rises. They become equal at equilibrium.

Equilibrium can be approached from either direction and reaches the same state for the same total composition and temperature.

<Note title="Rates versus concentrations">
Rates depend on concentrations; as reactants are used up, the forward rate falls.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Describe">
A reaction starts with only products. Describe the rates over time.

1. Reverse rate is high initially and falls; forward rate rises from zero.

**Answer:** they converge until equal at equilibrium.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

For $$\ce{A(g) + B(g) <=> 2C(g)}$$, K = 6.6. A mixture has [A] = 0.4 M, [B] = 0.9 M and [C] = 0.2 M. In which direction will the reaction proceed?

**Answer:** It proceeds forward, toward products, because Q = 0.111

**Why:** Q = [C]²/([A][B]) = 0.111, which is less than K.

**Working:** Q = 0.2²/(0.4·0.9) = 0.111; K = 6.6.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- It proceeds in reverse, toward reactants, because Q = 0.111: If Q is less than K, the reaction proceeds forward.
- It is at equilibrium, because Q = K: Q = 0.111 ≠ 6.6.
- It proceeds forward, toward products, because Q = 0.556: Square [C], since its coefficient is 2.

### Worked exam question 2

A reaction has K = 5. What is K for the reaction reversed and multiplied by 2?

**Answer:** $$0.04\ \mathrm{}$$

**Why:** Reverse → 1/K; multiply by n → Kⁿ.

**Working:** K' = 0.04.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$10\ \mathrm{}$$: K is raised to a power, not multiplied.
- $$0.2\ \mathrm{}$$: Reversing gives the reciprocal.
- $$125\ \mathrm{}$$: Multiplying by n raises K to the nth power.

**Q.** The Ksp of $$\ce{PbI2}$$ is 7.1 \times 10^\{-9\}. What is its molar solubility in pure water?

**Q.** The Ksp of $$\ce{AgBr}$$ is 5 \times 10^\{-13\}. What is its molar solubility in a solution that already contains 0.05 M of its anion?
