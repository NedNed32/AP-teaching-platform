Strong acid + strong base: neutral at equivalence. Weak acid + strong base: basic at equivalence. Strong acid + weak base: acidic.

A **buffer** contains comparable amounts of a weak acid and its conjugate base. Added $$\ce{H+}$$ reacts with $$\ce{A-}$$; added $$\ce{OH-}$$ reacts with $$\ce{HA}$$.

A buffer can be made by partially neutralizing a weak acid with a strong base.

<Note title="Reaction first, equilibrium second">
When a strong acid or base is added, first do the complete neutralization (stoichiometry), then the equilibrium.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Buffer reaction">
Write the reaction when HCl is added to an acetate buffer.

1. H⁺ reacts with the base component.

**Answer:** $$\ce{H+ + CH3COO- -> CH3COOH}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A buffer contains 0.4 mol of a weak acid HA (pKa 3.75) and 0.33 mol of its conjugate base A⁻. Then 0.13 mol of NaOH is added. What is the pH?

**Answer:** 3.98

**Why:** First react: OH⁻ converts HA to A⁻. Then pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).

**Working:** ratio = 0.46/0.27; pH = 3.75 + log(1.7) = 3.98.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 3.52: The ratio is [A⁻]/[HA].
- 3.67: Update moles after the added acid or base reacts.
- 3.75: Only true when [A⁻] = [HA].

### Worked exam question 2

A buffer contains 0.21 mol of a weak acid HA (pKa 6.35) and 0.05 mol of A⁻. What is its pH?

**Answer:** 5.73

**Why:** pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).

**Working:** ratio = 0.05/0.21; pH = 6.35 + log(0.238) = 5.73.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 6.97: The ratio is [A⁻]/[HA].
- 6.35: Only true when [A⁻] = [HA].
- 6.03: Recompute the ratio.
