$$\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ$$

| ΔH | ΔS | Favorable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| − | + | always |
| + | − | never |
| − | − | at low T |
| + | + | at high T |

Crossover temperature: $$T = \Delta H/\Delta S$$.

<Note title="Convert units">
Divide ΔS (J/K) by 1000 before combining with ΔH (kJ).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Calculate">
ΔH° = −92 kJ, ΔS° = −0.198 kJ/K. Is the reaction favorable at 298 K?

1. $$\Delta G = -92 - 298(-0.198) = -92 + 59 = -33$$ kJ.

**Answer:** yes, ΔG = −33 kJ.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

For a reaction, ΔH° = -92 kJ/mol and ΔS° = -176 J/(mol·K). What is ΔG° at 809 K, and is the reaction favorable?

**Answer:** 50.4 kJ/mol; not favorable

**Why:** ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, with ΔS in kJ/(mol·K).

**Working:** ΔG = -92 − 809(-0.176) = 50.4 kJ/mol.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1.42 \times 10^\{5\} kJ/mol; not favorable: Convert ΔS from J to kJ.
- -234 kJ/mol; favorable: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS (subtract).
- 50.4 kJ/mol; favorable: Negative ΔG means favorable.

### Worked exam question 2

A reaction has ΔH° = 25 kJ/mol and ΔS° = 206 J/(mol·K). Above what temperature is it thermodynamically favorable?

**Answer:** $$121\ \mathrm{K}$$

**Why:** Set ΔG = 0: T = ΔH/ΔS (with ΔH in J).

**Working:** T = 25000/206 = 121 K.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.121\ \mathrm{K}$$: Convert ΔH to J (or ΔS to kJ).
- $$8240\ \mathrm{K}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$-152\ \mathrm{K}$$: The answer is in kelvins.
