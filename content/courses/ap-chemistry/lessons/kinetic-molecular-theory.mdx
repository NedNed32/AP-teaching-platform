Average kinetic energy depends only on temperature: $$KE_{avg} \propto T$$.

At the same temperature, lighter gases move faster ($$KE = \tfrac12 mv^2$$).

Maxwell–Boltzmann curves: higher temperature or lower molar mass gives a broader curve shifted to higher speeds; the area under each curve stays the same.

<Note title="Same temperature, same energy">
Different gases at the same temperature have the same average kinetic energy, not the same average speed.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Compare">
Gases H₂ and O₂ are at the same temperature. Compare their average kinetic energies and speeds.

1. Same temperature: same average KE.
2. H₂ is lighter, so faster.

**Answer:** equal average KE; H₂ moves faster.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Samples of $$\ce{O2}$$, $$\ce{H2}$$, $$\ce{N2}$$, $$\ce{CH4}$$ are all at the same temperature. Which has the highest average molecular speed?

**Answer:** $$\ce{H2}$$

**Why:** At the same temperature, average kinetic energy is equal, so the lightest gas moves fastest.

**Working:** Molar masses: O2 32, H2 2.02, N2 28, CH4 16.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$\ce{O2}$$: O2 has a larger molar mass (32 g/mol).
- $$\ce{N2}$$: N2 has a larger molar mass (28 g/mol).
- $$\ce{CH4}$$: CH4 has a larger molar mass (16 g/mol).
