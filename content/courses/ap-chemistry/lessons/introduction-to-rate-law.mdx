$$\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]^m[\ce{B}]^n$$

From initial rates: compare two trials in which only one concentration changes. If doubling [A] doubles the rate, the order is 1; quadruples, 2; no change, 0.

Orders come from experiment, not from coefficients (except for elementary steps).

<Note title="Units of k">
Overall order 0: M/s; 1: s⁻¹; 2: M⁻¹s⁻¹.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Find the order">
When [A] triples with [B] constant, the rate increases ninefold. What is the order in A?

1. $$3^m = 9$$, so $$m = 2$$.

**Answer:** second order in A.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Initial-rate data for A + B → products:

*Initial rates*

| Trial | [A] (M) | [B] (M) | Initial rate (M/s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.1 | 0.1 | 0.08 |
| 2 | 0.2 | 0.1 | 0.16 |
| 3 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.08 |

What is the rate law?

**Answer:** $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]$$

**Why:** Doubling [A] multiplies the rate by 2; doubling [B] multiplies it by 1.

**Working:** Trials 1→2: rate ×2, so order 1 in A. Trials 1→3: rate ×1, so order 0 in B.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{B}]$$: Compare trials where only one concentration changes.
- $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]$$: Orders are found from the data, not from coefficients.
- $$\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]^2$$: Recheck how the rate changes when [A] doubles.

### Free-response practice: Kinetics of a decomposition

The decomposition of compound X was monitored at constant temperature.

*Concentration of X over time*

| Time (s) | [X] (M) | ln[X] | 1/[X] (M⁻¹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.8 | -0.22 | 1.25 |
| 10 | 0.198 | -1.62 | 5.05 |
| 20 | 0.113 | -2.18 | 8.85 |
| 30 | 0.0791 | -2.54 | 12.65 |
| 40 | 0.0608 | -2.80 | 16.45 |

**(a)** Calculate the average rate of disappearance of X between 0 and 20 s.

To earn the point: Correct value with units. Model answer: (0.8 − 0.113)/20 = 0.0344 M/s

**(b)** Determine the order of the reaction with respect to X. Justify your answer using the data.

To earn the point: Order 2. Model answer: 1/[X] increases linearly with time.

To earn the point: Justification from the table. Model answer: Equal time intervals give equal changes in 1/[X].

**(c)** Write the rate law and determine the rate constant, with units.

To earn the point: Rate law. Model answer: rate = k[X]²

To earn the point: Value and units of k. Model answer: k = 0.38 M⁻¹s⁻¹ (from the slope)

**(d)** Calculate the half-life of the reaction (for the initial concentration).

To earn the point: Correct half-life. Model answer: t½ = 1/(k[X]₀) = 3.29 s

**(e)** A proposed mechanism has a slow first step: 2X → X₂. Is the mechanism consistent with the rate law? Explain.

To earn the point: Yes; the slow step's elementary rate law matches the observed rate law. Model answer: rate = k[X]²

**(f)** A catalyst is added. Explain its effect on the rate and on the rate constant, using activation energy.

To earn the point: Rate and k increase. Model answer: The catalyst provides a new pathway.

To earn the point: Lower activation energy means a larger fraction of collisions are successful. Model answer: ΔH is unchanged.
