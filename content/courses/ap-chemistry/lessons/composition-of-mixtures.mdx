Unlike pure substances, mixtures have variable composition. Elemental analysis can reveal how much of each component is present: if the measured mass percent of an element differs from that of the pure compound, the sample is impure or a mixture.

$$\text{percent purity} = \frac{\text{mass of pure substance}}{\text{mass of sample}} \times 100\%$$

<Note title="Detecting impurities">
Compare measured percent composition with the theoretical value for the pure substance.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Impure sample">
Pure NaCl is 39.3% Na. A sample tests 35.0% Na, and any impurity contains no Na. What percent of the sample is NaCl?

1. $$35.0/39.3 = 0.891$$.

**Answer:** about 89.1%.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

How many formula units or molecules are in 123 g of $$\ce{MgCl2}$$?

**Answer:** $$7.78 \times 10^{23}\ \mathrm{particles}$$

**Why:** Convert grams to moles, then multiply by Avogadro's number.

**Working:** 1.29 mol × 6.022 × 10²³ = 7.78 \times 10^\{23\}.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$7.41 \times 10^{25}\ \mathrm{particles}$$: Convert grams to moles first.
- $$4.66 \times 10^{23}\ \mathrm{particles}$$: Moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
- $$1.29\ \mathrm{particles}$$: Multiply moles by 6.022 × 10²³.

### Worked exam question 2

How many moles are in 128 g of $$\ce{KCl}$$?

**Answer:** $$1.72\ \mathrm{mol}$$

**Why:** n = m/M with M = 74.55 g/mol.

**Working:** 128 g ÷ 74.55 g/mol = 1.72 mol.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$9540\ \mathrm{mol}$$: Divide mass by molar mass.
- $$0.582\ \mathrm{mol}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$3.43\ \mathrm{mol}$$: Recheck the molar mass.

### Worked exam question 3

A mass spectrum of a sample of gallium shows two peaks: mass 69 with relative abundance 83% and mass 71 with 17%. What is the average atomic mass of the sample?

**Answer:** $$69.3\ \mathrm{amu}$$

**Why:** Weight each isotope mass by its fractional abundance.

**Working:** 69(0.83) + 71(0.17) = 69.34 amu.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$70\ \mathrm{amu}$$: The isotopes are not equally abundant.
- $$70.7\ \mathrm{amu}$$: Pair each mass with its own abundance.
- $$6930\ \mathrm{amu}$$: Convert percentages to fractions.
