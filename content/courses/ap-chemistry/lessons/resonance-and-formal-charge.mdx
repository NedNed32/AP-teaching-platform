**Resonance**: in $$\ce{NO3^-}$$, the double bond can be drawn in three positions. The real ion has three identical bonds, each with bond order $$4/3$$.

**Formal charge**:

$$FC = V - N - \tfrac12 B$$

The best structure minimizes formal charges and places negative charges on the most electronegative atoms.

<Note title="Bond length from resonance">
Resonance bonds are intermediate in length and strength between single and double bonds.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Formal charge">
Find the formal charge on the central S in SO₂ drawn with one S=O and one S–O bond (S has one lone pair).

1. S: $$6 - 2 - \tfrac12(6) = 1$$.

**Answer:** +1 (the singly bonded O is −1).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Steel is harder than pure iron because:

**A.** Small carbon atoms in the gaps (interstitial alloy) hinder layers of iron atoms from sliding. Interstitial atoms disrupt slipping.

**Q.** Why is solid NaCl a poor conductor while molten NaCl conducts?

**A.** Ions are fixed in the solid but mobile in the liquid. Conduction needs mobile charges.

**Q.** Which Lewis structure is preferred when formal charges are considered?

**A.** The one with formal charges closest to zero and any negative charge on the most electronegative atom. That is the formal charge criterion.

**Q.** Why are metals good conductors of electricity?

**A.** Their valence electrons are delocalized and free to move. The electron-sea model.

**Q.** The nitrate ion has three resonance structures. What is true of its N–O bonds?

**A.** All three are identical, intermediate between single and double bonds. Resonance averages the bonds.
