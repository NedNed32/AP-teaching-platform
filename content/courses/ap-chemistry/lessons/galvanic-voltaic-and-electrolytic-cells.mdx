| | Galvanic | Electrolytic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ΔG | negative | positive (driven by power source) |
| E°cell | positive | negative |
| anode | oxidation, negative | oxidation, positive |

"An Ox, Red Cat": anode oxidation, reduction cathode. Anions in the salt bridge move toward the anode.

<Note title="Mass changes">
The anode metal dissolves (loses mass); metal ions plate onto the cathode (gains mass).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Identify">
In a Zn/Cu cell, which electrode is the anode?

1. Zn is more easily oxidized.

**Answer:** zinc.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

*Standard reduction potentials*

| Half-reaction | E° (V) |
| --- | --- |
| $$\ce{Zn^{2+} + 2e- -> Zn}$$ | -0.76 |
| $$\ce{Pb^{2+} + 2e- -> Pb}$$ | -0.13 |

A galvanic cell is built from Zn/$$\ce{Zn^{2+}}$$ and Pb/$$\ce{Pb^{2+}}$$ half-cells. Which electrode is the anode?

**Answer:** Zn, where oxidation occurs

**Why:** The half-reaction with the lower reduction potential runs as oxidation at the anode.

**Working:** Zn (E° -0.76 V) is oxidized at the anode; Pb is reduced at the cathode.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Pb, where oxidation occurs: The higher potential is reduced at the cathode.
- Zn, where reduction occurs: Reduction happens at the cathode.
- Pb, where reduction occurs at the anode: Reduction occurs at the cathode, not the anode.

### Worked exam question 2

*Standard reduction potentials*

| Half-reaction | E° (V) |
| --- | --- |
| $$\ce{Al^{3+} + 3e- -> Al}$$ | -1.66 |
| $$\ce{Cu^{2+} + 2e- -> Cu}$$ | 0.34 |

For the galvanic cell made from these half-cells, what is ΔG°? (F = 96,485 C/mol)

**Answer:** $$-1160\ \mathrm{kJ}$$

**Why:** ΔG° = −nFE° with n = 6 electrons transferred and E° = 2 V.

**Working:** ΔG° = −(6)(96485)(2) = -1160 kJ.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1160\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: ΔG° is negative for a positive E°.
- $$-193\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: n = 6 electrons are transferred.
- $$-1.16 \times 10^{6}\ \mathrm{kJ}$$: Convert J to kJ.

### Worked exam question 3

*Standard reduction potentials*

| Half-reaction | E° (V) |
| --- | --- |
| $$\ce{Cu^{2+} + 2e- -> Cu}$$ | 0.34 |
| $$\ce{Ag+ + e- -> Ag}$$ | 0.8 |

What is E°cell for a galvanic cell made from these half-cells?

**Answer:** $$0.46\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode.

**Working:** 0.8 − (0.34) = 0.46 V.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.14\ \mathrm{V}$$: Subtract the anode's potential.
- $$-0.46\ \mathrm{V}$$: Cathode minus anode; a galvanic cell is positive.
- $$0.92\ \mathrm{V}$$: E° does not change when half-reactions are multiplied.

### Free-response practice: A galvanic cell

A galvanic cell is made of a Mg electrode in 1.0 M $$\ce{Mg^{2+}}$$ and a Ni electrode in 1.0 M $$\ce{Ni^{2+}}$$. Standard reduction potentials: $$\ce{Mg^{2+} + 2e- -> Mg}$$ E° = -2.37 V; $$\ce{Ni^{2+} + 2e- -> Ni}$$ E° = -0.25 V.

**(a)** Write the balanced net ionic equation for the cell reaction.

To earn the point: Correct equation. Model answer: Mg(s) + Ni²⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + Ni(s)

**(b)** Calculate E°cell.

To earn the point: Correct value. Model answer: -0.25 − (-2.37) = 2.12 V

**(c)** Calculate ΔG° for the cell reaction.

To earn the point: ΔG° = −nFE° with n = 2. Model answer: −(2)(96485)(2.12) = -409 kJ

**(d)** The concentration of Mg²⁺ is increased to 2.0 M. Predict the effect on the cell potential and justify.

To earn the point: E decreases because Q increases (a product's concentration rises). Model answer: Q greater than 1 → E less than E°.
