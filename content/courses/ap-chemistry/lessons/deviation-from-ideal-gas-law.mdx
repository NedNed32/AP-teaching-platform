Real gases deviate when:

- **High pressure**: particle volume is a significant fraction of the container.
- **Low temperature**: attractions slow particles and reduce collisions with walls.

Attractions make the measured pressure **lower** than predicted; particle volume makes the real volume **larger** than predicted.

Small, nonpolar gases (He, H₂) behave most ideally.

<Note title="Two effects">
Attractions lower pressure; finite particle volume raises volume. Name the one that matches the question.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Predict">
Which gas has the largest deviation at the same conditions: Ne, CH₄ or H₂O(g)?

1. H₂O has hydrogen bonding, the strongest attractions.

**Answer:** H₂O(g).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** In paper chromatography with a nonpolar solvent, which component travels farthest?

**A.** The least polar component. It is most attracted to the nonpolar mobile phase.

**Q.** Why does I₂ have a higher boiling point than Br₂?

**A.** I₂ has a larger, more polarizable electron cloud, so stronger London dispersion forces. Dispersion forces grow with polarizability.

**Q.** A solid melts at 1,710 °C, is very hard and does not conduct electricity. It is most likely:

**A.** A covalent network solid. Strong covalent network bonds.

**Q.** Under which conditions does a real gas deviate most from ideal behavior?

**A.** High pressure and low temperature. Particle volume and attractions matter most.

**Q.** Which intermolecular force is present in all molecules?

**A.** London dispersion forces. They arise from temporary dipoles in every electron cloud.

**Q.** Which substance is most soluble in hexane (C₆H₁₄)?

**A.** $$\ce{C8H18}$$. Nonpolar dissolves in nonpolar.

**Q.** Which substance has the highest boiling point?

**A.** $$\ce{H2O}$$. Water forms extensive hydrogen bonds.
