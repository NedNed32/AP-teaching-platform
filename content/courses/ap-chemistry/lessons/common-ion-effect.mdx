Adding a common ion raises Q above Ksp, so the solid precipitates until Q = Ksp again.

In 0.10 M NaCl: $$K_{sp} = s(0.10)$$, so $$s = K_{sp}/0.10$$.

<Note title="Approximate">
The common ion's concentration is usually much larger than s, so treat it as constant.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Calculate">
Find the molar solubility of AgCl (Ksp 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰) in 0.010 M NaCl.

1. $$s = 1.8 \times 10^{-10}/0.010$$.

**Answer:** $$1.8 \times 10^{-8}$$ M.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** The Ksp of $$\ce{PbI2}$$ is 7.1 \times 10^\{-9\}. What is its molar solubility in pure water?

**A.** $$1.21 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{M}$$. Ksp = s(2s)² = 4s³. s = 1.21 \times 10^\{-3\} M.

**Q.** The Ksp of $$\ce{AgBr}$$ is 5 \times 10^\{-13\}. What is its molar solubility in a solution that already contains 0.05 M of its anion?

**A.** $$1 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{M}$$. Ksp = s(c), so s = Ksp/c. s = 5 \times 10^\{-13\}/0.05 = 1 \times 10^\{-11\} M (common-ion effect).

### Free-response practice: Solubility of a slightly soluble salt

The Ksp of $$\ce{CaF2}$$ is 3.9 \times 10^\{-11\} at 25 °C.

**(a)** Write the Ksp expression.

To earn the point: Correct expression. Model answer: Ksp = [M²⁺][F⁻]²

**(b)** Calculate the molar solubility in pure water.

To earn the point: 4s³ = Ksp. Model answer: s = (3.9 \times 10^\{-11\}/4)^(1/3) = 2.14 \times 10^\{-4\} M

**(c)** Calculate the molar solubility in a solution containing 0.01 M F⁻ (from a soluble salt).

To earn the point: Common-ion calculation. Model answer: s = Ksp/(0.01)² = 3.9 \times 10^\{-7\} M

**(d)** Explain how lowering the pH affects the solubility.

To earn the point: Correct reasoning about whether the anion is basic. Model answer: Solubility increases: H⁺ reacts with F⁻ (a base), shifting the equilibrium toward dissolving.
