Le Châtelier's principle can be explained quantitatively:

- **Q less than K**: too few products; the forward reaction proceeds.
- **Q greater than K**: too many products; the reverse reaction proceeds.
- **Q = K**: at equilibrium.

Concentration and pressure changes change Q; temperature changes K.

<Note title="Explain with Q">
Strong answers say what happens to Q and why the system must change to restore Q = K.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Direction">
For a reaction with K = 10, a mixture has Q = 2.5. Which way does it proceed?

1. Q is less than K.

**Answer:** forward, toward products.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

For $$\ce{A(g) + B(g) <=> 2C(g)}$$, K = 6.6. A mixture has [A] = 0.4 M, [B] = 0.9 M and [C] = 0.2 M. In which direction will the reaction proceed?

**Answer:** It proceeds forward, toward products, because Q = 0.111

**Why:** Q = [C]²/([A][B]) = 0.111, which is less than K.

**Working:** Q = 0.2²/(0.4·0.9) = 0.111; K = 6.6.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- It proceeds in reverse, toward reactants, because Q = 0.111: If Q is less than K, the reaction proceeds forward.
- It is at equilibrium, because Q = K: Q = 0.111 ≠ 6.6.
- It proceeds forward, toward products, because Q = 0.556: Square [C], since its coefficient is 2.
