| State | Arrangement | Motion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| solid | ordered, close | vibrate in place |
| liquid | disordered, close | move past each other |
| gas | far apart | move freely and rapidly |

Particle diagrams should show this spacing. Phase changes involve overcoming or forming intermolecular forces, not breaking covalent bonds.

<Note title="Vapor pressure and boiling">
A liquid boils when its vapor pressure equals the external pressure.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Compare liquids">
Liquid A has a higher vapor pressure than liquid B at 25 °C. Which has stronger IMFs?

1. Weaker IMFs let molecules escape more easily.

**Answer:** liquid B.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

What is the pressure of 0.87 mol of gas in a 38 L container at 305 K?

**Answer:** $$0.573\ \mathrm{atm}$$

**Why:** P = nRT/V.

**Working:** P = (0.87)(0.08206)(305)/38 = 0.573 atm.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.0601\ \mathrm{atm}$$: Temperature is already in kelvins.
- $$6.98\ \mathrm{atm}$$: Include R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K).
- $$58.1\ \mathrm{atm}$$: With L and atm, use R = 0.08206.

### Worked exam question 2

What volume does 1.42 mol of gas occupy at 2.43 atm and 417 K?

**Answer:** $$20\ \mathrm{L}$$

**Why:** V = nRT/P.

**Working:** V = nRT/P = 20 L.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.15 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{L}$$: V = nRT/P.
- $$31.8\ \mathrm{L}$$: 22.4 L/mol applies only at 0 °C and 1 atm.
- $$2030\ \mathrm{L}$$: Use R = 0.08206 with atm and L.

### Worked exam question 3

How many moles of gas are in a 13 L flask at 0.618 atm and 408 K?

**Answer:** $$0.24\ \mathrm{mol}$$

**Why:** n = PV/RT.

**Working:** n = PV/RT = 0.24 mol.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$269\ \mathrm{mol}$$: n = PV/(RT): divide by RT.
- $$0.0197\ \mathrm{mol}$$: Include R.
- $$0.58\ \mathrm{mol}$$: Conditions are not 0 °C and 1 atm.

### Worked exam question 4

A gas mixture contains 0.9 mol N₂, 0.6 mol O₂ and 0.2 mol Ar at a total pressure of 4.1 atm. What is the partial pressure of N₂?

**Answer:** $$2.17\ \mathrm{atm}$$

**Why:** P(N₂) = X(N₂) × P(total).

**Working:** X = 0.9/1.7 = 0.529; P = 2.17 atm.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3.69\ \mathrm{atm}$$: Use the mole fraction, not moles.
- $$1.37\ \mathrm{atm}$$: The gases are not present in equal amounts.
- $$4.61\ \mathrm{atm}$$: Divide by total moles.

### Worked exam question 5

22 mL of 6.6 M solution is diluted to 290 mL. What is the new concentration?

**Answer:** $$0.501\ \mathrm{M}$$

**Why:** M₁V₁ = M₂V₂.

**Working:** M₂ = (6.6)(22)/290 = 0.501 M.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$87\ \mathrm{M}$$: Dilution decreases concentration.
- $$4.83\ \mathrm{M}$$: Use M₁V₁ = M₂V₂.
- $$0.542\ \mathrm{M}$$: V₂ is the total final volume.

### Worked exam question 6

What is the molarity of a solution made by dissolving 0.09 mol of solute in enough water to make 522 mL of solution?

**Answer:** $$0.172\ \mathrm{M}$$

**Why:** M = mol ÷ liters.

**Working:** 0.09 ÷ 0.522 L = 0.172 M.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.72 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{M}$$: Convert mL to L.
- $$0.047\ \mathrm{M}$$: Divide by volume.
- $$5.8\ \mathrm{M}$$: The ratio is inverted.
