A mass spectrum plots relative abundance against mass. Each peak is an isotope.

$$\text{average mass} = \sum (\text{isotope mass} \times \text{fractional abundance})$$

The average lies closest to the most abundant isotope.

<Note title="Percent to fraction">
Convert percentages to decimals before multiplying.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Average mass">
An element has isotopes of mass 63 (69%) and 65 (31%). Find the average atomic mass.

1. $$63(0.69) + 65(0.31) = 43.47 + 20.15$$.

**Answer:** 63.6 amu (copper).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A mass spectrum of a sample of gallium shows two peaks: mass 69 with relative abundance 83% and mass 71 with 17%. What is the average atomic mass of the sample?

**Answer:** $$69.3\ \mathrm{amu}$$

**Why:** Weight each isotope mass by its fractional abundance.

**Working:** 69(0.83) + 71(0.17) = 69.34 amu.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$70\ \mathrm{amu}$$: The isotopes are not equally abundant.
- $$70.7\ \mathrm{amu}$$: Pair each mass with its own abundance.
- $$6930\ \mathrm{amu}$$: Convert percentages to fractions.
