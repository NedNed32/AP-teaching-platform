Exponential growth cannot last forever: food, space or resources eventually run short. The logistic model builds in a ceiling, the **carrying capacity**, and growth slows as the population approaches it.

## The equation

$$
\frac{dP}{dt} = kP\left(1 - \frac{P}{L}\right) \qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad \frac{dP}{dt} = \frac{k}{L}P(L - P).
$$

- When $$P$$ is small, $$1 - \frac{P}{L} \approx 1$$ and growth is nearly exponential.
- When $$P$$ is near $$L$$, the factor is near 0 and growth stalls.

## What you can read without solving

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Limiting value as $$t \to \infty$$ | $$L$$ (if $$P(0) > 0$$) |
| Population when growing fastest | $$\frac{L}{2}$$ (the inflection point) |
| Equilibrium solutions | $$P = 0$$ and $$P = L$$ |
| If $$P(0) > L$$ | $$P$$ decreases toward $$L$$ |

The fastest growth is at $$\frac{L}{2}$$ because $$\frac{dP}{dt}$$ is a downward parabola in $$P$$ with its peak halfway between its zeros.

<Note title="Rewrite into standard form">
An equation like $$\frac{dy}{dt} = 0.002y(500 - y)$$ is logistic with $$L = 500$$. Factor to find $$L$$ before answering.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Reading a logistic model">
A rumor spreads according to $$\frac{dR}{dt} = 0.004R(1000 - R)$$, with $$R(0) = 20$$. Find $$\lim_{t \to \infty} R(t)$$ and the value of $$R$$ when the rumor spreads fastest.

1. The factor $$(1000 - R)$$ shows $$L = 1000$$.
2. Fastest growth at $$\frac{L}{2} = 500$$.

**Answer:** $$R \to 1000$$; the rumor spreads fastest when 500 people have heard it.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A population satisfies $$\frac{dP}{dt} = 0.01P(800 - P)$$ with $$P(0) = 80$$. What is $$\lim_{t \to \infty} P(t)$$?

**Answer:** $$800$$

**Why:** The carrying capacity: the factor $$(800 - P)$$ makes growth stop at $$P = 800$$.

**Working:** Since $$0 < P(0) < 800$$, $$\frac{dP}{dt} > 0$$ and $$P$$ increases toward the equilibrium $$P = 800$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$400$$: This is where the population grows fastest, not its limit.
- $$\infty$$: Logistic growth levels off; it does not grow without bound.
- $$80$$: The population changes from its initial value; it is not constant.

### Worked exam question 2

A population satisfies $$\frac{dP}{dt} = 0.002P(1200 - P)$$ with $$P(0) = 60$$. For what value of $$P$$ is the population growing fastest?

**Answer:** $$600$$

**Why:** Half the carrying capacity, where the parabola $$\frac{dP}{dt}$$ in $$P$$ has its maximum.

**Working:** $$\frac{dP}{dt} = 0.002P(1200 - P)$$ is a downward parabola in $$P$$ with zeros at 0 and 1200, so it is largest at $$P = 600$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1200$$: At the carrying capacity the growth rate is 0.
- $$60$$: The initial population is not where growth is fastest.
- $$300$$: The maximum of $$P(L - P)$$ is at $$\frac{L}{2}$$.
