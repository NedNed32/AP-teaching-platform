A vector-valued function packages parametric equations into one object: the position vector $$\vec{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t) \rangle$$ points from the origin to a moving particle.

## Differentiate each component

$$
\vec{r}\,'(t) = \langle x'(t), y'(t) \rangle.
$$

For motion:

| Vector | Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| position | $$\vec{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t) \rangle$$ | where the particle is |
| velocity | $$\vec{v}(t) = \langle x'(t), y'(t) \rangle$$ | direction and rate of motion |
| acceleration | $$\vec{a}(t) = \langle x''(t), y''(t) \rangle$$ | how velocity changes |
| speed | $$\lvert\vec{v}(t)\rvert = \sqrt{[x'(t)]^2 + [y'(t)]^2}$$ | a scalar |

The velocity vector is tangent to the path and points in the direction of motion.

<Note title="Slope of the path">
The slope of the path at time $$t$$ is $$\frac{y'(t)}{x'(t)}$$, the ratio of the velocity components, exactly as for parametric curves.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Velocity, speed, acceleration">
A particle has position $$\vec{r}(t) = \langle 3t^2, 4t \rangle$$. Find its velocity, speed and acceleration at $$t = 1$$.

1. $$\vec{v}(t) = \langle 6t, 4 \rangle$$, so $$\vec{v}(1) = \langle 6, 4 \rangle$$.
2. Speed: $$\sqrt{36 + 16} = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13}$$.
3. $$\vec{a}(t) = \langle 6, 0 \rangle$$.

**Answer:** $$\vec{v}(1) = \langle 6, 4 \rangle$$, speed $$2\sqrt{13} \approx 7.211$$, $$\vec{a}(1) = \langle 6, 0 \rangle$$.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** A particle moves in the plane with $$x'(t) = \frac{5}{2}t$$ and $$y'(t) = 3t^2$$. What is the speed of the particle at $$t = 2$$?

**A.** $$13$$. $$\sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = 13$$. At $$t = 2$$, $$\vec{v} = \langle 5, 12 \rangle$$, so the speed is $$\sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$$.
