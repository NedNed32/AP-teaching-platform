Integrating a vector function works the same way as differentiating one: handle each component separately.

## Indefinite and definite integrals

$$
\int \langle f(t), g(t) \rangle\,dt = \left\langle F(t) + C_1, G(t) + C_2 \right\rangle,
$$

$$
\int_a^b \langle f(t), g(t) \rangle\,dt = \left\langle \int_a^b f(t)\,dt, \int_a^b g(t)\,dt \right\rangle.
$$

## Position from velocity

The net change theorem works componentwise:

$$
x(b) = x(a) + \int_a^b x'(t)\,dt, \qquad y(b) = y(a) + \int_a^b y'(t)\,dt.
$$

This is the most common use of vector integrals on the AP exam, often with a calculator.

<Note title="One component at a time">
On free-response questions you may be given only $$\frac{dx}{dt}$$ and $$\frac{dy}{dt}$$ plus a starting point. Find each coordinate with its own net-change integral, then report the point.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Where is the particle?">
A particle has velocity $$\vec{v}(t) = \langle \cos t, 2t \rangle$$ and is at $$(1, 0)$$ at $$t = 0$$. Find its position at $$t = \pi$$.

1. $$x(\pi) = 1 + \int_0^\pi \cos t\,dt = 1 + 0 = 1$$.
2. $$y(\pi) = 0 + \int_0^\pi 2t\,dt = \pi^2$$.

**Answer:** $$(1, \pi^2)$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A particle has velocity $$\vec{v}(t) = \langle 2t - 2, 3t^{2} \rangle$$ and position $$(-2, 2)$$ at $$t = 0$$. Where is the particle at $$t = 3$$?

**Answer:** $$(1, 29)$$

**Why:** Add the integral of each velocity component to the starting coordinate.

**Working:** $$x(3) = -2 + \int_0^{3} (2t - 2)\,dt = 1$$ and $$y(3) = 2 + \int_0^{3} (3t^{2})\,dt = 29$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$(3, 27)$$: This is the displacement; add the starting position.
- $$(4, 27)$$: This is the velocity at the final time, not the position.
- $$(2, 29)$$: This adds the final velocity instead of the accumulated change in position.
