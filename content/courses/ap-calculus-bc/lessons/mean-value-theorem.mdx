If your average speed on a trip was 60 mph, then at some moment your speedometer read exactly 60. The Mean Value Theorem turns that intuition into a guarantee for any smooth function.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/mvt.svg" alt="A downward parabola with a secant line joining two points, and a dashed tangent line parallel to the secant touching the curve at a point c between them." caption="Somewhere between a and b, the tangent is parallel to the secant." />

## The theorem

If $$f$$ is

1. **continuous** on the closed interval $$[a, b]$$, and
2. **differentiable** on the open interval $$(a, b)$$,

then there is at least one $$c$$ in $$(a, b)$$ with

$$
f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.
$$

Geometrically: some tangent line is parallel to the secant line through the endpoints. In words: the instantaneous rate equals the average rate at least once.

## Typical exam use

A table gives values of a differentiable function. "Must there be a time $$c$$ where $$f'(c) = 2$$?" Compute the average rate on an interval of the table; if it equals 2, the MVT says yes.

<Note title="Name both conditions">
A full justification says that $$f$$ is differentiable (so also continuous) on the interval, computes the average rate of change, and cites the Mean Value Theorem. If you are only told $$f$$ is continuous, the MVT cannot be used; consider the IVT instead.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Finding the guaranteed value">
Find the value of $$c$$ guaranteed by the MVT for $$f(x) = x^3$$ on $$[0, 3]$$.

1. $$f$$ is a polynomial, so the hypotheses hold.
2. Average rate: $$\frac{27 - 0}{3} = 9$$.
3. $$f'(c) = 3c^2 = 9$$ gives $$c^2 = 3$$, so $$c = \sqrt{3}$$ (the negative root is outside the interval).

**Answer:** $$c = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Let $$f(x) = x^{2} - 4x$$. What value of $$c$$ satisfies the conclusion of the Mean Value Theorem for $$f$$ on the interval $$[0, 6]$$?

**Answer:** $$3$$

**Why:** The average rate of change is $$\frac{f(6) - f(0)}{6} = 2$$, and $$f'(c) = 2c - 4 = 2$$ gives $$c = 3$$.

**Working:** $$\frac{f(6) - f(0)}{6 - 0} = 2$$. Set $$f'(c) = 2c - 4 = 2$$: $$c = 3$$, which lies in $$(0, 6)$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0$$: The MVT guarantees a point strictly inside the interval, and this endpoint doesn't satisfy the equation.
- $$6$$: The MVT guarantees a point strictly inside the interval, and this endpoint doesn't satisfy the equation.
- $$2$$: This is where f′(c) = 0, the vertex, not where f′(c) equals the average rate of change.
