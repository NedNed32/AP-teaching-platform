Where are the highest and lowest points of a graph? Two facts frame every answer: when a highest point is guaranteed to exist, and where to look for it.

## Absolute versus relative

- An **absolute** (global) maximum is the largest value of $$f$$ on the whole interval.
- A **relative** (local) maximum is larger than all *nearby* values.

An absolute maximum can occur at an endpoint; a relative extremum, in the AP definition, occurs only at interior points.

## The Extreme Value Theorem

If $$f$$ is **continuous** on a **closed** interval $$[a, b]$$, then $$f$$ attains an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum on $$[a, b]$$.

Both conditions matter: $$x^2$$ on the open interval $$(0, 1)$$ has no maximum, and a function with a jump can skip over its would-be peak.

## Critical points

Relative extrema can only occur where the tangent is horizontal or does not exist. So the **critical points**, where $$f'(c) = 0$$ or $$f'(c)$$ is undefined, are the only interior candidates.

<Note title="Critical does not mean extremum">
$$f(x) = x^3$$ has $$f'(0) = 0$$, but no maximum or minimum at 0. A critical point is a candidate that must be tested (topics 5.4 and 5.7).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Finding all critical points">
Find the critical points of $$f(x) = x^{2/3}(x - 5)$$.

1. Expand: $$f(x) = x^{5/3} - 5x^{2/3}$$.
2. $$f'(x) = \frac{5}{3}x^{2/3} - \frac{10}{3}x^{-1/3} = \frac{5(x - 2)}{3x^{1/3}}$$.
3. $$f'(x) = 0$$ at $$x = 2$$; $$f'(x)$$ is undefined at $$x = 0$$ (where $$f$$ is defined).

**Answer:** the critical points are $$x = 0$$ and $$x = 2$$.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** Let $$f(x) = 2x^{3} - 6x^{2} - 18x$$. At what value of $$x$$ does $$f$$ have a relative minimum?

**A.** $$3$$. $$f'(x) = 6(x + 1)(x - 3)$$ changes from negative to positive at $$x = 3$$. $$f'(x) = 6x^2 - 12x - 18 = 6(x + 1)(x - 3)$$. Testing signs: $$f'$$ is positive left of $$-1$$, negative between $$-1$$ and $$3$$, positive right of $$3$$. So there is a relative maximum at $$-1$$ and a relative minimum at $$3$$.
