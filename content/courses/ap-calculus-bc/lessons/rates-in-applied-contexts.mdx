Motion is just one context. The same tools apply to any quantity that changes: water in a tank, people in a line, temperature, cost, or concentration of a drug in the blood.

## Same mathematics, new words

| Context | Function | Derivative means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tank | gallons $$W(t)$$ | rate water level changes, gal/hr |
| Population | people $$P(t)$$ | growth rate, people/yr |
| Economics | cost $$C(x)$$ | marginal cost, dollars/item |
| Medicine | concentration $$D(t)$$ | rate drug level changes, mg/L per hr |

## Net rates

Many exam questions describe a quantity with an inflow and an outflow. If water enters at $$E(t)$$ and leaves at $$L(t)$$, the amount $$W(t)$$ in the tank satisfies

$$
W'(t) = E(t) - L(t).
$$

The amount is increasing when $$E(t) > L(t)$$ and decreasing when $$E(t) < L(t)$$.

<Note title="Rate of a rate">
If $$R(t)$$ is already a rate (gallons per hour), then $$R'(t)$$ has units of gallons per hour per hour. Interpret it as how fast the rate itself is changing.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Inflow and outflow">
Sand enters a pile at $$E(t) = 10 + \sin t$$ and erodes at $$L(t) = 2t$$ cubic feet per hour. Is the pile growing or shrinking at $$t = 6$$?

1. $$E(6) = 10 + \sin 6 \approx 9.721$$.
2. $$L(6) = 12$$.
3. Net rate: $$E(6) - L(6) \approx -2.279$$.

**Answer:** the pile is shrinking at about 2.279 cubic feet per hour.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Let $$W(t)$$ be the amount of water in a tank, in gallons, at time t hours. Which of the following is the best interpretation of $$W'(3) = 4$$?

**Answer:** The amount of water is increasing at a rate of 4 gallons per hour at time $$t = 3$$ hours.

**Why:** A derivative at a point is an instantaneous rate of change, with units of output per unit of input.

**Working:** $$W'(3)$$ is the instantaneous rate of change of $$W$$ at time $$t = 3$$ hours. Its sign (positive) says the amount of water is increasing, and its units are gallons per hour.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- The amount of water at time $$t = 3$$ hours is 4.: This describes $$W(3)$$, the value of the function, not its rate of change.
- The amount of water changes by 4 over the first 3 hours.: This describes a total change over an interval, which is an integral of the rate, not the derivative at one point.
- The average rate of change of the amount of water over the first 3 hours is 4 gallons per hour.: The derivative is an instantaneous rate at one input, not an average over an interval.

### Worked exam question 2

The temperature of a room, in degrees Fahrenheit, is modeled by $$H(t) = 68 + 9\sin\left(\frac{t}{7}\right)e^{-t/20}$$, where $$t$$ is measured in hours. What is the instantaneous rate of change of the temperature at $$t = 6$$, in degrees Fahrenheit per hour?

**Answer:** $$0.371$$

**Why:** The calculator's numerical derivative of $$H$$ at $$t = 6$$ gives 0.371; the temperature is increasing.

**Working:** The instantaneous rate of change is $$H'(6) \approx 0.371$$ degrees Fahrenheit per hour, from the calculator's numerical derivative.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$73.040$$: This is the temperature itself, not its rate of change.
- $$0.840$$: This is the average rate of change over $$0 \leq t \leq 6$$, not the instantaneous rate.
- $$2.229$$: This multiplies the rate by the time, which is not a rate.
