For polynomials, finding a limit is easy: substitute. $$\lim_{x \to 2} (x^2 + 3) = 2^2 + 3 = 7$$. Try the same on $$\frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}$$ at $$x = 3$$, though, and you get $$\frac{0}{0}$$. That isn't an answer. It's a signal that the expression is hiding a factor that cancels.

## Why 0/0 is not an answer

$$\frac{0}{0}$$ is called an **indeterminate form**. Knowing that the top and bottom both approach 0 tells you nothing about what their ratio approaches: depending on the function, the limit could be 0, 5, $$-\frac{1}{4}$$, or might not exist at all. To find it, rewrite the expression so that substitution no longer divides by zero.

## Strategy 1: factor and cancel

$$
\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} = \lim_{x \to 3} \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{x - 3} = \lim_{x \to 3} (x + 3) = 6
$$

Cancelling $$x - 3$$ looks illegal, since it is zero at $$x = 3$$. But a limit only looks at inputs *near* 3, never at 3 itself, and for all of those inputs $$x - 3 \neq 0$$. The two expressions agree everywhere the limit looks, so they have the same limit.

## Strategy 2: multiply by the conjugate

When a square root causes the $$\frac{0}{0}$$, multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate:

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x + 4} - 2}{x} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2}{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{(x + 4) - 4}{x\left(\sqrt{x + 4} + 2\right)} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2} = \frac{1}{4}
$$

## Strategy 3: combine fractions

When the expression contains fractions within a fraction, combine them over a common denominator first:

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{1}{x + 2} - \frac{1}{2}}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{2 - (x + 2)}{2(x + 2)}}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-1}{2(x + 2)} = -\frac{1}{4}
$$

<Note title="Check what substitution gives you">
If you still get $$\frac{0}{0}$$ after rewriting, rewrite again. If you get a nonzero number over 0, such as $$\frac{5}{0}$$, no algebra will help: the function grows without bound near that point, and the limit does not exist as a number (topic 1.14).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Factor the top and the bottom">
Find $$\lim_{x \to -1} \frac{x^2 + 3x + 2}{x^2 - 1}$$.

1. Substituting $$x = -1$$ gives $$\frac{1 - 3 + 2}{1 - 1} = \frac{0}{0}$$, so rewrite.
2. Factor both: $$\frac{(x + 1)(x + 2)}{(x + 1)(x - 1)}$$.
3. For $$x \neq -1$$, cancel $$x + 1$$ to get $$\frac{x + 2}{x - 1}$$.
4. Substitute: $$\frac{-1 + 2}{-1 - 1} = -\frac{1}{2}$$.

**Answer:** $$\lim_{x \to -1} \frac{x^2 + 3x + 2}{x^2 - 1} = -\frac{1}{2}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

What is $$\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^{2} - 7x + 10}{x - 2}$$?

**Answer:** $$-3$$

**Why:** The numerator factors as $$(x - 2)(x - 5)$$. Cancel $$x - 2$$ and substitute: $$2 - (5) = -3$$.

**Working:** Direct substitution gives $$\frac{0}{0}$$, so factor: $$x^{2} - 7x + 10 = (x - 2)(x - 5)$$. For $$x \neq 2$$ the expression equals $$x - 5$$, so the limit is $$2 - (5) = -3$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0$$: Substituting gives $$\frac{0}{0}$$, an indeterminate form, not the value 0.
- $$7$$: This adds the roots. After cancelling, the expression is $$x - 5$$, which approaches $$-3$$.
- The limit does not exist.: The expression is undefined at the point, but its outputs nearby approach a single number, so the limit exists.

**Q.** What is $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x + 4} - 2}{x}$$?

**A.** $$\frac{1}{4}$$. Rationalizing gives $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2}$$, which approaches $$\frac{1}{4}$$. Multiply by $$\frac{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2}{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2}$$. The numerator becomes $$x$$, which cancels, leaving $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2} \to \frac{1}{4}$$.
