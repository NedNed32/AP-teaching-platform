Rectangular areas add up thin rectangles. Polar areas add up thin **pie slices** (sectors) fanning out from the origin.

## The formula

A sector of radius $$r$$ and angle $$d\theta$$ has area $$\frac{1}{2}r^2\,d\theta$$. Adding them:

$$
A = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta [r(\theta)]^2\,d\theta.
$$

## Finding the limits

The limits are the angles that sweep out the region exactly once. Useful ways to find them:

- Where $$r = 0$$ (the curve passes through the origin), such as the ends of a petal.
- Where the curve meets a given ray $$\theta = c$$.
- Symmetry: compute half the region and double it.

<Note title="Square the whole radius">
$$r^2$$ means the whole expression squared: $$(1 + \cos\theta)^2 = 1 + 2\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta$$. On calculator questions you can enter $$\left(r(\theta)\right)^2$$ directly.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Area of the shaded cardioid region">
Find the area of the region inside $$r = 1 + \cos\theta$$ for $$0 \leq \theta \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$$ (the shaded part of the figure in topic 9.7).

1. $$A = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi/2} (1 + 2\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta)\,d\theta$$.
2. Use $$\cos^2\theta = \frac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2}$$: the integral is $$\left[\frac{3\theta}{2} + 2\sin\theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{4}\right]_0^{\pi/2} = \frac{3\pi}{4} + 2$$.
3. Multiply by $$\frac{1}{2}$$.

**Answer:** $$\frac{3\pi}{8} + 1 \approx 2.178$$.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** Which of the following gives the area of the region enclosed by the polar curve $$r = 4 + 2\cos\theta$$?

**A.** $$\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} \left(4 + 2\cos\theta\right)^2 d\theta$$. Polar area is half the integral of $$r^2$$ over one full trace. $$A = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta$$. Because $$4 > 2$$, $$r > 0$$ for all $$\theta$$ and the curve closes after $$2\pi$$.

**Q.** What is the area of one petal of the rose $$r = 4\sin(5\theta)$$?

**A.** $$\frac{4\pi}{5}$$. $$\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi/5} 16\sin^2(5\theta)\,d\theta = \frac{16\pi}{20}$$. One petal runs from $$\theta = 0$$ to $$\theta = \frac{\pi}{5}$$, where $$r = 0$$. Using $$\sin^2 u = \frac{1 - \cos 2u}{2}$$, the area is $$\frac{16}{2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2\cdot 5} = \frac{4\pi}{5}$$.

### Free-response practice: A polar region

The curves $$r = 3 + 2\cos\theta$$ and $$r = 4$$ are drawn in the polar plane for $$0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$$.

**(a)** Find the area of the region enclosed by $$r = 3 + 2\cos\theta$$.

To earn the point: Uses the polar area formula. Model answer: $$\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} \left(3 + 2\cos\theta\right)^2 d\theta$$

To earn the point: Gives the area. Model answer: $$34.558$$

**(b)** Find the area of the region inside $$r = 3 + 2\cos\theta$$ and outside $$r = 4$$.

To earn the point: Finds the intersection angles. Model answer: $$\theta = \pm1.047$$

To earn the point: Writes outer squared minus inner squared. Model answer: $$\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1.047}^{1.047} \left(r^2 - 4^2\right) d\theta$$

To earn the point: Gives the area. Model answer: $$6.022$$

**(c)** Find $$\frac{dr}{d\theta}$$ at $$\theta = 2$$. Is the curve moving toward or away from the origin at that angle? Explain.

To earn the point: Gives the derivative. Model answer: $$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2\sin(2) \approx -1.819$$
