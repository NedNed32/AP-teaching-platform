Integrals obey a handful of rules that follow from thinking of them as signed area. They let you compute new integrals from ones you already know, without ever finding an antiderivative.

## The properties

| Property | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| Zero width | $$\int_a^a f(x)\,dx = 0$$ |
| Reverse limits | $$\int_b^a f(x)\,dx = -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$ |
| Constant multiple | $$\int_a^b k f(x)\,dx = k\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$ |
| Sum and difference | $$\int_a^b [f(x) \pm g(x)]\,dx = \int_a^b f \pm \int_a^b g$$ |
| Additivity | $$\int_a^b f + \int_b^c f = \int_a^c f$$ |
| Constant | $$\int_a^b k\,dx = k(b - a)$$ |

## Symmetry shortcuts

- If $$f$$ is **even**, $$\int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = 2\int_0^a f(x)\,dx$$.
- If $$f$$ is **odd**, $$\int_{-a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = 0$$.

## Geometry

When $$f$$ is made of lines and semicircles, evaluate integrals with area formulas: triangles, rectangles, trapezoids and $$\frac{1}{2}\pi r^2$$.

<Note title="Constants integrate to rectangles">
The integral of the constant 2 from 1 to 4 is $$2 \times 3 = 6$$, not 2. Forgetting to multiply by the interval length is a classic distractor.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Combining given integrals">
Given $$\int_{-2}^{3} f(x)\,dx = 7$$ and $$\int_{3}^{6} f(x)\,dx = -4$$, find $$\int_{6}^{-2} 2f(x)\,dx$$.

1. Additivity: $$\int_{-2}^{6} f = 7 + (-4) = 3$$.
2. Reverse: $$\int_6^{-2} f = -3$$.
3. Constant multiple: $$2(-3)$$.

**Answer:** $$-6$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

If $$\int_0^{3} f(x)\,dx = -4$$ and $$\int_0^{5} f(x)\,dx = 12$$, what is $$\int_{3}^{5} \left(-3f(x) + 5\right)dx$$?

**Answer:** $$-38$$

**Why:** $$\int_{3}^{5} f = 12 - (-4) = 16$$, so the integral is $$-3(16) + 5(2)$$.

**Working:** $$\int_{3}^{5} f(x)\,dx = \int_0^{5} f - \int_0^{3} f = 16$$. By linearity, the requested integral is $$-3 \cdot 16 + 5 \cdot 2 = -38$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$-43$$: The integral of the constant $$5$$ over an interval of length $$2$$ is $$10$$, not $$5$$.
- $$-14$$: This adds the two given integrals; the integral from a to b is their difference.
- $$26$$: This forgets to multiply the integral of $$f$$ by $$-3$$.
