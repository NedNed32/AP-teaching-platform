More rectangles give a better approximation. With infinitely many, infinitely thin rectangles, the approximation becomes exact, and that limit is the **definite integral**.

## A Riemann sum in sigma notation

Split $$[a, b]$$ into $$n$$ equal pieces of width $$\Delta x = \frac{b - a}{n}$$. The right endpoints are $$x_i = a + i\,\Delta x$$. The right Riemann sum is

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i)\,\Delta x = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f\left(a + \frac{(b - a)i}{n}\right)\frac{b - a}{n}.
$$

## The definite integral

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i)\,\Delta x.
$$

The integral sign is a stretched S for "sum", $$f(x)$$ is the height of each strip and $$dx$$ is its infinitely small width.

## Converting a limit of sums to an integral

To recognize $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\ldots)$$ as an integral:

1. Find $$\Delta x$$: the factor like $$\frac{3}{n}$$ gives $$b - a = 3$$.
2. Find $$x_i$$: the expression inside $$f$$, such as $$2 + \frac{3i}{n}$$, gives $$a = 2$$.
3. Then $$b = a + (b - a)$$ and $$f$$ is whatever is applied to $$x_i$$.

<Note title="More than one correct integral">
The same limit can match several integrals, for example $$\int_2^5 \sqrt{x}\,dx$$ and $$\int_0^3 \sqrt{2 + x}\,dx$$. Multiple-choice answers usually offer only one of them.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Recognizing an integral">
Write $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{4}{n}\cos\left(1 + \frac{4i}{n}\right)$$ as a definite integral.

1. $$\Delta x = \frac{4}{n}$$, so $$b - a = 4$$.
2. $$x_i = 1 + \frac{4i}{n}$$, so $$a = 1$$ and $$b = 5$$.
3. The function applied is cosine.

**Answer:** $$\int_1^5 \cos x\,dx$$.
</WorkedExample>
