At a horizontal tangent, the shape of the curve decides everything: a cup shape means a valley, a cap shape means a peak. The second derivative tells you the shape at a single point, so you do not need a full sign chart.

## The test

Suppose $$f'(c) = 0$$.

- $$f''(c) < 0$$ (concave down) gives a **relative maximum** at $$c$$.
- $$f''(c) > 0$$ (concave up) gives a **relative minimum** at $$c$$.
- $$f''(c) = 0$$: **inconclusive**. The point could be a maximum ($$-x^4$$), a minimum ($$x^4$$) or neither ($$x^3$$).

## When to use which test

| Situation | Better choice |
| --- | --- |
| $$f''$$ is easy to evaluate | Second Derivative Test |
| $$f''(c) = 0$$ or is hard to find | First Derivative Test |
| You need intervals of increase anyway | First Derivative Test |
| Implicit or differential-equation context | Second Derivative Test |

<Note title="Differential equations use it too">
In Unit 7, if $$\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$$ at a point, compute $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$$ there to classify the point on the solution curve. It is the same test.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying with the second derivative">
Classify the critical points of $$f(x) = x^3 - 12x$$.

1. $$f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12 = 0$$ at $$x = \pm 2$$.
2. $$f''(x) = 6x$$.
3. $$f''(-2) = -12 < 0$$ and $$f''(2) = 12 > 0$$.

**Answer:** relative maximum at $$x = -2$$, relative minimum at $$x = 2$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A twice-differentiable function $$f$$ satisfies $$f'(2) = 0$$ and $$f''(2) = 5$$. Which of the following must be true?

**Answer:** $$f$$ has a relative minimum at $$x = 2$$.

**Why:** f′ = 0 and f″ > 0: the graph is concave up at a horizontal tangent.

**Working:** By the Second Derivative Test, $$f'(c) = 0$$ with $$f''(c) > 0$$ gives a relative minimum.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$f$$ has a relative maximum at $$x = 2$$.: A maximum needs f″ &lt; 0.
- $$f$$ has a point of inflection at $$x = 2$$.: f″ is not zero, so this is not a candidate for inflection.
- There is not enough information to classify $$x = 2$$.: The second derivative test applies because f″ ≠ 0.
