Regions between two polar curves are like polar washers: subtract the inner sector from the outer one.

## The formula

On an interval of angles where $$R(\theta) \geq r(\theta) \geq 0$$:

$$
A = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta \left([R(\theta)]^2 - [r(\theta)]^2\right)d\theta.
$$

## Steps

1. Sketch both curves.
2. Find intersection angles by setting $$R(\theta) = r(\theta)$$.
3. Decide which curve is farther from the origin on each interval.
4. Integrate outer squared minus inner squared; split the integral if the roles switch.

<Note title="Intersections can hide at the origin">
Two polar curves may both pass through the origin at different angles. Setting the equations equal will not find that intersection; check where each curve has $$r = 0$$.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Inside a circle, outside a cardioid">
Find the area inside $$r = 3\cos\theta$$ and outside $$r = 1 + \cos\theta$$.

1. Intersections: $$3\cos\theta = 1 + \cos\theta$$ gives $$\cos\theta = \frac{1}{2}$$, $$\theta = \pm\frac{\pi}{3}$$.
2. Between them, the circle is outside.
3. $$A = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3}\left(9\cos^2\theta - (1 + \cos\theta)^2\right)d\theta = \int_0^{\pi/3}\left(8\cos^2\theta - 2\cos\theta - 1\right)d\theta$$.
4. $$= \left[4\theta + 2\sin 2\theta - 2\sin\theta - \theta\right]_0^{\pi/3} = \pi + \sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3}$$.

**Answer:** $$\pi$$.
</WorkedExample>

### Free-response practice: A polar region

The curves $$r = 3 + 2\cos\theta$$ and $$r = 4$$ are drawn in the polar plane for $$0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$$.

**(a)** Find the area of the region enclosed by $$r = 3 + 2\cos\theta$$.

To earn the point: Uses the polar area formula. Model answer: $$\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} \left(3 + 2\cos\theta\right)^2 d\theta$$

To earn the point: Gives the area. Model answer: $$34.558$$

**(b)** Find the area of the region inside $$r = 3 + 2\cos\theta$$ and outside $$r = 4$$.

To earn the point: Finds the intersection angles. Model answer: $$\theta = \pm1.047$$

To earn the point: Writes outer squared minus inner squared. Model answer: $$\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1.047}^{1.047} \left(r^2 - 4^2\right) d\theta$$

To earn the point: Gives the area. Model answer: $$6.022$$

**(c)** Find $$\frac{dr}{d\theta}$$ at $$\theta = 2$$. Is the curve moving toward or away from the origin at that angle? Explain.

To earn the point: Gives the derivative. Model answer: $$\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2\sin(2) \approx -1.819$$

To earn the point: Interprets the sign, noting that $$r > 0$$. Model answer: Since $$r > 0$$ and $$\frac{dr}{d\theta} < 0$$, the curve is moving toward the origin.

**(d)** Find the slope of the line tangent to $$r = 3 + 2\cos\theta$$ at $$\theta = 2$$.

To earn the point: Uses $$\frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta}$$ with $$x = r\cos\theta$$ and $$y = r\sin\theta$$. Model answer: $$\frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta} = \frac{-2.556}{-1.214}$$

To earn the point: Gives the slope. Model answer: $$2.105$$
