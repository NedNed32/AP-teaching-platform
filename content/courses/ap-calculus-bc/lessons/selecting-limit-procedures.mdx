There is no single method for every limit, but there is a reliable order of attack. Always start by substituting, then let the result tell you what to do next.

## A decision guide

1. **Substitute.** If you get a number, that is the limit (the function is continuous there).
2. **Nonzero over zero**, such as $$\frac{3}{0}$$: the limit is infinite or does not exist. Check the sign on each side (topic 1.14).
3. **$$\frac{0}{0}$$**: rewrite the expression.
   - Polynomials: **factor and cancel**.
   - Square roots: **multiply by the conjugate**.
   - Fractions within fractions: **combine over a common denominator**.
   - Trigonometric: use $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$$ and $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x} = 0$$.
   - Later, in Unit 4: **L'Hospital's Rule**.
4. **Piecewise or absolute value**: find each one-sided limit separately.
5. **$$x \to \pm\infty$$**: compare degrees or divide by the highest power (topic 1.15).

## The two special trig limits

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1 \qquad\qquad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x} = 0
$$

To use them, make the denominator match the angle:

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{2x} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{3x} = \frac{3}{2}.
$$

<Note title="Absolute values hide a piecewise function">
$$|x - 2|$$ equals $$x - 2$$ when $$x \geq 2$$ and $$-(x - 2)$$ when $$x < 2$$. Limits at 2 of expressions such as $$\frac{|x - 2|}{x - 2}$$ need a left-hand and a right-hand calculation.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Choosing the method">
Find $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x}$$.

1. Substitution gives $$\frac{0}{0}$$, and the expression is trigonometric.
2. Rewrite: $$\frac{\tan x}{x} = \frac{\sin x}{x} \cdot \frac{1}{\cos x}$$.
3. Use the limit laws: $$1 \cdot \frac{1}{\cos 0} = 1$$.

**Answer:** $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

What is $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(8x)}{2x}$$?

**Answer:** $$4$$

**Why:** Write it as $$\frac{8}{2} \cdot \frac{\sin(8x)}{8x}$$; the second factor approaches 1.

**Working:** Using $$\lim_{u \to 0} \frac{\sin u}{u} = 1$$ with $$u = 8x$$: $$\frac{\sin(8x)}{2x} = \frac{8}{2} \cdot \frac{\sin(8x)}{8x} \to \frac{8}{2}$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1$$: $$\frac{\sin u}{u} \to 1$$ only when the angle and the denominator match. Here they differ by a factor of $$\frac{8}{2}$$.
- $$\frac{1}{4}$$: This inverts the ratio of the coefficients.
- $$0$$: Substitution gives $$\frac{0}{0}$$, which is indeterminate, not 0.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** What is $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x + 4} - 2}{x}$$?

**A.** $$\frac{1}{4}$$. Rationalizing gives $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2}$$, which approaches $$\frac{1}{4}$$. Multiply by $$\frac{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2}{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2}$$. The numerator becomes $$x$$, which cancels, leaving $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 4} + 2} \to \frac{1}{4}$$.
