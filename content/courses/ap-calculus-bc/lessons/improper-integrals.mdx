Can a region that stretches out forever have finite area? Sometimes, yes. Improper integrals answer the question by replacing the troublesome limit of integration with a variable and taking a limit.

## Infinite limits of integration

$$
\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx = \lim_{b \to \infty} \int_a^b f(x)\,dx.
$$

If the limit is a finite number, the integral **converges** to it; otherwise it **diverges**.

## Unbounded integrands

If $$f$$ has a vertical asymptote at $$x = a$$:

$$
\int_a^c f(x)\,dx = \lim_{t \to a^+} \int_t^c f(x)\,dx.
$$

Check for asymptotes **inside** the interval too. $$\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx$$ looks harmless, but the integrand blows up at 0; splitting there shows it diverges.

## The p-integrals

$$
\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^p}\,dx \text{ converges exactly when } p > 1, \qquad \int_0^1 \frac{1}{x^p}\,dx \text{ converges exactly when } p < 1.
$$

These feed directly into the p-series test (topic 10.5).

<Note title="Show the limit">
The AP exam requires limit notation in the work: write $$\lim_{b \to \infty}$$ and evaluate it. Writing $$\frac{1}{\infty} = 0$$ does not earn the point.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="An exponential tail">
Evaluate $$\int_0^\infty x e^{-x^2}\,dx$$.

1. $$\int_0^b xe^{-x^2}\,dx = \left[-\frac{1}{2}e^{-x^2}\right]_0^b = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}e^{-b^2}$$.
2. $$\lim_{b \to \infty}\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}e^{-b^2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}$$.

**Answer:** the integral converges to $$\frac{1}{2}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** What is $$\int_0^{\infty} e^{-4x}\,dx$$?

**A.** $$\frac{1}{4}$$. $$\lim_{b \to \infty}\left[-\frac{1}{4}e^{-4x}\right]_0^b = \frac{1}{4}$$. $$\int_0^b e^{-4x}dx = \frac{1}{4}\left(1 - e^{-4b}\right) \to \frac{1}{4}$$.

**Q.** What is $$\int_0^{1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx$$?

**A.** $$2$$. $$\lim_{t \to 0^+}\left[2\sqrt{x}\right]_t^{1} = 2$$. The integrand blows up at 0, so use a limit: $$\lim_{t \to 0^+}\left(2\sqrt{1} - 2\sqrt{t}\right) = 2$$.

**Q.** What is $$\int_{2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^{2}}\,dx$$?

**A.** $$\frac{1}{2}$$. $$\lim_{b \to \infty}\left[\frac{x^{-1}}{-1}\right]_{2}^{b} = \frac{1}{1\cdot 2^{1}}$$. Write it as a limit: $$\lim_{b \to \infty}\int_{2}^{b} x^{-2}\,dx = \lim_{b \to \infty}\left(\frac{1}{1\cdot 2^{1}} - \frac{1}{1b^{1}}\right)$$.
