Polar coordinates describe a point by its distance $$r$$ from the origin and its angle $$\theta$$. Curves like circles centered at the origin, spirals and flower-shaped roses are far simpler in polar form.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/polar-cardioid.svg" alt="The cardioid r = 1 + cos theta, a heart-like curve with its point at the origin and widest at x = 2, with the region for theta from 0 to pi over 2 shaded." caption="The cardioid r = 1 + cos θ." />

## Conversions

$$
x = r\cos\theta, \qquad y = r\sin\theta, \qquad r^2 = x^2 + y^2.
$$

## Slope of a polar curve

Treat $$\theta$$ as a parameter: $$x(\theta) = r(\theta)\cos\theta$$ and $$y(\theta) = r(\theta)\sin\theta$$. Then

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\frac{dy}{d\theta}}{\frac{dx}{d\theta}} = \frac{r'\sin\theta + r\cos\theta}{r'\cos\theta - r\sin\theta}.
$$

## What dr over dθ means

$$\frac{dr}{d\theta}$$ is **not** the slope. It is the rate at which the distance from the origin changes:

- $$r > 0$$ and $$r' > 0$$: moving away from the origin.
- $$r > 0$$ and $$r' < 0$$: moving toward the origin.

The same applies to $$\frac{dx}{d\theta}$$ and $$\frac{dy}{d\theta}$$: they tell you whether the curve moves right or left, up or down, as $$\theta$$ increases.

<Note title="Use the product rule">
$$y = r(\theta)\sin\theta$$ is a product, so $$\frac{dy}{d\theta} = r'(\theta)\sin\theta + r(\theta)\cos\theta$$. Forgetting either term is the usual error.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Slope on a cardioid">
Find the slope of $$r = 1 + \cos\theta$$ at $$\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$$.

1. $$r = 1$$, $$r' = -\sin\theta = -1$$ at $$\frac{\pi}{2}$$.
2. $$\frac{dy}{d\theta} = r'\sin\theta + r\cos\theta = -1 + 0 = -1$$.
3. $$\frac{dx}{d\theta} = r'\cos\theta - r\sin\theta = 0 - 1 = -1$$.

**Answer:** $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-1}{-1} = 1$$.
</WorkedExample>

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** What is the slope of the line tangent to the polar curve $$r = 2 + 3\cos\theta$$ at $$\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$$?

**A.** $$\frac{3}{2}$$. $$\frac{dy}{d\theta} = -3$$ and $$\frac{dx}{d\theta} = -2$$. With $$x = r\cos\theta$$ and $$y = r\sin\theta$$: at $$\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$$, $$r = 2$$ and $$r' = (-3)$$. So $$\frac{dy}{d\theta} = r'\sin\theta + r\cos\theta = -3$$ and $$\frac{dx}{d\theta} = r'\cos\theta - r\sin\theta = -2$$; the slope is $$\frac{3}{2}$$.
