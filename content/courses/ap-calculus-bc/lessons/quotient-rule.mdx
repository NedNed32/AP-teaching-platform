The quotient rule handles one function divided by another. Its main danger is the order of subtraction, so learn it with a rhythm.

## The rule

$$
\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}
$$

A common chant: **"low d-high minus high d-low, over the square of what's below."** Here *high* is the numerator, *low* the denominator, and *d* means "derivative of".

## When not to use it

- **Constant numerator:** $$\frac{3}{x^2 + 1}$$ can be written $$3(x^2 + 1)^{-1}$$ and done with the chain rule.
- **Monomial denominator:** $$\frac{x^2 + 4x}{x}$$ simplifies to $$x + 4$$ first.

<Note title="Order matters">
Because subtraction is not commutative, $$\frac{fg' - f'g}{g^2}$$ gives the negative of the correct answer. Multiple-choice distractors are often built from exactly this mistake.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Derivative of tangent">
Use the quotient rule to show $$\frac{d}{dx}\tan x = \sec^2 x$$.

1. $$\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$$.
2. $$\frac{\cos x \cdot \cos x - \sin x \cdot (-\sin x)}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{\cos^2 x + \sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x}$$.
3. The numerator is 1 by the Pythagorean identity.

**Answer:** $$\frac{1}{\cos^2 x} = \sec^2 x$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

If $$f(x) = \frac{2x - 6}{-4x - 1}$$, what is $$f'(2)$$?

**Answer:** $$-\frac{26}{81}$$

**Why:** The quotient rule gives $$f'(x) = \frac{-26}{(-4x - 1)^2}$$, and at $$x = 2$$ the denominator is $$81$$.

**Working:** $$f'(x) = \frac{(-4x - 1)(2) - (2x - 6)(-4)}{(-4x - 1)^2} = \frac{-26}{(-4x - 1)^2}$$. At $$x = 2$$: $$\frac{-26}{81}$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$\frac{26}{81}$$: This subtracts in the wrong order: the quotient rule is (bottom · top′ − top · bottom′) / bottom².
- $$-\frac{1}{2}$$: This divides the derivative of the numerator by the derivative of the denominator, which is not the quotient rule.
- $$\frac{26}{9}$$: This forgets to square the denominator.
