Derivatives take a quantity and give its rate. Unit 6 runs the other way: given a rate, how much has accumulated? The answer turns out to be an area.

## Rate times time

If a car drives at a constant 60 miles per hour for 3 hours, it covers $$60 \times 3 = 180$$ miles. On a graph of velocity against time, that product is the area of a rectangle of height 60 and width 3.

The same idea works for any rate:

| Rate | Area under the rate graph |
| --- | --- |
| velocity (m/s) | displacement (m) |
| flow rate (L/min) | volume (L) |
| growth rate (people/yr) | change in population (people) |

## When the rate is not constant

If the rate changes, the region under its graph is no longer a rectangle. We can still find its area, either exactly (with geometry or, soon, integrals) or approximately (with rectangles, topic 6.2). The units are always *rate units times input units*.

## Signed area

When a rate is negative, the quantity is decreasing. Area below the axis therefore counts as **negative** change. The total of the positive and negative areas is the **net change**.

<Note title="Net change versus total amount">
Net change tells you how much a quantity changed. To find the final amount, add the net change to the starting amount: final = initial + net change.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Area from a piecewise-linear rate">
A runner's velocity rises steadily from 0 to 6 m/s over 4 seconds, then stays at 6 m/s for 5 more seconds. How far does the runner go?

1. First 4 seconds: a triangle with area $$\frac{1}{2}(4)(6) = 12$$.
2. Next 5 seconds: a rectangle with area $$5 \times 6 = 30$$.

**Answer:** $$12 + 30 = 42$$ meters.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Water flows into a tank at a rate of $$R(t)$$ gallons per minute, where $$R$$ is a continuous function of time $$t$$ in minutes. Selected values are shown.

*Rate of flow R(t)*

| $$t$$ (minutes) | $$R(t)$$ (gallons per minute) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 5 |
| 2 | 12 |
| 3 | 10 |
| 7 | 3 |
| 10 | 9 |

Using a left Riemann sum with the four subintervals indicated by the table, what is the approximation of $$\int_0^{10} R(t)\,dt$$?

**Answer:** $$71$$

**Why:** Multiply each subinterval's width by the appropriate height and add.

**Working:** The widths are 2, 1, 4, 3. Use the left endpoint of each subinterval, multiply by the width and add: $$71$$ gallons.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$73$$: This is the right Riemann sum.
- $$72$$: This is the trapezoidal sum.
- $$60$$: This treats every subinterval as width 2, but the subintervals have unequal widths.

### Worked exam question 3

Water flows into a tank at a rate of $$R(t)$$ gallons per minute, where $$R$$ is a continuous function of time $$t$$ in minutes. Selected values are shown.

*Rate of flow R(t)*

| $$t$$ (minutes) | $$R(t)$$ (gallons per minute) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 11 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 14 |
| 7 | 15 |
| 10 | 14 |

Using a trapezoidal sum with the four subintervals indicated by the table, what is the approximation of $$\int_0^{10} R(t)\,dt$$?

**Answer:** $$\frac{245}{2}$$

**Why:** Multiply each subinterval's width by the appropriate height and add.

**Working:** The widths are 2, 1, 4, 3. Average the endpoint heights on each subinterval, multiply by the width and add: $$\frac{245}{2}$$ gallons.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$125$$: This is the left Riemann sum.
- $$120$$: This is the right Riemann sum.
- $$84$$: This treats every subinterval as width 2, but the subintervals have unequal widths.

### Worked exam question 4

Sand is added to a pile at a rate modeled by $$R(t) = 13 + 4\sin\left(\frac{t}{6}\right)\sqrt{t + 1}$$ cubic feet per hour, for $$0 \leq t \leq 12$$. At time $$t = 0$$ the pile contains 203 cubic feet of sand. How much sand is in the pile at $$t = 12$$?

**Answer:** $$455.654$$

**Why:** $$203 + \int_0^{12} R(t)\,dt$$.

**Working:** Amount = initial amount + accumulated change = $$203 + \int_0^{12} R(t)\,dt \approx 203 + 252.654 = 455.654$$ cubic feet.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$252.654$$: This is only the sand added; include the initial amount.
- $$229.114$$: This adds the rate at one instant, not the accumulated amount.
- $$516.369$$: This assumes the rate stays at R(T) for the whole time.
