In Unit 4 you differentiated position to get velocity. Now you go backwards: integrate velocity to recover how far a particle moved and where it ends up.

## Three different questions

| Question | Integral |
| --- | --- |
| How far did position change (displacement)? | $$\int_a^b v(t)\,dt$$ |
| Where is the particle at time $$b$$? | $$x(b) = x(a) + \int_a^b v(t)\,dt$$ |
| How far did it travel in total? | $$\int_a^b \lvert v(t)\rvert\,dt$$ |

Similarly, $$v(b) = v(a) + \int_a^b a(t)\,dt$$.

## Total distance by hand

Without a calculator, split the interval at the times where $$v$$ changes sign, integrate on each piece, and add the absolute values. With a calculator, integrate $$|v(t)|$$ directly.

<Note title="Position needs a starting point">
The integral of velocity gives a change in position, not the position itself. If a question asks *where* the particle is, add the initial position.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Splitting at a turn">
A particle has $$v(t) = t^2 - 4t + 3$$ on $$0 \leq t \leq 3$$. Find its displacement and total distance.

1. $$v(t) = (t - 1)(t - 3)$$: positive on $$(0, 1)$$, negative on $$(1, 3)$$.
2. $$\int_0^1 v\,dt = \left[\frac{t^3}{3} - 2t^2 + 3t\right]_0^1 = \frac{4}{3}$$.
3. $$\int_1^3 v\,dt = 0 - \frac{4}{3} = -\frac{4}{3}$$.
4. Displacement: $$\frac{4}{3} - \frac{4}{3} = 0$$. Distance: $$\frac{4}{3} + \frac{4}{3}$$.

**Answer:** displacement 0; total distance $$\frac{8}{3}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A particle moves along a line with velocity $$v(t) = t^{2} - t$$. What is the total distance traveled by the particle from $$t = 0$$ to $$t = 2$$?

**Answer:** $$1$$

**Why:** Total distance is $$\int_0^{2} |v(t)|\,dt$$; split at $$t = 1$$, where $$v$$ changes sign.

**Working:** $$\int_0^{1} -v\,dt = \frac{1}{6}$$ and $$\int_{1}^{2} v\,dt = \frac{5}{6}$$. Total distance $$= 1$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$\frac{2}{3}$$: This is the displacement; it subtracts the distance traveled backward.
- $$2$$: This is the velocity at the final time.
- $$\frac{4}{3}$$: This counts the backward part twice.

### Worked exam question 2

A particle moves along the x-axis with velocity $$v(t) = t^{2} - 2t$$, and its position at $$t = 0$$ is $$x(0) = 4$$. What is the position of the particle at $$t = 3$$?

**Answer:** $$4$$

**Why:** $$x(3) = x(0) + \int_0^{3} v(t)\,dt$$.

**Working:** $$x(3) = 4 + \int_0^{3} (t^{2} - 2t)\,dt = 4 + 0 = 4$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0$$: This is the displacement; add the starting position.
- $$7$$: This adds the velocity at one instant, not the accumulated change in position.
- $$\frac{20}{3}$$: This adds the total distance, but position uses the signed displacement.

### Free-response practice: A particle in motion

A particle moves along the x-axis. Its velocity is given by $$v(t) = 3\sin\left(\frac{t^2}{5}\right) - 1.5$$ for $$0 \leq t \leq 6$$. At time $$t = 0$$ the particle is at position $$x = 3$$.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the particle at time $$t = 2.5$$. Is the speed of the particle increasing or decreasing at that time? Give a reason.

To earn the point: Gives the correct acceleration. Model answer: $$a(2.5) = v'(2.5) \approx 0.946$$

To earn the point: Concludes about the speed by comparing the signs of velocity and acceleration. Model answer: $$v(2.5) \approx 1.347$$. Velocity and acceleration have the same sign, so the speed is increasing.

**(b)** Find the position of the particle at time $$t = 6$$.

To earn the point: Uses the initial position plus the integral of velocity. Model answer: $$x(6) = 3 + \int_0^{6} v(t)\,dt$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct position. Model answer: $$-2.612$$

**(c)** Find the total distance traveled by the particle over $$0 \leq t \leq 6$$.

To earn the point: Integrates the absolute value of velocity (or splits at the sign changes). Model answer: $$\int_0^{6} |v(t)|\,dt$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct distance. Model answer: $$9.572$$

**(d)** At what times in the interval $$0 < t < 6$$ does the particle change direction? Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Identifies the times where $$v$$ changes sign, with justification. Model answer: $$v(t)$$ changes sign at $$t \approx 1.618$$, $$t \approx 3.618$$ and $$t \approx 5.834$$, so the particle changes direction there.

### Free-response practice: Particle motion without a calculator

A particle moves along the x-axis with velocity $$v(t) = 3t^{2} - 12t + 9$$ for $$t \geq 0$$. At time $$t = 0$$ the particle is at $$x = 2$$.

**(a)** At what times does the particle change direction? Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Gives both times with a sign-change justification. Model answer: $$v(t) = 3(t - 1)(t - 3)$$ changes sign at $$t = 1$$ and $$t = 3$$.

**(b)** Is the speed of the particle increasing or decreasing at time $$t = 4$$? Give a reason.

To earn the point: Finds the velocity and acceleration at the time. Model answer: $$v(4) = 9$$, $$a(4) = 12$$

To earn the point: Concludes from the signs. Model answer: Same signs, so the speed is increasing.

**(c)** Find the position of the particle at time $$t = 5$$.

To earn the point: Uses the initial position and an antiderivative of $$v$$. Model answer: $$x(t) = t^{3} - 6t^{2} + 9t + 2$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct position. Model answer: $$x(5) = 22$$

**(d)** Find the total distance traveled by the particle from $$t = 0$$ to $$t = 5$$.

To earn the point: Splits the motion at the turning points. Model answer: $$|x(1) - x(0)| + |x(3) - x(1)| + |x(5) - x(3)|$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct distance. Model answer: $$28$$
