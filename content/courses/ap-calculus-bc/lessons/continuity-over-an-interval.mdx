Many of the most important theorems in calculus, including the Intermediate Value Theorem, the Extreme Value Theorem and the Mean Value Theorem, begin with "if $$f$$ is continuous on $$[a, b]$$". So you need to know quickly where familiar functions are continuous.

## Functions that are continuous on their domains

Each of these is continuous at every point where it is defined:

- polynomials (everywhere)
- rational functions (everywhere except where the denominator is 0)
- roots, such as $$\sqrt{x}$$ on $$[0, \infty)$$
- $$\sin x$$ and $$\cos x$$ (everywhere); $$\tan x$$ except at odd multiples of $$\frac{\pi}{2}$$
- $$e^x$$ (everywhere) and $$\ln x$$ on $$(0, \infty)$$

Sums, differences, products, quotients (where the denominator is nonzero) and compositions of continuous functions are continuous.

## Closed intervals

At an endpoint you can only approach from inside the interval, so continuity on $$[a, b]$$ asks for **one-sided** continuity at $$a$$ and $$b$$.

<Note title="Differentiable implies continuous">
On free-response questions you are often told a function is differentiable. That is enough to conclude it is continuous, because differentiability implies continuity (topic 2.4). You can then use the IVT or EVT.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Finding the intervals of continuity">
Where is $$g(x) = \frac{\ln x}{x - 2}$$ continuous?

1. $$\ln x$$ requires $$x > 0$$.
2. The denominator is 0 at $$x = 2$$.
3. Elsewhere $$g$$ is a quotient of continuous functions.

**Answer:** $$g$$ is continuous on $$(0, 2)$$ and $$(2, \infty)$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The function $$f$$ is continuous on the closed interval $$[0, 4]$$. Selected values of $$f$$ are shown in the table.

*Values of f*

| $$x$$ | $$f(x)$$ |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 5 |
| 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 3 |
| 3 | -1 |
| 4 | 2 |

What is the least number of solutions that the equation $$f(x) = 0$$ must have on the interval $$[0, 4]$$?

**Answer:** $$2$$

**Why:** The sign of $$f$$ changes 2 times between consecutive table values, and the Intermediate Value Theorem guarantees a zero in each such interval.

**Working:** Because $$f$$ is continuous, the Intermediate Value Theorem guarantees a solution of $$f(x) = 0$$ on every interval where the values change sign. Counting the sign changes in the table gives 2.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1$$: This undercounts: each sign change between consecutive values guarantees its own zero.
- $$3$$: The IVT only guarantees zeros where the sign changes; other zeros are possible but not required.
- $$4$$: The IVT only guarantees zeros where the sign changes; other zeros are possible but not required.
