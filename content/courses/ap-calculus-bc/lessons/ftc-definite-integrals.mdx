Computing areas from limits of sums is hard. The second part of the Fundamental Theorem replaces all of that with one subtraction, using any antiderivative.

## FTC part 2

If $$f$$ is continuous on $$[a, b]$$ and $$F$$ is any antiderivative of $$f$$ (so $$F' = f$$), then

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a) = F(x)\Big|_a^b.
$$

The constant of integration cancels in the subtraction, so you can ignore it.

## The net change form

Applying the theorem to $$F'$$ gives the most useful form for applications:

$$
F(b) = F(a) + \int_a^b F'(x)\,dx.
$$

*Final amount = starting amount + accumulated change.* Every rate-in context on the exam uses this.

<Note title="Use it in both directions">
Sometimes you know $$f(b)$$ and need $$f(a)$$: rearrange to $$f(a) = f(b) - \int_a^b f'(x)\,dx$$. And on calculator questions, the integral of a rate is computed numerically, then added to the initial value.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Evaluating with FTC">
Evaluate $$\int_0^{\pi/2} (\cos x + 2x)\,dx$$.

1. Antiderivative: $$\sin x + x^2$$.
2. Upper limit: $$\sin\frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = 1 + \frac{\pi^2}{4}$$.
3. Lower limit: $$\sin 0 + 0 = 0$$.

**Answer:** $$1 + \frac{\pi^2}{4} \approx 3.467$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

What is $$\int_{-1}^{1} \left(-6x^{2} + 6x - 4\right)dx$$?

**Answer:** $$-12$$

**Why:** An antiderivative is $$F(x) = -2x^{3} + 3x^{2} - 4x$$; $$F(1) - F(-1) = -12$$.

**Working:** By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, $$\int_{-1}^{1} f(x)\,dx = F(1) - F(-1)$$ with $$F(x) = -2x^{3} + 3x^{2} - 4x$$. That is $$-3 - (9) = -12$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$-3$$: This is F(b) only; subtract F(a) as well.
- $$12$$: This subtracts values of the integrand instead of an antiderivative.
- $$6$$: This adds F(a) instead of subtracting it.

### Worked exam question 2

Sand is added to a pile at a rate modeled by $$R(t) = 13 + 4\sin\left(\frac{t}{6}\right)\sqrt{t + 1}$$ cubic feet per hour, for $$0 \leq t \leq 12$$. At time $$t = 0$$ the pile contains 203 cubic feet of sand. How much sand is in the pile at $$t = 12$$?

**Answer:** $$455.654$$

**Why:** $$203 + \int_0^{12} R(t)\,dt$$.

**Working:** Amount = initial amount + accumulated change = $$203 + \int_0^{12} R(t)\,dt \approx 203 + 252.654 = 455.654$$ cubic feet.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$252.654$$: This is only the sand added; include the initial amount.
- $$229.114$$: This adds the rate at one instant, not the accumulated amount.
- $$516.369$$: This assumes the rate stays at R(T) for the whole time.

**Q.** What is $$\int_0^{\pi/6} \sin(6x)\,dx$$?

**A.** $$\frac{1}{3}$$. $$-\frac{1}{6}\cos(6x)\Big|_0^{\pi/6} = -\frac{1}{6}(-1 - 1) = \frac{2}{6}$$. $$\int \sin(6x)\,dx = -\frac{1}{6}\cos(6x) + C$$. Evaluating: $$-\frac{1}{6}\left[\cos\pi - \cos 0\right] = \frac{2}{6}$$.

### Free-response practice: Water in a tank

Water enters a tank at a rate modeled by $$E(t) = 13 + 3\sin\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)$$ gallons per hour and leaves the tank at a rate modeled by $$L(t) = 5\sqrt{t + 1}$$ gallons per hour, for $$0 \leq t \leq 8$$. At time $$t = 0$$ the tank contains 137 gallons of water.

**(a)** How many gallons of water enter the tank during the time interval $$0 \leq t \leq 8$$?

To earn the point: Writes a definite integral of $$E$$ over the interval. Model answer: $$\int_0^{8} E(t)\,dt$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct value. Model answer: 113.922 gallons

**(b)** Is the amount of water in the tank increasing or decreasing at time $$t = 2$$? Give a reason for your answer.

To earn the point: Concludes correctly, with a reason that compares $$E$$ and $$L$$ (or uses the sign of $$E - L$$). Model answer: $$E(2) - L(2) \approx 6.864$$, which is positive, so the amount is increasing.

**(c)** At what time $$t$$, for $$0 \leq t \leq 8$$, is the amount of water in the tank greatest? Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Sets $$E(t) - L(t) = 0$$ to find the critical points. Model answer: $$t \approx 6.080$$

To earn the point: Evaluates the amount at the critical points and the endpoints (Candidates Test). Model answer: $$W(0.000) \approx 137.000$$, $$W(6.080) \approx 168.547$$, $$W(8.000) \approx 164.255$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct time with justification. Model answer: The amount is greatest at $$t \approx 6.080$$.

**(d)** How many gallons of water are in the tank at time $$t = 8$$?

To earn the point: Uses the initial amount plus the integral of the net rate. Model answer: $$137 + \int_0^{8} (E(t) - L(t))\,dt$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct value. Model answer: 164.255 gallons
