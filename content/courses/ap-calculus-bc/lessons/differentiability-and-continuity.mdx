A derivative is a limit, so it can fail to exist. Knowing *where* it fails, and how that relates to continuity, is tested both in multiple choice and in free-response justifications.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/not-differentiable.svg" alt="Three graphs side by side: a V-shaped corner, a cusp shaped like a bird's beak, and a curve that becomes vertical at one point." caption="Three continuous graphs that are not differentiable at one point." />

## The one-way implication

$$
\text{differentiable at } a \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{continuous at } a.
$$

The converse is false: a function can be continuous at a point yet have no derivative there.

## Where derivatives fail to exist

1. **Discontinuities** of any kind (hole, jump, asymptote).
2. **Corners**: one-sided slopes are different numbers.
3. **Cusps**: slopes blow up in opposite directions.
4. **Vertical tangents**: slopes blow up in the same direction.

## Checking a piecewise function

For $$f$$ to be differentiable at a break point $$c$$:

1. $$f$$ must be continuous at $$c$$ (values match), and
2. the derivatives of the two pieces must agree at $$c$$ (slopes match).

<Note title="Contrapositive">
The useful direction on the exam is often the contrapositive: if $$f$$ is **not continuous** at $$a$$, then $$f$$ is **not differentiable** at $$a$$. You do not need to check slopes at a jump.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Values and slopes must match">
Find $$a$$ and $$b$$ so that $$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 & x \leq 1 \\ ax + b & x > 1 \end{cases}$$ is differentiable at $$x = 1$$.

1. Continuity: $$1^2 = a + b$$, so $$a + b = 1$$.
2. Matching slopes: the left piece has derivative $$2x$$, which is 2 at $$x = 1$$; the right piece has slope $$a$$. So $$a = 2$$.
3. Then $$b = 1 - 2 = -1$$.

**Answer:** $$a = 2$$ and $$b = -1$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Let $$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^{2} & x \leq 1 \\ 2x - 3 & x > 1 \end{cases}$$. Which of the following is true?

**Answer:** $$f$$ is neither continuous nor differentiable at $$x = 1$$.

**Why:** The left piece gives $$f(1) = 1$$ with slope $$2$$; the right piece approaches $$-1$$ with slope $$2$$. The one-sided limits differ, so $$f$$ is not continuous, and therefore not differentiable.

**Working:** The left piece gives $$f(1) = 1$$ with slope $$2$$; the right piece approaches $$-1$$ with slope $$2$$. A jump discontinuity rules out continuity, and a function that isn't continuous at a point can't be differentiable there.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$f$$ is continuous and differentiable at $$x = 1$$.: The one-sided limits differ, so $$f$$ has a jump.
- $$f$$ is differentiable but not continuous at $$x = 1$$.: Differentiability implies continuity, so this can never happen.
- $$f$$ is continuous but not differentiable at $$x = 1$$.: The one-sided limits differ, so $$f$$ is not continuous.
