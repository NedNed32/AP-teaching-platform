Sometimes vertical slices are awkward: one curve is a sideways parabola, or the top boundary changes partway across. Turning the slices sideways often makes the integral simpler.

## The formula

If $$f(y) \geq g(y)$$ for $$c \leq y \leq d$$:

$$
\text{Area} = \int_c^d [f(y) - g(y)]\,dy = \int_c^d (\text{right} - \text{left})\,dy.
$$

The limits are **y-values**, and the functions must be written as $$x$$ in terms of $$y$$.

## Choosing the direction

| Situation | Better choice |
| --- | --- |
| Curves given as $$y = f(x)$$, one curve always on top | slices in $$x$$ |
| Curves given as $$x = g(y)$$ | slices in $$y$$ |
| Top boundary changes partway across | slices in $$y$$ may avoid two integrals |

<Note title="Rewrite the curves">
To integrate in $$y$$, solve each equation for $$x$$: $$y = \sqrt{x}$$ becomes $$x = y^2$$ (for $$y \geq 0$$), and $$y = 2x - 4$$ becomes $$x = \frac{y + 4}{2}$$.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A region best sliced horizontally">
Find the area bounded by $$x = y^2$$ and $$x = y + 2$$.

1. Intersections: $$y^2 = y + 2$$ gives $$y = -1$$ and $$y = 2$$.
2. At $$y = 0$$: $$y + 2 = 2 > 0 = y^2$$, so the line is on the right.
3. $$\int_{-1}^{2} (y + 2 - y^2)\,dy = \left[\frac{y^2}{2} + 2y - \frac{y^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{2} = \frac{10}{3} - \left(-\frac{7}{6}\right)$$.

**Answer:** $$\frac{9}{2}$$.
</WorkedExample>

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** What is the area of the region enclosed by the graphs of $$y = x^2$$ and $$y = 3x$$?

**A.** $$\frac{9}{2}$$. $$\int_0^{3} (3x - x^2)\,dx$$: top curve minus bottom curve. The curves meet at $$0$$ and $$3$$. Area $$= \int_0^{3} (3u - u^2)\,du = \frac{3u^2}{2} - \frac{u^3}{3}\Big|_0^{3} = \frac{9}{2}$$.

**Q.** What is the area of the region enclosed by the graphs of $$y = x^2$$ and $$y = 2x$$?

**A.** $$\frac{4}{3}$$. $$\int_0^{2} (2x - x^2)\,dx$$: top curve minus bottom curve. The curves meet at $$0$$ and $$2$$. Area $$= \int_0^{2} (2u - u^2)\,du = \frac{2u^2}{2} - \frac{u^3}{3}\Big|_0^{2} = \frac{4}{3}$$.

**Q.** What is the area of the region enclosed by the curves $$x = y^2$$ and $$x = y$$?

**A.** $$\frac{1}{6}$$. $$\int_0^{1} (y - y^2)\,dy$$: right curve minus left curve. The curves meet at $$0$$ and $$1$$. Area $$= \int_0^{1} (u - u^2)\,du = \frac{u^2}{2} - \frac{u^3}{3}\Big|_0^{1} = \frac{1}{6}$$.

**Q.** What is the area of the region enclosed by the graphs of $$y = x^2$$ and $$y = x$$?
