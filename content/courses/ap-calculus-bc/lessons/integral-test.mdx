A series with positive terms is like a staircase of rectangles of width 1. Comparing that staircase with the area under a smooth curve turns a series question into an improper-integral question.

## The test

Let $$a_n = f(n)$$, where $$f$$ is **positive, continuous and decreasing** for $$x \geq k$$. Then

$$
\sum_{n=k}^{\infty} a_n \text{ and } \int_k^\infty f(x)\,dx \text{ either both converge or both diverge.}
$$

**Why:** the rectangles of height $$f(n)$$ fit just under (or just over) the curve, so the series and the integral are trapped close together.

## The integral is not the sum

If both converge, they usually converge to **different** values. The test decides convergence only.

<Note title="Check the hypotheses">
On a free-response justification, state that $$f$$ is positive, continuous and decreasing on the interval. Showing decreasing may require $$f'(x) < 0$$.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Applying the integral test">
Does $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{e^{n^2}}$$ converge?

1. $$f(x) = xe^{-x^2}$$ is positive and continuous for $$x \geq 1$$, and $$f'(x) = e^{-x^2}(1 - 2x^2) < 0$$ there, so it is decreasing.
2. $$\int_1^\infty xe^{-x^2}\,dx = \lim_{b \to \infty}\left[-\frac{1}{2}e^{-x^2}\right]_1^b = \frac{1}{2e}$$.

**Answer:** the integral converges, so the series converges by the integral test.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

What is the sum of $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 5\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n$$?

**Answer:** $$10$$

**Why:** Geometric with first term $$\frac{10}{3}$$ and ratio $$\frac{2}{3}$$.

**Working:** $$|r| = \left|\frac{2}{3}\right| < 1$$, so the series converges to $$\frac{\frac{10}{3}}{1 - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)} = 10$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$15$$: The first term depends on the starting index; check the term for the first value of n.
- $$3$$: The formula is $$\frac{a}{1 - r}$$, not $$\frac{a}{1 + r}$$.
- $$5$$: This divides by $$r$$ instead of $$1 - r$$.

### Worked exam question 2

Which of the following series converge?

I. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \cos\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)$$

II. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^3}$$

III. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$$

**Answer:** II and III only

**Why:** I: diverges (terms approach 1 (nth term test)). II: converges (absolute values form a p-series with p = 3). III: converges (alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges).

**Working:** Series I diverges: terms approach 1 (nth term test). Series II converges: absolute values form a p-series with p = 3. Series III converges: alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- I and III only: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
- III only: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
- None of them: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which of the following best describes $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$$?

**A.** It converges conditionally.. alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges. Test the absolute values first, then the series itself: alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges.

**Q.** Which of the following best describes $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{n + 1}$$?

**A.** It diverges.. terms approach 1, not 0 (nth term test). Test the absolute values first, then the series itself: terms approach 1, not 0 (nth term test).

**Q.** Which of the following best describes $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$$?

**A.** It converges conditionally.. alternating series test, but p = 1/2 for the absolute values. Test the absolute values first, then the series itself: alternating series test, but p = 1/2 for the absolute values.

**Q.** Which of the following best describes $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^3}$$?

**A.** It converges absolutely.. absolute values form a p-series with p = 3. Test the absolute values first, then the series itself: absolute values form a p-series with p = 3.
