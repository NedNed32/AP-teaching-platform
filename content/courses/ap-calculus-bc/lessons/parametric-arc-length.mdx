For a curve traced by $$(x(t), y(t))$$, a tiny step in time moves the point by $$dx$$ horizontally and $$dy$$ vertically. The Pythagorean theorem gives the length of that step.

## The formula

$$
L = \int_a^b \sqrt{[x'(t)]^2 + [y'(t)]^2}\,dt.
$$

The integrand is the **speed** of the moving point. So the arc length of a curve traced once is the **total distance travelled** by a particle moving along it.

## Traced once

The formula counts every pass. If the parameter interval traces part of the curve twice, the integral gives twice that part's length. Check the parameter range against the picture.

<Note title="Calculator questions">
Arc length integrals usually need a calculator. Write the setup with the derivatives in place, then give the numerical value to three decimal places.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Length of a parametric arc">
Find the length of $$x = t^2$$, $$y = \frac{2}{3}t^3$$ for $$0 \leq t \leq 1$$.

1. $$x' = 2t$$, $$y' = 2t^2$$.
2. Speed: $$\sqrt{4t^2 + 4t^4} = 2t\sqrt{1 + t^2}$$ for $$t \geq 0$$.
3. $$\int_0^1 2t\sqrt{1 + t^2}\,dt = \frac{2}{3}(1 + t^2)^{3/2}\Big|_0^1 = \frac{2}{3}(2\sqrt{2} - 1)$$.

**Answer:** $$\frac{2}{3}(2\sqrt{2} - 1) \approx 1.219$$.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** A particle moves in the plane with $$x'(t) = 2\cos\left(\frac{t^2}{2}\right)$$ and $$y'(t) = e^{t/2}$$. What is the total distance the particle travels from $$t = 0$$ to $$t = 3$$?

**A.** $$8.619$$. Integrate the speed $$\sqrt{x'(t)^2 + y'(t)^2}$$. $$\int_0^{3} \sqrt{x'(t)^2 + y'(t)^2}\,dt \approx 8.619$$.
