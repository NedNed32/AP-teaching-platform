Can you add infinitely many numbers and get a finite answer? Walk halfway to a wall, then half the remaining distance, then half again: the steps $$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8} + \cdots$$ add up to exactly 1. Unit 10 makes sense of sums like this.

## Partial sums

An infinite series $$\sum a_n$$ is defined through its **partial sums**:

$$
S_1 = a_1, \quad S_2 = a_1 + a_2, \quad \ldots, \quad S_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n.
$$

If $$\lim_{N \to \infty} S_N = S$$ is finite, the series **converges** and its sum is $$S$$. Otherwise it **diverges**.

## Telescoping series

Some partial sums collapse:

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n + 1}\right) = 1 - \frac{1}{N + 1} \to 1.
$$

## Two sequences, not one

Keep straight the difference between the **terms** $$a_n$$ and the **partial sums** $$S_N$$. The terms of $$\sum \frac{1}{n}$$ approach 0, yet the partial sums grow without bound (topic 10.5).

<Note title="What the tests do">
For most series you cannot find $$S_N$$ in closed form. Topics 10.3 to 10.9 give tests that decide convergence without finding the sum.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Sum from partial sums">
The partial sums of $$\sum a_n$$ are $$S_N = \frac{2N}{N + 3}$$. Find $$a_1$$ and the sum of the series.

1. $$a_1 = S_1 = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$.
2. $$\lim_{N \to \infty}\frac{2N}{N + 3} = 2$$.

**Answer:** $$a_1 = \frac{1}{2}$$, and the series converges to 2.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Which of the following series converge?

I. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \cos\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)$$

II. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^3}$$

III. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$$

**Answer:** II and III only

**Why:** I: diverges (terms approach 1 (nth term test)). II: converges (absolute values form a p-series with p = 3). III: converges (alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges).

**Working:** Series I diverges: terms approach 1 (nth term test). Series II converges: absolute values form a p-series with p = 3. Series III converges: alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- I and III only: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
- III only: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
- None of them: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
