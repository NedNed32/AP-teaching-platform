Once the objective is a function of one variable on a known domain, optimization is a short procedure. The part students skip, and lose points on, is justifying that the answer is truly the best.

## The procedure

1. Differentiate the objective function.
2. Find critical points in the domain.
3. **Justify** which gives the maximum or minimum:
   - **Closed domain:** use the Candidates Test.
   - **Open domain with one critical point:** show $$f'$$ changes sign there (First Derivative Test), or that $$f''$$ has one sign there; with only one critical point, the relative extremum is absolute.
4. Answer the question asked, with units: the location, the optimal value, or both.

<Note title="One critical point is special">
If a continuous function on an interval has exactly one critical point and it is a relative maximum, it is the absolute maximum. This fact justifies most textbook optimization answers.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Closest point on a curve">
Find the point on $$y = \sqrt{x}$$ closest to $$(3, 0)$$.

1. Distance squared (easier to optimize and minimized at the same point): $$D(x) = (x - 3)^2 + (\sqrt{x})^2 = x^2 - 5x + 9$$.
2. $$D'(x) = 2x - 5 = 0$$ at $$x = \frac{5}{2}$$.
3. $$D'$$ changes from negative to positive at $$\frac{5}{2}$$, and it is the only critical point, so it gives the absolute minimum.

**Answer:** the closest point is $$\left(\frac{5}{2}, \sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}\right)$$.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** An open-top box is made from a 24-inch by 24-inch square of cardboard by cutting equal squares of side $$x$$ from each corner and folding up the sides. What value of $$x$$ gives the box its maximum volume?

**A.** $$4$$. $$V = x(24 - 2x)^2$$, $$V' = (24 - 2x)(24 - 6x) = 0$$ at $$x = 4$$. $$V'(x) = (24 - 2x)^2 - 4x(24 - 2x) = (24 - 2x)(24 - 6x)$$. On $$0 < x < 12$$, the only critical point is $$x = 4$$, where $$V'$$ changes from positive to negative.

**Q.** A farmer has 240 meters of fencing to enclose a rectangular field along a straight river. No fence is needed along the river. What is the largest possible area of the field, in square meters?

**A.** $$7200$$. With width $$x$$ perpendicular to the river, $$A = x(240 - 2x)$$, maximized at $$x = 60$$. $$A(x) = x(240 - 2x)$$, $$A'(x) = 240 - 4x = 0$$ at $$x = 60$$, and $$A'' = -4 < 0$$, so this is a maximum. The dimensions are $$60$$ by $$120$$, with area $$7200$$.
