The p-series are the reference collection for the whole unit: their convergence is known instantly, and the comparison tests (topic 10.6) measure other series against them.

## The p-series test

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^p} \quad\begin{cases}\text{converges} & p > 1 \\ \text{diverges} & p \leq 1\end{cases}
$$

This follows from the integral test, since $$\int_1^\infty \frac{dx}{x^p}$$ converges exactly when $$p > 1$$.

## The harmonic series diverges

$$
1 + \frac{1}{2} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}}_{> \frac{1}{2}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{5} + \cdots + \frac{1}{8}}_{> \frac{1}{2}} + \cdots
$$

Grouping terms gives infinitely many chunks each larger than $$\frac{1}{2}$$, so the partial sums grow without bound, though very slowly: it takes about 12,000 terms to pass 10.

<Note title="Rewrite before testing">
$$\sum \frac{1}{n\sqrt{n}} = \sum \frac{1}{n^{3/2}}$$ is a p-series with $$p = \frac{3}{2}$$, so it converges. Simplify powers of $$n$$ first.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying p-series">
Which converge: (I) $$\sum \frac{1}{n^{0.9}}$$, (II) $$\sum \frac{4}{n^2}$$, (III) $$\sum \frac{\sqrt{n}}{n^2}$$?

1. (I): $$p = 0.9 \leq 1$$, diverges.
2. (II): $$4\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$$, $$p = 2$$, converges.
3. (III): $$\frac{n^{1/2}}{n^2} = \frac{1}{n^{3/2}}$$, $$p = 1.5$$, converges.

**Answer:** II and III.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Which of the following series converge?

I. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \cos\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)$$

II. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^3}$$

III. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$$

**Answer:** II and III only

**Why:** I: diverges (terms approach 1 (nth term test)). II: converges (absolute values form a p-series with p = 3). III: converges (alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges).

**Working:** Series I diverges: terms approach 1 (nth term test). Series II converges: absolute values form a p-series with p = 3. Series III converges: alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- I and III only: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
- III only: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
- None of them: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
