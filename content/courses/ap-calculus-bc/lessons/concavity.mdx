Two increasing functions can look very different: one curves upward ever more steeply, the other levels off. **Concavity** captures this bending, and the second derivative measures it.

## Concavity and f double prime

| Condition | Slope $$f'$$ is | Graph is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $$f''(x) > 0$$ | increasing | concave up |
| $$f''(x) < 0$$ | decreasing | concave down |

A concave-up graph lies above its tangent lines; a concave-down graph lies below them.

## Points of inflection

A point of inflection is where concavity **changes**. Candidates are where $$f''(x) = 0$$ or $$f''$$ is undefined, but a candidate is an inflection point only if $$f''$$ changes sign there.

## From a graph of f prime

If you are given the graph of $$f'$$:

- $$f$$ is concave up where $$f'$$ is **increasing** (the graph of $$f'$$ rises).
- $$f$$ has inflection points where $$f'$$ has a relative maximum or minimum.

<Note title="Justifying an inflection point">
Write: "The graph of $$f$$ has a point of inflection at $$x = 3$$ because $$f''$$ changes sign at $$x = 3$$" (or "because $$f'$$ changes from increasing to decreasing"). "$$f''(3) = 0$$" alone is not a justification.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Concavity of a quartic">
Find the intervals of concavity and inflection points of $$f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2$$.

1. $$f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x$$, $$f''(x) = 12x^2 - 12 = 12(x - 1)(x + 1)$$.
2. $$f'' > 0$$ for $$x < -1$$ and $$x > 1$$; $$f'' < 0$$ on $$(-1, 1)$$.
3. $$f''$$ changes sign at $$x = \pm 1$$.

**Answer:** concave up on $$(-\infty, -1)$$ and $$(1, \infty)$$, concave down on $$(-1, 1)$$, inflection points at $$x = -1$$ and $$x = 1$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

At what value of $$x$$ does the graph of $$f(x) = x^{3} - 6x^{2} + 3x - 4$$ have a point of inflection?

**Answer:** $$2$$

**Why:** $$f''(x) = 6x - 12$$ is zero and changes sign at $$x = 2$$.

**Working:** $$f'(x) = 3x^{2} - 12x + 3$$ and $$f''(x) = 6x - 12$$. $$f''$$ is linear, so it changes sign at its zero, $$x = 2$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$-2$$: Sign error when solving f″(x) = 0.
- $$3$$: This is −b/(2a), the vertex formula for a parabola, applied to the cubic's coefficients. Set f″(x) = 0 instead.
- $$0$$: Inflection points are where f″ changes sign, not where f″ has its constant term.

**Q.** On which of the following intervals is the graph of $$f$$ concave down?

**A.** $$(4, 5)$$. $$f'$$ is decreasing there, so $$f'' < 0$$. $$f$$ is concave down where $$f'$$ is decreasing, which on this graph means a segment sloping downward.

**Q.** On the open interval $$0 < x < 6$$, at how many values of $$x$$ does $$f$$ have a relative maximum?

**A.** $$2$$. Count the places where f′ crosses the x-axis from above to below. $$f$$ has a relative maximum where $$f'$$ changes from positive to negative. On this graph that happens 2 times.

### Free-response practice: Analyzing a function

Let $$f(x) = 2x^{3} - 6x^{2} - 18x - 1$$.

**(a)** Find the x-coordinates of all relative extrema of $$f$$, and classify each as a relative maximum or relative minimum. Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Finds the critical points. Model answer: $$f'(x) = 6(x + 1)(x - 3) = 0$$ at $$x = -1$$ and $$x = 3$$

To earn the point: Classifies both with a sign-change justification. Model answer: $$f'$$ changes from positive to negative at $$-1$$ (relative maximum) and from negative to positive at $$3$$ (relative minimum).

**(b)** On what intervals is the graph of $$f$$ concave down? Give a reason.

To earn the point: Gives the interval with a reason based on $$f''$$. Model answer: $$f''(x) = 12x - 12 < 0$$ for $$x < 1$$

**(c)** Find the absolute maximum value of $$f$$ on the closed interval $$[-2, 3]$$. Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Evaluates $$f$$ at the critical points and endpoints. Model answer: $$f(-2) = -5$$, $$f(-1) = 9$$, $$f(3) = -55$$, $$f(3) = -55$$

To earn the point: Gives the absolute maximum value. Model answer: $$9$$, at $$x = -1$$

**(d)** Find $$\lim_{x \to -1} \frac{f(x) - f(-1)}{(x + 1)^2}$$.

To earn the point: Recognizes the form 0/0 and applies L'Hospital's Rule (twice, or once with $$f'(-1) = 0$$). Model answer: $$\lim \frac{f'(x)}{2(x + 1)} = \lim \frac{f''(x)}{2}$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct value. Model answer: $$\frac{f''(-1)}{2} = -12$$
