Most differential equations cannot be solved exactly. Euler's method builds an approximate solution by walking along the slope field: take a short step along the tangent line, recompute the slope, step again.

## The algorithm

Start at $$(x_0, y_0)$$ with step $$\Delta x$$. Repeat:

$$
x_{n+1} = x_n + \Delta x, \qquad y_{n+1} = y_n + F(x_n, y_n)\,\Delta x,
$$

where $$F(x, y) = \frac{dy}{dx}$$. A table keeps the work organized.

| $$x_n$$ | $$y_n$$ | slope $$F(x_n, y_n)$$ | $$\Delta y = \text{slope} \cdot \Delta x$$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Over or under?

Each step follows a tangent line. If the solution is **concave up**, tangent lines lie below it, so Euler's method **underestimates**; concave down gives an overestimate. Use $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$$ to decide.

<Note title="Two steps means two slopes">
A common error is reusing the first slope for every step. The second step uses the slope at the new point $$(x_1, y_1)$$, computed from the approximate value $$y_1$$.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Two steps">
For $$\frac{dy}{dx} = x + y$$ with $$y(0) = 1$$, use two steps of size 0.5 to approximate $$y(1)$$.

1. At $$(0, 1)$$: slope $$1$$, so $$y_1 = 1 + 1(0.5) = 1.5$$.
2. At $$(0.5, 1.5)$$: slope $$2$$, so $$y_2 = 1.5 + 2(0.5) = 2.5$$.

**Answer:** $$y(1) \approx 2.5$$. (The exact value is $$2e - 2 \approx 3.437$$; solutions are concave up here, so Euler underestimates.)
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Let $$y = f(x)$$ be the solution to $$\frac{dy}{dx} = y - x^2$$ with $$f(1) = 3$$. What is the approximation of $$f(2)$$ found with Euler's method, using two steps of equal size?

**Answer:** $$\frac{39}{8}$$

**Why:** Step 1: slope $$2$$, $$y_1 = 4$$. Step 2: slope $$1.75$$ at $$(1.5, 4)$$.

**Working:** Step size $$0.5$$. $$y_1 = 3 + (2)(0.5) = 4$$; $$y_2 = 4 + (1.75)(0.5) = \frac{39}{8}$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$5$$: This reuses the first slope for the second step; the slope must be recomputed at the new point.
- $$4$$: This is only the first step.
- $$4.75$$: This uses the second slope for the whole distance.
