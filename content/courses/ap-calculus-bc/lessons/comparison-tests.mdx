Many series look almost like a p-series or a geometric series. Comparison tests make "almost like" precise.

## Direct comparison

For positive terms with $$a_n \leq b_n$$:

- smaller than a convergent series: **converges**;
- bigger than a divergent series: **diverges**.

The inequality must point the right way. Being smaller than a divergent series tells you nothing.

## Limit comparison

When the inequality is awkward, compare the **ratio**:

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = c, \quad 0 < c < \infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum a_n \text{ and } \sum b_n \text{ behave the same.}
$$

Choose $$b_n$$ by keeping only the dominant terms of the numerator and denominator.

<Note title="Finite and positive">
The limit comparison test needs $$c$$ to be a positive finite number. If the limit is 0 or $$\infty$$, the test as stated gives no conclusion.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Limit comparison">
Does $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2n^2 + 1}{n^4 + n}$$ converge?

1. Dominant terms: $$\frac{2n^2}{n^4} = \frac{2}{n^2}$$, so compare with $$b_n = \frac{1}{n^2}$$.
2. $$\lim \frac{a_n}{b_n} = \lim \frac{(2n^2 + 1)n^2}{n^4 + n} = 2$$, positive and finite.
3. $$\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$$ converges ($$p = 2$$).

**Answer:** the series converges by the limit comparison test.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Which of the following series converge?

I. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \cos\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)$$

II. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^3}$$

III. $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$$

**Answer:** II and III only

**Why:** I: diverges (terms approach 1 (nth term test)). II: converges (absolute values form a p-series with p = 3). III: converges (alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges).

**Working:** Series I diverges: terms approach 1 (nth term test). Series II converges: absolute values form a p-series with p = 3. Series III converges: alternating series test, but the harmonic series diverges.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- I and III only: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
- III only: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
- None of them: Check each series with an appropriate test: I diverges, II converges, III converges.
