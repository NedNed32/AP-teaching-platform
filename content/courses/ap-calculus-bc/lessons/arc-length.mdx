How long is a curved path? Approximate it with many tiny straight segments, each found with the Pythagorean theorem, and let the segments shrink.

## The formula

A tiny piece of the curve has horizontal change $$dx$$ and vertical change $$dy = f'(x)\,dx$$, so its length is

$$
\sqrt{dx^2 + dy^2} = \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}\,dx.
$$

Adding them up:

$$
L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}\,dx.
$$

## In practice

Arc length integrals rarely have nice antiderivatives, so on the AP exam they appear in calculator-active questions or as "set up but do not evaluate". The work to show is the correct integrand and limits.

<Note title="Distance travelled">
For a particle moving along a curve, the same idea gives the distance travelled. With parametric motion it becomes $$\int \sqrt{(x')^2 + (y')^2}\,dt$$, which is the speed integrated over time (topic 9.3).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A calculator arc length">
Find the length of $$y = \ln x$$ from $$x = 1$$ to $$x = 3$$.

1. $$f'(x) = \frac{1}{x}$$, so the integrand is $$\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x^2}}$$.
2. $$L = \int_1^3 \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{x^2}}\,dx$$.

**Answer:** about $$2.302$$ (by calculator).
</WorkedExample>
