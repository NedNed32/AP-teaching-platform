A function $$y = f(x)$$ tells you *where* a curve is. Parametric equations also tell you *when*: each value of the parameter $$t$$, often time, gives a point $$(x(t), y(t))$$. They can draw loops and vertical lines that no function can.

## Slope of a parametric curve

By the chain rule, $$\frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{dy}{dx}\cdot\frac{dx}{dt}$$, so

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}.
$$

| Condition | Tangent line |
| --- | --- |
| $$\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$$, $$\frac{dx}{dt} \neq 0$$ | horizontal |
| $$\frac{dx}{dt} = 0$$, $$\frac{dy}{dt} \neq 0$$ | vertical |

## Tangent lines

At a parameter value $$t_0$$, the point is $$(x(t_0), y(t_0))$$ and the slope is $$\frac{dy}{dx}$$ at $$t_0$$. Then use point-slope form as usual.

<Note title="Direction of motion">
The sign of $$\frac{dx}{dt}$$ says whether the point moves right or left; the sign of $$\frac{dy}{dt}$$ says up or down. Arrows on a parametric graph show this orientation.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Tangent line to a parametric curve">
Find the tangent line to $$x = 2\cos t$$, $$y = 3\sin t$$ at $$t = \frac{\pi}{4}$$.

1. Point: $$\left(\sqrt{2}, \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)$$.
2. $$\frac{dx}{dt} = -2\sin t$$, $$\frac{dy}{dt} = 3\cos t$$, so $$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{3\cos t}{2\sin t} = -\frac{3}{2}$$ at $$t = \frac{\pi}{4}$$.

**Answer:** $$y - \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} = -\frac{3}{2}\left(x - \sqrt{2}\right)$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A curve is defined by $$x = t^{2} + t$$ and $$y = t^{3} - t$$. What is $$\frac{dy}{dx}$$ at $$t = -2$$?

**Answer:** $$-\frac{11}{3}$$

**Why:** $$\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{11}{-3}$$.

**Working:** $$\frac{dx}{dt} = 2t + 1$$ and $$\frac{dy}{dt} = 3t^{2} - 1$$. At $$t = -2$$: $$\frac{11}{-3}$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$-\frac{3}{11}$$: This is $$\frac{dx/dt}{dy/dt}$$, upside down.
- $$11$$: This is $$\frac{dy}{dt}$$ alone.
- $$-6$$: This divides second derivatives, which is not the slope.

### Free-response practice: Motion in the plane

A particle moves in the xy-plane so that for $$0 \leq t \leq 4$$, $$\frac{dx}{dt} = 3\sin\left(\frac{t^2}{3}\right) + 1$$ and $$\frac{dy}{dt} = \cos(t)\,e^{t/4}$$. At $$t = 0$$ the particle is at $$(-2, -1)$$.

**(a)** Find the speed of the particle and its acceleration vector at $$t = 3.5$$.

To earn the point: Gives the speed. Model answer: $$\sqrt{x'(3.5)^2 + y'(3.5)^2} \approx 2.661$$

To earn the point: Gives the acceleration vector. Model answer: $$\langle x''(3.5), y''(3.5) \rangle \approx \langle -4.119, 0.280 \rangle$$

**(b)** Find the slope of the line tangent to the path of the particle at $$t = 3.5$$.

To earn the point: Divides the velocity components. Model answer: $$\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} \approx 1.576$$

**(c)** Find the position of the particle at $$t = 4$$.

To earn the point: Uses the initial position with the integral of each component. Model answer: $$x(4) = -2 + \int_0^{4} x'(t)\,dt$$

To earn the point: Gives both coordinates. Model answer: $$(4.693, -3.590)$$

**(d)** Find the total distance traveled by the particle over $$0 \leq t \leq 4$$.

To earn the point: Integrates the speed. Model answer: $$\int_0^{4} \sqrt{x'(t)^2 + y'(t)^2}\,dt$$

To earn the point: Gives the distance. Model answer: $$10.928$$
