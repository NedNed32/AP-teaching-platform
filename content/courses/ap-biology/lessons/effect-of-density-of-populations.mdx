Populations do not grow forever.

| Density-dependent | Density-independent |
| --- | --- |
| competition for food, water, space | weather events |
| predation | natural disasters |
| disease and parasites | human disturbance like pollution |
| accumulation of wastes | seasonal changes |

Density-dependent factors act like **negative feedback**, keeping populations near carrying capacity. Predator and prey populations can oscillate together: more prey supports more predators, which then reduce prey.

<Note title="Graph clues">
If the death rate rises as density rises, the factor is density-dependent. If the same fraction dies at any density, it is density-independent.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classify">
In a crowded pond, tadpoles grow slowly and many starve; in a sparse pond, most survive. What kind of factor is food here?

1. Its effect depends on how many tadpoles are present.

**Answer:** density-dependent.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Why do invasive species often spread rapidly in new habitats?

**A.** They lack their natural predators, competitors and diseases. Without natural controls, they grow quickly.

**Q.** A relationship in which one species benefits and the other is unaffected is called:

**A.** Commensalism. One benefits, the other is neither helped nor harmed.

**Q.** Fertilizer runoff enters a lake, causing an algal bloom followed by fish deaths. What is the direct cause of the fish deaths?

**A.** Decomposers use up dissolved oxygen as the algae die. Eutrophication depletes oxygen.

**Q.** Which factor limits population growth in a density-dependent way?

**A.** Spread of a contagious disease. Disease spreads faster in crowded populations.

**Q.** Grassland A has 25 plant species; grassland B has 4. A drought kills drought-sensitive species. Which prediction is best supported?

**A.** Grassland A will recover productivity faster because some species likely tolerate drought. Biodiversity increases resilience.

**Q.** Why does energy, unlike matter, not cycle in ecosystems?

**A.** At each transfer, energy is lost as heat, which organisms cannot use. Energy flows one way.

**Q.** Pill bugs move faster and turn less in dry areas and slow down in moist areas, ending up mostly in moist areas. This behavior is:

**A.** Kinesis. A change in speed of random movement.

**Q.** Sea otters eat sea urchins, which eat kelp. When otters are removed, kelp forests disappear. Sea otters are best described as:

**A.** A keystone species. Their effect on the community is far larger than their numbers.

### Free-response practice: Population growth

Researchers grew a population of yeast cells in a container with a fixed supply of resources and counted the population every two days.

*Population size (yeast cells (thousands per mL))*

| Day | Population size |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 20 |
| 2 | 83 |
| 4 | 225 |
| 6 | 346 |
| 8 | 388 |
| 10 | 397 |
| 12 | 399 |
| 14 | 400 |

**(a)** Describe how you would construct an appropriate graph of the data: the type of graph, the axes and their labels, and the scale.

To earn the point: Line or scatter graph with time on the x-axis. Model answer: Day on the x-axis, population size on the y-axis.

To earn the point: Axes labeled with units and linear scales; points plotted accurately and connected. Model answer: y-axis from 0 to at least 400.

**(b)** Calculate the average rate of population growth between day 4 and day 8.

To earn the point: Correct calculation with units. Model answer: (388 − 225) ÷ 4 = 40.75 individuals per day

**(c)** Identify the type of population growth shown and estimate the carrying capacity.

To earn the point: Logistic growth. Model answer: An S-shaped curve that levels off.

To earn the point: Carrying capacity estimate consistent with the data. Model answer: About 400.

**(d)** Explain why the growth rate decreases as the population approaches the carrying capacity.

To earn the point: Identifies a density-dependent factor. Model answer: Competition for limited food and space, or buildup of wastes.

To earn the point: Links it to lower birth rates or higher death rates. Model answer: As density rises, fewer resources per individual reduce reproduction and survival.

**(e)** The researchers repeat the experiment with double the food supply. Predict the effect on the carrying capacity and justify your prediction.

To earn the point: Predicts a higher carrying capacity. Model answer: Greater than about 400.

To earn the point: Justifies: food was a limiting resource. Model answer: More resources allow the environment to support more individuals.
