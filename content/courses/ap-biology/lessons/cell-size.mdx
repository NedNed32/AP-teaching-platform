Cells exchange materials (nutrients, wastes, heat) across their surface, but their needs depend on their volume.

For a cube of side $$s$$: $$SA = 6s^2$$ and $$V = s^3$$, so $$SA:V = \frac{6}{s}$$.

| Side | SA | V | SA:V |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 6 | 1 | 6 |
| 2 | 24 | 8 | 3 |
| 4 | 96 | 64 | 1.5 |

As a cell grows, volume increases faster than surface area, so the SA:V ratio falls and exchange becomes less efficient.

## Adaptations

- Small size, or long, thin, flat shapes.
- Membrane folds such as microvilli on intestinal cells and root hairs on plant roots.
- In large organisms, specialized exchange organs (lungs, gills) with huge surface areas.

The exam formula sheet gives formulas for spheres, cubes and cylinders.

<Note title="Organisms too">
Small animals lose heat faster than large ones because of their higher SA:V ratio, which is why small mammals have high metabolic rates.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Compare two cells">
Cell A is a cube 3 µm on a side; cell B is 6 µm. Which exchanges materials more efficiently?

1. A: 54 ÷ 27 = 2.
2. B: 216 ÷ 216 = 1.

**Answer:** cell A, with the larger SA:V ratio.
</WorkedExample>

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Two cube-shaped cells have side lengths of 4 µm and 9 µm. Which statement correctly compares their surface area-to-volume ratios?

**A.** The 4 µm cell has a ratio of 1.5, which is greater than the 9 µm cell's 0.67, so it exchanges materials more efficiently. For a cube, SA:V = 6s²/s³ = 6/s. SA:V = 6/s: 1.5 for 4 µm and 0.67 for 9 µm. Smaller cells have more surface area per unit volume.
