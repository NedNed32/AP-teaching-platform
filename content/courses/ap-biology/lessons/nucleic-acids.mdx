Nucleic acids store and transmit hereditary information.

## Structure

Each nucleotide has a sugar, a phosphate and a base. The sugar of one nucleotide bonds to the phosphate of the next, forming a **sugar-phosphate backbone** with a 5′ end and a 3′ end.

| | DNA | RNA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| sugar | deoxyribose | ribose |
| bases | A, T, G, C | A, U, G, C |
| strands | usually two, antiparallel | usually one |

Purines (A, G) have two rings; pyrimidines (C, T, U) have one. Each base pair joins a purine and a pyrimidine, so the helix has a uniform width. G–C pairs form three hydrogen bonds; A–T pairs form two.

## Chargaff's rule

In double-stranded DNA, %A = %T and %G = %C.

<Note title="Writing the complement">
The complementary strand is antiparallel. If one strand reads 5′-ATGC-3′, its partner reads 3′-TACG-5′, which is written 5′-GCAT-3′.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Base composition">
A double-stranded DNA molecule is 22 percent cytosine. What percentage is thymine?

1. G = C = 22 percent, totaling 44.
2. The remaining 56 percent is split equally between A and T.

**Answer:** 28 percent.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A sample of double-stranded DNA is 36% guanine. What percentage of the bases are adenine?

**Answer:** 14%

**Why:** cytosine also makes up 36%; the remaining 28% is split equally between the other two bases.

**Working:** By base pairing, guanine = cytosine = 36%. The other two bases share 28%, so each is 14%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 36%: 36% is the amount of cytosine, which pairs with guanine.
- 64%: Both members of each base pair must be accounted for.
- 28%: That is the total for both remaining bases together.
