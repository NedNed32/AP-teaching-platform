Evolution is a change in **allele frequencies** in a population's **gene pool**.

| Mechanism | Random? | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mutation | yes | source of new alleles |
| genetic drift | yes | random changes; can lose alleles, especially in small populations |
| gene flow | no | makes populations more similar |
| natural selection | no | increases alleles that improve fitness |
| nonrandom mating | no | changes genotype frequencies |

**Bottlenecks** (for example, after a natural disaster or overhunting) and **founder events** reduce genetic diversity, which can leave populations less able to adapt.

<Note title="Diversity matters">
Populations with low genetic diversity are more vulnerable to disease and environmental change.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Identify the mechanism">
A storm randomly kills 90 percent of a beetle population, and an allele that was rare becomes common by chance. What mechanism is this?

1. The survivors were not chosen by their traits.

**Answer:** genetic drift (a bottleneck).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** An antibiotic is used to treat an infection, and resistant bacteria become common. Which statement is accurate?

**A.** Resistant bacteria were present before treatment and survived to reproduce. The antibiotic selected existing variants.

**Q.** Which is the best measure of an organism's evolutionary fitness?

**A.** The number of its offspring that survive to reproduce. Fitness is reproductive success.

**Q.** Breeders produced broccoli, kale and cabbage from wild mustard. How does this differ from natural selection?

**A.** Humans chose which plants reproduced. That is artificial selection.

**Q.** In a population, individuals of intermediate size have the highest survival, and the range of sizes narrows over generations. What type of selection is this?

**A.** Stabilizing selection. The intermediate phenotype is favored.

**Q.** Which condition would cause allele frequencies to change, violating Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium?

**A.** Individuals with one allele produce more offspring than others. That is natural selection.

**Q.** A small group of birds colonizes an island, and an allele rare on the mainland becomes common there. What explains this?

**A.** The founder effect. A few founders carried, by chance, a different set of alleles.

### Free-response practice: Allele frequencies in a population

In a population of 500 beetles, dark fur (allele B) is dominant to light fur (allele b). Researchers count 80 beetles with light fur. Assume the population is in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (p + q = 1; p² + 2pq + q² = 1).

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the b allele and the expected number of heterozygous beetles.

To earn the point: q from the recessive phenotype frequency. Model answer: q² = 80/500 = 0.16, so q = 0.4.

To earn the point: Expected heterozygotes. Model answer: 2pq × 500 = 2(0.6)(0.4)(500) = 240.

**(b)** Identify two conditions that must be met for the population to remain in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium.

To earn the point: Two correct conditions. Model answer: A large population, random mating, no mutation, no migration, no natural selection.

**(c)** Birds that prey on the beetles find beetles with dark fur more easily on the new background. Predict how the frequency of the b allele will change over several generations, and justify your prediction.

To earn the point: Predicts q increases. Model answer: Beetles with light fur (bb) survive and reproduce more.

To earn the point: Justifies with differential survival and reproduction. Model answer: Selection removes B alleles faster, because both BB and Bb beetles are eaten more.
