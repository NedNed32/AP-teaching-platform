## A G protein pathway

1. Epinephrine binds a G protein-coupled receptor.
2. The receptor activates a G protein, which activates the enzyme adenylyl cyclase.
3. Adenylyl cyclase converts ATP into many molecules of **cAMP** (second messenger).
4. cAMP activates **protein kinase A**, which starts a **phosphorylation cascade**.
5. Response: liver cells break down glycogen into glucose.

## Amplification

Because each enzyme acts many times, one hormone molecule can lead to millions of glucose molecules released.

## Turning it off

Ligands leave receptors, phosphatases remove phosphates and enzymes break down cAMP, so the cell can respond to new signals.

<Note title="Same signal, different responses">
Different cells can respond differently to the same ligand because they have different receptors or different proteins downstream.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Blocking cAMP breakdown">
A drug inhibits the enzyme that breaks down cAMP. Predict the effect on a liver cell exposed to epinephrine.

1. cAMP stays high after the signal.
2. Protein kinase A remains active.

**Answer:** glycogen breakdown continues longer than normal.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Why can cells divide uncontrollably when a gene for a growth-factor receptor is mutated so the receptor is always active?

**A.** The cell behaves as if it is always receiving signals to divide. A constitutively active receptor drives division without the signal.

**Q.** A steroid hormone affects target cells but not other cells. Which best explains this?

**A.** Only target cells contain the specific intracellular receptor. Responses require the right receptor.

**Q.** Bacteria release a signaling molecule and begin forming a biofilm only when the population is dense. What is this called?

**A.** Quorum sensing. Signals accumulate to a threshold at high density.

**Q.** Which is an example of positive feedback?

**A.** Oxytocin increases contractions during childbirth, which causes more oxytocin release. The response amplifies the stimulus.

**Q.** Blood glucose rises after a meal, insulin is released and glucose falls. What does this illustrate?

**A.** Negative feedback. The response reverses the change.

**Q.** How does a phosphorylation cascade amplify a signal?

**A.** Each activated kinase activates many molecules at the next step. Each step multiplies the number of active molecules.

**Q.** A mutation causes a G protein to remain permanently active. What is the most likely effect?

**A.** The response continues even without the signal. Downstream steps stay switched on.

**Q.** A cell with damaged DNA is stopped at the G1 checkpoint. Which protein is most directly responsible?

**A.** A tumor suppressor such as p53. p53 halts the cycle or triggers apoptosis when DNA is damaged.

### Free-response practice: A signaling pathway

In liver cells, the hormone epinephrine binds a G protein-coupled receptor. The activated G protein activates adenylyl cyclase, which converts ATP to cyclic AMP (cAMP). cAMP activates protein kinase A, which starts a phosphorylation cascade that leads to the breakdown of glycogen into glucose. An enzyme in the cell breaks down cAMP.

**(a)** Describe the role of cAMP in this pathway.

To earn the point: cAMP is a second messenger that relays and amplifies the signal inside the cell. Model answer: It activates protein kinase A.

**(b)** Explain how a single epinephrine molecule can cause the release of a large number of glucose molecules.

To earn the point: Amplification: each activated enzyme activates many molecules at the next step. Model answer: One adenylyl cyclase makes many cAMP; each kinase phosphorylates many targets.

**(c)** A mutation alters the enzyme that breaks down cAMP, so that it is inactive. Predict the effect on glycogen breakdown, and justify your prediction.

To earn the point: Correct prediction. Model answer: Glycogen breakdown continues longer than normal after the signal ends.

To earn the point: Justification using the pathway. Model answer: CAMP remains high, keeping protein kinase A active.
