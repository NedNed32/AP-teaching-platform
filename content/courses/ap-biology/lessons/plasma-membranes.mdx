The plasma membrane separates the cell from its environment and controls what enters and leaves.

## Components

- **Phospholipid bilayer**: hydrophilic heads face the water inside and outside; hydrophobic tails face each other.
- **Integral (transmembrane) proteins**: their hydrophobic regions sit in the bilayer and hydrophilic regions face water. They act as channels, carriers, pumps and receptors.
- **Peripheral proteins**: loosely attached to the surface.
- **Cholesterol** (animals): moderates fluidity.
- **Glycoproteins and glycolipids**: carbohydrate chains on the outer surface used for cell recognition.

Molecules move laterally within the membrane: it is fluid, not rigid.

## Cell walls

Plants (cellulose), fungi (chitin) and many prokaryotes (peptidoglycan) also have a cell wall outside the membrane, providing structure and protection against bursting.

<Note title="Amphipathic proteins">
A transmembrane protein's surface pattern matches the membrane: nonpolar amino acids where it crosses the lipid tails, polar ones where it meets water.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Predict a protein's position">
A protein has a stretch of 20 nonpolar amino acids flanked by polar regions. Where does it sit?

1. The nonpolar stretch is stable among the lipid tails.
2. The polar ends face the water on each side.

**Answer:** it spans the membrane as an integral protein.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A drug blocks the sodium–potassium pump in intestinal cells. Why does glucose uptake by sodium–glucose cotransport decrease?

**A.** The sodium gradient that powers the cotransporter runs down. Cotransport relies on the gradient built by the pump.

**Q.** The rate of glucose uptake into a cell increases with glucose concentration but levels off at high concentrations, and no ATP is used. What explains this?

**A.** Facilitated diffusion through a limited number of carrier proteins. Carriers become saturated.

**Q.** Which molecule can diffuse directly across a phospholipid bilayer most easily?

**A.** O₂. Small nonpolar molecules pass through the hydrophobic core.

**Q.** What is the main advantage of membrane-bound compartments in eukaryotic cells?

**A.** Different chemical reactions can occur at the same time under different conditions. Compartments separate incompatible processes.

**Q.** Which part of a transmembrane protein is most likely to be made of nonpolar amino acids?

**A.** The region that spans the hydrophobic interior of the membrane. Nonpolar R groups interact with lipid tails.

**Q.** Which observation supports the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria?

**A.** Mitochondria have circular DNA and ribosomes similar to those of bacteria. These are prokaryote-like features.

**Q.** A cell secretes large amounts of a digestive enzyme. Which organelles would be most abundant?

**A.** Rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi complex. Secreted proteins are made on the rough ER and processed by the Golgi.
