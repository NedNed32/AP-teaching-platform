Organisms constantly exchange matter with their environment to obtain the atoms they need to grow, reproduce and maintain organization.

## Where the elements go

| Element | Used to build |
| --- | --- |
| carbon | the backbone of all organic molecules |
| hydrogen, oxygen | all macromolecules |
| nitrogen | proteins (amino acids) and nucleic acids (bases) |
| phosphorus | nucleic acids (phosphate groups), phospholipids, ATP |
| sulfur | some amino acids (such as cysteine), allowing disulfide bridges |

Carbon can form four covalent bonds, so it builds long chains, branches and rings: the skeletons of biological molecules.

## Matter cycles

Plants take in carbon dioxide; nitrogen and phosphorus come from the soil, often recycled by decomposers. Animals obtain these elements by eating.

<Note title="Tracing atoms">
Radioactive or heavy isotopes of elements like phosphorus and sulfur let scientists track which molecules contain them. That is how experiments showed DNA, which has phosphorus, is the genetic material.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Labeling experiment">
Bacteria are grown with radioactive phosphorus. Which molecules will become labeled?

1. Phosphorus is in phosphate groups.
2. Phosphate groups occur in DNA, RNA, ATP and phospholipids.

**Answer:** nucleic acids, ATP and phospholipids, but not most proteins or carbohydrates.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Cellulose and starch are both polymers of glucose, yet humans digest starch but not cellulose. Which best explains this?

**A.** The glucose monomers are linked differently, so human enzymes do not fit cellulose's bonds. Enzyme specificity depends on bond and molecular shape.

**Q.** A researcher supplies cells with radioactive nitrogen. Which molecules will become labeled?

**A.** Proteins and nucleic acids. Nitrogen is in amino groups and nitrogenous bases.

**Q.** Which feature allows carbon to form the backbone of diverse biological molecules?

**A.** It can form four covalent bonds, building chains, branches and rings. Carbon's bonding versatility allows many structures.

**Q.** Which change would most likely disrupt the tertiary structure of a protein?

**A.** A change in pH that alters the charges on R groups. Tertiary structure depends on R-group interactions, including ionic bonds.

**Q.** Why are phospholipids able to form bilayers in water?

**A.** They have hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails. Tails cluster away from water and heads face it.

**Q.** Which property of water results most directly from hydrogen bonding between water molecules?

**A.** High specific heat. Many hydrogen bonds must be broken to raise water's temperature.
