Most biological macromolecules are **polymers**: chains of similar **monomers**.

| Polymer | Monomer | Bond |
| --- | --- | --- |
| polysaccharide | monosaccharide | glycosidic linkage |
| protein | amino acid | peptide bond |
| nucleic acid | nucleotide | phosphodiester bond |

Lipids are not true polymers, but fats are also built by dehydration synthesis (glycerol plus fatty acids).

## Building and breaking

- **Dehydration synthesis** joins monomers and releases water. It requires energy.
- **Hydrolysis** adds water to break the bond and releases energy.

Enzymes speed up both reactions.

<Note title="Counting waters">
Joining n monomers in a straight chain forms n − 1 bonds and releases n − 1 water molecules.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A polysaccharide">
A starch chain of 500 glucose units is completely hydrolyzed. How many water molecules are used?

1. The chain has 499 glycosidic bonds.
2. Each bond needs one water to break.

**Answer:** 499 water molecules.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

47 nucleotides are joined into one linear nucleic acid strand by dehydration synthesis. How many water molecules are released?

**Answer:** 46

**Why:** A chain of 47 monomers has 46 bonds, and each bond involves one water molecule.

**Working:** Linking 47 monomers forms 46 bonds; each releases one water molecule.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 47: There is one fewer bond than monomers in a linear chain.
- 48: There is one fewer bond than monomers.
- 92: Each bond involves one water molecule, not two.

### Worked exam question 2

A linear polypeptide made of 274 amino acids is completely hydrolyzed. How many water molecules are consumed?

**Answer:** 273

**Why:** A chain of 274 monomers has 273 bonds, and each bond involves one water molecule.

**Working:** Linking 274 monomers forms 273 bonds; each is broken using one water molecule.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 274: There is one fewer bond than monomers in a linear chain.
- 275: There is one fewer bond than monomers.
- 546: Each bond involves one water molecule, not two.
