Before a cell divides, its DNA is copied so each daughter cell receives a complete set.

## Enzymes

| Enzyme | Role |
| --- | --- |
| helicase | unwinds the helix |
| topoisomerase | relieves twisting ahead of the fork |
| primase | lays down an RNA primer |
| DNA polymerase | adds nucleotides 5′ to 3′; proofreads |
| ligase | joins Okazaki fragments |

## Leading and lagging strands

The strands are antiparallel and polymerase works only 5′ to 3′. The **leading strand** is built continuously toward the fork; the **lagging strand** is built in **Okazaki fragments** away from it.

<Note title="Semiconservative evidence">
Meselson and Stahl grew bacteria in heavy nitrogen, then switched to light nitrogen. After one generation all DNA was intermediate in density: each molecule had one old and one new strand.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Density after two generations">
In a Meselson–Stahl experiment, what DNA densities appear after two generations in light nitrogen?

1. Generation 1: all hybrid (heavy/light).
2. Generation 2: each hybrid gives one hybrid and one light molecule.

**Answer:** half hybrid, half light.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

One strand of DNA reads 5′-CAAATTAA-3′. What is the sequence of the complementary strand, written 5′ to 3′?

**Answer:** 5′-TTAATTTG-3′

**Why:** Pair each base and reverse the direction, because strands are antiparallel.

**Working:** The complement of 5′-CAAATTAA-3′ is 3′-GTTTAATT-5′, written 5′-TTAATTTG-3′.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 5′-GTTTAATT-3′: The complementary strand is antiparallel; written 5′ to 3′ it is reversed.
- 5′-UUAAUUUG-3′: DNA contains T, not U.
- 5′-AATTAAAC-3′: Each base must be paired, not just reversed.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Bacteria take up a plasmid carrying an antibiotic-resistance gene from their surroundings. What is this process?

**A.** Transformation. Uptake of DNA from the environment.

**Q.** Methylation of DNA in a gene's promoter usually has which effect?

**A.** It reduces transcription of the gene. Methylation tightens chromatin.

**Q.** Which modification of eukaryotic pre-mRNA allows one gene to code for more than one protein?

**A.** Alternative splicing. Different exon combinations make different mRNAs.

**Q.** A liver cell and a nerve cell from the same person have the same DNA but make different proteins. Why?

**A.** They express different genes because they have different transcription factors. Differential gene expression.

**Q.** A single nucleotide is deleted near the start of a gene. Why is the effect usually severe?

**A.** It shifts the reading frame, changing every codon after it. Frameshifts alter all downstream amino acids.

**Q.** In the lac operon, what happens when lactose is present and glucose is absent?

**A.** Lactose binds the repressor, which releases the operator, and the genes are transcribed. The lac operon is inducible.

**Q.** Why is the lagging strand synthesized as Okazaki fragments?

**A.** DNA polymerase adds nucleotides only to the 3′ end, and the strands are antiparallel. The lagging strand must be built away from the fork.
