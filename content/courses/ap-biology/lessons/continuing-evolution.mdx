Evolution is ongoing and can be observed.

- **Antibiotic resistance**: antibiotics kill susceptible bacteria; resistant ones survive and multiply.
- **Pesticide and herbicide resistance** in insects and weeds.
- **Viral evolution**: high mutation rates let viruses like influenza and HIV change rapidly.
- **Changing environments** drive change in populations, as with Darwin's finches during droughts.

The fossil record and genomic data show that evolution has occurred over long periods and continues.

<Note title="Mutation first">
In each case, the resistant variants existed (or arose randomly) before selection. The antibiotic or pesticide only changes which survive.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Resistance">
Why does stopping an antibiotic course early promote resistance?

1. The most susceptible bacteria die first.
2. Partially resistant survivors are left to multiply.

**Answer:** it selects for bacteria with more resistance.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** An antibiotic is used to treat an infection, and resistant bacteria become common. Which statement is accurate?

**A.** Resistant bacteria were present before treatment and survived to reproduce. The antibiotic selected existing variants.

**Q.** Which is the best measure of an organism's evolutionary fitness?

**A.** The number of its offspring that survive to reproduce. Fitness is reproductive success.

**Q.** Breeders produced broccoli, kale and cabbage from wild mustard. How does this differ from natural selection?

**A.** Humans chose which plants reproduced. That is artificial selection.

**Q.** In a population, individuals of intermediate size have the highest survival, and the range of sizes narrows over generations. What type of selection is this?

**A.** Stabilizing selection. The intermediate phenotype is favored.

**Q.** Which condition would cause allele frequencies to change, violating Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium?

**A.** Individuals with one allele produce more offspring than others. That is natural selection.

**Q.** A small group of birds colonizes an island, and an allele rare on the mainland becomes common there. What explains this?

**A.** The founder effect. A few founders carried, by chance, a different set of alleles.
