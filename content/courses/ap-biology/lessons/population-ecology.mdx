## Exponential growth

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = r_{max}N$$

With plenty of resources, the population grows faster the larger it gets: a J-shaped curve.

## Logistic growth

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = r_{max}N\left(\frac{K - N}{K}\right)$$

As N approaches the carrying capacity K, growth slows: an S-shaped curve. Growth is fastest at $$N = K/2$$.

## Population size from rates

Population change = births − deaths (+ immigration − emigration). The per capita growth rate $$r$$ is the birth rate minus the death rate per individual.

<Note title="Reading the model">
When N is small, (K − N)/K is near 1 and growth is nearly exponential. When N equals K, the factor is 0 and growth stops.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Logistic calculation">
A population has rmax = 0.4 per year, N = 300 and K = 1,200. What is dN/dt?

1. (K − N)/K = 900/1200 = 0.75.
2. dN/dt = 0.4 × 300 × 0.75 = 90.

**Answer:** 90 individuals per year.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A population has a maximum per capita growth rate of 0.3 per year, a carrying capacity of 500 and a current size of 200. Using dN/dt = rmaxN(K − N)/K, what is the population growth rate?

**Answer:** 36 individuals per year

**Why:** Substitute into the equation.

**Working:** 0.3 × 200 × (500 − 200)/500 = 36.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 60 individuals per year: Include the (K − N)/K factor for logistic growth.
- 24 individuals per year: The factor is (K − N)/K, not N/K.
- 150 individuals per year: Use the current size N.

### Worked exam question 2

A population of 160 has unlimited resources and a per capita growth rate of 0.3 per year. Using dN/dt = rmaxN, what is the population growth rate?

**Answer:** 48 individuals per year

**Why:** Substitute into the equation.

**Working:** 0.3 × 160 = 48.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 9.6 individuals per year: The factor is (K − N)/K, not N/K.
- 240 individuals per year: Use the current size N.
- 24 individuals per year: Recheck the multiplication.

### Free-response practice: Population growth

Researchers grew a population of yeast cells in a container with a fixed supply of resources and counted the population every two days.

*Population size (yeast cells (thousands per mL))*

| Day | Population size |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 20 |
| 2 | 83 |
| 4 | 225 |
| 6 | 346 |
| 8 | 388 |
| 10 | 397 |
| 12 | 399 |
| 14 | 400 |

**(a)** Describe how you would construct an appropriate graph of the data: the type of graph, the axes and their labels, and the scale.

To earn the point: Line or scatter graph with time on the x-axis. Model answer: Day on the x-axis, population size on the y-axis.

To earn the point: Axes labeled with units and linear scales; points plotted accurately and connected. Model answer: y-axis from 0 to at least 400.

**(b)** Calculate the average rate of population growth between day 4 and day 8.

To earn the point: Correct calculation with units. Model answer: (388 − 225) ÷ 4 = 40.75 individuals per day

**(c)** Identify the type of population growth shown and estimate the carrying capacity.

To earn the point: Logistic growth. Model answer: An S-shaped curve that levels off.

To earn the point: Carrying capacity estimate consistent with the data. Model answer: About 400.

**(d)** Explain why the growth rate decreases as the population approaches the carrying capacity.

To earn the point: Identifies a density-dependent factor. Model answer: Competition for limited food and space, or buildup of wastes.

To earn the point: Links it to lower birth rates or higher death rates. Model answer: As density rises, fewer resources per individual reduce reproduction and survival.

**(e)** The researchers repeat the experiment with double the food supply. Predict the effect on the carrying capacity and justify your prediction.

To earn the point: Predicts a higher carrying capacity. Model answer: Greater than about 400.

To earn the point: Justifies: food was a limiting resource. Model answer: More resources allow the environment to support more individuals.
