Each enzyme has optimal conditions.

- **Temperature**: rate rises with temperature (more collisions) up to an optimum, then falls as the enzyme denatures.
- **pH**: changes in H⁺ concentration alter charges on R groups, disrupting shape. Pepsin works best near pH 2; most cytoplasmic enzymes near pH 7.
- **Substrate concentration**: rate increases, then levels off when all active sites are occupied (saturation).
- **Enzyme concentration**: more enzyme, faster rate (if substrate is plentiful).

## Inhibitors

| Type | Binds | Overcome by more substrate? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| competitive | active site | yes |
| noncompetitive | allosteric site | no |

Low temperature slows enzymes without denaturing them; warming restores activity.

<Note title="Reversible or not">
Cooling is reversible; denaturation by high heat usually is not.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Identify the inhibitor">
An inhibitor reduces reaction rate at low substrate levels, but at very high substrate levels the rate reaches the same maximum as without inhibitor. What type is it?

1. Excess substrate outcompetes it.

**Answer:** a competitive inhibitor.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The activity of an enzyme was measured at different temperatures.

*Enzyme activity*

| Temperature (°C) | Rate (µmol/min) |
| --- | --- |
| 20 | 16 |
| 30 | 32 |
| 40 | 50 |
| 50 | 63 |
| 60 | 28 |
| 70 | 3 |

Which best explains the change in rate between 50 °C and 70 °C?

**Answer:** High temperature disrupted the enzyme's shape, so fewer active sites could bind substrate

**Why:** Above the optimum, the enzyme denatures.

**Working:** The rate peaks at 50 °C and falls sharply above it, consistent with denaturation of the enzyme.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Molecules moved more slowly, so there were fewer collisions: Molecules move faster at higher temperatures.
- The substrate was used up at high temperatures: The drop reflects the enzyme, not the supply of substrate.
- The enzyme's activation energy increased because more substrate was added: Activation energy is not changed by adding substrate.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** What is the source of the O₂ released by photosynthesis?

**A.** Water split by photosystem II. Electrons from water replace those lost by photosystem II.

**Q.** Cyanide blocks the final protein of the electron transport chain in mitochondria. Which is the most immediate effect?

**A.** The proton gradient is no longer maintained, so ATP synthase makes much less ATP. Electrons cannot pass to oxygen, so pumping stops.

**Q.** An inhibitor reduces an enzyme's rate, but adding a large amount of substrate restores the maximum rate. What kind of inhibitor is it?

**A.** Competitive. Excess substrate outcompetes an inhibitor for the active site.

**Q.** Organisms in cold environments often have membranes with more unsaturated fatty acids. How does this increase fitness?

**A.** Kinks in unsaturated tails keep membranes fluid at low temperatures. Fluid membranes keep transport working.

**Q.** A molecule makes the inner mitochondrial membrane leaky to protons. What is the effect?

**A.** Energy from electron transport is released as heat instead of making ATP. The gradient dissipates without passing through ATP synthase.

**Q.** How does an enzyme speed up a reaction?

**A.** It lowers the activation energy. Enzymes lower the energy barrier without changing ΔG.

**Q.** Why can yeast continue to make some ATP without oxygen?

**A.** Fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue. Glycolysis needs NAD⁺ to keep running.

**Q.** Where in the chloroplast is ATP made during photosynthesis?

**A.** At ATP synthase in the thylakoid membrane, as H⁺ flows out of the thylakoid space. Chemiosmosis in the light reactions makes ATP.

### Free-response practice: Enzyme activity and pH

A student measured the activity of the enzyme amylase, which breaks down starch and produces maltose. Equal amounts of enzyme and substrate were mixed in buffers at different pH values at 25 °C, and the initial rate of product formation was recorded.

*Initial reaction rate at each pH*

| pH | Rate (µmol/min) |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | 4 |
| 4 | 23 |
| 6 | 46 |
| 8 | 24 |
| 10 | 2 |

**(a)** Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable in the experiment, and identify one variable that should be held constant.

To earn the point: Independent variable: pH. Model answer: The pH of the buffer.

To earn the point: Dependent variable: initial reaction rate. Model answer: Rate of maltose production.

To earn the point: A valid controlled variable. Model answer: Temperature, enzyme concentration, substrate concentration or volume.

**(b)** Calculate the percent change in reaction rate when the pH changes from 6 to 4.

To earn the point: Correct calculation. Model answer: (23 − 46) ÷ 46 × 100 = -50%

**(c)** Explain why the reaction rate is low at pH 2 and pH 10.

To earn the point: Extreme pH changes the charges on amino acid R groups. Model answer: H⁺ concentration alters ionic and hydrogen bonds that hold the enzyme's shape.

To earn the point: The active site's shape changes (denaturation), so substrate binds poorly. Model answer: Fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form, lowering the rate.

**(d)** The student repeats the experiment at pH 6 with twice as much enzyme and the same amount of substrate. Predict the effect on the initial rate, and justify your prediction.

To earn the point: Predicts the initial rate increases. Model answer: Higher than 46 µmol/min, up to about double.

To earn the point: Justifies with more active sites available when substrate is plentiful. Model answer: More enzyme molecules can bind substrate at the same time.

**(e)** A competitive inhibitor is added to the reaction at the optimal pH. Describe how increasing the substrate concentration would affect the inhibition, and explain why.

To earn the point: Inhibition decreases at high substrate concentration. Model answer: The rate approaches the uninhibited maximum.

To earn the point: Explains competition for the active site. Model answer: Substrate molecules outcompete the inhibitor for the active site.
