## Recombination frequency

$$\text{recombination frequency} = \frac{\text{recombinant offspring}}{\text{total offspring}} \times 100$$

<Note title="Spotting linkage">
If a dihybrid test cross gives far more parental types than the expected 1 : 1 : 1 : 1, the genes are probably linked.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="X-linked cross">
A carrier woman (XᴴXʰ) and an unaffected man (XᴴY) have a son. What is the chance he has the X-linked recessive trait?

1. Sons get Y from the father and one X from the mother.
2. Half of the mother's eggs carry Xʰ.

**Answer:** 1/2.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A recessive trait is X-linked. An affected woman and an unaffected man have a son. What is the probability that the son is affected?

**A.** 1. Sons receive their X from their mother only. A son's X comes from his affected mother, so the probability is 1.

**Q.** A recessive trait is X-linked. A carrier woman and an unaffected man have a daughter. What is the probability that the daughter is affected?

**A.** 0. Daughters need a recessive X from each parent. A daughter gets the father's only X (dominant) and one of the mother's: probability 0.

**Q.** A recessive trait is X-linked. An affected woman and an unaffected man have a daughter. What is the probability that the daughter is affected?

**A.** 0. Daughters need a recessive X from each parent. A daughter gets the father's only X (dominant) and one of the mother's: probability 0.

**Q.** A recessive trait is X-linked. A carrier woman and an affected man have a son. What is the probability that the son is affected?

**A.** 1/2. Sons receive their X from their mother only. A son's X comes from his carrier mother, so the probability is 1/2.

**Q.** A recessive trait is X-linked. A carrier woman and an unaffected man have a son. What is the probability that the son is affected?

**A.** 1/2. Sons receive their X from their mother only. A son's X comes from his carrier mother, so the probability is 1/2.

**Q.** A recessive trait is X-linked. An affected woman and an affected man have a daughter. What is the probability that the daughter is affected?

**A.** 1. Daughters need a recessive X from each parent. A daughter gets the father's only X (recessive) and one of the mother's: probability 1.

**Q.** A recessive trait is X-linked. An affected woman and an affected man have a son. What is the probability that the son is affected?

**A.** 1. Sons receive their X from their mother only. A son's X comes from his affected mother, so the probability is 1.

**Q.** A recessive trait is X-linked. A carrier woman and an affected man have a daughter. What is the probability that the daughter is affected?

**A.** 1/2. Daughters need a recessive X from each parent. A daughter gets the father's only X (recessive) and one of the mother's: probability 1/2.

**Q.** Red and white snapdragons produce pink offspring. What pattern of inheritance is this?

**A.** Incomplete dominance. The heterozygote is intermediate.

**Q.** Hydrangeas with identical genotypes produce blue flowers in acidic soil and pink flowers in basic soil. What does this show?

**A.** The environment can affect the phenotype. Same genotype, different phenotypes.

**Q.** A dihybrid test cross produces 45% AB, 45% ab, 5% Ab and 5% aB offspring. What do these results suggest?

**A.** The genes are linked, about 10 map units apart. Recombinants are 10% of offspring.

**Q.** In which stage do homologous chromosomes separate?

**A.** Anaphase I of meiosis. Meiosis I separates homologs.

**Q.** Which trait is most likely inherited only through the mother?

**A.** A trait caused by a mitochondrial gene. Mitochondria are inherited from the egg.

**Q.** Nondisjunction of chromosome 21 in meiosis I produces gametes with which chromosome numbers (n = 23)?

**A.** Two with 24 and two with 22. One cell gets both homologs and the other none.

### Free-response practice: A dihybrid cross

Two pea plants heterozygous for height (T, tall, dominant) and flower color (P, purple, dominant) were crossed, producing 320 offspring.

*Offspring phenotypes*

| Phenotype | Observed |
| --- | --- |
| tall, purple | 222 |
| tall, white | 27 |
| short, purple | 27 |
| short, white | 44 |

**(a)** State the null hypothesis, and calculate the expected number of offspring in each category.

To earn the point: Null hypothesis: the genes assort independently, giving a 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio. Model answer: Any differences are due to chance.

To earn the point: Expected counts. Model answer: 180, 60, 60, 20

**(b)** Calculate the chi-square value and determine whether to reject the null hypothesis at p = 0.05 (critical value 7.81 for 3 degrees of freedom).

To earn the point: Correct chi-square value. Model answer: χ² ≈ 74.9

To earn the point: Correct conclusion with comparison to the critical value. Model answer: 74.9 is greater than 7.81, so reject the null hypothesis.

**(c)** Propose an explanation for the results.

To earn the point: Explanation consistent with the conclusion. Model answer: The genes are likely linked on the same chromosome, so parental combinations are inherited together more often.
