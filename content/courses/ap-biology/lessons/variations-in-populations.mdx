Populations with more **genetic variation** are more likely to include individuals that survive a new disease, climate shift or predator.

- In the Irish potato famine, widely planted, genetically similar potatoes all succumbed to the same pathogen.
- Cheetahs have very low genetic diversity after past bottlenecks, which raises concern about disease.
- Some alleles that are harmful in one environment are advantageous in another; heterozygotes for the sickle-cell allele are more resistant to malaria.

Conservation programs try to preserve genetic diversity, for example by moving individuals between isolated populations.

<Note title="Variation is not always visible">
Many alleles have no visible effect until conditions change.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Predict">
Two populations of a plant face a new fungus. Population A is genetically diverse; B is a set of clones. Which is more likely to persist?

1. A probably includes some resistant individuals.
2. B's plants share the same genotype and response.

**Answer:** population A.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Why are genetically uniform crops vulnerable to disease?

**A.** If one plant is susceptible, nearly all are, because they share the same alleles. Low genetic diversity reduces resilience.

**Q.** After a mass extinction, the surviving groups often diversify rapidly. Why?

**A.** Many ecological niches become available. Adaptive radiation fills vacant niches.

**Q.** Plants often form new species within a single area when errors in meiosis double their chromosome number. This is:

**A.** Sympatric speciation by polyploidy. Polyploids are reproductively isolated from the parent species.

**Q.** Two populations of frogs live in the same pond but breed in different months. What type of barrier is this?

**A.** Temporal isolation, a prezygotic barrier. They are separated by breeding time.

**Q.** The forelimbs of humans, bats and whales have the same bone pattern but different functions. These are:

**A.** Homologous structures, evidence of common ancestry. Shared structure from a common ancestor.

**Q.** On a phylogenetic tree, what does a node represent?

**A.** The most recent common ancestor of the lineages that branch from it. Nodes are branch points.

**Q.** Which feature is shared by all living organisms, supporting common ancestry?

**A.** A nearly universal genetic code. All organisms read codons the same way.

**Q.** Which observation best supports the RNA world hypothesis?

**A.** Some RNA molecules can catalyze reactions as well as store information. RNA can serve both roles.
