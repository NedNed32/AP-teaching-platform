Signaling has three stages.

1. **Reception**: the ligand binds a receptor, which changes shape.
2. **Transduction**: the change starts a cascade of molecular interactions inside the cell, often protein phosphorylations.
3. **Response**: the cell changes its activity, such as turning on genes, opening channels or activating enzymes.

## Receptor location

- **Membrane receptors** bind hydrophilic ligands (most peptides, neurotransmitters). Examples: G protein-coupled receptors, receptor tyrosine kinases, ligand-gated ion channels.
- **Intracellular receptors** bind hydrophobic ligands (steroid hormones) that cross the membrane; the hormone–receptor complex often acts as a transcription factor.

<Note title="Shape change is the message">
At every step the signal is passed along by a change in a protein's shape, often caused by binding or by adding a phosphate group.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Locate the receptor">
A hormone is a small, nonpolar steroid. Where is its receptor, and what is a likely response?

1. Nonpolar molecules cross the bilayer.
2. Steroid–receptor complexes often regulate transcription.

**Answer:** inside the cell; it likely changes gene expression.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Why can cells divide uncontrollably when a gene for a growth-factor receptor is mutated so the receptor is always active?

**A.** The cell behaves as if it is always receiving signals to divide. A constitutively active receptor drives division without the signal.

**Q.** A steroid hormone affects target cells but not other cells. Which best explains this?

**A.** Only target cells contain the specific intracellular receptor. Responses require the right receptor.

**Q.** Bacteria release a signaling molecule and begin forming a biofilm only when the population is dense. What is this called?

**A.** Quorum sensing. Signals accumulate to a threshold at high density.

**Q.** Which is an example of positive feedback?

**A.** Oxytocin increases contractions during childbirth, which causes more oxytocin release. The response amplifies the stimulus.

**Q.** Blood glucose rises after a meal, insulin is released and glucose falls. What does this illustrate?

**A.** Negative feedback. The response reverses the change.

**Q.** How does a phosphorylation cascade amplify a signal?

**A.** Each activated kinase activates many molecules at the next step. Each step multiplies the number of active molecules.

**Q.** A mutation causes a G protein to remain permanently active. What is the most likely effect?

**A.** The response continues even without the signal. Downstream steps stay switched on.

**Q.** A cell with damaged DNA is stopped at the G1 checkpoint. Which protein is most directly responsible?

**A.** A tumor suppressor such as p53. p53 halts the cycle or triggers apoptosis when DNA is damaged.
