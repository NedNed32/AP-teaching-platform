Sexual reproduction shuffles genes in three ways.

1. **Crossing over** (prophase I) creates new combinations of alleles on a chromosome.
2. **Independent assortment** (metaphase I): with $$n$$ pairs of chromosomes there are $$2^n$$ possible combinations. For humans, $$2^{23}$$, about 8 million.
3. **Random fertilization**: any sperm can fuse with any egg, multiplying the possibilities.

The resulting variation is the raw material for natural selection.

<Note title="Linked genes">
Genes close together on a chromosome tend to be inherited together. Crossing over between them is rarer the closer they are.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="How many gametes?">
An organism has 2n = 10. Ignoring crossing over, how many genetically different gametes can it produce?

1. n = 5 pairs.
2. 2⁵ = 32.

**Answer:** 32.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** An organism has a diploid number of 16. Ignoring crossing over, how many genetically different gametes can it produce by independent assortment?

**A.** 256. With 8 homologous pairs, there are 2^8 combinations. n = 8 pairs, each with two orientations at metaphase I: 2^8 = 256.
