Structure fits function at every scale of the cell.

## Mitochondria

A double membrane. The inner membrane is folded into **cristae**, providing large surface area for the electron transport chain. The **matrix** holds Krebs cycle enzymes.

## Chloroplasts

A double membrane surrounds **thylakoids** (stacked into grana), where light energy is captured. The **stroma** contains the enzymes of the Calvin cycle.

## Other examples

- Cells that secrete many proteins (pancreas cells) have extensive rough ER and Golgi.
- Muscle cells have many mitochondria for high ATP demand.
- Lysosome membranes keep digestive enzymes away from the rest of the cell.

<Note title="Compartments create gradients">
Membranes let organelles build up ion concentration differences, such as the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, that drive ATP synthesis.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Predicting cell features">
Cell X has abundant rough ER and Golgi; cell Y has many mitochondria. Which likely secretes digestive enzymes?

1. Secreted proteins need rough ER and Golgi.

**Answer:** cell X.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

Calculate the solute potential of a 0.3 M CaCl₂ solution at 27 °C. Use Ψs = −iCRT, with R = 0.0831 L·bar/(mol·K).

**Answer:** -22.44 bars

**Why:** i = 3, T = 300 K: −(3)(0.3)(0.0831)(300).

**Working:** Ψs = −(3)(0.3)(0.0831)(300) = -22.44 bars.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- -2.02 bars: Temperature must be in kelvins (add 273).
- -7.48 bars: CaCl₂ has an ionization constant of 3.
- 22.44 bars: Solute potential is negative: adding solute lowers water potential.

### Worked exam question 2

A plant cell has a water potential of -0.94 MPa. It is placed in a solution with a water potential of -0.1 MPa. Which describes the net movement of water?

**Answer:** Into the cell, because water moves toward the lower (more negative) water potential

**Why:** Water moves from -0.1 MPa to -0.94 MPa.

**Working:** Water flows from higher to lower water potential: from the solution into the cell.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Out of the cell, because the cell has the lower water potential: Water moves from higher to lower water potential.
- No net movement, because water potential does not affect osmosis: Differences in water potential drive osmosis.
- Into the cell, because water moves toward higher water potential: Water moves toward lower water potential.
