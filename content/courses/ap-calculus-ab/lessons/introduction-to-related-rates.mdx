When a balloon inflates, its radius and its volume both grow, and the formula $$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$ ties them together. If you know how fast one is changing, the chain rule tells you how fast the other is changing.

## Differentiating with respect to time

Every quantity that changes is a function of time $$t$$, even if the formula does not show it. Differentiate both sides with respect to $$t$$ using the chain rule:

$$
V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \frac{dr}{dt}.
$$

The result is an equation connecting the **rates** $$\frac{dV}{dt}$$ and $$\frac{dr}{dt}$$.

## Why the rates differ

At $$r = 1$$, the factor $$4\pi r^2$$ is about 12.6; at $$r = 10$$ it is about 1257. The same rate of radius growth pumps far more volume into a large balloon than a small one. Related rates capture this dependence on the current size.

<Note title="Differentiate first, substitute second">
Plug in the instantaneous values (like $$r = 10$$) only **after** differentiating. Substituting before differentiating turns a changing quantity into a constant and wipes out its rate.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Area of a growing circle">
The radius of a circular ripple grows at 3 cm/s. How fast is the area growing when the radius is 5 cm?

1. Equation: $$A = \pi r^2$$.
2. Differentiate: $$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt}$$.
3. Substitute $$r = 5$$ and $$\frac{dr}{dt} = 3$$: $$2\pi(5)(3)$$.

**Answer:** the area grows at $$30\pi \approx 94.248$$ square centimeters per second.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The edge length of a cube is increasing at a constant rate of 2 centimeters per second. At the instant when the edge length is 6 centimeters, what is the rate of change of the volume?

**Answer:** $$216$$

**Why:** Differentiate $$V = s^3$$ with respect to $$t$$: $$\frac{dV}{dt} = 3s^2\frac{ds}{dt}$$.

**Working:** $$\frac{dV}{dt} = 3s^2\frac{ds}{dt}$$. Substitute the dimension $$6$$ and its rate $$2$$ to get $$216$$ (cubic or square centimeters per second).

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$108$$: This leaves out a factor from differentiating the formula with respect to time.
- $$432$$: This forgets to multiply by the given rate of change of the dimension.
- $$36$$: This substitutes into the original formula instead of its derivative.

### Worked exam question 2

**Working:** $$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\frac{dr}{dt}$$. Substitute the dimension $$3$$ and its rate $$2$$ to get $$12\pi$$ (cubic or square centimeters per second).

- $$2\pi$$: This substitutes into the original formula instead of its derivative.
