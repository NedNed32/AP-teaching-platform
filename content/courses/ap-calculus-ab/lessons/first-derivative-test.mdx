If a function climbs and then falls, it must have peaked in between. The First Derivative Test makes that precise using the sign of $$f'$$ on each side of a critical point.

## The test

At a critical point $$c$$ (where $$f$$ is continuous):

| Sign of $$f'$$ left of $$c$$ | Sign right of $$c$$ | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| positive | negative | relative maximum |
| negative | positive | relative minimum |
| same on both sides | same | no extremum |

## Steps

1. Find the critical points.
2. Build a sign chart for $$f'$$.
3. Apply the table at each critical point.
4. If asked for the extreme *value*, substitute $$c$$ into $$f$$ (not $$f'$$).

<Note title="The standard justification">
"$$f$$ has a relative maximum at $$x = 2$$ because $$f'$$ changes from positive to negative at $$x = 2$$." Using the words **changes sign** is essential; saying only "$$f'(2) = 0$$" earns nothing.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying critical points">
Find and classify the relative extrema of $$f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 2$$.

1. $$f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x - 9 = 3(x - 3)(x + 1)$$; critical points $$x = -1$$ and $$x = 3$$.
2. Signs: positive for $$x < -1$$, negative on $$(-1, 3)$$, positive for $$x > 3$$.
3. At $$x = -1$$: positive to negative, relative maximum $$f(-1) = 7$$.
4. At $$x = 3$$: negative to positive, relative minimum $$f(3) = 27 - 54 - 27 + 2 = -52$$.

**Answer:** relative maximum 7 at $$x = -1$$; relative minimum $$-52$$ at $$x = 3$$.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** Let $$f(x) = 2x^{3} + 15x^{2} + 36x$$. At what value of $$x$$ does $$f$$ have a relative maximum?

**A.** $$-3$$. $$f'(x) = 6(x + 3)(x + 2)$$ changes from positive to negative at $$x = -3$$. $$f'(x) = 6x^2 + 30x + 36 = 6(x + 3)(x + 2)$$. Testing signs: $$f'$$ is positive left of $$-3$$, negative between $$-3$$ and $$-2$$, positive right of $$-2$$. So there is a relative maximum at $$-3$$ and a relative minimum at $$-2$$.

### Free-response practice: Analyzing a function

Let $$f(x) = 2x^{3} - 6x^{2} + 5$$.

**(a)** Find the x-coordinates of all relative extrema of $$f$$, and classify each as a relative maximum or relative minimum. Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Finds the critical points. Model answer: $$f'(x) = 6x(x - 2) = 0$$ at $$x = 0$$ and $$x = 2$$

To earn the point: Classifies both with a sign-change justification. Model answer: $$f'$$ changes from positive to negative at $$0$$ (relative maximum) and from negative to positive at $$2$$ (relative minimum).

**(b)** On what intervals is the graph of $$f$$ concave down? Give a reason.

To earn the point: Gives the interval with a reason based on $$f''$$. Model answer: $$f''(x) = 12x - 12 < 0$$ for $$x < 1$$

**(c)** Find the absolute maximum value of $$f$$ on the closed interval $$[-2, 3]$$. Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Evaluates $$f$$ at the critical points and endpoints. Model answer: $$f(-2) = -35$$, $$f(0) = 5$$, $$f(2) = -3$$, $$f(3) = 5$$

To earn the point: Gives the absolute maximum value. Model answer: $$5$$, at $$x = 0, 3$$

**(d)** Find $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{f(x) - f(0)}{x^2}$$.

To earn the point: Recognizes the form 0/0 and applies L'Hospital's Rule (twice, or once with $$f'(0) = 0$$). Model answer: $$\lim \frac{f'(x)}{2x} = \lim \frac{f''(x)}{2}$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct value. Model answer: $$\frac{f''(0)}{2} = -6$$
