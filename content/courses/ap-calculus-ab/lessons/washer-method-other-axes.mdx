This is the most demanding volume setup in AB Calculus, and a regular part of free-response Question 5 or 6. It combines everything: washers, distances to a shifted axis, and careful identification of which boundary is farther.

## Radii from a horizontal line y = k

| Axis position | Outer radius $$R$$ | Inner radius $$r$$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| below the region | top curve minus $$k$$ | bottom curve minus $$k$$ |
| above the region | $$k$$ minus bottom curve | $$k$$ minus top curve |

The roles of the curves **swap** when the axis is above the region: the bottom curve becomes the farther one.

For a vertical axis $$x = h$$, use the same logic with right and left boundaries written as functions of $$y$$.

## The setup

$$
V = \pi\int_a^b \left([R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2\right)dx.
$$

On many free-response questions you are asked only to *set up* the integral. Write it completely, with limits, and do not evaluate.

<Note title="Draw a radius">
On your sketch, draw a vertical segment from the axis to the nearer curve (that is $$r$$) and on to the farther curve (that is $$R$$). Labelling both lengths prevents most errors.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Revolving about a line above the region">
The region between $$y = x$$ and $$y = x^2$$ is revolved about $$y = 2$$. Find the volume.

1. The axis is above the region. Farther boundary: $$y = x^2$$; nearer: $$y = x$$.
2. $$R = 2 - x^2$$, $$r = 2 - x$$.
3. $$V = \pi\int_0^1 \left[(2 - x^2)^2 - (2 - x)^2\right]dx = \pi\int_0^1 \left(x^4 - 5x^2 + 4x\right)dx$$.
4. $$= \pi\left(\frac{1}{5} - \frac{5}{3} + 2\right)$$.

**Answer:** $$\frac{8\pi}{15} \approx 1.676$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Let $$R$$ be the region enclosed by $$y = x$$ and $$y = x^2$$. Which of the following gives the volume of the solid generated when $$R$$ is revolved about the line $$y = -1$$?

**A.** $$\pi\int_0^1 \left[(x + 1)^2 - (x^2 + 1)^2\right]dx$$. The radii are measured from $$y = -1$$: outer $$x + 1$$, inner $$x^2 + 1$$. Washers: $$V = \pi\int_0^1 \left[R(x)^2 - r(x)^2\right]dx$$ with $$R = x - (-1)$$ and $$r = x^2 - (-1)$$.

**Q.** Let $$R$$ be the region bounded by $$y = x^2$$ and $$y = 4$$. Which of the following gives the volume of the solid generated when $$R$$ is revolved about the line $$y = 4$$?

**A.** $$\pi\int_{-2}^{2} (4 - x^2)^2\,dx$$. Each disc has radius $$4 - x^2$$, the distance from the curve to the axis. Revolving about $$y = 4$$, which is the top boundary, gives discs of radius $$4 - x^2$$.

### Free-response practice: Area and volume

Let $$R$$ be the region enclosed by the graphs of $$f(x) = 3\cos\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)$$ and $$g(x) = x^2 - 2$$.

**(a)** Find the area of $$R$$.

To earn the point: Finds the limits of integration (the intersection points). Model answer: $$x \approx -1.927$$ and $$x \approx 1.927$$

To earn the point: Writes the integral of top minus bottom. Model answer: $$\int_{-1.927}^{1.927} (f(x) - g(x))\,dx$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct area. Model answer: $$12.792$$

**(b)** Find the volume of the solid generated when $$R$$ is revolved about the horizontal line $$y = -3$$.

To earn the point: Uses the washer method with radii measured from the line. Model answer: $$\pi\int_{-1.927}^{1.927} \left[(f(x) + 3)^2 - (g(x) + 3)^2\right]dx$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct volume. Model answer: $$300.232$$

**(c)** The region $$R$$ is the base of a solid. Cross sections perpendicular to the x-axis are squares. Find the volume of the solid.

To earn the point: Writes the integral of the side length squared. Model answer: $$\int_{-1.927}^{1.927} (f(x) - g(x))^2\,dx$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct volume. Model answer: $$51.077$$
