To average a list of numbers you add them and divide by how many there are. To average a function over an interval you do the same thing continuously: integrate, then divide by the length.

## The formula

$$
f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.
$$

Geometrically, it is the height of the rectangle on $$[a, b]$$ with the same area as the region under $$f$$.

## In context

If $$T(t)$$ is temperature over a day, its average value is the average temperature for the day, measured in degrees. Its units are the units of $$f$$, not of the integral.

## Mean Value Theorem for integrals

If $$f$$ is continuous on $$[a, b]$$, it actually *equals* its average value at least once: $$f(c) = f_{\text{avg}}$$ for some $$c$$ in $$[a, b]$$.

<Note title="Average value versus average rate of change">
Average **value** of $$f$$: $$\frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$. Average **rate of change** of $$f$$: $$\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$$. The exam frequently offers one as a distractor for the other.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Average from a table">
The rate of traffic $$R(t)$$, in cars per minute, is shown. Estimate the average rate over $$0 \leq t \leq 20$$ with a trapezoidal sum.

| $$t$$ | 0 | 5 | 15 | 20 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $$R(t)$$ | 10 | 14 | 20 | 12 |

1. Trapezoidal sum: $$\frac{5}{2}(24) + \frac{10}{2}(34) + \frac{5}{2}(32) = 60 + 170 + 80 = 310$$.
2. Divide by the length, 20.

**Answer:** about $$15.5$$ cars per minute.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

What is the average value of $$f(x) = -6x^{2} - 3$$ on the interval $$[0, 3]$$?

**Answer:** $$-21$$

**Why:** $$\frac{1}{3}\int_0^{3} f(x)\,dx = \frac{1}{3}\left(-63\right)$$.

**Working:** Average value $$= \frac{1}{3 - 0}\int_0^{3} (-6x^{2} - 3)\,dx = \frac{1}{3}\left[-2x^3 - 3x\right]_0^{3} = -21$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$-30$$: This averages the endpoint values, which only works for linear functions.
- $$-63$$: This is the integral; divide by the length of the interval.
- $$-18$$: This is the average rate of change of f, not the average value.
