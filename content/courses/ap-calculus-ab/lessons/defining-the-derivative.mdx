The derivative is the instantaneous rate of change, written as a limit. Learning to *recognize* this limit in disguise is a skill the multiple-choice section tests every year.

## Two equivalent definitions

$$
f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h} \qquad\text{or}\qquad f'(a) = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}.
$$

Replacing the fixed point $$a$$ with a variable $$x$$ gives the **derivative function**:

$$
f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}.
$$

## Notation

If $$y = f(x)$$, all of these mean the same thing: $$f'(x)$$, $$y'$$, $$\frac{dy}{dx}$$, $$\frac{d}{dx} f(x)$$. The value at a point can be written $$f'(3)$$ or $$\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{x = 3}$$.

## Recognizing a derivative in disguise

A limit of the form $$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{g(c + h) - g(c)}{h}$$ is simply $$g'(c)$$. Identify the function and the point, then differentiate with the rules you will learn in 2.5 to 2.10. For example,

$$
\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^{2 + h} - e^2}{h} = \frac{d}{dx} e^x \Big|_{x = 2} = e^2.
$$

<Note title="Tangent line equation">
The tangent line at $$x = a$$ goes through $$(a, f(a))$$ with slope $$f'(a)$$, so its equation is $$y = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$$. This one formula drives local linearity (topic 4.6).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Derivative from the definition">
Use the definition to find $$f'(x)$$ for $$f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$$.

1. $$\frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h} = \frac{\frac{1}{x + h} - \frac{1}{x}}{h} = \frac{x - (x + h)}{h\,x(x + h)} = \frac{-1}{x(x + h)}$$.
2. Take the limit as $$h \to 0$$: $$\frac{-1}{x \cdot x}$$.

**Answer:** $$f'(x) = -\frac{1}{x^2}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Which of the following is an equation of the line tangent to the graph of $$f(x) = x^{2} + 3x - 1$$ at $$x = 2$$?

**Answer:** $$y - 9 = 7(x - 2)$$

**Why:** The point is $$(2, 9)$$ and the slope is $$f'(2) = 7$$.

**Working:** $$f(2) = 9$$ and $$f'(x) = 2x + 3$$, so $$f'(2) = 7$$. Point-slope form: $$y - 9 = 7(x - 2)$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$y - 9 = 9(x - 2)$$: This uses the function value $$9$$ as the slope.
- $$y - 2 = 7(x - 9)$$: This swaps the x- and y-coordinates of the point of tangency.
- $$y - 9 = 4(x - 2)$$: This slope drops the derivative of $$3x$$.

**Q.** What is $$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(3 + h)^{2} - 9}{h}$$?

**A.** $$6$$. This is the derivative of $$x^{2}$$ at $$x = 3$$: $$2(3)^{1} = 6$$. The expression is $$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(3 + h) - f(3)}{h}$$ for $$f(x) = x^{2}$$, which is $$f'(3) = 2 \cdot 3^{1} = 6$$.
