A positive slope means the graph is going up; a negative slope means it is going down. So the sign of $$f'$$ tells you everything about where $$f$$ rises and falls.

## The test

- If $$f'(x) > 0$$ on an interval, $$f$$ is **increasing** there.
- If $$f'(x) < 0$$ on an interval, $$f$$ is **decreasing** there.

## Making a sign chart

1. Find $$f'(x)$$ and factor it.
2. Find the critical points and any points where $$f$$ is undefined.
3. Mark them on a number line.
4. Test one value in each interval (or reason from the factors).
5. Read off where $$f'$$ is positive and negative.

## Reading from a graph of f prime

When a question gives the graph of $$f'$$ rather than $$f$$, read the sign of $$f'$$ directly: $$f$$ increases where the graph of $$f'$$ is **above** the x-axis, regardless of whether $$f'$$ is rising or falling.

<Note title="Justify with f prime, not f">
Write "$$f$$ is increasing on $$(1, 3)$$ because $$f'(x) > 0$$ on that interval." Statements like "the graph goes up" or "$$f$$ is positive" do not earn credit.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A sign chart">
Find where $$f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3$$ is increasing.

1. $$f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x^2 = 4x^2(x - 3)$$.
2. Critical points: $$x = 0$$ and $$x = 3$$.
3. Since $$4x^2 \geq 0$$, the sign of $$f'$$ is the sign of $$x - 3$$: negative for $$x < 3$$ (except 0, where it is zero), positive for $$x > 3$$.

**Answer:** $$f$$ is increasing on $$(3, \infty)$$ and decreasing on $$(-\infty, 3)$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The derivative of a function $$f$$ is $$f'(x) = x(x - 3)$$. On which intervals is $$f$$ increasing?

**Answer:** $$x < 0$$ and $$x > 3$$

**Why:** $$f'(x) > 0$$ when both factors have the same sign: $$x < 0$$ or $$x > 3$$.

**Working:** $$f$$ increases where $$f'(x) > 0$$. A sign chart for $$x(x - 3)$$ is positive, negative, positive across $$0$$ and $$3$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0 < x < 3$$ only: Between the roots the factors have opposite signs, so f′ &lt; 0 and f is decreasing.
- $$x > 3$$ only: This misses $$x < 0$$, where both factors are negative so f′ > 0.
- $$x < 0$$ only: This misses $$x > 3$$, where both factors are positive.
