Some functions are too wild to evaluate directly but can be trapped between two well-behaved ones. If the two outer functions head to the same place, the one in the middle has no choice but to go there too.

## The theorem

If $$g(x) \leq f(x) \leq h(x)$$ for all $$x$$ near $$a$$ (except possibly at $$a$$), and

$$
\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = \lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L,
$$

then $$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$$.

## Using it with oscillating factors

The most common setting is a product of something that shrinks to 0 and something that is merely bounded. Since $$-1 \leq \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \leq 1$$,

$$
-|x| \leq x \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \leq |x|.
$$

Both $$-|x|$$ and $$|x|$$ approach 0 as $$x \to 0$$, so $$\lim_{x \to 0} x \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = 0$$, even though $$\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$$ alone has no limit at 0.

## Why sin x over x approaches 1

A geometric argument with the unit circle shows that for small positive $$x$$,

$$
\cos x \leq \frac{\sin x}{x} \leq 1.
$$

As $$x \to 0$$, $$\cos x \to 1$$, so the Squeeze Theorem gives $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$$. This limit is what makes the derivative of $$\sin x$$ equal $$\cos x$$ (topic 2.7).

<Note title="Writing a squeeze justification">
On a free-response question, state the inequality, state that both bounding functions have the same limit, and name the Squeeze Theorem. All three parts earn the point.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Squeezing a bounded factor">
Find $$\lim_{x \to 0} x^4 \cos\left(\frac{2}{x}\right)$$.

1. For $$x \neq 0$$: $$-1 \leq \cos\left(\frac{2}{x}\right) \leq 1$$.
2. Multiply by $$x^4 \geq 0$$: $$-x^4 \leq x^4 \cos\left(\frac{2}{x}\right) \leq x^4$$.
3. $$\lim_{x \to 0} (-x^4) = 0$$ and $$\lim_{x \to 0} x^4 = 0$$.

**Answer:** by the Squeeze Theorem, the limit is 0.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

What is $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan(7x)}{5x}$$?

**Answer:** $$\frac{7}{5}$$

**Why:** Write it as $$\frac{7}{5} \cdot \frac{\tan(7x)}{7x}$$; the second factor approaches 1.

**Working:** Using $$\lim_{u \to 0} \frac{\tan u}{u} = 1$$ with $$u = 7x$$: $$\frac{\tan(7x)}{5x} = \frac{7}{5} \cdot \frac{\tan(7x)}{7x} \to \frac{7}{5}$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1$$: $$\frac{\tan u}{u} \to 1$$ only when the angle and the denominator match. Here they differ by a factor of $$\frac{7}{5}$$.
- $$\frac{5}{7}$$: This inverts the ratio of the coefficients.
- $$0$$: Substitution gives $$\frac{0}{0}$$, which is indeterminate, not 0.

### Worked exam question 2

The functions $$f$$, $$g$$, and $$h$$ satisfy $$g(x) \leq f(x) \leq h(x)$$ for all $$x$$, where $$g(x) = -2 - 3(x - 4)^2$$ and $$h(x) = -2 + 3(x - 4)^2$$. What is $$\lim_{x \to 4} f(x)$$?

**Answer:** $$-2$$

**Why:** Both $$g$$ and $$h$$ approach $$-2$$ as $$x \to 4$$, so the Squeeze Theorem forces $$f$$ to approach $$-2$$ too.

**Working:** $$\lim_{x \to 4} g(x) = -2$$ and $$\lim_{x \to 4} h(x) = -2$$. Since $$f$$ is trapped between them, the Squeeze Theorem gives $$\lim_{x \to 4} f(x) = -2$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$4$$: This is the input value being approached, not the output.
- $$-4$$: This adds the limits of $$g$$ and $$h$$; the squeeze gives their common value.
- It cannot be determined without a formula for $$f$$.: The Squeeze Theorem determines the limit without a formula for $$f$$, because the bounds share the same limit.
