## Average rate of change

Over an interval $$[a, b]$$, the average rate of change of $$f$$ is

$$
\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.
$$

Geometrically, it is the slope of the **secant line** through $$(a, f(a))$$ and $$(b, f(b))$$. Its units are output units per input unit, for example meters per second.

## Instantaneous rate of change

Write the second input as $$a + h$$. The average rate becomes the **difference quotient** $$\frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$$. Letting $$h \to 0$$ gives the instantaneous rate:

$$
\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}.
$$

When this limit exists it is the slope of the **tangent line** at $$x = a$$. In topic 2.2 it gets its name: the derivative $$f'(a)$$.

<Note title="Average versus instantaneous wording">
On the exam, "average rate of change over an interval" means a secant slope (no calculus needed). "Rate of change at $$t = 3$$" means a derivative. Read the question carefully: the two are often offered as competing answer choices.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="From secants to a tangent">
For $$f(x) = x^2$$, find the instantaneous rate of change at $$x = 3$$.

1. Difference quotient: $$\frac{(3 + h)^2 - 9}{h} = \frac{6h + h^2}{h} = 6 + h$$ for $$h \neq 0$$.
2. Take the limit: $$\lim_{h \to 0} (6 + h) = 6$$.

**Answer:** the instantaneous rate of change at $$x = 3$$ is 6.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The temperature of a liquid, in degrees Celsius, is modeled by a differentiable function $$H$$ of time $$t$$, in minutes. Selected values are shown.

*Temperature H(t)*

| $$t$$ (minutes) | $$H(t)$$ (°C) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 34 |
| 2 | 26 |
| 5 | 16 |
| 7 | 47 |
| 10 | 17 |

What is the average rate of change of $$H$$ over the interval $$5 \leq t \leq 10$$, in degrees Celsius per minute?

**Answer:** $$\frac{1}{5}$$

**Why:** $$\frac{H(10) - H(5)}{10 - 5} = \frac{1}{5}$$.

**Working:** Average rate of change $$= \frac{H(10) - H(5)}{10 - 5} = \frac{17 - 16}{5} = \frac{1}{5}$$ degrees per minute.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1$$: This is the total change in temperature; it still needs to be divided by the elapsed time.
- $$5$$: This divides time by temperature, which is upside down.
- $$\frac{33}{2}$$: This is the average temperature, not the average rate of change.
