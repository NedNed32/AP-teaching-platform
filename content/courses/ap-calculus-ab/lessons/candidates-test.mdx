The Extreme Value Theorem promises that a continuous function on $$[a, b]$$ has an absolute maximum and minimum. The Candidates Test finds them: there are only a few places they can be, so check them all.

## The test

1. Confirm $$f$$ is continuous on $$[a, b]$$.
2. Find all critical points **inside** $$(a, b)$$.
3. Evaluate $$f$$ at each critical point and at $$a$$ and $$b$$.
4. The largest value is the absolute maximum; the smallest is the absolute minimum.

Present the values in a table so the comparison is clear to a grader.

## With accumulation functions

The test works the same when $$f$$ is defined by an integral, such as $$g(x) = \int_0^x h(t)\,dt$$. The critical points are where $$h(x) = 0$$, and the values come from areas (topic 6.5).

<Note title="Answer the question asked">
"What is the absolute maximum **value**?" wants $$f(c)$$. "**Where** does the absolute maximum occur?" wants $$c$$. Many lost points come from giving one when the other was asked.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A table of candidates">
Find the absolute extrema of $$f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 12x + 1$$ on $$[-2, 3]$$.

1. $$f'(x) = 6x^2 - 6x - 12 = 6(x - 2)(x + 1)$$; critical points $$x = -1$$ and $$x = 2$$, both in the interval.
2. Evaluate:

| $$x$$ | $$-2$$ | $$-1$$ | $$2$$ | $$3$$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $$f(x)$$ | $$-3$$ | $$8$$ | $$-19$$ | $$-8$$ |

**Answer:** the absolute maximum is 8 at $$x = -1$$; the absolute minimum is $$-19$$ at $$x = 2$$.
</WorkedExample>
