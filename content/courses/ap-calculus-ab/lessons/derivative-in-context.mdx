Every free-response exam asks you to interpret a derivative in words. A complete interpretation has four parts, and graders check for each one.

## The four parts

Interpret $$W'(3) = -12$$, where $$W(t)$$ is gallons of water in a tank at time $$t$$ hours:

> At time $$t = 3$$ hours, the amount of water in the tank is decreasing at a rate of 12 gallons per hour.

1. **When:** at $$t = 3$$ hours.
2. **What:** the amount of water in the tank.
3. **Direction:** decreasing (from the negative sign).
4. **Rate with units:** 12 gallons per hour.

## Units

The units of $$f'(x)$$ are always *output units per input unit*. The units of $$f''(x)$$ are output units per input unit **squared**, for example gallons per hour per hour.

## Instantaneous, not average or total

A derivative is a rate **at an instant**. Common wrong interpretations describe a total change ("the tank lost 12 gallons") or an average over an interval. Neither matches $$W'(3)$$.

<Note title="Use the words in the problem">
Name the quantity using the problem's own description, not just the letter. "The rate of change of $$W$$ is $$-12$$" does not earn the interpretation point.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Interpreting a second derivative">
$$v(t)$$ is the velocity of a car in miles per hour, $$t$$ hours after noon. Interpret $$v'(2) = 6$$.

1. When: at 2 p.m. ($$t = 2$$).
2. What: the car's velocity.
3. Direction: increasing.
4. Units: miles per hour per hour.

**Answer:** at 2 p.m. the velocity of the car is increasing at 6 miles per hour per hour.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Let $$C(x)$$ be the cost, in dollars, of producing x items. Which of the following is the best interpretation of $$C'(2) = -2$$?

**Answer:** The cost is decreasing at a rate of 2 dollars per item when 2 items have been produced.

**Why:** A derivative at a point is an instantaneous rate of change, with units of output per unit of input.

**Working:** $$C'(2)$$ is the instantaneous rate of change of $$C$$ when 2 items have been produced. Its sign (negative) says the cost is decreasing, and its units are dollars per item.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- The cost when 2 items have been produced is -2.: This describes $$C(2)$$, the value of the function, not its rate of change.
- The cost changes by -2 over the first 2 items.: This describes a total change over an interval, which is an integral of the rate, not the derivative at one point.
- The average rate of change of the cost over the first 2 items is -2 dollars per item.: The derivative is an instantaneous rate at one input, not an average over an interval.

### Worked exam question 2

The temperature of a room, in degrees Fahrenheit, is modeled by $$H(t) = 53 + 14\sin\left(\frac{t}{5}\right)e^{-t/20}$$, where $$t$$ is measured in hours. What is the instantaneous rate of change of the temperature at $$t = 8$$, in degrees Fahrenheit per hour?

**Answer:** $$-0.524$$

**Why:** The calculator's numerical derivative of $$H$$ at $$t = 8$$ gives -0.524; the temperature is decreasing.

**Working:** The instantaneous rate of change is $$H'(8) \approx -0.524$$ degrees Fahrenheit per hour, from the calculator's numerical derivative.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$62.380$$: This is the temperature itself, not its rate of change.
- $$1.173$$: This is the average rate of change over $$0 \leq t \leq 8$$, not the instantaneous rate.
- $$-4.191$$: This multiplies the rate by the time, which is not a rate.
