The graph of an inverse function is the graph of $$f$$ reflected across $$y = x$$. Reflection swaps rise and run, so the slopes are reciprocals.

## The formula

If $$g$$ is the inverse of $$f$$, then $$f(g(x)) = x$$. Differentiating with the chain rule gives $$f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) = 1$$, so

$$
g'(x) = \frac{1}{f'(g(x))}.
$$

At a specific point: if $$f(a) = b$$, then

$$
g'(b) = \frac{1}{f'(a)}.
$$

## The three-step routine

1. You are asked for $$g'(b)$$. Find the $$a$$ with $$f(a) = b$$ (often by inspection or from a table).
2. Compute $$f'(a)$$.
3. Take the reciprocal.

<Note title="Do not evaluate f prime at b">
The most common mistake is computing $$\frac{1}{f'(b)}$$. The derivative of $$f$$ must be evaluated at the matching input $$a$$, not at $$b$$.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Inverse of a cubic">
Let $$f(x) = x^3 + 2x + 1$$ and $$g = f^{-1}$$. Find $$g'(4)$$.

1. Solve $$f(a) = 4$$: try $$a = 1$$: $$1 + 2 + 1 = 4$$. So $$a = 1$$.
2. $$f'(x) = 3x^2 + 2$$, so $$f'(1) = 5$$.

**Answer:** $$g'(4) = \frac{1}{5}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Let $$f(x) = x^{3} + 4x + 1$$, and let $$g$$ be the inverse function of $$f$$. What is $$g'(6)$$?

**Answer:** $$\frac{1}{7}$$

**Why:** $$f(1) = 6$$, so $$g'(6) = \frac{1}{f'(1)} = \frac{1}{7}$$.

**Working:** If $$f(1) = 6$$, then $$g(6) = 1$$ and $$g'(6) = \frac{1}{f'(g(6))} = \frac{1}{f'(1)}$$. Since $$f'(x) = 3x^{2} + 4$$, $$f'(1) = 7$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$7$$: This is $$f'(1)$$; the inverse's derivative is its reciprocal.
- $$\frac{1}{112}$$: This evaluates $$f'$$ at $$6$$ instead of at the input $$1$$ with $$f(1) = 6$$.
- $$\frac{1}{6}$$: This takes the reciprocal of a function value, not of the derivative.

### Worked exam question 2

Let $$f(x) = x^{3} + 2x + e^{x/2}$$, and let $$g$$ be the inverse of $$f$$. What is the value of $$g'(7)$$?

**Answer:** $$0.120$$

**Why:** Solve $$f(x) = 7$$ to get $$x \approx 1.335$$, then $$g'(7) = \frac{1}{f'(1.335)}$$.

**Working:** Use the calculator to solve $$f(x) = 7$$: $$x \approx 1.335$$, so $$g(7) \approx 1.335$$. Then $$g'(7) = \frac{1}{f'(g(7))} \approx \frac{1}{8.323} \approx 0.120$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$8.323$$: This is f′ at the matching input; the inverse's derivative is its reciprocal.
- $$0.006$$: This evaluates $$f'$$ at $$7$$ instead of at the input where $$f(x) = 7$$.
- $$1.335$$: This is $$g(7)$$, not $$g'(7)$$.
