When a quantity grows (or shrinks) at a rate proportional to its own size, it follows the most important differential equation in applied mathematics.

## The model

$$
\frac{dy}{dt} = ky \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y = y_0 e^{kt},
$$

where $$y_0 = y(0)$$ is the initial amount.

- $$k > 0$$: exponential **growth** (populations, compound interest).
- $$k < 0$$: exponential **decay** (radioactivity, drug elimination).

## Finding k from data

Two pieces of information determine the model: the initial value and one other value. Substitute and solve with a logarithm.

| Given | Equation for $$k$$ |
| --- | --- |
| doubles every $$T$$ | $$e^{kT} = 2$$, $$k = \frac{\ln 2}{T}$$ |
| half-life $$T$$ | $$e^{kT} = \frac{1}{2}$$, $$k = -\frac{\ln 2}{T}$$ |
| $$y(t_1) = y_1$$ | $$k = \frac{1}{t_1}\ln\frac{y_1}{y_0}$$ |

## Shifted models

Newton's law of cooling, $$\frac{dT}{dt} = k(T - A)$$, is exponential in the *difference* from room temperature $$A$$: $$T - A = (T_0 - A)e^{kt}$$.

<Note title="Rate depends on the amount">
In an exponential model, a larger amount changes faster. That is why the rate $$\frac{dy}{dt}$$ is greatest when $$y$$ is greatest, a fact often used in multiple-choice questions.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Radioactive decay">
A substance has a half-life of 10 years. How much of a 50-gram sample remains after 25 years?

1. $$k = -\frac{\ln 2}{10}$$.
2. $$y(25) = 50e^{-25\ln 2/10} = 50 \cdot 2^{-2.5}$$.

**Answer:** about $$8.839$$ grams.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A population $$P$$ grows according to $$\frac{dP}{dt} = kP$$, and it doubles every 4 years. If $$P(0) = 500$$, what is $$P(12)$$?

**Answer:** $$4000$$

**Why:** $$12$$ years is 3 doubling periods, so the population is multiplied by $$2^{3}$$.

**Working:** The solution is $$P(t) = 500 \cdot 2^{t/4}$$. So $$P(12) = 500 \cdot 2^{3} = 4000$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2000$$: This adds the original amount once per period (linear growth); exponential growth multiplies by 2 each period.
- $$1000$$: This counts only one doubling period.
- $$2048000$$: This treats each year as a doubling period.

### Worked exam question 2

A quantity $$y$$ satisfies $$\frac{dy}{dt} = ky$$ and doubles every 7 hours. What is the value of $$k$$?

**Answer:** $$\frac{\ln 2}{7}$$

**Why:** $$y = y_0e^{kt}$$ and $$e^{7k} = 2$$.

**Working:** $$y(7) = 2y_0$$ means $$e^{7k} = 2$$, so $$k = \frac{\ln 2}{7}$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$\frac{2}{7}$$: Doubling means e^\{kT\} = 2; solve with a logarithm.
- $$7\ln 2$$: Solving $$7k = \ln 2$$ requires dividing by $$7$$.
- $$\frac{1}{14}$$: This is not a solution of e^\{kT\} = 2.
