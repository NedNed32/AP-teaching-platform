A differential equation $$\frac{dy}{dx} = F(x, y)$$ tells you the slope of a solution through any point. Drawing that slope as a short segment at many points produces a **slope field**, a picture of every solution at once.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/slope-field.svg" alt="A slope field on a 7-by-7 grid for dy/dx = x - y: segments are horizontal along the line y = x, steeply positive in the lower right, and steeply negative in the upper left." caption="Slope field for dy/dx = x − y." />

## How to sketch one

1. At each requested point, compute $$\frac{dy}{dx}$$.
2. Draw a short segment centered on the point with that slope: horizontal for 0, rising for positive, falling for negative, steeper for larger magnitudes.

## Patterns that speed things up

| Equation depends on | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| $$x$$ only, like $$\frac{dy}{dx} = x^2$$ | all segments in a **column** are parallel |
| $$y$$ only, like $$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2y$$ | all segments in a **row** are parallel |
| both | look for lines where the slope is 0 or constant |

For $$\frac{dy}{dx} = x - y$$, the slope is 0 along the line $$y = x$$ and equal to 1 along $$y = x - 1$$.

<Note title="Precision on the exam">
Graders check the sign and relative steepness of each segment, and that zero slopes are drawn horizontally. They do not measure angles exactly.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Slopes at a few points">
For $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y}$$, find the slopes at $$(1, 1)$$, $$(-1, 1)$$, $$(2, -1)$$ and $$(0, 2)$$.

1. $$(1, 1)$$: $$1$$.
2. $$(-1, 1)$$: $$-1$$.
3. $$(2, -1)$$: $$-2$$.
4. $$(0, 2)$$: $$0$$, a horizontal segment.

**Answer:** slopes 1, $$-1$$, $$-2$$ and 0.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

A slope field for a differential equation is shown at the points with integer coordinates.

<Figure src="assets/guides/sketching-slope-fields-1.svg" alt="A slope field drawn on the grid from x = -2 to 2 and y = -2 to 2." caption="Graph for the question below." />

**Q.** Which of the following differential equations could have the slope field shown?

**A.** $$\frac{dy}{dx} = -x$$. Check where the segments are horizontal and where the slopes are positive or negative; this equation matches at every point. Compare slopes at specific points. For example, at $$(1, 0)$$ the field's segment has slope $$-1$$, and at $$(0, 1)$$ slope $$0$$, which matches $$\frac{dy}{dx} = -x$$.

### Free-response practice: A differential equation

Consider the differential equation $$\frac{dy}{dx} = 4x(y - 1)$$. Let $$y = f(x)$$ be the particular solution with $$f(1) = 0$$.

**(a)** Find the slope of the slope-field segment at each of the points $$(-1, 2)$$, $$(0, 3)$$, $$(1, 0)$$ and $$(2, 1)$$.

To earn the point: Gives all four slopes correctly. Model answer: $$(-1, 2): -4$$; $$(0, 3): 0$$; $$(1, 0): -4$$; $$(2, 1): 0$$

**(b)** Write an equation for the line tangent to the graph of $$f$$ at $$x = 1$$, and use it to approximate $$f(1.2)$$.

To earn the point: Finds the slope at the point and writes the tangent line. Model answer: $$\frac{dy}{dx}\Big|_{(1, 0)} = -4$$; $$y = 0 - 4(x - 1)$$

To earn the point: Gives the approximation. Model answer: $$f(1.2) \approx -0.8$$

**(c)** Find $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$$ in terms of $$x$$ and $$y$$. Is the approximation in part (b) an overestimate or an underestimate? Explain.

To earn the point: Finds the second derivative. Model answer: $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 4(y - 1) + 4x\frac{dy}{dx} = 4(y - 1)(1 + 4x^2)$$

To earn the point: Uses the sign of the second derivative at the point to conclude. Model answer: At $$(1, 0)$$, $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -20$$, so f is concave down and the tangent line lies above the curve: an overestimate.

**(d)** Find the particular solution $$y = f(x)$$ with $$f(1) = 0$$.

To earn the point: Separates the variables. Model answer: $$\frac{dy}{y - 1} = 4x\,dx$$

To earn the point: Antidifferentiates both sides. Model answer: $$\ln|y - 1| = 2x^2 + C$$

To earn the point: Uses the initial condition. Model answer: $$C = \ln1 - 2$$

To earn the point: Solves for $$y$$. Model answer: $$y = 1 - e^{2(x^2 - 1)}$$
