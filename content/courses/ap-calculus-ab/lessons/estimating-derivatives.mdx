Real data rarely comes with a formula. Free-response questions often give a table of measurements and ask you to estimate a derivative. The answer is always a secant slope.

## From a table

To estimate $$f'(a)$$, use the two table values that **bracket** $$a$$ most closely:

$$
f'(a) \approx \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1}, \qquad x_1 < a < x_2.
$$

If $$a$$ is itself a table entry, the symmetric choice $$\frac{f(a + h) - f(a - h)}{2h}$$ is usually more accurate than a one-sided quotient.

## From a graph

Draw (or imagine) the tangent line at the point and read two points on it. The slope of that line estimates the derivative. Where the graph is a straight segment, the derivative equals the slope of the segment exactly.

## With a calculator

On calculator-active questions, use the numerical derivative feature, for example `nDeriv` on TI calculators. Report answers correct to three decimal places.

<Note title="Show the difference quotient">
An AP grader wants to see the quotient itself, such as $$\frac{H(7) - H(5)}{7 - 5}$$, not just a number. Include the units when the question has a context.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Estimating from data with units">
The temperature $$T(t)$$, in °C, of a cooling drink at time $$t$$ minutes:

| $$t$$ | 0 | 4 | 10 | 15 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $$T(t)$$ | 80 | 66 | 51 | 43 |

Estimate $$T'(7)$$.

1. The closest bracketing values are $$t = 4$$ and $$t = 10$$.
2. $$\frac{T(10) - T(4)}{10 - 4} = \frac{51 - 66}{6} = -2.5$$.

**Answer:** $$T'(7) \approx -2.5$$ °C per minute: the drink is cooling at about 2.5 degrees per minute.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The temperature of a liquid, in degrees Celsius, is modeled by a differentiable function $$H$$ of time $$t$$, in minutes. Selected values are shown.

*Temperature H(t)*

| $$t$$ (minutes) | $$H(t)$$ (°C) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 34 |
| 2 | 26 |
| 5 | 16 |
| 7 | 47 |
| 10 | 17 |

What is the average rate of change of $$H$$ over the interval $$5 \leq t \leq 10$$, in degrees Celsius per minute?

**Answer:** $$\frac{1}{5}$$

**Why:** $$\frac{H(10) - H(5)}{10 - 5} = \frac{1}{5}$$.

**Working:** Average rate of change $$= \frac{H(10) - H(5)}{10 - 5} = \frac{17 - 16}{5} = \frac{1}{5}$$ degrees per minute.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1$$: This is the total change in temperature; it still needs to be divided by the elapsed time.
- $$5$$: This divides time by temperature, which is upside down.
- $$\frac{33}{2}$$: This is the average temperature, not the average rate of change.

### Worked exam question 2

The function $$g$$ is differentiable. Selected values of $$g$$ are shown.

*Values of g*

| $$x$$ | $$g(x)$$ |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | -1 |
| 3 | 12 |
| 4 | 7 |
| 6 | -8 |
| 9 | -8 |

Using the data in the table and the values on either side of $$x = 4$$, what is the best estimate of $$g'(4)$$?

**Answer:** $$-\frac{20}{3}$$

**Why:** $$\frac{g(6) - g(3)}{6 - 3} = \frac{-20}{3}$$.

**Working:** The slope of the secant line through the neighboring points estimates the derivative: $$g'(4) \approx \frac{-8 - (12)}{3} = -\frac{20}{3}$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$\frac{7}{4}$$: This divides the output by the input, which is not a rate of change.
- $$-15$$: This forgets to divide by the change in x.
- $$-20$$: This is the change in g without dividing by the change in x.

### Free-response practice: Rate of people entering a park

People enter an amusement park at a rate modeled by a differentiable function $$P$$, where $$P(t)$$ is measured in people per minute and $$t$$ is measured in minutes. Selected values are shown.

*Values of P(t)*

| $$t$$ (minutes) | $$P(t)$$ (people per minute) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 60 |
| 3 | 71 |
| 5 | 40 |
| 9 | 71 |
| 12 | 77 |

**(a)** Use the data to estimate $$P'(7)$$. Show the computations and indicate units.

To earn the point: Uses a difference quotient on the interval containing the time. Model answer: $$\frac{P(9) - P(5)}{9 - 5} = \frac{31}{4}$$

To earn the point: Gives correct units. Model answer: people per minute per minute

**(b)** Use a trapezoidal sum with the four subintervals shown in the table to approximate $$\int_0^{12} P(t)\,dt$$. Explain the meaning of this integral in the context of the problem.

To earn the point: Sets up the trapezoidal sum with unequal widths. Model answer: $$\frac{3}{2}(60 + 71)$$ + $$\frac{2}{2}(71 + 40)$$ + $$\frac{4}{2}(40 + 71)$$ + $$\frac{3}{2}(71 + 77)$$

To earn the point: Gives the correct value. Model answer: $$\frac{1503}{2}$$

To earn the point: Interprets the integral with units. Model answer: The total number of people who enter the park during the first 12 minutes.

**(c)** Must there be a time $$c$$, for $$3 < c < 9$$, such that $$P'(c) = 0$$? Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Answers yes, citing the Mean Value Theorem (or Rolle's Theorem) with its conditions. Model answer: Yes. $$P$$ is differentiable (so continuous), and $$P(3) = P(9) = 71$$. By the Mean Value Theorem there is a $$c$$ with $$P'(c) = \frac{71 - 71}{6} = 0$$.

**(d)** Must there be a time $$t$$, for $$0 < t < 3$$, at which $$P(t) = 69$$? Justify your answer.

To earn the point: Answers yes, citing the Intermediate Value Theorem and continuity. Model answer: Yes. $$P$$ is continuous, and $$69$$ is between $$P(0) = 60$$ and $$P(3) = 71$$, so the IVT guarantees such a $$t$$.
