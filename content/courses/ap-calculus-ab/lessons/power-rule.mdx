Computing every derivative from the limit definition would be exhausting. The power rule is the first shortcut, and it covers a surprising number of functions once you rewrite them as powers.

## The rule

$$
\frac{d}{dx} x^n = n x^{n - 1}
$$

Bring the exponent down as a multiplier, then lower the exponent by one. For a positive integer $$n$$ it follows from expanding $$(x + h)^n$$ in the definition: every term except $$n x^{n-1} h$$ has at least $$h^2$$ and disappears in the limit.

## Rewrite first

| Original | Rewritten | Derivative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $$\sqrt{x}$$ | $$x^{1/2}$$ | $$\frac{1}{2}x^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$$ |
| $$\frac{1}{x}$$ | $$x^{-1}$$ | $$-x^{-2} = -\frac{1}{x^2}$$ |
| $$\frac{4}{x^5}$$ | $$4x^{-5}$$ | $$-20x^{-6}$$ |
| $$x\sqrt{x}$$ | $$x^{3/2}$$ | $$\frac{3}{2}x^{1/2}$$ |

<Note title="Powers of x only">
The power rule is for a variable raised to a constant. It does not apply to $$2^x$$ (a constant raised to a variable) or directly to $$(3x + 1)^5$$, which needs the chain rule (topic 3.1).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Slope from the power rule">
Find the slope of $$y = \frac{8}{\sqrt{x}}$$ at $$x = 4$$.

1. Rewrite: $$y = 8x^{-1/2}$$.
2. Differentiate: $$y' = -4x^{-3/2}$$.
3. Evaluate: $$-4 \cdot 4^{-3/2} = -4 \cdot \frac{1}{8}$$.

**Answer:** the slope is $$-\frac{1}{2}$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

If $$f(x) = -3x^{3} + 3x^{2} - x - 7$$, what is $$f'(-1)$$?

**Answer:** $$-16$$

**Why:** $$f'(x) = -9x^{2} + 6x - 1$$, so $$f'(-1) = -16$$.

**Working:** By the power rule, $$f'(x) = -9x^{2} + 6x - 1$$. Substituting $$x = -1$$ gives $$-16$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0$$: This is $$f(-1)$$, the value of the function, not its derivative.
- $$-15$$: This drops the derivative of the linear term, which is its coefficient.
- $$-23$$: The derivative of a constant is 0; this keeps the constant term.

### Worked exam question 2

Which of the following is an equation of the line tangent to the graph of $$f(x) = x^{2} + 3x - 1$$ at $$x = 2$$?

**Answer:** $$y - 9 = 7(x - 2)$$

**Why:** The point is $$(2, 9)$$ and the slope is $$f'(2) = 7$$.

**Working:** $$f(2) = 9$$ and $$f'(x) = 2x + 3$$, so $$f'(2) = 7$$. Point-slope form: $$y - 9 = 7(x - 2)$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$y - 9 = 9(x - 2)$$: This uses the function value $$9$$ as the slope.
- $$y - 2 = 7(x - 9)$$: This swaps the x- and y-coordinates of the point of tangency.
- $$y - 9 = 4(x - 2)$$: This slope drops the derivative of $$3x$$.
