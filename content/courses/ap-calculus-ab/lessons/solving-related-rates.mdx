Harder related-rates problems need a strategy: a diagram, an equation, and sometimes a trick to remove an unwanted variable before differentiating.

## A six-step strategy

1. **Draw** a diagram and label the quantities that change with variables.
2. **List** what is given (values and rates) and what is asked.
3. **Write** an equation relating the quantities (Pythagorean theorem, area, volume, similar triangles).
4. **Eliminate** any variable whose rate you do not know, if needed.
5. **Differentiate** with respect to $$t$$.
6. **Substitute** the values at the instant in question and solve, with units.

## Ladders and right triangles

A ladder of length $$L$$ against a wall: $$x^2 + y^2 = L^2$$. Differentiating gives $$x\frac{dx}{dt} + y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$$. When the base slides out ($$\frac{dx}{dt} > 0$$), the top slides down ($$\frac{dy}{dt} < 0$$).

## Cones and similar triangles

Water in an inverted cone has radius $$r$$ and depth $$h$$, both changing. If the full cone has top radius $$R$$ and height $$H$$, similar triangles give $$r = \frac{R}{H}h$$. Substitute this into $$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$$ so only $$h$$ remains, then differentiate.

<Note title="Signs carry meaning">
A quantity that is decreasing has a negative rate. Use the sign in your answer and say in words what it means, for example "the top of the ladder is sliding down at 0.75 ft/s".
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Filling a conical tank">
Water flows into an inverted cone (top radius 4 m, height 8 m) at 2 cubic meters per minute. How fast is the depth rising when the water is 4 m deep?

1. Similar triangles: $$r = \frac{4}{8}h = \frac{h}{2}$$.
2. $$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi\left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 h = \frac{\pi}{12}h^3$$.
3. Differentiate: $$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{\pi}{4}h^2\frac{dh}{dt}$$.
4. Substitute: $$2 = \frac{\pi}{4}(16)\frac{dh}{dt} = 4\pi\frac{dh}{dt}$$.

**Answer:** $$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \approx 0.159$$ meters per minute.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The edge length of a cube is increasing at a constant rate of 2 centimeters per second. At the instant when the edge length is 6 centimeters, what is the rate of change of the volume?

**Answer:** $$216$$

**Why:** Differentiate $$V = s^3$$ with respect to $$t$$: $$\frac{dV}{dt} = 3s^2\frac{ds}{dt}$$.

**Working:** $$\frac{dV}{dt} = 3s^2\frac{ds}{dt}$$. Substitute the dimension $$6$$ and its rate $$2$$ to get $$216$$ (cubic or square centimeters per second).

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$108$$: This leaves out a factor from differentiating the formula with respect to time.
- $$432$$: This forgets to multiply by the given rate of change of the dimension.
- $$36$$: This substitutes into the original formula instead of its derivative.

### Worked exam question 2

The radius of a circle is increasing at a constant rate of 2 centimeters per second. At the instant when the radius is 3 centimeters, what is the rate of change of the area?

**Answer:** $$12\pi$$

**Why:** Differentiate $$A = \pi r^2$$ with respect to $$t$$: $$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\frac{dr}{dt}$$.

**Working:** $$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\frac{dr}{dt}$$. Substitute the dimension $$3$$ and its rate $$2$$ to get $$12\pi$$ (cubic or square centimeters per second).

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$18\pi$$: This leaves out a factor from differentiating the formula with respect to time.
- $$6\pi$$: This forgets to multiply by the given rate of change of the dimension.
- $$2\pi$$: This substitutes into the original formula instead of its derivative.

**Q.** A 5-foot ladder leans against a vertical wall. The bottom of the ladder slides away from the wall at a constant rate of 3 feet per second. How fast is the top of the ladder moving, in feet per second, when the bottom is 3 feet from the wall?

**A.** $$-\frac{9}{4}$$. $$x^2 + y^2 = 25$$ gives $$x\frac{dx}{dt} + y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$$. With $$x = 3$$, $$y = 4$$, $$\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{3(3)}{4}$$. Let $$x$$ be the distance from the wall and $$y$$ the height. $$2x x' + 2y y' = 0$$, so $$y' = -\frac{x x'}{y} = -\frac{3 \cdot 3}{4} = -\frac{9}{4}$$ feet per second (the top is moving down).
