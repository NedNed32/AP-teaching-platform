A function is **discontinuous** at a point when you cannot draw its graph through that point without lifting your pencil. There are three ways this can happen, and each one has a different limit behavior.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/discontinuity-types.svg" alt="Three pieces of one graph: on the left, a line with a hole at x = -3 and a separate dot above it; in the middle, a horizontal segment that jumps at x = -1; on the right, a curve with a vertical asymptote at x = 3." caption="Removable at x = −3, jump at x = −1, infinite at x = 3." />

## The three types

| Type | One-sided limits | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Removable** | Both exist and are equal | A hole, possibly with a dot elsewhere |
| **Jump** | Both exist but differ | The graph jumps to a new height |
| **Infinite** | At least one is $$\pm\infty$$ | A vertical asymptote |

A fourth, rarer kind is an **oscillating** discontinuity, like $$\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$$ at 0, where the values never settle.

## Recognizing them from formulas

- **Rational functions:** factor. A factor that cancels from the denominator gives a **removable** discontinuity; a factor that remains gives an **infinite** one.
- **Piecewise functions:** compare the left and right pieces at the break. Different limits give a **jump**.

<Note title="Why the name removable">
A removable discontinuity can be fixed by redefining the function at a single point to equal the limit. A jump or infinite discontinuity cannot be repaired that way (topic 1.13).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying from a formula">
Classify the discontinuities of $$f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - x - 6}$$.

1. Factor: $$\frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 2)}$$.
2. At $$x = 3$$ the factor cancels; the simplified form $$\frac{x + 3}{x + 2}$$ gives the limit $$\frac{6}{5}$$. **Removable.**
3. At $$x = -2$$ the denominator is 0 but the numerator is $$1$$. **Infinite** (vertical asymptote).

**Answer:** removable at $$x = 3$$, infinite at $$x = -2$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Let $$f(x) = \frac{x^{2} + 2x - 3}{x^{2} + x - 2}$$. Which of the following lists every vertical asymptote of the graph of $$f$$?

**Answer:** $$x = -2$$

**Why:** The factor $$x - 1$$ cancels, leaving a hole at $$x = 1$$. Only $$x = -2$$ makes the simplified denominator zero.

**Working:** Factor: $$f(x) = \frac{(x - 1)(x + 3)}{(x - 1)(x + 2)}$$. The common factor gives a hole at $$x = 1$$; near $$x = -2$$ the denominator approaches 0 while the numerator does not, so $$x = -2$$ is the only vertical asymptote.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$x = 1 \text{ and } x = -2$$: At $$x = 1$$ the numerator is also zero; the common factor cancels, giving a removable discontinuity (a hole), not an asymptote.
- $$x = 1$$: $$x = 1$$ is a hole, because its factor cancels.
- $$x = -3$$: $$x = -3$$ is a zero of the numerator, so it is an x-intercept, not an asymptote.
