$$\Phi_B = \int \vec B \cdot d\vec A \qquad \Phi_B = BA\cos\theta\ \text{(uniform field)}$$

For a nonuniform field, such as near a long wire, integrate over the area. A rectangle of length $$L$$ from distance $$a$$ to $$b$$ beside a wire:

$$\Phi = \int_a^b \frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}L\,dr = \frac{\mu_0IL}{2\pi}\ln\frac{b}{a}$$

<Note title="Ways to change flux">
Flux changes if B, the area, or the angle between them changes. Each is a way to induce an emf.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Flux through a coil">
A 0.30 T field passes through a 0.020 m² loop at 30° to the normal. Find the flux.

1. $$\Phi = 0.30(0.020)\cos 30°$$.

**Answer:** about $$5.2 \times 10^{-3}$$ Wb.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 100-turn coil of area 0.18 m² is perpendicular to a magnetic field that changes by 1 T in 0.3 s. What is the magnitude of the average induced emf?

**Answer:** $$60\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** ε = N ΔΦ/Δt.

**Working:** ε = 100(0.18)(1)/0.3 = 60 V.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.6\ \mathrm{V}$$: Multiply by the number of turns.
- $$5.4\ \mathrm{V}$$: Divide by the time.
- $$333\ \mathrm{V}$$: Flux includes the area.

### Worked exam question 2

A rectangular loop of length 0.9 m lies in the plane of a long straight wire carrying 18 A, parallel to it. Its near and far sides are 0.05 m and 0.11 m from the wire. What is the magnetic flux through the loop?

**Answer:** $$2.55 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{Wb}$$

**Why:** Φ = ∫(μ₀I/2πr)L dr = (μ₀IL/2π) ln(b/a).

**Working:** Φ = (μ₀IL/2π) ln(0.11/0.05) = 2.55 \times 10^\{-6\} Wb.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3.89 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{Wb}$$: The field is not uniform across the loop.
- $$1.94 \times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{Wb}$$: Integrate 1/r to get a logarithm.
- $$4.86 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{Wb}$$: Integrate across the loop.
