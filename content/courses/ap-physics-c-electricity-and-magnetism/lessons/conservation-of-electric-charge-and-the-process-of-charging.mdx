Charge is **conserved**: it is transferred, never created or destroyed.

- **Friction**: electrons transfer between materials.
- **Conduction**: a charged object touches a conductor and shares charge. Identical conducting spheres share charge equally.
- **Induction**: a nearby charge pushes charges within a conductor; grounding then lets charge flow, leaving the conductor with the opposite sign.

Neutral objects are attracted to charged ones through **polarization**.

<Note title="Ground">
Earth acts as a huge reservoir of charge: connecting a conductor to ground lets charge flow on or off until the conductor's potential is zero.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Charging by induction">
A negative rod is held near a neutral metal sphere, the sphere is grounded briefly, then the ground and rod are removed. What is the sphere's charge?

1. The rod repels electrons, which flow to ground.
2. With the ground removed first, the sphere keeps a deficit of electrons.

**Answer:** positive.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Two identical small conducting spheres carry 5 µC and -6 µC. They are touched together and separated. What is the charge on each?

**Answer:** -0.5 µC

**Why:** Identical conductors share the total charge equally.

**Working:** Total -1 µC, split equally: -0.5 µC each.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- -1 µC: The total is shared between two spheres.
- 5.5 µC: Add the charges with their signs.
- 5 µC and -6 µC: Charge flows between touching conductors.
