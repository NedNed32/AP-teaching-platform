$$\Delta V = IR \qquad R = \frac{\rho L}{A}$$

Resistance rises with length and falls with cross-sectional area. Resistivity depends on the material and usually increases with temperature for metals.

For a material with varying cross section or resistivity, integrate: $$R = \int \frac{\rho\,dx}{A(x)}$$.

<Note title="Graph test">
An I–V graph of an ohmic device is a straight line through the origin with slope 1/R.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Copper wire">
Find the resistance of 20 m of copper wire (ρ = 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m) with a cross-sectional area of 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ m².

1. $$R = (1.7 \times 10^{-8})(20)/(1.0 \times 10^{-6})$$.

**Answer:** 0.34 Ω.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A wire 45 m long has a diameter of 0.7 mm and resistivity $$1 \times 10^{-7}$$ Ω·m. What is its resistance?

**Answer:** $$11.7\ \mathrm{\Omega}$$

**Why:** R = ρL/A with A = π(d/2)².

**Working:** A = 3.85 \times 10^\{-7\} m²; R = 11.7 Ω.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.92\ \mathrm{\Omega}$$: Use the radius, d/2.
- $$8.55 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{\Omega}$$: R = ρL/A.
- $$1.17 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{\Omega}$$: Convert mm to m.

### Free-response practice: Measuring resistivity

Students measure the resistance of different lengths of a wire with a diameter of 0.5 mm, using a battery, an ammeter and a voltmeter.

*Resistance versus length*

| Length L (m) | Resistance R (Ω) |
| --- | --- |
| 0.2 | 1.1 |
| 0.4 | 2.27 |
| 0.6 | 3.36 |
| 0.8 | 4.43 |
| 1 | 5.7 |

**(a)** Describe (draw) how the ammeter and voltmeter should be connected, and how R is determined.

To earn the point: Ammeter in series with the wire; voltmeter in parallel across it. Model answer: R = V/I for each length.

To earn the point: Keep the current small or brief so the wire does not heat up. Model answer: Heating would change the resistivity.

**(b)** Plot (describe) R versus L and draw a best-fit line. Calculate its slope.

To earn the point: Linear relationship through (near) the origin. Model answer: R ∝ L

To earn the point: Slope. Model answer: about 5.68 Ω/m

**(c)** Use the slope to calculate the resistivity of the wire.

To earn the point: Uses slope = ρ/A with A = π(d/2)². Model answer: A = 1.96 \times 10^\{-7\} m²

To earn the point: Correct resistivity. Model answer: ρ ≈ 1.12 \times 10^\{-6\} Ω·m

**(d)** The students then use a wire of the same material with twice the diameter. Predict the new slope of R versus L and explain.

To earn the point: The slope becomes one quarter as large because the area quadruples. Model answer: about 1.42 Ω/m
