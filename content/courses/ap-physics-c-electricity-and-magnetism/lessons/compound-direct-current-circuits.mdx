$$R_{series} = R_1 + R_2 + \cdots \qquad \frac{1}{R_{parallel}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \cdots$$

## Strategy

1. Collapse combinations step by step to find $$R_{eq}$$.
2. Find the total current from the source.
3. Work backward, using $$V = IR$$, to find each branch's current and voltage.

Parallel resistance is always smaller than the smallest branch.

<Note title="Two-resistor shortcut">
For two resistors in parallel, R = R1R2/(R1 + R2).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Mixed circuit">
A 4 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel pair of 6 Ω and 3 Ω, across 18 V. Find the current in the 6 Ω resistor.

1. $$R_{eq} = 4 + 2 = 6$$ Ω; $$I = 3$$ A.
2. Parallel voltage: $$3 \times 2 = 6$$ V.
3. $$I_6 = 6/6 = 1$$ A.

**Answer:** 1 A.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 5 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel pair of 9 Ω and 11 Ω, connected to an ideal 16 V battery. What is the current from the battery?

**Answer:** $$1.61\ \mathrm{A}$$

**Why:** Reduce the parallel pair, add the series resistor, then work back.

**Working:** R_eq = 5 + 4.95 = 9.95 Ω; I = 1.61 A; I in 9 Ω = 0.884 A.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.64\ \mathrm{A}$$: R₂ and R₃ are in parallel.
- $$3.2\ \mathrm{A}$$: Use the equivalent resistance.
- $$3.23\ \mathrm{A}$$: Include R₁.

### Worked exam question 2

A 5 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel pair of 5 Ω and 4 Ω, connected to an ideal 9 V battery. What is the equivalent resistance?

**Answer:** $$7.22\ \mathrm{\Omega}$$

**Why:** Reduce the parallel pair, add the series resistor, then work back.

**Working:** R_eq = 5 + 2.22 = 7.22 Ω; I = 1.25 A; I in 5 Ω = 0.554 A.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$14\ \mathrm{\Omega}$$: R₂ and R₃ are in parallel.
- $$1.54\ \mathrm{\Omega}$$: R₁ is in series.
- $$9.5\ \mathrm{\Omega}$$: Parallel: R₂R₃/(R₂ + R₃).

### Worked exam question 3

A 8 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel pair of 10 Ω and 7 Ω, connected to an ideal 23 V battery. What is the current in the 10 Ω resistor?

**Answer:** $$0.782\ \mathrm{A}$$

**Why:** Reduce the parallel pair, add the series resistor, then work back.

**Working:** R_eq = 8 + 4.12 = 12.1 Ω; I = 1.9 A; I in 10 Ω = 0.782 A.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.949\ \mathrm{A}$$: The branches have different resistances.
- $$2.3\ \mathrm{A}$$: Only part of the battery's voltage is across the pair.
- $$1.12\ \mathrm{A}$$: That is the other branch.
