$$C = \frac{Q}{\Delta V} \qquad C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} \qquad U = \tfrac12 C(\Delta V)^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C}$$

| Arrangement | Equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| parallel | $$C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2$$ |
| series | $$\frac{1}{C_{eq}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2}$$ |

Capacitors in series carry the same charge; in parallel they share the same voltage.

The energy is stored in the field, with density $$u = \tfrac12\varepsilon_0E^2$$.

<Note title="Connected or isolated">
If a capacitor stays connected to a battery, V is fixed. If it is disconnected, Q is fixed. Decide which before predicting changes.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Pulling plates apart">
A charged capacitor is disconnected, then its plate separation is doubled. What happens to its energy?

1. Q is fixed; C halves.
2. $$U = Q^2/(2C)$$ doubles.

**Answer:** the energy doubles (the work done pulling the plates apart).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A parallel-plate capacitor has plates of area 0.14 m² separated by 4 mm of air. What is its capacitance?

**Answer:** $$3.1 \times 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{F}$$

**Why:** C = κε₀A/d.

**Working:** C = (8.85 × 10⁻¹²)(0.14)/(0.004) = 3.1 \times 10^\{-10\} F.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.53 \times 10^{-13}\ \mathrm{F}$$: C = ε₀A/d.
- $$3.1 \times 10^{-13}\ \mathrm{F}$$: Convert mm to m.
- $$7.74 \times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{F}$$: C ∝ 1/d.

### Worked exam question 2

A 5 µF capacitor is in series with a parallel combination of 6 µF and 4 µF. What is the equivalent capacitance?

**Answer:** 3.33 µF

**Why:** Capacitors add in parallel; reciprocals add in series.

**Working:** C_eq = 3.33 µF.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 15 µF: Not all the capacitors are in parallel.
- 7.4 µF: The series and parallel rules are swapped (they are the reverse of resistors).
- 1.62 µF: Not all the capacitors are in series.

### Worked exam question 3

A 3 µF capacitor is charged to 60 V. How much energy does it store?

**Answer:** $$5.4 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{J}$$

**Why:** U = ½CV².

**Working:** U = ½(3 × 10⁻⁶)(60)² = 5.4 \times 10^\{-3\} J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.0108\ \mathrm{J}$$: U = ½CV².
- $$9 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{J}$$: Square the voltage.
- $$5400\ \mathrm{J}$$: Convert µF to F.

**Q.** A parallel-plate capacitor is charged and remains connected to the battery. A dielectric with κ = 2 is inserted to fill the gap. What happens to the capacitor's stored energy?

**A.** It is multiplied by 2. With the battery connected, V is fixed and C rises by κ. C becomes κC. V fixed: Q = CV and U = ½CV² rise by κ.

**Q.** A parallel-plate capacitor is charged and remains connected to the battery. A dielectric with κ = 5 is inserted to fill the gap. What happens to the capacitor's voltage?

**A.** It is unchanged. With the battery connected, V is fixed and C rises by κ. C becomes κC. V fixed: Q = CV and U = ½CV² rise by κ.

### Free-response practice: Charging a capacitor

An initially uncharged 8 µF capacitor, a 20 kΩ resistor, an ideal 11 V battery and an open switch are connected in series. The switch is closed at t = 0.

**(a)** Write the loop equation for the circuit and solve it to find q(t).

To earn the point: Correct differential equation. Model answer: $$\varepsilon - R\frac{dq}{dt} - \frac{q}{C} = 0$$

To earn the point: Correct solution. Model answer: $$q(t) = C\varepsilon\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$$

**(b)** Calculate the time constant, the initial current and the final charge on the capacitor.

To earn the point: Time constant. Model answer: τ = RC = 0.16 s

To earn the point: Initial current. Model answer: I₀ = ε/R = 5.5 \times 10^\{-4\} A

To earn the point: Final charge. Model answer: Q = Cε = 8.8 \times 10^\{-5\} C

**(c)** Sketch (describe) graphs of the capacitor's voltage and the current versus time.

To earn the point: Capacitor voltage rises from 0 toward ε, concave down. Model answer: Approaches 11 V.

To earn the point: Current decays exponentially from I₀ toward 0. Model answer: About 37% of I₀ at t = τ.

**(d)** Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor after a long time, and compare it with the energy supplied by the battery.

To earn the point: Stored energy. Model answer: U = ½Cε² = 4.84 \times 10^\{-4\} J

To earn the point: The battery supplies Qε, twice as much; half is dissipated in the resistor. Model answer: 9.68 \times 10^\{-4\} J supplied
