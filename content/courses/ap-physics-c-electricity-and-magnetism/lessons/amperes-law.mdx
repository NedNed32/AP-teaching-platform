$$\oint \vec B \cdot d\vec l = \mu_0I_{enc}$$

Like Gauss's law, it is always true but useful only with symmetry.

| Source | Amperian loop | Field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| long wire, outside | circle | $$\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$$ |
| long solid wire, inside | circle | $$\frac{\mu_0Ir}{2\pi R^2}$$ |
| solenoid ($$n$$ turns per meter) | rectangle | $$\mu_0nI$$ inside, about 0 outside |
| toroid | circle | $$\frac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi r}$$ |

<Note title="Enclosed current">
For a nonuniform current density J(r), find I_enc = ∫J 2πr dr.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Solenoid">
A solenoid with 2000 turns per meter carries 1.5 A. Find B inside.

1. $$B = (4\pi \times 10^{-7})(2000)(1.5)$$.

**Answer:** about $$3.8 \times 10^{-3}$$ T.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A long straight wire carries 7 A. What is the magnetic field 0.08 m from it?

**Answer:** $$1.75 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$

**Why:** B = μ₀I/(2πr).

**Working:** B = (4π × 10⁻⁷)(7)/(2π·0.08) = 1.75 \times 10^\{-5\} T.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$5.5 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$: That is the field at the center of a loop.
- $$1.09 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{T}$$: A long wire's field falls off as 1/r.
- $$4.42 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{T}$$: B = μ₀I/(2πr).

### Worked exam question 2

A solenoid 0.2 m long has 1200 turns and carries 6 A. What is the magnetic field inside it?

**Answer:** $$0.0452\ \mathrm{T}$$

**Why:** B = μ₀nI with n = N/L.

**Working:** B = μ₀(N/L)I = 0.0452 T.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$9.05 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{T}$$: Use turns per meter, N/L.
- $$7.2 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{T}$$: That resembles a wire's field; use B = μ₀nI.
- $$3.77 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$: Include the number of turns.
