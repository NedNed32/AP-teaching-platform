**Charging** (emf ε, through R):

$$\varepsilon - IR - \frac{q}{C} = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad q(t) = C\varepsilon\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right) \qquad I(t) = \frac{\varepsilon}{R}e^{-t/RC}$$

**Discharging**:

$$q(t) = Q_0e^{-t/RC} \qquad I(t) = \frac{Q_0}{RC}e^{-t/RC}$$

After one time constant, a charging capacitor has 63% of its final charge; a discharging one has 37% left. After about 5τ, it is effectively complete.

<Note title="Limits first">
At t = 0 an uncharged capacitor is a short circuit; at t = ∞ it is an open circuit. These limits answer many questions without solving the equation.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Charging">
A 2.0 µF capacitor charges through 500 kΩ from a 9.0 V battery. Find τ and the voltage after 2.0 s.

1. $$\tau = RC = 1.0$$ s.
2. $$V = 9.0(1 - e^{-2}) = 9.0(0.865)$$.

**Answer:** τ = 1.0 s; about 7.8 V.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 17 µF capacitor starts uncharged and is charged through a 70 kΩ resistor by a 6 V battery. What is the capacitor's voltage after 0.595 s?

**Answer:** $$2.36\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** τ = RC = 1.19 s; V = ε(1 − e^(−t/τ)).

**Working:** τ = 1.19 s; V = 2.36 V.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3.64\ \mathrm{V}$$: That is the charging resistor's voltage.
- $$3\ \mathrm{V}$$: The change is exponential, not linear.
- $$3 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{V}$$: Convert kΩ and µF to Ω and F.

### Free-response practice: Charging a capacitor

An initially uncharged 8 µF capacitor, a 20 kΩ resistor, an ideal 11 V battery and an open switch are connected in series. The switch is closed at t = 0.

**(a)** Write the loop equation for the circuit and solve it to find q(t).

To earn the point: Correct differential equation. Model answer: $$\varepsilon - R\frac{dq}{dt} - \frac{q}{C} = 0$$

To earn the point: Correct solution. Model answer: $$q(t) = C\varepsilon\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)$$

**(b)** Calculate the time constant, the initial current and the final charge on the capacitor.

To earn the point: Time constant. Model answer: τ = RC = 0.16 s

To earn the point: Initial current. Model answer: I₀ = ε/R = 5.5 \times 10^\{-4\} A

To earn the point: Final charge. Model answer: Q = Cε = 8.8 \times 10^\{-5\} C

**(c)** Sketch (describe) graphs of the capacitor's voltage and the current versus time.

To earn the point: Capacitor voltage rises from 0 toward ε, concave down. Model answer: Approaches 11 V.

To earn the point: Current decays exponentially from I₀ toward 0. Model answer: About 37% of I₀ at t = τ.

**(d)** Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor after a long time, and compare it with the energy supplied by the battery.

To earn the point: Stored energy. Model answer: U = ½Cε² = 4.84 \times 10^\{-4\} J

To earn the point: The battery supplies Qε, twice as much; half is dissipated in the resistor. Model answer: 9.68 \times 10^\{-4\} J supplied
