$$C = \kappa\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d}$$

The dielectric's molecules polarize, creating an internal field that opposes the applied field. For the same charge, the field and voltage are reduced by $$\kappa$$, so capacitance rises by $$\kappa$$.

| Situation | Effect of inserting a dielectric |
| --- | --- |
| connected to battery (V fixed) | Q and U increase by κ |
| isolated (Q fixed) | V and U decrease by a factor of κ |

<Note title="Breakdown">
Dielectrics also allow higher voltages before sparks jump the gap.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Isolated capacitor">
An isolated 4.0 µF capacitor holds 20 µC. A dielectric with κ = 2.5 is inserted. Find the new voltage.

1. $$C = 10$$ µF.
2. $$V = 20/10 = 2.0$$ V.

**Answer:** 2.0 V (down from 5.0 V).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A parallel-plate capacitor is charged and remains connected to the battery. A dielectric with κ = 2 is inserted to fill the gap. What happens to the capacitor's stored energy?

**A.** It is multiplied by 2. With the battery connected, V is fixed and C rises by κ. C becomes κC. V fixed: Q = CV and U = ½CV² rise by κ.

**Q.** A parallel-plate capacitor is charged and remains connected to the battery. A dielectric with κ = 5 is inserted to fill the gap. What happens to the capacitor's voltage?

**A.** It is unchanged. With the battery connected, V is fixed and C rises by κ. C becomes κC. V fixed: Q = CV and U = ½CV² rise by κ.

**Q.** A parallel-plate capacitor is charged and then disconnected from the battery. A dielectric with κ = 2 is inserted to fill the gap. What happens to the capacitor's charge?

**A.** It is unchanged. Disconnected, Q is fixed and C rises by κ. C becomes κC. Q fixed: V = Q/C and U = Q²/(2C) fall by κ.
