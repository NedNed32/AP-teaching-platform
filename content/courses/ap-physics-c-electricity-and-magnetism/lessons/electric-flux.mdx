$$\Phi_E = \int \vec E \cdot d\vec A \qquad \Phi_E = EA\cos\theta\ \text{(uniform field, flat surface)}$$

$$\theta$$ is the angle between $$\vec E$$ and the surface's normal. For closed surfaces, the normal points outward: field lines leaving count as positive flux, entering as negative.

<Note title="Counting lines">
Flux is proportional to the number of field lines crossing a surface.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Tilted surface">
A uniform field of 300 N/C passes through a 0.20 m² square whose normal is at 60° to the field. Find the flux.

1. $$\Phi = 300(0.20)\cos 60°$$.

**Answer:** 30 N·m²/C.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A uniform field of 800 N/C passes through a flat 0.6 m² surface whose normal makes 30° with the field. What is the flux through the surface?

**Answer:** $$416\ \mathrm{N{\cdot}m^2/C}$$

**Why:** Φ = EA cos θ.

**Working:** Φ = 800(0.6) cos 30° = 416 N·m²/C.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$240\ \mathrm{N{\cdot}m^2/C}$$: θ is measured from the normal, so use cos θ.
- $$480\ \mathrm{N{\cdot}m^2/C}$$: Account for the angle.
- $$1330\ \mathrm{N{\cdot}m^2/C}$$: Flux is E times A.

### Worked exam question 2

A closed surface encloses 6 nC of charge. What is the net electric flux through it?

**Answer:** $$678\ \mathrm{N{\cdot}m^2/C}$$

**Why:** Gauss's law: Φ = q_enc/ε₀.

**Working:** Φ = q/ε₀ = 678 N·m²/C, whatever the surface's shape.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$5.31 \times 10^{-20}\ \mathrm{N{\cdot}m^2/C}$$: Divide by ε₀.
- $$53.9\ \mathrm{N{\cdot}m^2/C}$$: Φ = q/ε₀ = 4πkq.
- $$54\ \mathrm{N{\cdot}m^2/C}$$: Φ = q/ε₀, without 4π.
