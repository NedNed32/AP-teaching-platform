$$\sum I_{in} = \sum I_{out}$$

## Multi-loop circuits

1. Label a current in each branch.
2. Write junction equations.
3. Write loop equations for enough independent loops.
4. Solve the system.

<Note title="Checking">
Verify the answer by checking a loop you did not use.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Two sources">
In a circuit, junction A has I1 entering and I2, I3 leaving. Loop equations give I2 = 1.5 A and I3 = 0.5 A. Find I1.

1. $$I_1 = I_2 + I_3$$.

**Answer:** 2.0 A.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A single loop contains a 10 V battery and a 2 V battery connected so their emfs oppose each other, plus resistors of 3 Ω and 5 Ω. What is the current?

**Answer:** $$1\ \mathrm{A}$$

**Why:** Loop rule: ε₁ − ε₂ − I(R₁ + R₂) = 0.

**Working:** I = (10 − 2)/(3 + 5) = 1 A.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.5\ \mathrm{A}$$: The batteries oppose each other.
- $$1.25\ \mathrm{A}$$: Include the opposing battery.
- $$2.67\ \mathrm{A}$$: Both resistors are in the loop.
