$$\varepsilon = -N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$

The minus sign is **Lenz's law**: the induced current creates a field that opposes the change in flux (not the flux itself).

**Motional emf**: a rod of length $$L$$ sliding at speed $$v$$ on rails in a field $$B$$: $$\varepsilon = BLv$$.

A coil rotating in a field produces an alternating emf: $$\varepsilon = NBA\omega\sin\omega t$$ (a generator).

<Note title="Applying Lenz's law">
Ask: is the flux increasing or decreasing? The induced field points opposite to an increase, or along a decrease.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Changing field">
A 50-turn coil of area 0.040 m² sits in a field that rises from 0 to 0.50 T in 0.10 s. Find the emf.

1. $$\varepsilon = 50 \times 0.040 \times 0.50/0.10$$.

**Answer:** 10 V.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 100-turn coil of area 0.18 m² is perpendicular to a magnetic field that changes by 1 T in 0.3 s. What is the magnitude of the average induced emf?

**Answer:** $$60\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** ε = N ΔΦ/Δt.

**Working:** ε = 100(0.18)(1)/0.3 = 60 V.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.6\ \mathrm{V}$$: Multiply by the number of turns.
- $$5.4\ \mathrm{V}$$: Divide by the time.
- $$333\ \mathrm{V}$$: Flux includes the area.

### Worked exam question 2

A 0.3 m conducting rod slides at 1 m/s along rails connected by a 12 Ω resistor, perpendicular to a uniform 0.7 T field. What is the induced current?

**Answer:** $$0.0175\ \mathrm{A}$$

**Why:** ε = BLv, I = ε/R, F = ILB = B²L²v/R.

**Working:** ε = 0.21 V, I = 0.0175 A, F = 3.67 \times 10^\{-3\} N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.52\ \mathrm{A}$$: Divide the emf by R.
- $$0.0583\ \mathrm{A}$$: ε = BLv includes L.
- $$0.21\ \mathrm{A}$$: That is the emf, in volts.

### Worked exam question 3

A 0.6 m conducting rod slides at 15 m/s along rails connected by a 2 Ω resistor, perpendicular to a uniform 0.8 T field. What is the force needed to keep the rod moving at constant speed?

**Answer:** $$1.73\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** ε = BLv, I = ε/R, F = ILB = B²L²v/R.

**Working:** ε = 7.2 V, I = 3.6 A, F = 1.73 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3.6\ \mathrm{N}$$: That is the current; F = ILB.
- $$5.76\ \mathrm{N}$$: F = B²L²v/R.
- $$2.16\ \mathrm{N}$$: F = B²L²v/R.

### Worked exam question 4

A 0.7 m conducting rod slides at 3 m/s along rails connected by a 2 Ω resistor, perpendicular to a uniform 0.5 T field. What is the induced emf?

**Answer:** $$1.05\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** ε = BLv, I = ε/R, F = ILB = B²L²v/R.

**Working:** ε = 1.05 V, I = 0.525 A, F = 0.184 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.14\ \mathrm{V}$$: ε = BLv.
- $$2.1\ \mathrm{V}$$: That is not the emf; ε = BLv.
- $$0.35\ \mathrm{V}$$: Include the speed.

### Free-response practice: A sliding rod in a magnetic field

A conducting rod of mass 0.2 kg and length 0.4 m slides without friction on horizontal rails connected by a 5 Ω resistor. A uniform 0.9 T magnetic field points vertically through the rails. The rod is given an initial speed of 10 m/s and then released.

**(a)** Describe qualitatively how the rod's speed changes after it is released, and explain using Lenz's law.

To earn the point: The rod slows down, approaching zero. Model answer: Its speed decreases ever more slowly.

To earn the point: The induced current produces a magnetic force opposing the motion (Lenz's law). Model answer: The force opposes the change in flux.

**(b)** Derive an expression for the magnetic force on the rod in terms of B, L, v and R.

To earn the point: Uses ε = BLv and I = ε/R. Model answer: $$I = \frac{BLv}{R}$$

To earn the point: Correct force. Model answer: $$F = ILB = \frac{B^2L^2v}{R}$$

**(c)** Write and solve a differential equation for v(t).

To earn the point: Newton's second law. Model answer: $$m\frac{dv}{dt} = -\frac{B^2L^2}{R}v$$

To earn the point: Exponential solution. Model answer: $$v = v_0e^{-t/\tau}$$, $$\tau = \frac{mR}{B^2L^2}$$ = 7.72 s

**(d)** Calculate the initial current, and explain where the rod's initial kinetic energy ends up.

To earn the point: Initial current. Model answer: I₀ = BLv₀/R = 0.72 A

To earn the point: All of the kinetic energy is dissipated as thermal energy in the resistor. Model answer: 10 J
