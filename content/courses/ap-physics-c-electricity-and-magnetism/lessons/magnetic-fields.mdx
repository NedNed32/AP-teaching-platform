Magnetic fields are produced by moving charges (currents) and by magnetic materials.

- Field lines run from north to south outside a magnet and form **closed loops**.
- There are no magnetic monopoles: the net magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero.
- A current loop acts as a **magnetic dipole**, with dipole moment $$\mu = IA$$.

Earth's field is about $$5 \times 10^{-5}$$ T.

<Note title="Compasses">
A compass needle aligns with the local magnetic field; its north pole points along the field direction.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Dipole moment">
A 0.050 m radius loop carries 2.0 A. Find its magnetic dipole moment.

1. $$\mu = IA = 2.0\pi(0.050)^2$$.

**Answer:** about $$1.6 \times 10^{-2}$$ A·m².
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

A proton moves at $$1 \times 10^{5}$$ m/s perpendicular to a uniform 0.11 T magnetic field. What is the radius of its path?

**Answer:** $$9.49 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** qvB = mv²/r, so r = mv/(qB).

**Working:** r = mv/(qB) = 9.49 \times 10^\{-3\} m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$105\ \mathrm{m}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$949\ \mathrm{m}$$: r = mv/(qB).
- $$5.18 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m}$$: Use the proton's mass.

### Worked exam question 2

A long straight wire carries 7 A. What is the magnetic field 0.08 m from it?

**Answer:** $$1.75 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$

**Why:** B = μ₀I/(2πr).

**Working:** B = (4π × 10⁻⁷)(7)/(2π·0.08) = 1.75 \times 10^\{-5\} T.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$5.5 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$: That is the field at the center of a loop.
- $$1.09 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{T}$$: A long wire's field falls off as 1/r.
- $$4.42 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{T}$$: B = μ₀I/(2πr).

### Worked exam question 3

Two long parallel wires 0.04 m apart carry 15 A and 2 A in the same direction. What is the force per meter between them?

**Answer:** $$1.5 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N/m}$$, and the wires attract

**Why:** F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd); parallel currents attract.

**Working:** F/L = 1.5 \times 10^\{-4\} N/m; the wires attract.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.5 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N/m}$$, and the wires repel: Parallel currents attract; antiparallel currents repel.
- $$3.75 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{N/m}$$, and the wires attract: The force per length falls off as 1/d.
- $$7.5 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{N/m}$$, and the wires attract: F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd).
