$$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2} \qquad k = 8.99 \times 10^9\ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$$

Like charges repel; opposite charges attract. The force acts along the line joining the charges.

With several charges, find each force separately and add them as **vectors** (components). Symmetry often cancels some components.

Charge is quantized in units of $$e = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}$$ C.

<Note title="Electric versus gravitational">
The electric force between two protons is about 10³⁶ times their gravitational attraction, which is why gravity is ignored in most electrostatics problems.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Two charges">
Find the force between +2.0 µC and −3.0 µC charges 0.30 m apart.

1. $$F = (8.99 \times 10^9)\frac{(2.0 \times 10^{-6})(3.0 \times 10^{-6})}{0.090}$$.

**Answer:** 0.60 N, attractive.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Two point charges of 7 µC and −5 µC are 0.43 m apart. What is the magnitude of the force between them?

**Answer:** $$1.7\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** F = k|q₁q₂|/r².

**Working:** F = (8.99 × 10⁹)(7 × 10⁻⁶)(5 × 10⁻⁶)/0.43² = 1.7 N, attractive.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.732\ \mathrm{N}$$: Coulomb's law uses r².
- $$1.7 \times 10^{12}\ \mathrm{N}$$: Convert µC to C.
- $$0.425\ \mathrm{N}$$: Use the full separation r.

### Worked exam question 2

Charges of +6 µC and +3 µC are 0.4 m apart. What is the magnitude of the electric field at the midpoint between them?

**Answer:** $$6.74 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{N/C}$$

**Why:** Like charges' fields point in opposite directions at the midpoint: subtract.

**Working:** Each field is k q/(d/2)²: 1.35 \times 10^\{6\} and 6.74 \times 10^\{5\} N/C, opposite, giving 6.74 \times 10^\{5\} N/C.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.02 \times 10^{6}\ \mathrm{N/C}$$: Check the directions of the two fields at the midpoint.
- $$5.06 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{N/C}$$: Each charge is d/2 from the midpoint.
- $$1.69 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{N/C}$$: The distance is d/2, so r² = d²/4.
