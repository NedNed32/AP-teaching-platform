$$I = \frac{dQ}{dt} \qquad Q = \int I\,dt$$

In a metal, electrons drift slowly (millimeters per second) opposite the conventional current direction:

$$I = nqAv_d$$

where $$n$$ is the number of carriers per volume and $$v_d$$ the drift speed.

<Note title="Fast signal, slow drift">
Lights turn on instantly because the electric field travels through the wire almost at the speed of light, even though each electron drifts slowly.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Charge from current">
Current through a wire is $$I(t) = 4t$$ A. How much charge passes from t = 0 to t = 3 s?

1. $$Q = \int_0^3 4t\,dt = 2t^2\big|_0^3$$.

**Answer:** 18 C.
</WorkedExample>
