$$V = \frac{U}{q} \qquad V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r} \qquad \Delta V = -\int \vec E \cdot d\vec r$$

Potential is a **scalar**: add the potentials of several charges with signs, no components needed.

A charge moving through potential difference $$\Delta V$$ changes its potential energy by $$q\Delta V$$. An electron accelerated through 1 V gains 1 eV = $$1.6 \times 10^{-19}$$ J.

<Note title="Zero field, nonzero potential">
At the midpoint between two equal positive charges, E = 0 but V is not zero.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Potential at a point">
Charges +3.0 nC and −1.0 nC are 0.20 m and 0.10 m from point P. Find V at P.

1. $$V = 8.99 \times 10^9\left(\frac{3.0 \times 10^{-9}}{0.20} - \frac{1.0 \times 10^{-9}}{0.10}\right)$$.
2. $$= 8.99 \times 10^9(5.0 \times 10^{-9}) = 45$$ V.

**Answer:** 45 V.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Point P is 0.2 m from a +1 µC charge and 0.3 m from a −5 µC charge. What is the electric potential at P?

**Answer:** $$-1.05 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** Potential is a scalar: add kq/r with signs.

**Working:** V = k(q₁/r₁ + q₂/r₂) = -1.05 \times 10^\{5\} V.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.95 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{V}$$: The negative charge contributes negative potential.
- $$-2.75 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{V}$$: Potential uses 1/r, not 1/r².
- $$1.05 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{V}$$: Check the signs.

### Worked exam question 2

How much work must an external agent do to move a +5 µC charge slowly from a point at 86 V to a point at 26 V?

**Answer:** $$-3 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{J}$$

**Why:** W_ext = qΔV = q(V_b − V_a).

**Working:** W = (5 × 10⁻⁶)(26 − 86) = -3 \times 10^\{-4\} J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{J}$$: ΔV is final minus initial.
- $$-300\ \mathrm{J}$$: Convert µC to C.
- $$5.6 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{J}$$: Use the difference in potential.
