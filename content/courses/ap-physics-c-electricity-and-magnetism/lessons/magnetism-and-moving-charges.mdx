$$\vec F = q\vec v \times \vec B \qquad F = |q|vB\sin\theta$$

Use the right-hand rule for positive charges; reverse it for negative charges.

In a uniform field perpendicular to the velocity, the charge moves in a circle:

$$|q|vB = \frac{mv^2}{r} \quad\Rightarrow\quad r = \frac{mv}{|q|B} \qquad T = \frac{2\pi m}{|q|B}$$

**Velocity selector**: crossed fields pass undeflected charges with $$v = E/B$$.

<Note title="No work">
Because F is perpendicular to v, a magnetic field changes only the direction of motion, never the kinetic energy.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Proton orbit">
A proton moves at $$2.0 \times 10^6$$ m/s perpendicular to a 0.40 T field. Find the radius.

1. $$r = \frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27})(2.0 \times 10^6)}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19})(0.40)}$$.

**Answer:** about 0.052 m.
</WorkedExample>

### Worked exam question 1

A proton moves at $$1 \times 10^{5}$$ m/s perpendicular to a uniform 0.11 T magnetic field. What is the radius of its path?

**Answer:** $$9.49 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** qvB = mv²/r, so r = mv/(qB).

**Working:** r = mv/(qB) = 9.49 \times 10^\{-3\} m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$105\ \mathrm{m}$$: The ratio is inverted.
- $$949\ \mathrm{m}$$: r = mv/(qB).
- $$5.18 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m}$$: Use the proton's mass.
