$$\varepsilon_L = -L\frac{dI}{dt} \qquad L_{solenoid} = \frac{\mu_0N^2A}{\ell} \qquad U = \tfrac12 LI^2$$

An inductor resists changes in current, the way mass resists changes in velocity. The energy is stored in the magnetic field, with density $$u = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$$.

<Note title="Sparks">
Suddenly breaking a circuit with an inductor makes dI/dt huge, producing a large emf that can cause a spark.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Back emf">
Current in a 0.20 H inductor drops from 3.0 A to 0 in 0.050 s. Find the average emf.

1. $$\varepsilon = 0.20 \times 3.0/0.050$$.

**Answer:** 12 V.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The current in a 0.4 H inductor changes by 8 A in 0.19 s. What is the magnitude of the average induced emf?

**Answer:** $$16.8\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** ε = L ΔI/Δt.

**Working:** ε = 0.4(8)/0.19 = 16.8 V.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.608\ \mathrm{V}$$: Divide by Δt.
- $$105\ \mathrm{V}$$: ε = L dI/dt.
- $$67.4\ \mathrm{V}$$: That is power from energy; use ε = L dI/dt.

### Worked exam question 2

A 0.38 H inductor carries 2 A. How much energy is stored in its magnetic field?

**Answer:** $$0.76\ \mathrm{J}$$

**Why:** U = ½LI².

**Working:** U = ½(0.38)(2)² = 0.76 J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.52\ \mathrm{J}$$: U = ½LI².
- $$0.38\ \mathrm{J}$$: Square the current.
- $$2\ \mathrm{J}$$: Include L.
