Switch closed at $$t = 0$$ (emf ε, resistance R, inductance L):

$$\varepsilon - IR - L\frac{dI}{dt} = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad I(t) = \frac{\varepsilon}{R}\left(1 - e^{-tR/L}\right)$$

When the source is removed, the current decays: $$I = I_0e^{-tR/L}$$.

| Time | Capacitor | Inductor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| just after switching | acts like a wire | acts like a break |
| long after | acts like a break | acts like a wire |

<Note title="Continuity">
Current through an inductor cannot change instantaneously, just as voltage across a capacitor cannot.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Current after one time constant">
A 12 V battery, 4.0 Ω resistor and 2.0 H inductor are connected. Find τ and I after 0.50 s.

1. $$\tau = 2.0/4.0 = 0.50$$ s.
2. $$I = 3.0(1 - e^{-1}) = 3.0(0.632)$$.

**Answer:** τ = 0.50 s; about 1.9 A.
</WorkedExample>

### Worked exam question 1

An ideal 11 V battery is connected at $$t = 0$$ to a 11 Ω resistor and a 1.8 H inductor in series. What is the current at $$t = 0.164$$ s?

**Answer:** $$0.633\ \mathrm{A}$$

**Why:** τ = L/R = 0.164 s; I = (ε/R)(1 − e^(−t/τ)).

**Working:** I = (11/11)(1 − e^(−0.164/0.164)) = 0.633 A.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1\ \mathrm{A}$$: The current has not reached its final value.
- $$0.367\ \mathrm{A}$$: The current grows from zero.
- $$0.961\ \mathrm{A}$$: τ = L/R.
