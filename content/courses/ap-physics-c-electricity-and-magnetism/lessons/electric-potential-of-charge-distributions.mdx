$$V = \int \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{dq}{r}$$

| Distribution | Potential |
| --- | --- |
| ring, on axis | $$\frac{kQ}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}}$$ |
| conducting sphere, outside | $$\frac{kQ}{r}$$ |
| conducting sphere, inside | $$\frac{kQ}{R}$$ (constant) |

Alternatively, integrate the field from a reference point: $$V(r) = -\int_\infty^r \vec E \cdot d\vec r$$.

<Note title="Field from potential">
Once V is known as a function of position, E = −dV/dx gives the field.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Ring field from potential">
Use $$V(x) = \frac{kQ}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}}$$ to find E on the axis.

1. $$E = -\frac{dV}{dx} = \frac{kQx}{(x^2 + a^2)^{3/2}}$$.

**Answer:** $$\frac{kQx}{(x^2+a^2)^{3/2}}$$, matching the direct integral.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

Point P is 0.2 m from a +1 µC charge and 0.3 m from a −5 µC charge. What is the electric potential at P?

**Answer:** $$-1.05 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{V}$$

**Why:** Potential is a scalar: add kq/r with signs.

**Working:** V = k(q₁/r₁ + q₂/r₂) = -1.05 \times 10^\{5\} V.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.95 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{V}$$: The negative charge contributes negative potential.
- $$-2.75 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{V}$$: Potential uses 1/r, not 1/r².
- $$1.05 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{V}$$: Check the signs.

### Worked exam question 2

How much work must an external agent do to move a +5 µC charge slowly from a point at 86 V to a point at 26 V?

**Answer:** $$-3 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{J}$$

**Why:** W_ext = qΔV = q(V_b − V_a).

**Working:** W = (5 × 10⁻⁶)(26 − 86) = -3 \times 10^\{-4\} J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{J}$$: ΔV is final minus initial.
- $$-300\ \mathrm{J}$$: Convert µC to C.
- $$5.6 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{J}$$: Use the difference in potential.

### Free-response practice: A uniformly charged insulating sphere

A solid insulating sphere of radius R = 0.05 m has a total charge Q = +7 nC distributed uniformly throughout its volume.

**(a)** Using Gauss's law, derive expressions for the electric field magnitude inside (r &lt; R) and outside (r > R) the sphere.

To earn the point: Spherical Gaussian surface; enclosed charge inside is Q(r/R)³. Model answer: $$E(4\pi r^2) = \frac{Q r^3}{\varepsilon_0 R^3}$$

To earn the point: Correct inside expression. Model answer: $$E = \frac{Qr}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3}$$

To earn the point: Correct outside expression. Model answer: $$E = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}$$

**(b)** Calculate the field magnitude at r = 0.025 m and at r = 0.1 m.

To earn the point: Both values correct. Model answer: 1.26 \times 10^\{4\} N/C and 6290 N/C

**(c)** Sketch (describe) a graph of E versus r from 0 to 3R.

To earn the point: Linear increase from 0 to a maximum at R, then 1/r² decrease. Model answer: Peak value kQ/R² = 2.52 \times 10^\{4\} N/C at r = R.

**(d)** Calculate the electric potential at the surface and at the center (V = 0 at infinity).

To earn the point: Surface potential. Model answer: V(R) = kQ/R = 1260 V

To earn the point: Center potential by integrating the inside field. Model answer: $$V(0) = \frac{3kQ}{2R}$$ = 1890 V
