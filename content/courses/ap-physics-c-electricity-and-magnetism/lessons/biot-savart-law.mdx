$$d\vec B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{I\,d\vec l \times \hat r}{r^2} \qquad \mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\ \text{T·m/A}$$

| Source | Field |
| --- | --- |
| long straight wire | $$\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}$$ |
| center of a loop | $$\frac{\mu_0I}{2R}$$ |
| center of an arc of angle θ | $$\frac{\mu_0I\theta}{4\pi R}$$ |

Straight segments that point directly at the field point contribute nothing (dl is parallel to r).

<Note title="Right-hand rule for wires">
Point your thumb along the current: your fingers curl in the direction of the field.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Loop center">
A 0.10 m radius loop carries 5.0 A. Find B at its center.

1. $$B = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7})(5.0)}{2(0.10)}$$.

**Answer:** about $$3.1 \times 10^{-5}$$ T.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A long straight wire carries 7 A. What is the magnetic field 0.08 m from it?

**Answer:** $$1.75 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$

**Why:** B = μ₀I/(2πr).

**Working:** B = (4π × 10⁻⁷)(7)/(2π·0.08) = 1.75 \times 10^\{-5\} T.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$5.5 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$: That is the field at the center of a loop.
- $$1.09 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{T}$$: A long wire's field falls off as 1/r.
- $$4.42 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{T}$$: B = μ₀I/(2πr).

### Worked exam question 2

A flat circular coil of 22 turns and radius 0.18 m carries 1 A. What is the field at its center?

**Answer:** $$7.68 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$

**Why:** B = Nμ₀I/(2R).

**Working:** B = 22(4π × 10⁻⁷)(1)/(2·0.18) = 7.68 \times 10^\{-5\} T.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.44 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$$: That is a straight wire's field.
- $$3.49 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{T}$$: Include the number of turns.
- $$1.54 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{T}$$: B = μ₀I/(2R) per turn.
