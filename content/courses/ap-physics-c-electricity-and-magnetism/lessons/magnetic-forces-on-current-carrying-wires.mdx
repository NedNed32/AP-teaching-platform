$$\vec F = I\vec L \times \vec B \qquad F = ILB\sin\theta$$

For a curved wire, integrate $$d\vec F = I\,d\vec l \times \vec B$$.

**Parallel wires** (distance d): $$\frac{F}{L} = \frac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}$$; same-direction currents attract.

**Current loop**: $$\tau = \mu B\sin\theta$$ with $$\mu = NIA$$. This torque drives electric motors.

<Note title="Closed loop in a uniform field">
The net force on any closed current loop in a uniform field is zero, though there can be a torque.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Force on a wire">
A 0.50 m wire carrying 3.0 A is perpendicular to a 0.20 T field. Find the force.

1. $$F = 3.0(0.50)(0.20)$$.

**Answer:** 0.30 N.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Two long parallel wires 0.04 m apart carry 15 A and 2 A in the same direction. What is the force per meter between them?

**Answer:** $$1.5 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N/m}$$, and the wires attract

**Why:** F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd); parallel currents attract.

**Working:** F/L = 1.5 \times 10^\{-4\} N/m; the wires attract.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$1.5 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N/m}$$, and the wires repel: Parallel currents attract; antiparallel currents repel.
- $$3.75 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{N/m}$$, and the wires attract: The force per length falls off as 1/d.
- $$7.5 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{N/m}$$, and the wires attract: F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd).

### Worked exam question 2

A 0.7 m wire carrying 8 A makes a 30° angle with a uniform 1 T magnetic field. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on it?

**Answer:** $$2.8\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** F = ILB sin θ.

**Working:** F = 8(0.7)(1) sin 30° = 2.8 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$4.85\ \mathrm{N}$$: Use sin θ, from the cross product.
- $$5.6\ \mathrm{N}$$: Include the angle.
- $$11.4\ \mathrm{N}$$: F = ILB sin θ.
