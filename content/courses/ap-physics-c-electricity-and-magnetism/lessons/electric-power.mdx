$$P = I\Delta V = I^2R = \frac{(\Delta V)^2}{R}$$

Choose the form that uses what is shared: current in series, voltage in parallel.

Energy: $$E = Pt$$. Utilities bill in kilowatt-hours: $$1\ \text{kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6$$ J.

<Note title="Brightness">
A bulb's brightness depends on the power it dissipates, not on its resistance alone.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Heater">
A 120 V heater draws 12.5 A. Find its power and resistance.

1. $$P = 120(12.5) = 1500$$ W.
2. $$R = 120/12.5 = 9.6$$ Ω.

**Answer:** 1500 W and 9.6 Ω.
</WorkedExample>

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 49 Ω resistor is connected across 110 V. What power does it dissipate?

**Answer:** $$247\ \mathrm{W}$$

**Why:** P = V²/R.

**Working:** P = 110²/49 = 247 W.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.24\ \mathrm{W}$$: That is the current.
- $$5390\ \mathrm{W}$$: P = V²/R.
- $$5.04\ \mathrm{W}$$: P = I²R = V²/R.
