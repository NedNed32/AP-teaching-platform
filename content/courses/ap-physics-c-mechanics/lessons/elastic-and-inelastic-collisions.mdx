All isolated collisions conserve momentum.

| Type | Kinetic energy |
| --- | --- |
| elastic | conserved |
| inelastic | decreases |
| perfectly inelastic (stick together) | maximum decrease |

Perfectly inelastic: $$v_f = \frac{m_1v_1 + m_2v_2}{m_1 + m_2}$$.

For a 1-D elastic collision, the relative velocity reverses: $$v_1 - v_2 = -(v_1' - v_2')$$.

In two dimensions, conserve momentum separately in x and y.

<Note title="Ballistic pendulum">
Use momentum for the collision (bullet embeds in block), then energy for the swing up. Energy is not conserved during the collision itself.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Kinetic energy lost">
A 2.0 kg cart at 6.0 m/s hits and sticks to a 1.0 kg cart at rest. Find the kinetic energy lost.

1. $$v_f = 12/3.0 = 4.0$$ m/s.
2. $$K_i = 36$$ J; $$K_f = \tfrac12(3.0)(16) = 24$$ J.

**Answer:** 12 J.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 8 kg cart moving at 12 m/s collides with and sticks to a 7 kg cart at rest. What is their speed after the collision?

**Answer:** $$6.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Momentum is conserved; kinetic energy is not.

**Working:** v_f = 8·12/15 = 6.4 m/s; kinetic energy lost = 269 J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$12\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The carts share the momentum.
- $$5.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Use m₁v₁ for the initial momentum.
- $$8.76\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Kinetic energy is not conserved here.

### Worked exam question 2

A 3 kg ball moving at +6 m/s collides elastically head-on with a 4 kg ball at rest. What is the velocity of the 4 kg ball afterward (positive in the original direction)?

**Answer:** $$5.14\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Elastic: conserve momentum and kinetic energy (relative velocity reverses).

**Working:** v₁' = (m₁ − m₂)v/(m₁ + m₂) = -0.857 m/s; v₂' = 2m₁v/(m₁ + m₂) = 5.14 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.57\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That would be a sticking collision.
- $$4.5\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Kinetic energy is conserved too.
- $$6\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Only equal masses exchange velocities.

### Worked exam question 3

A 29 g bullet moving at 420 m/s embeds in a 3.2 kg block hanging from strings. How high does the block rise?

**Answer:** $$0.726\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** Momentum for the collision, then energy for the swing.

**Working:** V = 3.77 m/s after the collision; h = V²/(2g) = 0.726 m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$80.8\ \mathrm{m}$$: Kinetic energy is not conserved in the collision.
- $$0.385\ \mathrm{m}$$: h = V²/(2g).
- $$9000\ \mathrm{m}$$: Find the block's speed after the collision first.
