Rotation mirrors linear motion with angular quantities:

| Linear | Angular |
| --- | --- |
| $$x$$ | $$\theta$$ |
| $$v = dx/dt$$ | $$\omega = d\theta/dt$$ |
| $$a = dv/dt$$ | $$\alpha = d\omega/dt$$ |

For constant $$\alpha$$:

$$\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t \qquad \theta = \theta_0 + \omega_0 t + \tfrac12\alpha t^2 \qquad \omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\Delta\theta$$

<Note title="Radians">
Use radians in rotational equations. Convert rpm by multiplying by 2π/60.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Spinning down">
A wheel spinning at 30 rad/s slows uniformly to rest in 10 s. How many revolutions does it make?

1. $$\alpha = -3.0$$ rad/s².
2. $$\Delta\theta = \tfrac12(30)(10) = 150$$ rad.
3. $$150/2\pi = 23.9$$ rev.

**Answer:** about 24 revolutions.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A wheel spinning at 32 rad/s slows uniformly to rest in 9 s. How many revolutions does it make while stopping?

**Answer:** $$22.9$$

**Why:** Δθ = ½ω₀t, then divide by 2π.

**Working:** Δθ = ½(32)(9) = 144 rad = 22.9 rev.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$144$$: That is in radians; convert to revolutions.
- $$45.8$$: The average angular speed is ω₀/2.
- $$11.5$$: Recheck the average.
