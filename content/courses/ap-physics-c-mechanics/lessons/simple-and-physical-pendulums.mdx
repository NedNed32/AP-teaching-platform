For small angles, $$\sin\theta \approx \theta$$, and the restoring torque becomes linear:

$$\tau = -mgd\,\theta \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = -\frac{mgd}{I}\theta$$

**Physical pendulum**: $$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}}$$

**Simple pendulum** ($$I = mL^2$$, $$d = L$$): $$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

Rod pivoted at one end: $$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}$$.

<Note title="Small-angle limit">
The formulas hold only for small amplitudes; at large angles the period grows slightly.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Measuring g">
A 0.90 m pendulum has a period of 1.90 s. Find g.

1. $$g = 4\pi^2 L/T^2 = 4\pi^2(0.90)/3.61$$.

**Answer:** about 9.84 m/s².
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A simple pendulum is 2.4 m long. What is its period for small oscillations?

**Answer:** $$3.11\ \mathrm{s}$$

**Why:** T = 2π√(L/g).

**Working:** T = 3.11 s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$12.7\ \mathrm{s}$$: The ratio is L/g.
- $$0.495\ \mathrm{s}$$: Include 2π.
- $$1.54\ \mathrm{s}$$: Take the square root.

### Worked exam question 2

A uniform rod of length 2.4 m swings about a pivot at one end with small amplitude. What is its period?

**Answer:** $$2.54\ \mathrm{s}$$

**Why:** T = 2π√(I/(mgd)) with I = mL²/3 and d = L/2.

**Working:** T = 2.54 s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3.11\ \mathrm{s}$$: A rod is a physical pendulum, not a simple one.
- $$1.8\ \mathrm{s}$$: d is L/2, the distance to the center of mass.
- $$4.4\ \mathrm{s}$$: I = mL²/3.

### Free-response practice: Measuring g with a pendulum

Students measure the period of a simple pendulum for several string lengths, using small amplitudes.

*Pendulum data*

| Length L (m) | Period T (s) |
| --- | --- |
| 0.2 | 0.91 |
| 0.4 | 1.27 |
| 0.6 | 1.56 |
| 0.8 | 1.82 |
| 1 | 2.04 |

**(a)** Describe a procedure the students could use to measure the period accurately, including how to reduce uncertainty.

To earn the point: Time many oscillations and divide. Model answer: Time 10 swings with a stopwatch and divide by 10.

To earn the point: Keep the amplitude small and repeat trials. Model answer: Release from under about 10° and average several trials.

**(b)** Identify quantities to graph that would give a straight line, and explain how g can be found from the graph.

To earn the point: Graph T² versus L. Model answer: $$T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{g}L$$

To earn the point: g from the slope. Model answer: g = 4π²/slope

**(c)** Plot (describe) the linearized data and draw a best-fit line. Use it to calculate an experimental value of g.

To earn the point: T² values computed and plotted. Model answer: 0.83, 1.61, 2.43, 3.31, 4.16 s²

To earn the point: Slope of the best-fit line. Model answer: about 4.18 s²/m

To earn the point: Experimental g. Model answer: g = 4π²/4.18 ≈ 9.44 m/s²

**(d)** The students repeat the experiment with a much larger amplitude. Predict how the measured periods would change and explain.

To earn the point: Periods increase slightly; the small-angle approximation (sin θ ≈ θ) no longer holds. Model answer: The restoring torque is less than proportional to θ at large angles.
