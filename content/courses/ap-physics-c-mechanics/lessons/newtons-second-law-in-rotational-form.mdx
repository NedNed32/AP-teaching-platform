$$\sum \tau = I\alpha$$

## A falling mass on a pulley

A mass $$m$$ hangs from a string wound on a pulley (disk of mass $$M$$, radius $$R$$):

- Mass: $$mg - T = ma$$
- Pulley: $$TR = \tfrac12 MR^2\alpha$$, with $$a = R\alpha$$

Solving: $$a = \frac{mg}{m + M/2}$$.

Tensions on either side of a massive pulley are different; their difference provides the torque.

<Note title="Same strategy as forces">
Draw a diagram for each object, write ΣF = ma or Στ = Iα for each, and link them with a = rα.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Hanging mass and disk">
A 2.0 kg mass hangs from a string on a 4.0 kg disk pulley. Find the acceleration.

1. $$a = \frac{2.0(9.8)}{2.0 + 2.0}$$.

**Answer:** 4.9 m/s².
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 6 kg mass hangs from a light string wrapped around a uniform disk pulley of mass 8 kg that turns on a frictionless axle. What is the acceleration of the mass?

**Answer:** $$5.88\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$

**Why:** a = mg/(m + M/2), using I = ½MR² and a = Rα.

**Working:** mg − T = ma and TR = ½MR²(a/R) give a = 5.88 m/s².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: The pulley's inertia reduces the acceleration.
- $$4.2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: For a disk, I/R² = M/2.
- $$2.67\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: I/R² = M/2.

**Q.** A uniform rod of length 3 m is pivoted at one end and released from rest in a horizontal position. What is its angular acceleration just after release?

**A.** $$4.9\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$$. τ = Mg(L/2) and I = ML²/3 give α = 3g/(2L). α = (MgL/2)/(ML²/3) = 3g/(2L) = 4.9 rad/s².
