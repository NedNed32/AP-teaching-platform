$$f_s \le \mu_s F_N \qquad f_k = \mu_k F_N$$

- Static friction takes whatever value (up to its maximum) keeps the surfaces from slipping.
- Kinetic friction has a fixed magnitude and opposes the relative motion.
- Usually $$\mu_k < \mu_s$$.

On an incline at angle $$\theta$$, a block slides at constant velocity when $$\tan\theta = \mu_k$$, and starts to slide when $$\tan\theta$$ exceeds $$\mu_s$$.

<Note title="Not always mu times mg">
The friction force depends on the normal force, which changes when there are other vertical forces or an incline.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Sliding down a ramp">
A block slides down a 30° incline with μk = 0.20. Find its acceleration.

1. $$a = g(\sin 30° - \mu_k\cos 30°)$$.
2. $$a = 9.8(0.500 - 0.173) = 3.2$$ m/s².

**Answer:** about 3.2 m/s² down the incline.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 6 kg block on a horizontal table is connected by a string over a light pulley to a hanging 5 kg block. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the table and the block is 0.1. What is the acceleration of the blocks?

**Answer:** $$3.92\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$

**Why:** Net force (m₂g − μm₁g) divided by the total mass.

**Working:** a = (5 − 0.1·6)(9.8)/11 = 3.92 m/s².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$4.45\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: Include friction on the table block.
- $$8.62\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: Both blocks accelerate; divide by the total mass.
- $$4.99\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: Friction opposes the motion.

### Worked exam question 2

A curve of radius 50 m is banked so that a car at 20 m/s needs no friction. What is the banking angle?

**Answer:** $$39.2°$$

**Why:** tan θ = v²/(rg).

**Working:** tan θ = 20²/(50·9.8) = 0.816, so θ = 39.2°.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$54.7°$$: Use tan θ = v²/(rg), from dividing the force components.
- $$50.8°$$: Measure the angle from the horizontal.
- $$2.34°$$: Square the speed.

### Worked exam question 3

A car rounds a flat curve of radius 150 m. The coefficient of static friction is 0.6. What is the maximum speed without skidding?

**Answer:** $$29.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Static friction supplies the centripetal force: μmg = mv²/r.

**Working:** v = √(μgr) = √(0.6·9.8·150) = 29.7 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$882\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Take the square root.
- $$38.3\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Include the friction coefficient.
- $$49.5\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: μ multiplies g r.

### Free-response practice: Block on an incline with a hanging mass

A block of mass 3 kg on a 37° incline is connected by a light string over a light, frictionless pulley at the top of the incline to a hanging block of mass 8 kg. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the incline is 0.25. The system is released from rest, and the hanging block descends.

**(a)** Draw (describe) a free-body diagram for the 3 kg block, labeling each force.

To earn the point: Weight down, normal force perpendicular to the incline, tension up the incline. Model answer: mg, F_N, F_T

To earn the point: Kinetic friction down the incline (opposite the motion). Model answer: f_k pointing down the slope

**(b)** Derive an expression for the acceleration of the system in terms of the masses, θ, μ and g, then calculate its value.

To earn the point: Newton's second law applied to each block (or the system). Model answer: $$m_2g - T = m_2a$$, $$T - m_1g\sin\theta - \mu m_1g\cos\theta = m_1a$$

To earn the point: Correct expression. Model answer: $$a = \frac{(m_2 - m_1\sin\theta - \mu m_1\cos\theta)g}{m_1 + m_2}$$

To earn the point: Correct value. Model answer: 4.99 m/s²

**(c)** Calculate the tension in the string.

To earn the point: Correct value. Model answer: T = m₂(g − a) = 38.5 N

**(d)** Calculate the speed of the blocks after the hanging block has descended 1 m, and the work done by friction on the incline block during that motion.

To earn the point: Speed from kinematics or energy. Model answer: v = √(2ad) = 3.16 m/s

To earn the point: Work by friction, with a negative sign. Model answer: W_f = −μm₁g cos θ·d = −5.87 J
