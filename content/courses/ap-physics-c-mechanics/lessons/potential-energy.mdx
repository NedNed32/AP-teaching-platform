For a conservative force:

$$\Delta U = -\int F\,dx \qquad F = -\frac{dU}{dx}$$

| System | Potential energy |
| --- | --- |
| object near Earth's surface | $$U = mgh$$ |
| spring | $$U = \tfrac12 kx^2$$ |
| two masses (general) | $$U = -\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r}$$ |

On a U(x) graph, the force points downhill. Minima are stable equilibria; maxima are unstable equilibria.

<Note title="Zero is a choice">
Only changes in potential energy matter. For gravitation, U = 0 is chosen at infinite separation, which makes U negative.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Force from potential">
$$U(x) = 2x^3 - 6x$$ J. Find the equilibrium positions.

1. $$F = -\frac{dU}{dx} = -(6x^2 - 6)$$.
2. $$F = 0$$ when $$x = \pm 1$$ m.

**Answer:** x = 1 m (stable, a minimum of U) and x = −1 m (unstable).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** The potential energy of a particle is $$U(x) = 2x^3 - 15x$$ J. At what positive x is the particle in stable equilibrium?

**A.** $$1.58\ \mathrm{m}$$. Equilibrium where dU/dx = 0; stable at a minimum. dU/dx = 6x² − 15 = 0 gives x = 1.58 m; d²U/dx² > 0 there, so it is stable.
