$$P_{avg} = \frac{\Delta E}{\Delta t} \qquad P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec F \cdot \vec v$$

A motor lifting a load at constant speed delivers $$P = mgv$$. A car at constant speed delivers power equal to the drag force times its speed.

Energy can be found from power by integration: $$W = \int P\,dt$$.

<Note title="Area under a P–t graph">
The area under a power–time graph is the energy transferred.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Lifting">
A motor lifts a 200 kg load 15 m in 12 s at constant speed. Find the power.

1. $$W = mgh = 200(9.8)(15) = 29{,}400$$ J.
2. $$P = 29{,}400/12 = 2450$$ W.

**Answer:** about 2.5 kW.
</WorkedExample>

### Worked exam question 1

A motor lifts a 330 kg load 10 m at constant speed in 17 s. What average power does it deliver?

**Answer:** $$1900\ \mathrm{W}$$

**Why:** P = mgh/t.

**Working:** P = 330(9.8)(10)/17 = 1900 W.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3.23 \times 10^{4}\ \mathrm{W}$$: That is the work; divide by time.
- $$194\ \mathrm{W}$$: Include g.
- $$5500\ \mathrm{W}$$: P = W/t.
