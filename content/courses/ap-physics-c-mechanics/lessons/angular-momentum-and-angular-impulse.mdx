$$L = I\omega \qquad \vec L = \vec r \times \vec p \quad (L = mvr_\perp)$$

$$\sum \tau = \frac{dL}{dt} \qquad \Delta L = \int \tau\,dt$$

A particle moving in a straight line past a point has constant angular momentum about that point (if no net torque), equal to $$mv$$ times the perpendicular distance.

<Note title="Direction">
Angular momentum is a vector along the axis, given by the right-hand rule.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Angular impulse">
A torque of 3.0 N·m acts for 2.0 s on a disk (I = 0.40 kg·m²) initially at rest. Find ω.

1. $$\Delta L = 3.0 \times 2.0 = 6.0$$ kg·m²/s.
2. $$\omega = 6.0/0.40 = 15$$ rad/s.

**Answer:** 15 rad/s.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

A solid sphere rolls without slipping from rest down a ramp of height 1.4 m. What is its speed at the bottom?

**Answer:** $$4.43\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** mgh = ½mv²(1 + β) with I = ⅖MR².

**Working:** v = √(2gh/(1 + 0.4)) = 4.43 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$5.24\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Some energy goes into rotation.
- $$2.8\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Rotational energy is ½Iω² = ½βmv².
- $$3.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That is only correct for a hoop.

### Worked exam question 2

A uniform disk of mass 4 kg and radius 0.3 m spins at 9 rad/s on a frictionless axle. A 1.5 kg lump of clay is dropped onto the rim and sticks. What is the new angular speed?

**Answer:** $$5.14\ \mathrm{rad/s}$$

**Why:** Angular momentum is conserved: I_iω_i = I_fω_f.

**Working:** I_i = 0.18, I_f = 0.315 kg·m²; ω_f = 5.14 rad/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$6.55\ \mathrm{rad/s}$$: Use rotational inertias, not masses.
- $$6.8\ \mathrm{rad/s}$$: Kinetic energy is not conserved in a sticking collision.
- $$2.57\ \mathrm{rad/s}$$: The clay's inertia is mR².
