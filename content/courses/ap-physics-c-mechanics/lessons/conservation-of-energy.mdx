$$K_i + U_i + W_{nc} = K_f + U_f$$

If only conservative forces do work, $$W_{nc} = 0$$ and mechanical energy is constant.

## Typical problems

- Speed at the bottom of a frictionless slope: $$v = \sqrt{2gh}$$, independent of the slope's shape.
- Spring launching a block: $$\tfrac12 kx^2 = \tfrac12 mv^2$$.
- Escape speed: $$\tfrac12 mv^2 - \frac{GMm}{R} = 0$$, so $$v = \sqrt{2GM/R}$$.
- Friction: $$W_{nc} = -f_k d$$.

<Note title="Energy or forces?">
Use energy when the question asks about speeds and positions. Use forces and kinematics when it asks about time or acceleration.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Spring and friction">
A spring (k = 200 N/m) compressed 0.10 m launches a 0.50 kg block across a floor with μk = 0.25. How far does the block slide after leaving the spring?

1. Spring energy: $$\tfrac12(200)(0.10)^2 = 1.0$$ J.
2. Friction: $$0.25(0.50)(9.8) = 1.225$$ N.
3. $$d = 1.0/1.225 = 0.82$$ m.

**Answer:** about 0.82 m (ignoring friction while in contact with the spring).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A spring with $$k = 390$$ N/m is compressed 0.08 m and launches a 0.7 kg block across a frictionless surface. What is the block's speed after leaving the spring?

**Answer:** $$1.89\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** ½kx² = ½mv².

**Working:** v = x√(k/m) = 0.08√(390/0.7) = 1.89 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$44.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Use energy: v = x√(k/m).
- $$2.67\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The ½ appears on both sides.
- $$3.57\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Take the square root.

### Worked exam question 2

A block starts from rest and slides 7 m down a 45° incline with coefficient of kinetic friction 0.2. What is its speed at the bottom?

**Answer:** $$8.81\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** mgh − μmg cos θ·L = ½mv².

**Working:** h = 4.95 m. v = √(2g(h − μL cos θ)) = 8.81 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$11.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Use the vertical drop, L sin θ.
- $$9.85\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Friction removes energy.
- $$8.34\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Friction depends on the normal force, mg cos θ.

### Worked exam question 3

A planet has mass $$5 \times 10^{25}$$ kg and radius $$7 \times 10^{6}$$ m. What is the escape speed from its surface? ($$G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}$$ N·m²/kg²)

**Answer:** $$3.09 \times 10^{4}\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** ½mv² = GMm/R.

**Working:** v = √(2GM/R) = 3.09 \times 10^\{4\} m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.18 \times 10^{4}\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That is the circular orbit speed; escape needs √2 times more.
- $$9.53 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Take the square root.
- $$11.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Potential energy uses 1/R.

### Worked exam question 4

A 29 g bullet moving at 420 m/s embeds in a 3.2 kg block hanging from strings. How high does the block rise?

**Answer:** $$0.726\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** Momentum for the collision, then energy for the swing.

**Working:** V = 3.77 m/s after the collision; h = V²/(2g) = 0.726 m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$80.8\ \mathrm{m}$$: Kinetic energy is not conserved in the collision.
- $$0.385\ \mathrm{m}$$: h = V²/(2g).
- $$9000\ \mathrm{m}$$: Find the block's speed after the collision first.
