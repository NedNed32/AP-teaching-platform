For a point at distance $$r$$ from the axis:

$$s = r\theta \qquad v = r\omega \qquad a_t = r\alpha \qquad a_c = \omega^2 r$$

Every point of a rigid body shares the same $$\omega$$ and $$\alpha$$, but points farther out move faster.

A string unwinding from a pulley without slipping moves at the rim speed: $$v = r\omega$$, $$a = r\alpha$$.

<Note title="Total acceleration">
A point on a speeding-up wheel has both tangential and centripetal acceleration; add them as perpendicular vectors.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Rim speed">
A 0.30 m radius wheel turns at 120 rpm. Find the speed of a point on the rim.

1. $$\omega = 120 \times 2\pi/60 = 12.6$$ rad/s.
2. $$v = 0.30 \times 12.6 = 3.8$$ m/s.

**Answer:** about 3.8 m/s.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

A wheel spinning at 32 rad/s slows uniformly to rest in 9 s. How many revolutions does it make while stopping?

**Answer:** $$22.9$$

**Why:** Δθ = ½ω₀t, then divide by 2π.

**Working:** Δθ = ½(32)(9) = 144 rad = 22.9 rev.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$144$$: That is in radians; convert to revolutions.
- $$45.8$$: The average angular speed is ω₀/2.
- $$11.5$$: Recheck the average.

### Worked exam question 2

Point masses of 5 kg and 4 kg are at the ends of a massless rod 1 m long. What is the rotational inertia about the rod's center?

**Answer:** $$2.25\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$

**Why:** Sum of mr² with r = L/2.

**Working:** I = (5 + 4)(1/2)² = 2.25 kg·m².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$9\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: Each mass is L/2 from the center.
- $$4.5\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: Square the distance.
- $$5\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: Measure from the center, not an end.

### Worked exam question 3

What is the rotational inertia of a uniform rod of mass 7 kg and length 2 m about an axis through one end, perpendicular to the rod?

**Answer:** $$9.33\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$

**Why:** I = ML²/3 (parallel-axis theorem from ML²/12).

**Working:** I = ML²/12 + M(L/2)² = ML²/3 = 9.33 kg·m².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.33\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: That is about the center.
- $$28\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: The mass is spread along the rod.
- $$14\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: That is a disk's formula.

### Worked exam question 4

A uniform disk of mass 4 kg and radius 0.6 m rotates about an axis perpendicular to it, 1.5 m from its center. What is its rotational inertia?

**Answer:** $$9.72\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$

**Why:** Parallel-axis theorem: I = ½MR² + Md².

**Working:** I = ½(4)(0.6)² + 4(1.5)² = 9.72 kg·m².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.72\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: The axis is not through the center.
- $$9\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: Include the disk's own I about its center.
- $$6.72\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: The parallel-axis term is Md².

### Worked exam question 5

A uniform 5 m beam weighing 200 N rests on supports at both ends. A 400 N load sits 3 m from the left end. What force does the right support exert?

**Answer:** $$340\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** Take torques about the left support.

**Working:** F_R·5 = 200·2.5 + 400·3, so F_R = 340 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$260\ \mathrm{N}$$: That is the left support's force.
- $$300\ \mathrm{N}$$: The load is not at the center.
- $$240\ \mathrm{N}$$: Include the beam's weight at its center.
