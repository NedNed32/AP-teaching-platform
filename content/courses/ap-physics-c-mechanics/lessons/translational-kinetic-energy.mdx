$$K = \tfrac12 mv^2$$

Kinetic energy depends on the square of the speed, so doubling speed quadruples $$K$$. It is a scalar, measured in joules.

The **work–energy theorem** links force and kinetic energy:

$$W_{net} = \Delta K$$

Kinetic energy also depends on the reference frame, since velocity does.

<Note title="Momentum and kinetic energy">
K = p²/(2m). Two objects with equal momentum have kinetic energies inversely proportional to their masses.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Stopping distance">
A car's speed doubles. By what factor does its braking distance change (same braking force)?

1. Work by friction equals the kinetic energy: $$fd = \tfrac12 mv^2$$.
2. $$d \propto v^2$$.

**Answer:** it increases by a factor of 4.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

A force $$F(x) = 3x$$ N acts along the x-axis. How much work does it do on an object moving from $$x = 1$$ m to $$x = 2$$ m?

**Answer:** $$4.5\ \mathrm{J}$$

**Why:** W = ∫F dx.

**Working:** W = 3[x^2/2] from 1 to 2 = 4.5 J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3\ \mathrm{J}$$: Integrate; do not just evaluate F.
- $$6\ \mathrm{J}$$: The force is not constant.
- $$9\ \mathrm{J}$$: Divide by n + 1 when integrating.

### Worked exam question 2

A spring with $$k = 390$$ N/m is compressed 0.08 m and launches a 0.7 kg block across a frictionless surface. What is the block's speed after leaving the spring?

**Answer:** $$1.89\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** ½kx² = ½mv².

**Working:** v = x√(k/m) = 0.08√(390/0.7) = 1.89 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$44.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Use energy: v = x√(k/m).
- $$2.67\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The ½ appears on both sides.
- $$3.57\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Take the square root.

### Worked exam question 3

A block starts from rest and slides 7 m down a 45° incline with coefficient of kinetic friction 0.2. What is its speed at the bottom?

**Answer:** $$8.81\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** mgh − μmg cos θ·L = ½mv².

**Working:** h = 4.95 m. v = √(2g(h − μL cos θ)) = 8.81 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$11.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Use the vertical drop, L sin θ.
- $$9.85\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Friction removes energy.
- $$8.34\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Friction depends on the normal force, mg cos θ.
