An object moving in a circle at speed $$v$$ accelerates toward the center:

$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r \qquad \sum F_{\text{in}} = \frac{mv^2}{r}$$

If speed changes, there is also a tangential acceleration $$a_t = dv/dt$$, and the total acceleration is $$\sqrt{a_c^2 + a_t^2}$$.

## Examples

- Car on a flat curve: friction provides the inward force, $$v_{max} = \sqrt{\mu_s g r}$$.
- Banked curve without friction: $$\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg}$$.
- Vertical circle: at the top, $$F_T + mg = mv^2/r$$.

<Note title="Period">
For uniform circular motion, v = 2πr/T.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Flat curve">
A car rounds a flat curve of radius 50 m. If μs = 0.80, find the maximum speed.

1. $$\mu_s mg = mv^2/r$$.
2. $$v = \sqrt{0.80 \times 9.8 \times 50} = 19.8$$ m/s.

**Answer:** about 20 m/s.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A curve of radius 50 m is banked so that a car at 20 m/s needs no friction. What is the banking angle?

**Answer:** $$39.2°$$

**Why:** tan θ = v²/(rg).

**Working:** tan θ = 20²/(50·9.8) = 0.816, so θ = 39.2°.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$54.7°$$: Use tan θ = v²/(rg), from dividing the force components.
- $$50.8°$$: Measure the angle from the horizontal.
- $$2.34°$$: Square the speed.

### Worked exam question 2

A car rounds a flat curve of radius 150 m. The coefficient of static friction is 0.6. What is the maximum speed without skidding?

**Answer:** $$29.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Static friction supplies the centripetal force: μmg = mv²/r.

**Working:** v = √(μgr) = √(0.6·9.8·150) = 29.7 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$882\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Take the square root.
- $$38.3\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Include the friction coefficient.
- $$49.5\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: μ multiplies g r.

### Worked exam question 3

A 2.5 kg ball on a 0.5 m string moves in a vertical circle. At the top of the circle its speed is 7 m/s. What is the tension in the string there?

**Answer:** $$221\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** At the top, T + mg = mv²/r.

**Working:** T = mv²/r − mg = 221 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$245\ \mathrm{N}$$: Include gravity.
- $$270\ \mathrm{N}$$: Check the direction of gravity relative to the center.
- $$10.5\ \mathrm{N}$$: Centripetal force uses v².
