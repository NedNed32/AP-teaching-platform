$$\vec\tau = \vec r \times \vec F \qquad \tau = rF\sin\theta = r_\perp F$$

Torque depends on the size of the force, where it is applied, and its direction. By convention, counterclockwise torques are positive.

Gravity acts at the center of mass, so a uniform beam's weight produces a torque as if it were concentrated at the midpoint.

<Note title="Right-hand rule">
Curl the fingers of your right hand from r toward F: your thumb points in the direction of the torque vector, along the axis.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Wrench">
A 40 N force is applied 0.25 m from a bolt at 60° to the wrench. Find the torque.

1. $$\tau = 0.25 \times 40 \times \sin 60°$$.

**Answer:** about 8.7 N·m.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A uniform 5 m beam weighing 200 N rests on supports at both ends. A 400 N load sits 3 m from the left end. What force does the right support exert?

**Answer:** $$340\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** Take torques about the left support.

**Working:** F_R·5 = 200·2.5 + 400·3, so F_R = 340 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$260\ \mathrm{N}$$: That is the left support's force.
- $$300\ \mathrm{N}$$: The load is not at the center.
- $$240\ \mathrm{N}$$: Include the beam's weight at its center.

**A.** $$4.9\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$$. τ = Mg(L/2) and I = ML²/3 give α = 3g/(2L). α = (MgL/2)/(ML²/3) = 3g/(2L) = 4.9 rad/s².
