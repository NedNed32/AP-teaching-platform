$$I = \sum m_i r_i^2 \qquad I = \int r^2\,dm$$

| Object | Axis | I |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hoop | center | $$MR^2$$ |
| solid disk or cylinder | center | $$\tfrac12 MR^2$$ |
| solid sphere | center | $$\tfrac25 MR^2$$ |
| rod | center | $$\tfrac{1}{12}ML^2$$ |
| rod | end | $$\tfrac13 ML^2$$ |

**Parallel-axis theorem**: $$I = I_{cm} + Md^2$$. For a rod about its end: $$\tfrac{1}{12}ML^2 + M(L/2)^2 = \tfrac13 ML^2$$.

<Note title="Distribution matters">
Two objects with the same mass can have very different rotational inertia, depending on how far the mass is from the axis.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Integration">
Find I for a uniform rod of mass M and length L about one end.

1. $$dm = (M/L)\,dx$$.
2. $$I = \int_0^L x^2 \frac{M}{L}\,dx = \frac{M}{L}\cdot\frac{L^3}{3}$$.

**Answer:** $$\tfrac13 ML^2$$.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Point masses of 5 kg and 4 kg are at the ends of a massless rod 1 m long. What is the rotational inertia about the rod's center?

**Answer:** $$2.25\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$

**Why:** Sum of mr² with r = L/2.

**Working:** I = (5 + 4)(1/2)² = 2.25 kg·m².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$9\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: Each mass is L/2 from the center.
- $$4.5\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: Square the distance.
- $$5\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: Measure from the center, not an end.

### Worked exam question 2

What is the rotational inertia of a uniform rod of mass 7 kg and length 2 m about an axis through one end, perpendicular to the rod?

**Answer:** $$9.33\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$

**Why:** I = ML²/3 (parallel-axis theorem from ML²/12).

**Working:** I = ML²/12 + M(L/2)² = ML²/3 = 9.33 kg·m².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.33\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: That is about the center.
- $$28\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: The mass is spread along the rod.
- $$14\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: That is a disk's formula.

### Worked exam question 3

A uniform disk of mass 4 kg and radius 0.6 m rotates about an axis perpendicular to it, 1.5 m from its center. What is its rotational inertia?

**Answer:** $$9.72\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$

**Why:** Parallel-axis theorem: I = ½MR² + Md².

**Working:** I = ½(4)(0.6)² + 4(1.5)² = 9.72 kg·m².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.72\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: The axis is not through the center.
- $$9\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: Include the disk's own I about its center.
- $$6.72\ \mathrm{kg{\cdot}m^2}$$: The parallel-axis term is Md².
