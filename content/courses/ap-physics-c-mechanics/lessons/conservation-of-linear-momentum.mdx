If $$\sum \vec F_{ext} = 0$$:

$$\sum m_i\vec v_{i,\text{before}} = \sum m_i\vec v_{i,\text{after}}$$

During short collisions, internal forces are much larger than external ones, so momentum is approximately conserved even with friction or gravity present.

Recoil: a gun of mass $$M$$ firing a bullet of mass $$m$$ at $$v$$ recoils at $$mv/M$$.

<Note title="Center of mass">
With no external force, the center of mass velocity never changes, whatever happens inside the system.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Recoil">
A 60 kg skater at rest throws a 3.0 kg ball forward at 10 m/s. Find the skater's velocity.

1. $$0 = 60v + 3.0(10)$$.
2. $$v = -0.50$$ m/s.

**Answer:** 0.50 m/s backward.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 8 kg cart moving at 12 m/s collides with and sticks to a 7 kg cart at rest. What is their speed after the collision?

**Answer:** $$6.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Momentum is conserved; kinetic energy is not.

**Working:** v_f = 8·12/15 = 6.4 m/s; kinetic energy lost = 269 J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$12\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The carts share the momentum.
- $$5.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Use m₁v₁ for the initial momentum.
- $$8.76\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Kinetic energy is not conserved here.

### Worked exam question 2

A 500 kg car heading east at 5 m/s collides with a 3000 kg car heading north at 14 m/s, and they lock together. What is their speed just after the collision?

**Answer:** $$12\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Conserve momentum in x and y separately, then combine.

**Working:** p_x = 2500, p_y = 42000; v = √(p_x² + p_y²)/(m₁ + m₂) = 12 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$12.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Momentum is a vector; add components at right angles.
- $$14.9\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Weight each velocity by its mass.
- $$84.1\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Divide by the total mass.
