$$\vec p = m\vec v \qquad \vec p_{sys} = \sum m_i\vec v_i = M\vec v_{cm}$$

Momentum is a vector: in two dimensions, track x and y components separately.

Newton's second law in its general form: $$\sum \vec F = \frac{d\vec p}{dt}$$.

<Note title="Exploding objects">
A system at rest that explodes still has zero total momentum: the pieces fly apart with momenta that add to zero.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="System momentum">
A 2.0 kg cart moves at +3.0 m/s and a 1.0 kg cart at −4.0 m/s. Find the velocity of the center of mass.

1. $$p = 6.0 - 4.0 = 2.0$$ kg·m/s.
2. $$v_{cm} = 2.0/3.0 = 0.67$$ m/s.

**Answer:** +0.67 m/s.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

A 8 kg cart moving at 12 m/s collides with and sticks to a 7 kg cart at rest. What is their speed after the collision?

**Answer:** $$6.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Momentum is conserved; kinetic energy is not.

**Working:** v_f = 8·12/15 = 6.4 m/s; kinetic energy lost = 269 J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$12\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The carts share the momentum.
- $$5.6\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Use m₁v₁ for the initial momentum.
- $$8.76\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Kinetic energy is not conserved here.

### Worked exam question 2

A 3 kg ball moving at +6 m/s collides elastically head-on with a 4 kg ball at rest. What is the velocity of the 4 kg ball afterward (positive in the original direction)?

**Answer:** $$5.14\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Elastic: conserve momentum and kinetic energy (relative velocity reverses).

**Working:** v₁' = (m₁ − m₂)v/(m₁ + m₂) = -0.857 m/s; v₂' = 2m₁v/(m₁ + m₂) = 5.14 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.57\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That would be a sticking collision.
- $$4.5\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Kinetic energy is conserved too.
- $$6\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Only equal masses exchange velocities.

### Worked exam question 3

A 4 kg object at rest experiences a net force $$F(t) = 9t$$ N from $$t = 0$$ to $$t = 4$$ s. What is its final speed?

**Answer:** $$18\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Impulse ∫F dt equals the change in momentum.

**Working:** J = ∫F dt = 72 N·s; v = J/m = 18 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$9\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Integrate the force over time.
- $$36\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The force is not constant.
- $$72\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Divide by the mass to get speed.
