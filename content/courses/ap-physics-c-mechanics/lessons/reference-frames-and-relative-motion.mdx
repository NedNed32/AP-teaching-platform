Motion is always measured relative to a reference frame.

$$\vec v_{A,\text{ground}} = \vec v_{A,B} + \vec v_{B,\text{ground}}$$

Two frames moving at constant velocity relative to each other are both **inertial**: they agree on accelerations and forces, though they disagree on velocities and positions.

In two dimensions, add the velocity vectors by components.

<Note title="Subscript check">
Write subscripts so the inner ones match and cancel: v(A rel B) + v(B rel C) = v(A rel C).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Crossing a river">
A boat heads straight across a river at 4.0 m/s relative to the water; the current is 3.0 m/s. Find the boat's speed relative to the shore.

1. The velocities are perpendicular.
2. $$v = \sqrt{4.0^2 + 3.0^2} = 5.0$$ m/s.

**Answer:** 5.0 m/s, angled downstream.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A boat points straight across a river 200 m wide, moving at 4 m/s relative to the water. The current flows at 3 m/s. How far downstream does the boat land?

**Answer:** $$150\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** The current does not change the time to cross; it only carries the boat downstream.

**Working:** t = 200/4 = 50 s; drift = 3·50 = 150 m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$120\ \mathrm{m}$$: Use the crossing time w/v_boat.
- $$267\ \mathrm{m}$$: Drift = current speed × crossing time.
- $$66.7\ \mathrm{m}$$: That is a time, not a distance.

### Worked exam question 2

A boat points straight across a river 140 m wide, moving at 3 m/s relative to the water. The current flows at 1 m/s. How long does the crossing take?

**Answer:** $$46.7\ \mathrm{s}$$

**Why:** The current does not change the time to cross; it only carries the boat downstream.

**Working:** t = 140/3 = 46.7 s; drift = 1·46.7 = 46.7 m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$44.3\ \mathrm{s}$$: The perpendicular component alone sets the crossing time.
- $$70\ \mathrm{s}$$: The current is perpendicular to the heading.
- $$35\ \mathrm{s}$$: The current is perpendicular to the heading.
