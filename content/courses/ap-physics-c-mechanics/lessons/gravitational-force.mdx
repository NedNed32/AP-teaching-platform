$$F_g = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2} \qquad G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2$$

The gravitational field of a spherical body of mass $$M$$ at distance $$r$$ from its center:

$$g = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

Near Earth's surface, $$g \approx 9.8$$ m/s². Weight is $$mg$$; an object's mass does not change from place to place, but its weight does.

Inside a uniform solid sphere, $$g$$ increases linearly with $$r$$ from zero at the center.

<Note title="Apparent weight">
A scale reads the normal force, not mg. In an elevator accelerating upward, the scale reads m(g + a).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Field on another planet">
A planet has twice Earth's mass and twice Earth's radius. What is g at its surface?

1. $$g \propto M/r^2 = 2/4 = 1/2$$ of Earth's.

**Answer:** about 4.9 m/s².
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A planet has mass $$5 \times 10^{25}$$ kg and radius $$7 \times 10^{6}$$ m. What is the escape speed from its surface? ($$G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}$$ N·m²/kg²)

**Answer:** $$3.09 \times 10^{4}\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** ½mv² = GMm/R.

**Working:** v = √(2GM/R) = 3.09 \times 10^\{4\} m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.18 \times 10^{4}\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That is the circular orbit speed; escape needs √2 times more.
- $$9.53 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Take the square root.
- $$11.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Potential energy uses 1/R.
