$$x_{cm} = \frac{\sum m_i x_i}{\sum m_i} \qquad x_{cm} = \frac{1}{M}\int x\,dm$$

For a rod with linear density $$\lambda(x)$$: $$dm = \lambda\,dx$$ and $$M = \int \lambda\,dx$$.

Newton's second law for a system: $$\sum \vec F_{ext} = M\vec a_{cm}$$. Internal forces cancel in pairs, so they do not move the center of mass.

<Note title="Defining the system">
Choose what is inside the system first. Forces between objects inside it are internal; everything else is external.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Nonuniform rod">
A rod from x = 0 to L has $$\lambda = kx$$. Find $$x_{cm}$$.

1. $$M = \int_0^L kx\,dx = \tfrac12 kL^2$$.
2. $$\int x\,dm = \int_0^L kx^2\,dx = \tfrac13 kL^3$$.
3. $$x_{cm} = \frac{kL^3/3}{kL^2/2} = \tfrac23 L$$.

**Answer:** two thirds of the way from the light end.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Masses of 7 kg and 1 kg hang on either side of a light, frictionless pulley. What is the tension in the string?

**Answer:** $$17.2\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** T = 2m₁m₂g/(m₁ + m₂).

**Working:** a = (7 − 1)(9.8)/8 = 7.35 m/s²; T = m₂(g + a) = 17.2 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$68.6\ \mathrm{N}$$: The heavier mass accelerates down, so T is less than its weight.
- $$9.8\ \mathrm{N}$$: The lighter mass accelerates up, so T exceeds its weight.
- $$39.2\ \mathrm{N}$$: Use T = m₂(g + a).

### Worked exam question 2

Masses of 4 kg and 3 kg hang on either side of a light, frictionless pulley. What is the magnitude of their acceleration?

**Answer:** $$1.4\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$

**Why:** a = (m₁ − m₂)g/(m₁ + m₂).

**Working:** a = (4 − 3)(9.8)/7 = 1.4 m/s²; T = m₂(g + a) = 33.6 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.45\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: The whole system accelerates: divide by both masses.
- $$68.6\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: The fraction is inverted.
- $$9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: The masses are connected, so a is less than g.

**Q.** A rod lies along the x-axis from $$x = 0$$ to $$x = 5$$ m and has linear mass density $$\lambda = kx^3$$. Where is its center of mass?

**A.** $$4\ \mathrm{m}$$. x_cm = ∫xλ dx / ∫λ dx. x_cm = [kL^5/(5)]/[kL^4/(4)] = 4L/5 = 4 m.
