Physics quantities are either **scalars** (magnitude only) or **vectors** (magnitude and direction).

| Scalars | Vectors |
| --- | --- |
| distance, speed, mass, time, energy | displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum |

In one dimension, choose a positive direction. A vector's sign then gives its direction: $$v = -3\ \text{m/s}$$ means 3 m/s in the negative direction.

Vectors add with signs: walking +5 m then −8 m gives a displacement of −3 m but a distance of 13 m.

<Note title="Choose axes first">
Every problem starts with a coordinate system. The physics does not depend on the choice, but the signs of your answers do.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Distance versus displacement">
A runner goes 40 m east, then 70 m west. Find the distance and the displacement (east positive).

1. Distance: 40 + 70 = 110 m.
2. Displacement: +40 − 70 = −30 m.

**Answer:** 110 m traveled; displacement 30 m west.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

A particle's position is $$x(t) = 1t^3 + 2t^2 - 7t$$, with x in meters and t in seconds. What is its velocity at $$t = 3$$ s?

**Answer:** $$32\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Differentiate once: v = 3at² + 2bt + c.

**Working:** v(t) = 3t² + 4t - 7, so at t = 3 the value is 32.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$24\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That is the position; differentiate it.
- $$8\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Bring each exponent down as a factor when differentiating.
- $$39\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The constant term c is part of the velocity.

### Worked exam question 2

A particle's position is $$x(t) = -1t^3 - 2t^2 - 3t$$, with x in meters and t in seconds. What is its acceleration at $$t = 2$$ s?

**Answer:** $$-16\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$

**Why:** Differentiate twice: a = 6at + 2b.

**Working:** a(t) = -6t - 4, so at t = 2 the value is -16.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$-23\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: That is the velocity; differentiate again.
- $$-10\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: Bring each exponent down as a factor when differentiating.
- $$-12\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: The derivative of 2bt is 2b, which also remains.

### Worked exam question 3

An object's acceleration is $$a(t) = 3t$$ m/s². At $$t = 0$$ it has velocity 3 m/s and is at $$x = 0$$. What is its position at $$t = 3$$ s?

**Answer:** $$22.5\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** Integrate twice, using the initial conditions.

**Working:** v(t) = 3 + 3t²/2 and x(t) = 3t + 3t³/6. At t = 3: 22.5.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$49.5\ \mathrm{m}$$: Integrating tⁿ divides by n + 1.
- $$13.5\ \mathrm{m}$$: Include the initial velocity.
- $$16.5\ \mathrm{m}$$: That is the velocity.

### Worked exam question 4

An object's acceleration is $$a(t) = 6t$$ m/s². At $$t = 0$$ it has velocity 5 m/s and is at $$x = 0$$. What is its velocity at $$t = 2$$ s?

**Answer:** $$17\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Integrate a(t) and add the initial velocity.

**Working:** v(t) = 5 + 6t²/2. At t = 2: 17.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$29\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Integrating tⁿ divides by n + 1.
- $$12\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Include the initial velocity.
- $$18\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That is the position.

### Worked exam question 5

A car moving at 12 m/s brakes with a constant deceleration of 2 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping?

**Answer:** $$36\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** Use v² = v₀² + 2aΔx with v = 0.

**Working:** Δx = v₀²/(2a) = 12²/(2 × 2) = 36 m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$72\ \mathrm{m}$$: Divide by 2a, not a.
- $$6\ \mathrm{m}$$: That is the stopping time in seconds.
- $$18\ \mathrm{m}$$: Δx = v₀²/(2a).

### Worked exam question 6

A cart moving at 22 m/s accelerates uniformly at 7 m/s² over 75 m. What is its final speed?

**Answer:** $$39.2\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Use v² = v₀² + 2aΔx.

**Working:** v = √(22² + 2·7·75) = 39.2 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$54.4\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Square roots do not add term by term.
- $$31.8\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The equation has 2aΔx.
- $$1530\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Take the square root.
