Rolling without slipping: $$v_{cm} = R\omega$$, $$a_{cm} = R\alpha$$.

Energy for an object starting from rest at height $$h$$, with $$I = \beta MR^2$$:

$$Mgh = \tfrac12 Mv^2(1 + \beta) \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{\frac{2gh}{1 + \beta}}$$

| Object | β | Speed at bottom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hoop | 1 | $$\sqrt{gh}$$ |
| disk | 1/2 | $$\sqrt{4gh/3}$$ |
| solid sphere | 2/5 | $$\sqrt{10gh/7}$$ |

Down an incline: $$a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + \beta}$$.

<Note title="Mass and radius cancel">
Every solid sphere reaches the bottom at the same speed, whatever its mass or size. Only the shape (β) matters.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Cylinder on a ramp">
A solid cylinder rolls from rest down a 1.5 m high ramp. Find its speed at the bottom.

1. $$v = \sqrt{4gh/3} = \sqrt{4(9.8)(1.5)/3}$$.

**Answer:** about 4.4 m/s.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A solid sphere rolls without slipping from rest down a ramp of height 1.4 m. What is its speed at the bottom?

**Answer:** $$4.43\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** mgh = ½mv²(1 + β) with I = ⅖MR².

**Working:** v = √(2gh/(1 + 0.4)) = 4.43 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$5.24\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Some energy goes into rotation.
- $$2.8\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Rotational energy is ½Iω² = ½βmv².
- $$3.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That is only correct for a hoop.

### Free-response practice: A rolling race

A solid cylinder (I = ½MR²) and a solid sphere (I = ⅖MR²) of equal mass M and radius R are released from rest at the top of the same ramp of height 0.8 m. Both roll without slipping.

**(a)** Without calculating, predict which object reaches the bottom first. Justify your prediction using energy.

To earn the point: The solid sphere arrives first. Model answer: It has the smaller rotational inertia per MR².

To earn the point: Justification: less of its energy goes into rotation, so more is translational kinetic energy. Model answer: Both convert the same mgh.

**(b)** Derive an expression for the speed at the bottom of an object with rotational inertia βMR², in terms of g, h and β.

To earn the point: Energy conservation including both forms of kinetic energy, with v = Rω. Model answer: $$Mgh = \tfrac12 Mv^2 + \tfrac12\beta MR^2\left(\frac{v}{R}\right)^2$$

To earn the point: Correct result. Model answer: $$v = \sqrt{\frac{2gh}{1 + \beta}}$$

**(c)** Calculate the speed of each object at the bottom.

To earn the point: Both speeds correct. Model answer: solid cylinder: 3.23 m/s; solid sphere: 3.35 m/s

**(d)** Explain whether your derived expression is consistent with your prediction in part (a), and why neither speed depends on M or R.

To earn the point: Smaller β gives larger v, consistent with the prediction. Model answer: β = 0.4 for the solid sphere.

To earn the point: M and R cancel in the energy equation. Model answer: Only the shape factor β matters.
