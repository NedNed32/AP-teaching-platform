$$\vec J = \int \vec F\,dt = \Delta\vec p$$

For a constant force, $$J = F\Delta t$$. The same change in momentum over a longer time requires a smaller average force.

A ball bouncing back reverses direction, so $$\Delta p = m(v_f - v_i)$$ includes both speeds: bouncing transfers more impulse than stopping.

<Note title="Watch the signs">
A ball hitting a wall at +8 m/s and rebounding at −6 m/s has Δv = −14 m/s, not −2 m/s.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Variable force">
A force $$F(t) = 12t^2$$ N acts on a 2.0 kg object at rest from t = 0 to t = 2 s. Find its final speed.

1. $$J = \int_0^2 12t^2\,dt = 4t^3\big|_0^2 = 32$$ N·s.
2. $$v = 32/2.0 = 16$$ m/s.

**Answer:** 16 m/s.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A 4 kg object at rest experiences a net force $$F(t) = 9t$$ N from $$t = 0$$ to $$t = 4$$ s. What is its final speed?

**Answer:** $$18\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** Impulse ∫F dt equals the change in momentum.

**Working:** J = ∫F dt = 72 N·s; v = J/m = 18 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$9\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Integrate the force over time.
- $$36\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: The force is not constant.
- $$72\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Divide by the mass to get speed.

### Worked exam question 2

A 0.9 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and rebounds at 8 m/s. The contact lasts 0.06 s. What is the average force from the wall?

**Answer:** $$270\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** Δp = m(v_f − v_i) with the direction reversed.

**Working:** Δp = 0.9(8 + 10) = 16.2 kg·m/s; F = Δp/Δt = 270 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$30\ \mathrm{N}$$: The velocity reverses, so the speeds add.
- $$150\ \mathrm{N}$$: Include the rebound.
- $$0.972\ \mathrm{N}$$: Divide by time.
