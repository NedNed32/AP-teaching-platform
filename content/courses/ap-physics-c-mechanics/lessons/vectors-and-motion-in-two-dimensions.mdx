Treat x and y motion independently; they share only time.

$$v_{0x} = v_0\cos\theta \qquad v_{0y} = v_0\sin\theta$$

For a projectile (no air resistance):

$$x = v_{0x}t \qquad y = v_{0y}t - \tfrac12 gt^2$$

On level ground: time of flight $$\frac{2v_0\sin\theta}{g}$$, range $$\frac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$$.

Position vectors can also be given as functions, $$\vec r(t) = x(t)\hat i + y(t)\hat j$$; differentiate each component.

<Note title="Same time, different motion">
A ball dropped and a ball thrown horizontally from the same height land at the same time: horizontal motion does not affect vertical motion.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Horizontal launch">
A ball rolls off a 1.8 m table at 3.0 m/s. How far from the table does it land? (g = 9.8 m/s²)

1. Fall time: $$1.8 = \tfrac12(9.8)t^2$$, so $$t = 0.61$$ s.
2. $$x = 3.0 \times 0.61 = 1.8$$ m.

**Answer:** about 1.8 m.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A ball is launched from level ground at 26 m/s at 60° above the horizontal. Ignoring air resistance, what is its time of flight?

**Answer:** $$4.6\ \mathrm{s}$$

**Why:** Treat horizontal and vertical motion separately.

**Working:** v₀x = 13 m/s, v₀y = 22.5 m/s. T = 2v₀y/g = 4.6 s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$2.3\ \mathrm{s}$$: That is the time to the top; the flight is twice as long.
- $$2.65\ \mathrm{s}$$: Use the vertical component.
- $$5.31\ \mathrm{s}$$: Use v₀ sin θ.

### Worked exam question 2

A ball is launched from level ground at 24 m/s at 20° above the horizontal. Ignoring air resistance, what is its horizontal range?

**Answer:** $$37.8\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** Treat horizontal and vertical motion separately.

**Working:** v₀x = 22.6 m/s, v₀y = 8.21 m/s. R = v₀² sin 2θ/g = 37.8 m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$20.1\ \mathrm{m}$$: Range uses sin 2θ.
- $$18.9\ \mathrm{m}$$: Use the full time of flight.
- $$58.8\ \mathrm{m}$$: That is the maximum range, at 45°.

### Worked exam question 3

A ball is launched from level ground at 25 m/s at 37° above the horizontal. Ignoring air resistance, what is its maximum height?

**Answer:** $$11.5\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** Treat horizontal and vertical motion separately.

**Working:** v₀x = 20 m/s, v₀y = 15 m/s. H = v₀y²/(2g) = 11.5 m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$20.3\ \mathrm{m}$$: Use the vertical component, v₀ sin θ.
- $$23.1\ \mathrm{m}$$: H = (v₀ sin θ)²/(2g).
- $$31.9\ \mathrm{m}$$: Only the vertical component matters.

### Worked exam question 4

A ball rolls off a horizontal ledge 3 m high at 14 m/s. How far from the base of the ledge does it land?

**Answer:** $$11\ \mathrm{m}$$

**Why:** Fall time from the height, times the horizontal speed.

**Working:** t = √(2·3/9.8) = 0.782 s; x = 14·0.782 = 11 m.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$7.75\ \mathrm{m}$$: Fall time is √(2h/g).
- $$8.57\ \mathrm{m}$$: Take the square root for the time.
- $$20\ \mathrm{m}$$: Horizontal and vertical motion are independent.
