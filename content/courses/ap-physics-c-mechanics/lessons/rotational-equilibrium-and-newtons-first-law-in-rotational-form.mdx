An extended object is in equilibrium when

$$\sum \vec F = 0 \qquad \sum \tau = 0$$

about **any** axis. A rotating object with no net torque keeps a constant angular velocity (Newton's first law in rotational form).

## Strategy

1. Draw a force diagram showing where each force acts.
2. Pick an axis through an unknown force.
3. Sum torques; then sum forces.

<Note title="Seesaw balance">
Two children balance when m1d1 = m2d2.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Beam on supports">
A 4.0 m uniform beam weighing 200 N rests on supports at its ends. A 600 N person stands 1.0 m from the left end. Find the force from the right support.

1. Torques about the left end: $$F_R(4.0) = 200(2.0) + 600(1.0)$$.
2. $$F_R = 1000/4.0 = 250$$ N.

**Answer:** 250 N (the left support provides 550 N).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A uniform 5 m beam weighing 200 N rests on supports at both ends. A 400 N load sits 3 m from the left end. What force does the right support exert?

**Answer:** $$340\ \mathrm{N}$$

**Why:** Take torques about the left support.

**Working:** F_R·5 = 200·2.5 + 400·3, so F_R = 340 N.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$260\ \mathrm{N}$$: That is the left support's force.
- $$300\ \mathrm{N}$$: The load is not at the center.
- $$240\ \mathrm{N}$$: Include the beam's weight at its center.
