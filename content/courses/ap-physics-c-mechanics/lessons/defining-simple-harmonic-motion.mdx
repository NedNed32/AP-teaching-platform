SHM occurs whenever the net force is a linear restoring force:

$$F = -kx \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -\frac{k}{m}x = -\omega^2 x$$

Solution: $$x(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$$, where $$A$$ is the amplitude and $$\phi$$ the phase constant set by initial conditions.

Many systems are approximately SHM for small displacements: any smooth potential energy minimum looks like a parabola near the bottom.

<Note title="Identifying ω">
Rearrange the equation into d²x/dt² = −(something)x. The square root of that something is ω.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="From the equation">
A system obeys $$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -36x$$. Find its period.

1. $$\omega = 6$$ rad/s.
2. $$T = 2\pi/6 = 1.05$$ s.

**Answer:** about 1.05 s.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

An oscillator's position is $$x(t) = 0.09\cos(15t)$$ m. What is its maximum speed?

**Answer:** $$1.35\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** v_max = Aω.

**Working:** v_max = Aω = 1.35 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$20.2\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: That is a_max.
- $$0.09\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Multiply by ω.
- $$167\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: v_max = Aω.

### Worked exam question 2

An oscillator's position is $$x(t) = 0.12\cos(3t)$$ m. What is its maximum acceleration?

**Answer:** $$1.08\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$

**Why:** a_max = Aω².

**Working:** a_max = Aω² = 1.08 m/s².

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$0.36\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: That is v_max.
- $$3.24\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: Differentiate twice: Aω².
- $$9\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$$: Multiply by the amplitude.

**Q.** A system obeys $$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -9x$$. What is the period of its motion?

**A.** $$2.09\ \mathrm{s}$$. ω = √9 = 3 rad/s, T = 2π/ω. ω = 3 rad/s; T = 2π/3 = 2.09 s.
