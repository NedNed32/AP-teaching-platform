$$W = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} F(x)\,dx \qquad W = Fd\cos\theta\ \text{(constant force)}$$

Work is positive when the force has a component along the displacement, negative when opposite, and zero when perpendicular.

**Variable force**: integrate, or find the area under an F–x graph. Stretching a spring from 0 to $$x$$ takes $$W = \tfrac12 kx^2$$.

**Work–energy theorem**: $$W_{net} = \Delta K$$.

<Note title="Sign of friction work">
Kinetic friction does negative work on a sliding object, converting mechanical energy into internal (thermal) energy.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Variable force">
A force $$F(x) = 3x^2$$ N acts on an object from x = 1 m to x = 3 m. Find the work.

1. $$W = \int_1^3 3x^2\,dx = [x^3]_1^3$$.
2. $$27 - 1 = 26$$ J.

**Answer:** 26 J.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A force $$F(x) = 3x$$ N acts along the x-axis. How much work does it do on an object moving from $$x = 1$$ m to $$x = 2$$ m?

**Answer:** $$4.5\ \mathrm{J}$$

**Why:** W = ∫F dx.

**Working:** W = 3[x^2/2] from 1 to 2 = 4.5 J.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$3\ \mathrm{J}$$: Integrate; do not just evaluate F.
- $$6\ \mathrm{J}$$: The force is not constant.
- $$9\ \mathrm{J}$$: Divide by n + 1 when integrating.

### Worked exam question 2

A block starts from rest and slides 7 m down a 45° incline with coefficient of kinetic friction 0.2. What is its speed at the bottom?

**Answer:** $$8.81\ \mathrm{m/s}$$

**Why:** mgh − μmg cos θ·L = ½mv².

**Working:** h = 4.95 m. v = √(2g(h − μL cos θ)) = 8.81 m/s.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$11.7\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Use the vertical drop, L sin θ.
- $$9.85\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Friction removes energy.
- $$8.34\ \mathrm{m/s}$$: Friction depends on the normal force, mg cos θ.
