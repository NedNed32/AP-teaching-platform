Inflation is a rise in the **general** price level, not in one good's price. To measure it, economists price a fixed basket of goods over time.

## Building a price index

1. Choose a **market basket** of goods a typical consumer buys.
2. Price the basket in each year.
3. Divide by the cost in the **base year** and multiply by 100.

The base year always has an index of 100.

## Inflation rate

$$
\text{inflation rate} = \frac{P_{\text{this year}} - P_{\text{last year}}}{P_{\text{last year}}} \times 100.
$$

## Adjusting for inflation

To express a past amount in today's dollars:

$$
\text{value today} = \text{value then} \times \frac{\text{CPI today}}{\text{CPI then}}.
$$

## Why the CPI overstates inflation

- **Substitution bias:** people switch to cheaper goods, but the basket is fixed.
- **New products** are added late.
- **Quality improvements** are partly counted as price increases.

<Note title="Index level versus inflation">
A CPI of 120 does not mean 20 percent inflation this year; it means prices are 20 percent higher than in the base year. Inflation compares consecutive years.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A two-good basket">
A basket has 10 pizzas and 5 movie tickets. Prices were 8 and 10 in the base year, and 9 and 12 this year. Find the CPI and the inflation since the base year.

1. Base-year cost: $$10(8) + 5(10) = 130$$.
2. This year: $$10(9) + 5(12) = 150$$.
3. CPI: $$\frac{150}{130} \times 100 \approx 115.4$$.

**Answer:** CPI about 115.4; prices have risen about 15.4 percent since the base year.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A market basket contains 5 units of good A and 7 units of good B. Prices are shown.

*Prices*

| Good | Base year price | Current year price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 3 | 4 |
| B | 8 | 8 |

What is the price index for the current year (base year = 100)?

**Answer:** 107.0

**Why:** Current cost 76 ÷ base cost 71 × 100.

**Working:** Basket cost: base year $$5(3) + 7(8) = 71$$; current year $$5(4) + 7(8) = 76$$. Index $$= \frac{76}{71} \times 100 \approx 107.0$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 93.4: This divides the base-year cost by the current cost: upside down.
- 109.1: The index weights prices by the quantities in the basket.
- 7.0: This is the percentage change since the base year, not the index.

### Worked exam question 2

A price index rises from 227 in one year to 238 the next year. What is the inflation rate?

**Answer:** 4.8%

**Why:** $$\frac{238 - 227}{227} \times 100$$.

**Working:** Inflation rate = (238 − 227) ÷ 227 × 100 ≈ 4.8%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 11%: This is the change in index points, not the percentage change.
- 4.6%: Divide by the earlier year's index, not the later one.
- 138%: This compares with the base year, not with the previous year.
