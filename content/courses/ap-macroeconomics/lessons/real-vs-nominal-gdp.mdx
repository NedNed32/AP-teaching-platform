If nominal GDP rises 6 percent, did the economy produce 6 percent more? Not necessarily: part of the rise may be higher prices. Real GDP strips out price changes so that it tracks output alone.

## Two measures

- **Nominal GDP** uses each year's own prices.
- **Real GDP** uses the prices of a fixed **base year**.

## The GDP deflator

$$
\text{GDP deflator} = \frac{\text{nominal GDP}}{\text{real GDP}} \times 100 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{real GDP} = \frac{\text{nominal GDP}}{\text{deflator}/100}.
$$

## Growth rates

$$
\%\Delta\,\text{real GDP} \approx \%\Delta\,\text{nominal GDP} - \text{inflation rate}.
$$

<Note title="Deflator versus CPI">
The GDP deflator covers all domestically produced final goods and services, with weights that change each year. The CPI covers a fixed basket that consumers buy, including imports.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Real GDP from data">
An economy produces only apples. Base year: 100 apples at 2 each. Current year: 120 apples at 3 each. Find nominal GDP, real GDP and the deflator for the current year.

1. Nominal: $$120 \times 3 = 360$$.
2. Real (base-year price): $$120 \times 2 = 240$$.
3. Deflator: $$\frac{360}{240} \times 100 = 150$$.

**Answer:** nominal 360, real 240, deflator 150. Output grew 20 percent; the price level rose 50 percent.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

An economy's nominal GDP is 8,470 billion and its GDP deflator is 110. What is real GDP?

**Answer:** 7,700 billion

**Why:** $$\frac{8470}{110/100}$$.

**Working:** Real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ (deflator ÷ 100) = 8470 ÷ 1.1 = 7700 billion.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 9,317 billion: This multiplies by the deflator instead of dividing.
- 8,360 billion: The deflator is an index; divide by it (over 100), do not subtract it.
- 8,470 billion: Nominal and real GDP are equal only when the deflator is 100.

### Free-response practice: Measuring the economy

In an economy, 133 million people are employed, 8 million are unemployed, and 56 million adults are not in the labor force. Last year (the base year) the GDP deflator was 100; this year it is 104, and nominal GDP is 7,592 billion.

**(a)** Calculate the unemployment rate. Show your work.

To earn the point: Divides the unemployed by the labor force. Model answer: 8 ÷ (133 + 8) × 100 ≈ 5.7%

**(b)** Calculate the labor force participation rate.

To earn the point: Divides the labor force by the adult population. Model answer: 141 ÷ 197 × 100 ≈ 71.6%

**(c)** Calculate real GDP this year, in base-year prices.

To earn the point: Divides nominal GDP by the deflator over 100. Model answer: 7,592 ÷ 1.04 = 7,300 billion

**(d)** Some unemployed workers become discouraged and stop looking for work. What happens to the measured unemployment rate? Explain.

To earn the point: It falls, because discouraged workers leave the labor force and are no longer counted as unemployed. Model answer: Both the number unemployed and the labor force fall, and the rate decreases.
