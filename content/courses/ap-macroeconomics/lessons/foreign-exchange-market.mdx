The foreign exchange market is a supply-and-demand market where the good being traded is a currency. Its price is the exchange rate.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/fx-market.svg" alt="The market for dollars: supply and demand for dollars cross at E1; a dashed demand curve to the right crosses supply at E2, a higher price of the dollar." caption="An increase in demand for dollars appreciates the dollar." />

## Setting up the graph (market for dollars)

- Vertical axis: price of a dollar in a foreign currency (for example euros per dollar).
- Horizontal axis: quantity of dollars.
- **Demand for dollars:** foreigners buying US goods, services and assets.
- **Supply of dollars:** Americans buying foreign goods, services and assets.

## Linked markets

Every transaction in dollars and euros appears in both markets. If demand for dollars rises because Europeans want US goods, they must sell euros, so the **supply of euros** rises in the euro market. The dollar appreciates and the euro depreciates at the same time.

<Note title="Label the price carefully">
On the exam, write the axis as "price of dollars in euros" or "euros per dollar". An unlabeled or reversed axis loses the graph point.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Two linked markets">
Japanese consumers buy more American movies. What happens in the market for dollars and in the market for yen?

1. Japanese buyers need dollars: demand for dollars increases; the dollar appreciates.
2. To get dollars they sell yen: supply of yen increases; the yen depreciates.

**Answer:** demand for dollars rises (dollar appreciates); supply of yen rises (yen depreciates).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Ferrin's currency is the ferro. If consumers in Aldoria buy more goods made in Ferrin, what happens in the foreign exchange market for the ferro?

**Answer:** The demand for the ferro increases, and the ferro appreciates.

**Why:** Foreigners demand the currency to buy its goods and assets; residents supply it to buy foreign goods and assets.

**Working:** Foreign buyers change their purchases, so the demand for the ferro increases and the ferro appreciates.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- The demand for the ferro increases, and the ferro depreciates.: Identify who changes their behavior, foreigners (demand) or residents (supply), and in which direction.
- The supply of the ferro increases, and the ferro depreciates.: Identify who changes their behavior, foreigners (demand) or residents (supply), and in which direction.
- The demand for the ferro decreases, and the ferro depreciates.: Identify who changes their behavior, foreigners (demand) or residents (supply), and in which direction.

### Free-response practice: The foreign exchange market

Consumers in Corvana develop a strong preference for goods produced in Juvia. Juvia's currency is the juve; Corvana's is the corva.

**(a)** Describe a correctly labeled graph of the foreign exchange market for the juve, and show the effect of this change.

To earn the point: Labels the axes: price of the juve (for example corvas per juve) and quantity of juves. Model answer: Vertical axis: corvas per juve.

To earn the point: Shows the correct curve shifting in the correct direction. Model answer: Demand for juves shifts right.

**(b)** What happens to the value of the juve relative to the corva?

To earn the point: Correct direction. Model answer: The juve appreciates.

**(c)** Given your answer in part (b), what happens to Juvia's exports to Corvana? Explain.

To earn the point: Correct effect with reasoning about relative prices. Model answer: The appreciation makes Juvia's goods more expensive in corvas, so exports tend to fall from what they would otherwise be (partly offsetting the stronger tastes).
