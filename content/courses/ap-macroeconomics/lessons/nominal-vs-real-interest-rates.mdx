A 5 percent interest rate means very different things if prices are stable or rising 8 percent a year. The **real** interest rate measures what lenders actually gain in purchasing power.

## The relationship

$$
\text{real interest rate} = \text{nominal interest rate} - \text{inflation rate}.
$$

Rearranged: nominal = real + expected inflation. Lenders build **expected** inflation into the nominal rates they charge.

## When inflation surprises

| Actual inflation versus expected | Real rate earned | Who gains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| higher than expected | lower than planned | borrowers |
| lower than expected | higher than planned | lenders |

## Which rate matters where

- The **money market** (topic 4.5) determines the **nominal** rate.
- The **loanable funds market** (topic 4.7) determines the **real** rate.
- Investment decisions depend on the **real** rate: what borrowing truly costs.

<Note title="Negative real rates">
If inflation exceeds the nominal rate, the real interest rate is negative: savers lose purchasing power even as their account balance grows.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A surprise">
A loan is made at 6 percent nominal when inflation is expected to be 2 percent. Actual inflation is 4 percent. Find the expected and actual real rates, and say who benefits.

1. Expected real rate: $$6 - 2 = 4\%$$.
2. Actual real rate: $$6 - 4 = 2\%$$.
3. The lender earns less than planned.

**Answer:** expected 4 percent, actual 2 percent; the borrower benefits.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A savings account pays a nominal interest rate of 10%, and the inflation rate is 5%. What is the real interest rate?

**Answer:** 5%

**Why:** Real rate = nominal rate − inflation.

**Working:** Real interest rate = 10% − 5% = 5%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 15%: Inflation is subtracted, not added.
- -5%: The subtraction is reversed.
- 50%: The rates are subtracted, not multiplied.

### Worked exam question 2

A lender wants to earn a real return of 4% and expects inflation of 5%. What nominal interest rate should it charge?

**Answer:** 9%

**Why:** Nominal rate = real rate + expected inflation.

**Working:** Nominal rate = 4% + 5% = 9%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 4%: This ignores inflation; the lender would earn less in real terms.
- 5%: This covers inflation only, with no real return.
- -1%: Expected inflation is added, not subtracted.
