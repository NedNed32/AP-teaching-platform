The unemployment rate is the most-watched sign of how an economy is doing. To read it correctly you need to know who counts as unemployed, and why.

## Who is in the labor force

| Category | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Employed | anyone who did paid work, even part-time |
| Unemployed | no job, available, and actively looked in the past four weeks |
| Not in the labor force | students, retirees, homemakers, **discouraged workers** |

$$
\text{unemployment rate} = \frac{\text{unemployed}}{\text{employed} + \text{unemployed}} \times 100, \qquad \text{participation rate} = \frac{\text{labor force}}{\text{adult population}} \times 100.
$$

Discouraged workers stop looking, leave the labor force, and so make the official unemployment rate look **lower** than the true picture.

## Three types of unemployment

| Type | Cause | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frictional | time spent searching between jobs | a graduate looking for a first job |
| Structural | skills or locations no longer match jobs | a typesetter replaced by software |
| Cyclical | a downturn in the business cycle | factory layoffs in a recession |

The **natural rate** = frictional + structural. When cyclical unemployment is zero, the economy is at **full employment**, producing at potential output.

<Note title="Full employment is not zero unemployment">
There is always some frictional and structural unemployment, so the natural rate is positive (often around 4 to 5 percent in the United States).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Rates from population data">
Adult population 250 million; employed 150 million; unemployed 10 million. Find the unemployment and participation rates.

1. Labor force: $$150 + 10 = 160$$ million.
2. Unemployment rate: $$\frac{10}{160} = 6.25\%$$.
3. Participation rate: $$\frac{160}{250} = 64\%$$.

**Answer:** 6.25 percent unemployment, 64 percent participation.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

In a country, 97 million people are employed, 13 million are unemployed, and 69 million adults are not in the labor force. What is the labor force participation rate?

**Answer:** 61.5%

**Why:** $$\frac{110}{179} \times 100$$.

**Working:** Labor force = 110 million; adult population = 179 million. Participation rate = 110 ÷ 179 × 100 ≈ 61.5%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 54.2%: The labor force includes the unemployed, not only the employed.
- 113.4%: The denominator is the adult population.
- 38.5%: This is the share of adults not in the labor force.

### Worked exam question 2

In a country, 147 million people are employed, 14 million are unemployed, and 70 million adults are not in the labor force. What is the unemployment rate?

**Answer:** 8.7%

**Why:** $$\frac{14}{147 + 14} \times 100$$.

**Working:** Labor force = 147 + 14 = 161 million. Unemployment rate = 14 ÷ 161 × 100 ≈ 8.7%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 9.5%: The denominator is the labor force (employed + unemployed), not only the employed.
- 6.1%: People not in the labor force are excluded from the denominator.
- 91.3%: This is the share of the labor force that is employed.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A retiree stops working and does not look for a job. Which best describes this situation?

**A.** Not unemployed (not in the labor force). This matches the cause of the joblessness. Frictional unemployment comes from job search, structural from a mismatch between skills and jobs, and cyclical from recessions. People not looking for work are not in the labor force.

**Q.** Construction workers are laid off because a recession reduces spending on new homes. Which best describes this situation?

**A.** Cyclical. This matches the cause of the joblessness. Frictional unemployment comes from job search, structural from a mismatch between skills and jobs, and cyclical from recessions. People not looking for work are not in the labor force.

**Q.** A worker quits to look for a better-paying job in another city. Which best describes this situation?

**A.** Frictional. This matches the cause of the joblessness. Frictional unemployment comes from job search, structural from a mismatch between skills and jobs, and cyclical from recessions. People not looking for work are not in the labor force.

**Q.** Factory workers are laid off as overall demand falls during a downturn. Which best describes this situation?

**A.** Cyclical. This matches the cause of the joblessness. Frictional unemployment comes from job search, structural from a mismatch between skills and jobs, and cyclical from recessions. People not looking for work are not in the labor force.

**Q.** Bank tellers lose their jobs because customers switch to mobile banking. Which best describes this situation?

**A.** Structural. This matches the cause of the joblessness. Frictional unemployment comes from job search, structural from a mismatch between skills and jobs, and cyclical from recessions. People not looking for work are not in the labor force.

**Q.** Coal miners lose their jobs as power plants switch permanently to natural gas. Which best describes this situation?

**Q.** A recent college graduate is searching for a first job in her field. Which best describes this situation?

### Free-response practice: Measuring the economy

In an economy, 133 million people are employed, 8 million are unemployed, and 56 million adults are not in the labor force. Last year (the base year) the GDP deflator was 100; this year it is 104, and nominal GDP is 7,592 billion.

**(a)** Calculate the unemployment rate. Show your work.

To earn the point: Divides the unemployed by the labor force. Model answer: 8 ÷ (133 + 8) × 100 ≈ 5.7%

**(b)** Calculate the labor force participation rate.

To earn the point: Divides the labor force by the adult population. Model answer: 141 ÷ 197 × 100 ≈ 71.6%

**(c)** Calculate real GDP this year, in base-year prices.

To earn the point: Divides nominal GDP by the deflator over 100. Model answer: 7,592 ÷ 1.04 = 7,300 billion

**(d)** Some unemployed workers become discouraged and stop looking for work. What happens to the measured unemployment rate? Explain.

To earn the point: It falls, because discouraged workers leave the labor force and are no longer counted as unemployed. Model answer: Both the number unemployed and the labor force fall, and the rate decreases.
