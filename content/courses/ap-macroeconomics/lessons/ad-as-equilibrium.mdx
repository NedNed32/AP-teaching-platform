The AD–AS model combines spending (AD) with production (SRAS and LRAS). It is the central graph of AP Macroeconomics, and you will draw it on almost every free-response question.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/ad-as.svg" alt="An AD–AS graph with downward-sloping AD, upward-sloping SRAS and a vertical LRAS all crossing at one point E at full-employment output Yf." caption="Long-run equilibrium: AD, SRAS and LRAS meet at full-employment output." />

## Equilibrium

- **Short run:** AD meets SRAS, determining the price level and real GDP.
- **Long run:** that point also lies on LRAS, so the economy is at full employment.

## Gaps

| Position of short-run equilibrium | Gap | Unemployment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| left of LRAS | recessionary (negative) | above the natural rate |
| on LRAS | none | at the natural rate |
| right of LRAS | inflationary (positive) | below the natural rate |

<Note title="Labeling for full credit">
On the exam, label the axes **Price Level** and **Real GDP**, label each curve, mark the equilibrium price level and output (such as PL₁ and Y₁), and mark full-employment output Yf on the horizontal axis.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Reading a gap">
Equilibrium real GDP is 900 billion and potential output is 1,000 billion. Describe the gap and the labor market.

1. Output is 100 billion below potential: a recessionary gap.
2. Firms need fewer workers, so unemployment is above the natural rate (cyclical unemployment exists).

**Answer:** a recessionary gap of 100 billion, with unemployment above its natural rate.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Assume the economy is initially in long-run equilibrium. If nominal wages fall across the economy, what happens to the price level and real GDP in the short run?

**A.** Price level will decrease; real GDP will increase. SRAS shifts right along AD. The event shifts SRAS right. Along a downward-sloping AD, the price level and real GDP move in opposite directions.

**Q.** Assume the economy is initially in long-run equilibrium. If the country's currency depreciates, what happens to the price level and real GDP in the short run?

**A.** Price level will increase; real GDP will increase. AD shifts right along the upward-sloping SRAS. The event changes net exports, shifting AD right. Moving along SRAS, the price level and real GDP move in the same direction.

**Q.** Assume the economy is initially in long-run equilibrium. If a drought raises the price of agricultural inputs, what happens to the price level and real GDP in the short run?

**A.** Price level will increase; real GDP will decrease. SRAS shifts left along AD. The event shifts SRAS left. Along a downward-sloping AD, the price level and real GDP move in opposite directions (stagflation).

**Q.** The economy has a recessionary gap. Which of the following fiscal policies would be appropriate to close it?

**A.** a decrease in personal income taxes. Expansionary fiscal policy raises AD. A recessionary gap calls for expansionary fiscal policy: higher government purchases or transfers, or lower taxes, shift AD right.

**Q.** The economy has an inflationary gap. Which of the following fiscal policies would be appropriate to close it?

**A.** a decrease in government purchases. Contractionary fiscal policy lowers AD. An inflationary gap calls for contractionary fiscal policy: lower government purchases or higher taxes shift AD left.

### Free-response practice: Monetary policy in Corvana

Corvana has an open economy with flexible exchange rates. Its unemployment rate is currently 4%, and its natural rate of unemployment is 5%. Corvana's main trading partner is Bresk.

**(a)** Describe a correctly labeled AD–AS graph for Corvana, showing current output (Y₁), current price level (PL₁) and full-employment output (Yf). Is the economy in a recessionary or an inflationary gap?

To earn the point: Labels the axes (price level, real GDP) and the AD, SRAS and LRAS curves. Model answer: Vertical axis: price level; horizontal axis: real GDP; downward AD, upward SRAS, vertical LRAS.

To earn the point: Shows short-run equilibrium right of LRAS, with Y₁ above Yf. Model answer: Y₁ > Yf.

To earn the point: Identifies the gap. Model answer: An inflationary gap, because unemployment is below the natural rate.

**(b)** Identify the open market operation the central bank of Corvana should use to restore full employment.

To earn the point: Identifies the correct operation. Model answer: The central bank should sell government bonds.

**(c)** Describe a correctly labeled money market graph for Corvana and show the effect of the policy in part (b) on the nominal interest rate.

To earn the point: Labels the axes (nominal interest rate, quantity of money) with a vertical MS and downward MD. Model answer: Vertical axis: nominal interest rate; MS vertical; MD downward-sloping.

To earn the point: Shows MS shifting left and the nominal interest rate rising. Model answer: MS₁ to MS₂ left; i₁ to i₂ higher.

**(d)** Given your answer in part (c), what happens to real GDP and the price level in the short run? Explain.

To earn the point: Real GDP and the price level change in the correct direction. Model answer: Both fall.

To earn the point: Explains through interest-sensitive spending. Model answer: A higher interest rate reduces investment and consumption, shifting AD left.

**(e)** How will the change in the interest rate affect the value of the corva relative to Bresk's currency? Explain, and state the effect on Corvana's net exports.

To earn the point: Correct change in the currency's value with explanation. Model answer: The corva appreciates, because higher interest rates increase foreign demand for Corvana's financial assets.

To earn the point: Correct effect on net exports. Model answer: Net exports decrease.
