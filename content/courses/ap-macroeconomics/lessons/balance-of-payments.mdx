The balance of payments records every transaction between a country's residents and the rest of the world. It is double-entry bookkeeping for a whole nation.

## Two main accounts

| Account | Records | Credits (money flows in) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current account | goods, services, investment income, transfers | exports, income received from abroad |
| Financial account | purchases and sales of assets | foreigners buying domestic assets |

## Why it balances

Every dollar spent on imports must come back somehow: either as payment for exports or as foreigners buying domestic assets. So

$$
\text{current account} + \text{financial account} = 0.
$$

A **current account deficit** is matched by a **financial account surplus**: the country sells assets or borrows from abroad to pay for net imports.

<Note title="Credit or debit?">
Ask which way the money flows. Money coming into the country (exports, foreign purchases of domestic assets) is a credit; money leaving (imports, purchases of foreign assets) is a debit.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying transactions">
For the United States, classify: (I) a German tourist pays for a hotel in Chicago; (II) a Japanese investor buys US Treasury bonds; (III) an American buys shares in a French company.

1. (I) An export of a service: current account credit.
2. (II) Foreign purchase of a US asset: financial account credit (inflow).
3. (III) US purchase of a foreign asset: financial account debit (outflow).

**Answer:** current credit; financial credit; financial debit.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

An investor in Ferrin buys government bonds issued by Aldoria. How is this transaction recorded in Aldoria's balance of payments?

**Answer:** Financial account, as a credit (inflow)

**Why:** Goods, services and income go in the current account; asset purchases in the financial account. Money flowing in is a credit.

**Working:** Trade in goods and services is recorded in the current account; purchases and sales of assets in the financial account. Transactions that bring money into the country are credits.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Current account, as a credit: Decide whether goods or services (current account) or assets (financial account) change hands, then which way the money flows.
- Current account, as a debit: Decide whether goods or services (current account) or assets (financial account) change hands, then which way the money flows.
- Financial account, as a debit (outflow): Decide whether goods or services (current account) or assets (financial account) change hands, then which way the money flows.

### Worked exam question 2

A country's current account balance is a deficit of 80 billion. Ignoring any statistical discrepancy, what is its financial account balance?

**Answer:** A surplus of 80 billion

**Why:** The two accounts sum to zero.

**Working:** Current account + financial account = 0, so the financial account balance is +80 billion.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- A deficit of 80 billion: The financial account offsets the current account, so it has the opposite sign.
- Zero, because the accounts are independent: Every payment for net imports must be financed by a net inflow of capital, so the accounts are linked.
- A surplus of 160 billion: The offset is equal in size, not double.
