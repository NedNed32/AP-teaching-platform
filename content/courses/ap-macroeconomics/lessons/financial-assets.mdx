People hold wealth in different forms. Each financial asset trades off **liquidity** against **return**.

## Three assets

| Asset | Liquidity | Typical return | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Money (cash, checking deposits) | highest | little or none | low |
| Bonds | medium | fixed interest | moderate |
| Stocks | medium | dividends and price changes | higher |

## Bonds and interest rates move opposite

Suppose you hold a bond paying 50 a year, bought for 1,000 (5 percent). If new bonds now pay 10 percent, nobody pays 1,000 for your bond; its price falls until its 50 payment is a competitive return. So:

$$
\text{interest rates up} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{bond prices down}, \qquad \text{interest rates down} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{bond prices up}.
$$

## The opportunity cost of holding money

The **nominal interest rate** is the price of holding money: the interest given up by keeping wealth as cash instead of bonds. This idea drives money demand (topic 4.5).

<Note title="Assets versus liabilities">
For a bank, your deposit is a **liability** (the bank owes it to you) and a loan it makes is an **asset**. Keep this straight for the balance sheets in topic 4.4.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A bond price">
A bond pays 40 per year forever. If the market interest rate is 5 percent, about what will the bond sell for? What if rates fall to 4 percent?

1. At 5 percent: a price $$P$$ with $$\frac{40}{P} = 0.05$$ gives $$P = 800$$.
2. At 4 percent: $$\frac{40}{P} = 0.04$$ gives $$P = 1000$$.

**Answer:** about 800, rising to 1,000 when rates fall.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Market interest rates rise unexpectedly. Which of the following is most likely?

**A.** The price of existing bonds falls.. Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions. When market rates rise, new bonds pay more, so buyers pay less for existing lower-paying bonds.

**Q.** Market interest rates fall unexpectedly. Which of the following is most likely?

**A.** The price of existing bonds rises.. Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions. When market rates fall, existing bonds paying the old higher amount become more valuable.
