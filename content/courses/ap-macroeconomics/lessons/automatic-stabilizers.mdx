Some fiscal policy happens automatically. When the economy slows, tax collections fall and benefit payments rise without anyone voting on anything, cushioning the fall in spending.

## How they work

| Stabilizer | In a recession | In a boom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Progressive income tax | revenue falls faster than income | revenue rises faster than income |
| Unemployment insurance | payments rise | payments fall |
| Means-tested programs | more people qualify | fewer people qualify |

They make AD less volatile: in a downturn they leave households with more disposable income than they would otherwise have, and in a boom they take more away.

## Strengths and weaknesses

- **Strength:** no legislative or implementation lag.
- **Weakness:** they reduce the size of fluctuations but cannot by themselves eliminate a large gap.

<Note title="Budget balance moves with the cycle">
Because of automatic stabilizers, government budget deficits tend to grow in recessions and shrink in expansions even when no policy changes.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Spotting the stabilizer">
Which of these is an automatic stabilizer: (I) higher unemployment benefits paid as layoffs rise; (II) a new highway program passed to create jobs; (III) falling income-tax revenue as incomes drop?

1. (I) rises automatically with unemployment: yes.
2. (II) requires new legislation: discretionary.
3. (III) changes automatically with income: yes.

**Answer:** I and III.
</WorkedExample>

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which of the following is an example of an automatic stabilizer?

**A.** A progressive income tax whose revenue falls as incomes fall. It changes with the business cycle without new legislation. Automatic stabilizers are taxes and transfers that rise or fall automatically with income, cushioning the business cycle without new legislation.
