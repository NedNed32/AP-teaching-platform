Monetary policy is the central bank's management of the money supply and interest rates to stabilize prices and employment. In the United States the central bank is the Federal Reserve.

## The tools

| Tool | Expansionary (fight recession) | Contractionary (fight inflation) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open market operations | buy bonds | sell bonds |
| Discount rate | lower it | raise it |
| Reserve requirement | lower it | raise it |
| Interest on reserves | lower it | raise it |

## The chain of effects (expansionary)

$$
\text{buy bonds} \to \text{reserves up} \to \text{money supply up} \to \text{interest rate down} \to I, C \text{ up} \to AD \text{ right} \to \text{real GDP and price level up}.
$$

Contractionary policy runs the same chain in reverse.

## Two frameworks

- **Limited reserves:** reserves are scarce, so small open market operations move the federal funds rate.
- **Ample reserves:** banks hold plenty of reserves; the central bank sets **administered rates**, chiefly the interest on reserves, to steer the policy rate. This is how the Fed operates today.

<Note title="Who does what">
Monetary policy belongs to the central bank and works through interest rates. It does not involve government purchases or taxes; those are fiscal policy.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Fighting inflation">
An economy has an inflationary gap. Name an open market operation to close it and trace its effects.

1. Sell government bonds.
2. Bank reserves fall, the money supply shifts left, the nominal interest rate rises.
3. Investment and interest-sensitive consumption fall, so AD shifts left.

**Answer:** selling bonds lowers the price level and real GDP back toward full employment.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** The central bank lowers the discount rate. What happens to the nominal interest rate and aggregate demand in the short run?

**A.** Nominal interest rate will decrease; aggregate demand will increase. Expansionary policy lowers interest rates and raises investment. This is expansionary monetary policy: the money supply rises (or banks lend more), the nominal interest rate falls, investment and consumption rise, and AD shifts right.

**Q.** The central bank raises the interest rate paid on reserves. What happens to the nominal interest rate and aggregate demand in the short run?

**A.** Nominal interest rate will increase; aggregate demand will decrease. Contractionary policy raises interest rates and reduces investment. This is contractionary monetary policy: the money supply falls (or banks lend less), the nominal interest rate rises, investment falls, and AD shifts left.

**Q.** The central bank raises the discount rate. What happens to the nominal interest rate and aggregate demand in the short run?

**A.** Nominal interest rate will increase; aggregate demand will decrease. Contractionary policy raises interest rates and reduces investment. This is contractionary monetary policy: the money supply falls (or banks lend less), the nominal interest rate rises, investment falls, and AD shifts left.

**Q.** The central bank buys government bonds on the open market. What happens to the nominal interest rate and aggregate demand in the short run?

**A.** Nominal interest rate will decrease; aggregate demand will increase. Expansionary policy lowers interest rates and raises investment. This is expansionary monetary policy: the money supply rises (or banks lend more), the nominal interest rate falls, investment and consumption rise, and AD shifts right.

**Q.** The central bank lowers the interest rate paid on reserves. What happens to the nominal interest rate and aggregate demand in the short run?

### Free-response practice: Monetary policy in Corvana

Corvana has an open economy with flexible exchange rates. Its unemployment rate is currently 4%, and its natural rate of unemployment is 5%. Corvana's main trading partner is Bresk.

**(a)** Describe a correctly labeled AD–AS graph for Corvana, showing current output (Y₁), current price level (PL₁) and full-employment output (Yf). Is the economy in a recessionary or an inflationary gap?

To earn the point: Labels the axes (price level, real GDP) and the AD, SRAS and LRAS curves. Model answer: Vertical axis: price level; horizontal axis: real GDP; downward AD, upward SRAS, vertical LRAS.

To earn the point: Shows short-run equilibrium right of LRAS, with Y₁ above Yf. Model answer: Y₁ > Yf.

To earn the point: Identifies the gap. Model answer: An inflationary gap, because unemployment is below the natural rate.

**(b)** Identify the open market operation the central bank of Corvana should use to restore full employment.

To earn the point: Identifies the correct operation. Model answer: The central bank should sell government bonds.

**(c)** Describe a correctly labeled money market graph for Corvana and show the effect of the policy in part (b) on the nominal interest rate.

To earn the point: Labels the axes (nominal interest rate, quantity of money) with a vertical MS and downward MD. Model answer: Vertical axis: nominal interest rate; MS vertical; MD downward-sloping.

To earn the point: Shows MS shifting left and the nominal interest rate rising. Model answer: MS₁ to MS₂ left; i₁ to i₂ higher.

**(d)** Given your answer in part (c), what happens to real GDP and the price level in the short run? Explain.

To earn the point: Real GDP and the price level change in the correct direction. Model answer: Both fall.

To earn the point: Explains through interest-sensitive spending. Model answer: A higher interest rate reduces investment and consumption, shifting AD left.

**(e)** How will the change in the interest rate affect the value of the corva relative to Bresk's currency? Explain, and state the effect on Corvana's net exports.

To earn the point: Correct change in the currency's value with explanation. Model answer: The corva appreciates, because higher interest rates increase foreign demand for Corvana's financial assets.

To earn the point: Correct effect on net exports. Model answer: Net exports decrease.

### Free-response practice: Banking and the money supply

The required reserve ratio is 20%. A customer deposits 19,000 of currency into a checking account at First Bank. Assume banks hold no excess reserves before the deposit.

**(a)** By how much do First Bank's required reserves change? By how much do its excess reserves change?

To earn the point: Required reserves. Model answer: 0.2 × 19,000 = 3,800

To earn the point: Excess reserves. Model answer: 19,000 − 3,800 = 15,200

**(b)** What is the maximum amount by which the money supply can increase as a result of this deposit? Show your work.

To earn the point: Multiplies excess reserves by the money multiplier. Model answer: 15,200 × 5 = 76,000

**(c)** Explain why the actual increase in the money supply is likely to be smaller.

To earn the point: Banks may hold excess reserves, or the public may hold some money as cash (leakages). Model answer: If banks keep some excess reserves or borrowers withdraw cash, less is re-deposited and lent.

**(d)** If instead the central bank had bought this same amount of bonds from First Bank, would the maximum increase in the money supply be larger, smaller or the same? Explain.

To earn the point: Larger: the whole purchase is new excess reserves and new money. Model answer: Maximum increase 19,000 × 5 = 95,000.
