The loanable funds market brings together savers who lend and borrowers who want funds to invest. Its price is the **real** interest rate.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/loanable-funds.svg" alt="The loanable funds market: an upward-sloping supply of funds (saving) and a downward-sloping demand for funds (borrowing) crossing at the real interest rate." caption="Saving supplies funds; borrowing demands them; the real interest rate balances the two." />

## Who is on each side

| Supply (lenders) | Demand (borrowers) |
| --- | --- |
| household saving | business investment |
| government budget surpluses | government budget deficits |
| foreign capital inflows | |

## Shifts

| Event | Shift | Real interest rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Government deficit grows | demand right | rises |
| Business optimism rises | demand right | rises |
| Households save more | supply right | falls |
| Foreign capital flows in | supply right | falls |

<Note title="Two ways to show a deficit">
Some textbooks show a deficit as an increase in demand for loanable funds; others as a decrease in supply (less public saving). Either way the real interest rate rises. The AP exam accepts both if your graph is consistent.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A deficit and investment">
The government increases borrowing to finance new spending. What happens to the real interest rate and to private investment?

1. Demand for loanable funds shifts right.
2. The real interest rate rises.
3. Firms borrow less at the higher rate: private investment falls.

**Answer:** the real interest rate rises and private investment falls. This is crowding out (topic 5.5).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** In the loanable funds market, businesses expect higher future profits and plan more investment. What happens to the real interest rate and the equilibrium quantity of loanable funds?

**A.** Real interest rate will increase; quantity of loanable funds will increase. Demand for loanable funds increases. The event shifts the demand for loanable funds right. A demand shift moves the real rate and quantity in the same direction.

**Q.** In the loanable funds market, the government increases its budget deficit. What happens to the real interest rate and the equilibrium quantity of loanable funds?

**A.** Real interest rate will increase; quantity of loanable funds will increase. Demand for loanable funds increases. The event shifts the demand for loanable funds right. A demand shift moves the real rate and quantity in the same direction.

**Q.** In the loanable funds market, a tax incentive encourages households to save more. What happens to the real interest rate and the equilibrium quantity of loanable funds?

**A.** Real interest rate will decrease; quantity of loanable funds will increase. Supply of loanable funds increases. The event shifts the supply of loanable funds right. A supply shift moves the real rate and quantity in opposite directions.

**Q.** In the loanable funds market, foreign investors pull their funds out of the country. What happens to the real interest rate and the equilibrium quantity of loanable funds?

**A.** Real interest rate will increase; quantity of loanable funds will decrease. Supply of loanable funds decreases. The event shifts the supply of loanable funds left. A supply shift moves the real rate and quantity in opposite directions.

**Q.** In the loanable funds market, the government moves from a deficit to a budget surplus. What happens to the real interest rate and the equilibrium quantity of loanable funds?

**A.** Real interest rate will decrease; quantity of loanable funds will decrease. Demand for loanable funds decreases. The event shifts the demand for loanable funds left. A demand shift moves the real rate and quantity in the same direction.

**Q.** In the loanable funds market, households decide to save a larger share of their income. What happens to the real interest rate and the equilibrium quantity of loanable funds?

**A.** Real interest rate will decrease; quantity of loanable funds will increase. Supply of loanable funds increases. The event shifts the supply of loanable funds right. A supply shift moves the real rate and quantity in opposite directions.

**Q.** The government finances a large increase in spending by borrowing, while the economy is near full employment. Which of the following is most likely?

**A.** The real interest rate rises, private investment falls, and long-run growth slows.. Government borrowing raises demand for loanable funds, crowding out private investment. Deficit borrowing shifts demand for loanable funds right (or supply left), raising the real interest rate. Higher rates reduce private investment, slowing growth of the capital stock and of LRAS.

**Q.** A government runs persistent budget deficits financed by selling bonds to the public. Which of the following describes the likely long-run effect?

**A.** The real interest rate rises, private investment falls, and long-run growth slows.. Government borrowing raises demand for loanable funds, crowding out private investment. Deficit borrowing shifts demand for loanable funds right (or supply left), raising the real interest rate. Higher rates reduce private investment, slowing growth of the capital stock and of LRAS.
