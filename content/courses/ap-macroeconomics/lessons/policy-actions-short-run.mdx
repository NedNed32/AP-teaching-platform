Free-response questions rarely stop at one graph. A typical question starts with a gap, applies a policy, and asks you to trace the effects through two or three linked markets.

## The standard chains

**Expansionary monetary policy** (buy bonds):
money supply up, nominal interest rate down, investment up, AD right, real GDP and price level up.

**Expansionary fiscal policy** (raise G or cut taxes):
AD right, real GDP and price level up; government borrowing increases demand for loanable funds, raising the real interest rate and crowding out some investment.

## Combining policies

| Fiscal | Monetary | AD | Interest rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| expansionary | expansionary | right (certain) | indeterminate |
| expansionary | contractionary | indeterminate | up (certain) |
| contractionary | expansionary | indeterminate | down (certain) |
| contractionary | contractionary | left (certain) | indeterminate |

## Linking the graphs

- A higher **price level** increases money demand, raising the nominal interest rate.
- Higher **real GDP** also increases money demand.
- A change in **interest rates** affects capital flows and the exchange rate (Unit 6).

<Note title="Write the chain">
Graders reward an explicit chain: "Because the Fed buys bonds, the money supply increases, which lowers the nominal interest rate, which increases investment, shifting AD right." Each link earns or loses a point.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Tracing a fiscal expansion">
An economy is in a recession and the government increases purchases, financed by borrowing. What happens to AD, real GDP, the real interest rate and private investment?

1. Higher G shifts AD right: real GDP and the price level rise.
2. Borrowing raises demand for loanable funds: the real interest rate rises.
3. Higher interest rates reduce private investment (partial crowding out).

**Answer:** AD and real GDP rise, the real interest rate rises, and private investment falls.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** The government increases its purchases while the central bank buys government bonds. What happens to aggregate demand and the interest rate?

**A.** Aggregate demand will increase; the interest rate cannot be determined. Combine the effects of each policy. Fiscal policy: AD right, interest rate up (more or less borrowing). Monetary policy: AD right, interest rate down. Where the policies agree the result is certain; where they conflict it is indeterminate.

**Q.** The government increases its purchases while the central bank sells government bonds. What happens to aggregate demand and the interest rate?

**A.** Aggregate demand cannot be determined; the interest rate will increase. Combine the effects of each policy. Fiscal policy: AD right, interest rate up (more or less borrowing). Monetary policy: AD left, interest rate up. Where the policies agree the result is certain; where they conflict it is indeterminate.

**Q.** The government cuts its purchases while the central bank buys government bonds. What happens to aggregate demand and the interest rate?

**A.** Aggregate demand cannot be determined; the interest rate will decrease. Combine the effects of each policy. Fiscal policy: AD left, interest rate down (more or less borrowing). Monetary policy: AD right, interest rate down. Where the policies agree the result is certain; where they conflict it is indeterminate.

Monetary policy: AD left, interest rate up. Where the policies agree the result is certain; where they conflict it is indeterminate.

**Q.** An economy is in long-run equilibrium at full employment. The government permanently increases its purchases. After all adjustments, which of the following is true?

**A.** Real GDP returns to full-employment output and the price level is higher.. In the long run, output is set by LRAS; only the price level is higher. AD shifts right, creating an inflationary gap. Nominal wages then rise, SRAS shifts left, and output returns to full employment at a higher price level.
