GDP measures market production well. But a high GDP does not automatically mean people are better off, and the AP exam expects you to know why.

## What GDP leaves out

- **Non-market production:** cooking, cleaning, caring for family, volunteer work.
- **The underground economy:** unreported cash work and illegal transactions.
- **Leisure:** working longer hours raises GDP but may lower well-being.
- **Quality improvements and new products:** a phone today does far more than one at the same price years ago.
- **Environmental damage:** pollution and resource depletion are not subtracted.
- **Distribution:** GDP per capita is an average; it ignores inequality.

## Better comparisons

Use **real** GDP (adjusted for inflation) to compare years, and **real GDP per capita** to compare living standards across countries of different sizes. Even then, broader measures (health, education, environmental quality) complete the picture.

<Note title="Spending on disasters raises GDP">
Rebuilding after a hurricane adds to GDP, but nobody would call the hurricane good for the country. GDP counts activity, not well-being.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Comparing two countries">
Country A has GDP of 2,000 billion and 100 million people; country B has GDP of 600 billion and 20 million people. Which has the higher living standard by this measure?

1. A: $$\frac{2000}{100} = 20$$ thousand per person.
2. B: $$\frac{600}{20} = 30$$ thousand per person.

**Answer:** B has the higher GDP per capita, although A's total economy is larger.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which of the following is included in this year's GDP?

**A.** Military equipment purchased by the government. It is a final good or service produced domestically this year. GDP counts the market value of final goods and services produced within the country this year. It excludes used goods, intermediate goods, transfer payments, financial transactions, non-market work and production in other countries.

**Q.** Which of the following is NOT included in this year's GDP?

**A.** A car built in another country by a domestic firm. It is excluded: a used good, transfer, financial transaction, intermediate good, non-market activity or foreign production. GDP counts the market value of final goods and services produced within the country this year. It excludes used goods, intermediate goods, transfer payments, financial transactions, non-market work and production in other countries.
