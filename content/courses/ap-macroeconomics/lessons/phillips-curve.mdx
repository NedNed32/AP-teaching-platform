In the short run, policies that reduce unemployment tend to raise inflation, and vice versa. The Phillips curve shows this tradeoff, and the long-run version shows that it disappears once expectations adjust.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/phillips-curve.svg" alt="A downward-sloping short-run Phillips curve and a vertical long-run Phillips curve at the natural rate of unemployment." caption="SRPC slopes down; LRPC is vertical at the natural rate of unemployment." />

## Short run

The SRPC slopes **down**. When AD rises, output and prices rise together, so unemployment falls while inflation rises: a movement **up along** the SRPC.

## Long run

The LRPC is **vertical** at the natural rate of unemployment. Over time, people expect the higher inflation, wages adjust, and unemployment returns to its natural rate at a higher inflation rate.

## Mapping AD–AS to the Phillips curve

| AD–AS event | Phillips curve |
| --- | --- |
| AD increases | movement up and left along SRPC |
| AD decreases | movement down and right along SRPC |
| SRAS decreases (supply shock) | SRPC shifts right (up) |
| SRAS increases | SRPC shifts left (down) |
| LRAS shifts | LRPC shifts (natural rate changes) |

<Note title="Movement versus shift">
Demand changes move the economy **along** the SRPC. Supply shocks and changes in expected inflation **shift** the SRPC.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="An oil shock">
A sharp rise in oil prices hits the economy. Show the effect on the Phillips curve.

1. SRAS shifts left: the price level rises and output falls.
2. Higher inflation and higher unemployment together cannot be a move along the SRPC.
3. So the SRPC shifts right.

**Answer:** the SRPC shifts right, with higher inflation and higher unemployment (stagflation).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** If expected inflation rises, which of the following best describes the effect on the Phillips curve?

**A.** A rightward (upward) shift of the short-run Phillips curve. Demand changes move along the SRPC; supply shocks and expected inflation shift it. Changes in AD move the economy along the SRPC (inflation and unemployment move in opposite directions). Supply shocks and changes in expected inflation shift the SRPC.

**Q.** If expected inflation falls, which of the following best describes the effect on the Phillips curve?

**A.** A leftward (downward) shift of the short-run Phillips curve. Demand changes move along the SRPC; supply shocks and expected inflation shift it. Changes in AD move the economy along the SRPC (inflation and unemployment move in opposite directions). Supply shocks and changes in expected inflation shift the SRPC.

**Q.** If the price of oil rises sharply, shifting SRAS left, which of the following best describes the effect on the Phillips curve?

**A.** A rightward (upward) shift of the short-run Phillips curve. Demand changes move along the SRPC; supply shocks and expected inflation shift it. Changes in AD move the economy along the SRPC (inflation and unemployment move in opposite directions). Supply shocks and changes in expected inflation shift the SRPC.

**Q.** If aggregate demand increases, which of the following best describes the effect on the Phillips curve?

**A.** A movement up and to the left along the short-run Phillips curve. Demand changes move along the SRPC; supply shocks and expected inflation shift it. Changes in AD move the economy along the SRPC (inflation and unemployment move in opposite directions). Supply shocks and changes in expected inflation shift the SRPC.

**Q.** If productivity rises, shifting SRAS right, which of the following best describes the effect on the Phillips curve?

**A.** A leftward (downward) shift of the short-run Phillips curve. Demand changes move along the SRPC; supply shocks and expected inflation shift it. Changes in AD move the economy along the SRPC (inflation and unemployment move in opposite directions). Supply shocks and changes in expected inflation shift the SRPC.

**Q.** If aggregate demand decreases, which of the following best describes the effect on the Phillips curve?

**A.** A movement down and to the right along the short-run Phillips curve. Demand changes move along the SRPC; supply shocks and expected inflation shift it. Changes in AD move the economy along the SRPC (inflation and unemployment move in opposite directions). Supply shocks and changes in expected inflation shift the SRPC.

**Q.** An economy is in long-run equilibrium at full employment. The government permanently increases its purchases. After all adjustments, which of the following is true?

**A.** Real GDP returns to full-employment output and the price level is higher.. In the long run, output is set by LRAS; only the price level is higher. AD shifts right, creating an inflationary gap. Nominal wages then rise, SRAS shifts left, and output returns to full employment at a higher price level.

### Free-response practice: The Phillips curve

An economy is in long-run equilibrium with an unemployment rate of 5% and inflation of 2%. A sharp, lasting rise in the price of oil then hits the economy.

**(a)** Describe a correctly labeled graph with the short-run and long-run Phillips curves, labeling the current long-run equilibrium as point A.

To earn the point: Labels the axes (inflation rate, unemployment rate), a downward SRPC and a vertical LRPC. Model answer: LRPC vertical at 5%.

To earn the point: Point A where the SRPC crosses the LRPC. Model answer: A at (5%, 2%).

**(b)** Show the short-run effect of the event on the Phillips curve graph, labeling the new point B.

To earn the point: SRPC shifts right; B has higher inflation and higher unemployment. Model answer: B: unemployment above 5%, inflation above 2%.

**(c)** In the long run, what happens to the unemployment rate? Explain.

To earn the point: Returns to the natural rate, with an explanation based on wage or expectation adjustment. Model answer: Unemployment returns to 5%, because nominal wages and inflation expectations adjust, shifting SRAS and the SRPC.
