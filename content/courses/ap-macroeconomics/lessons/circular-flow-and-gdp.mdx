Macroeconomics studies the whole economy, so it needs a way to measure the whole economy's output. That measure is gross domestic product.

## The circular flow

Households own the factors of production and sell them to firms in **factor markets**, earning income. Firms use those factors to make goods and services, which households buy in **product markets**. Money flows one way around the circle and real resources and goods flow the other. Because every dollar spent is a dollar of someone's income, total spending equals total income equals total output.

## The expenditure approach

$$
GDP = C + I + G + X_n
$$

| Component | Includes | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consumption $$C$$ | household purchases of goods and services | largest component |
| Investment $$I$$ | new capital, new housing, inventory changes | not stocks or bonds |
| Government $$G$$ | government purchases of goods and services | not transfer payments |
| Net exports $$X_n$$ | exports minus imports | negative when imports exceed exports |

## Not counted in GDP

- **Intermediate goods** (counted inside the final good's price, to avoid double counting)
- **Used goods** (counted when new)
- **Financial transactions** (buying stocks or bonds)
- **Transfer payments**
- **Non-market and underground activity** (home cooking, illegal sales)
- Production by domestic firms **abroad** (that counts in the other country's GDP)

<Note title="Investment means capital">
In macroeconomics, investment is spending on new capital goods. Buying a share of stock is a financial transaction that moves ownership, not new production.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="GDP from a list">
Use the data (billions): consumption 600, purchases of stocks 80, business investment 150, government purchases 200, transfer payments 90, exports 70, imports 110. Find GDP.

1. Exclude stock purchases and transfer payments.
2. $$600 + 150 + 200 + (70 - 110) = 910$$.

**Answer:** GDP is 910 billion.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Selected data for an economy (in billions of dollars):

*National accounts data*

| Item | Billions |
| --- | --- |
| Gross private investment | 220 |
| Government transfer payments | 130 |
| Purchases of corporate stock | 80 |
| Consumption spending | 700 |
| Government purchases of goods and services | 260 |
| Exports | 110 |
| Imports | 230 |

What is the economy's GDP?

**Answer:** 1,060

**Why:** $$C + I + G + (X - M) = 700 + 220 + 260 + (110 - 230)$$.

**Working:** Using the expenditure approach and excluding transfers and stock purchases: GDP $$= 700 + 220 + 260 + 110 - 230 = 1060$$ billion.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1,190: Transfer payments are not purchases of goods or services, so they are excluded.
- 1,520: Imports are subtracted, not added.
- 1,140: Buying stock is a financial transaction, not production.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which of the following is included in this year's GDP?

**A.** Military equipment purchased by the government. It is a final good or service produced domestically this year. GDP counts the market value of final goods and services produced within the country this year. It excludes used goods, intermediate goods, transfer payments, financial transactions, non-market work and production in other countries.

**Q.** Which of the following is NOT included in this year's GDP?

**A.** A car built in another country by a domestic firm. It is excluded: a used good, transfer, financial transaction, intermediate good, non-market activity or foreign production. GDP counts the market value of final goods and services produced within the country this year. It excludes used goods, intermediate goods, transfer payments, financial transactions, non-market work and production in other countries.
