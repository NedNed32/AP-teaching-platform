"Deficit" and "debt" are often confused. The deficit is a flow over one year; the debt is a stock, the total owed.

## Flow and stock

$$
\text{debt}_{\text{end of year}} = \text{debt}_{\text{start of year}} + \text{deficit} \quad(\text{or} - \text{surplus}).
$$

A government finances a deficit by selling bonds; the debt is the stock of bonds outstanding.

## Why deficits happen

- **Recessions:** automatic stabilizers reduce tax revenue and raise transfer payments.
- **Expansionary fiscal policy:** deliberate spending increases or tax cuts.
- **Structural** spending commitments that exceed revenue at full employment.

## Concerns about a large debt

- **Interest payments** take up a growing share of the budget.
- **Crowding out:** government borrowing raises real interest rates and reduces private investment, slowing growth.
- Debt held by foreigners means interest payments leave the country.
- Less room to respond to the next recession.

<Note title="Measuring the burden">
Economists usually look at debt as a share of GDP. A debt that grows more slowly than GDP becomes a smaller burden over time.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Tracking the debt">
The debt starts at 10 trillion. Over three years the budget has a deficit of 0.8, a deficit of 0.5, then a surplus of 0.2 (trillions). What is the debt after three years?

1. $$10 + 0.8 + 0.5 - 0.2 = 11.1$$.

**Answer:** 11.1 trillion. The debt rose even though the deficit shrank and then turned into a surplus.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

At the start of the year, a government's debt is 1,600 billion. During the year it spends 300 billion (including transfers) and collects 160 billion in taxes. What is the debt at the end of the year?

**Answer:** 1,740 billion

**Why:** The deficit of 140 adds to the debt.

**Working:** Budget balance = taxes − spending = 160 − 300 = -140. Debt at year end = 1600 + 140 = 1740 billion.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1,460 billion: Sign error: a deficit increases the debt and a surplus reduces it.
- 140 billion: This is this year's deficit (or surplus), a flow, not the debt, a stock.
- 1,600 billion: The debt changes whenever the budget is not balanced.
