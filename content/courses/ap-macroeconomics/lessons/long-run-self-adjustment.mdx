If the government does nothing, does a recession last forever? In the AD–AS model, no: wages and input prices eventually adjust and the economy drifts back to full employment. The question is how long it takes.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/recessionary-gap.svg" alt="An AD–AS graph where AD and SRAS cross to the left of the vertical LRAS line, showing output below full employment." caption="A recessionary gap: equilibrium output is below Yf." />

## From a recessionary gap

1. Unemployment is above the natural rate, so workers accept lower nominal wages as contracts renew.
2. Lower wages cut costs: **SRAS shifts right**.
3. The price level falls and real GDP rises until output returns to potential.

## From an inflationary gap

1. Unemployment is below the natural rate; workers demand higher wages.
2. Higher costs: **SRAS shifts left**.
3. The price level rises further and output falls back to potential.

## Fast or slow?

Wages are sticky, especially downward, so self-adjustment from a recession can be slow and painful. That is the case for active fiscal and monetary policy (topics 3.8 and 4.6).

<Note title="Only SRAS moves">
In self-adjustment, AD and LRAS stay put. Only SRAS shifts, because the change comes from wages and other input prices.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Describing the adjustment">
An economy is in an inflationary gap. With no policy action, describe the long-run changes in nominal wages, SRAS, the price level and real GDP.

1. Tight labor markets push nominal wages up.
2. SRAS shifts left.
3. The price level rises; real GDP falls back to potential.

**Answer:** wages rise, SRAS shifts left, the price level rises, and real GDP returns to full-employment output.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** An economy is in an inflationary gap, and the government takes no policy action. Which of the following describes the long-run adjustment?

**A.** Nominal wages rise, and short-run aggregate supply shifts left.. Low unemployment pushes wages up, raising costs. With unemployment below the natural rate, nominal wages rise, SRAS shifts left, and output returns to potential at a higher price level.

**Q.** An economy is in a recessionary gap, and the government takes no policy action. Which of the following describes the long-run adjustment?

**A.** Nominal wages fall, and short-run aggregate supply shifts right.. High unemployment pushes wages down, lowering costs. With unemployment above the natural rate, nominal wages eventually fall, SRAS shifts right, and output returns to potential at a lower price level.
