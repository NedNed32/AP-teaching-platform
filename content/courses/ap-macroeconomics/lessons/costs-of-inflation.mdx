Inflation is not just higher prices: it changes who has purchasing power. Expected inflation can be built into contracts; **unexpected** inflation catches people by surprise and redistributes wealth.

## Winners and losers from unexpected inflation

| Hurt | Helped |
| --- | --- |
| Lenders and savers with fixed nominal rates | Borrowers with fixed nominal rates |
| People on fixed incomes (pensions without adjustment) | Those whose incomes rise faster than prices |
| Workers locked into fixed nominal wages | Employers paying those fixed wages |

The logic: a fixed nominal amount buys less when prices rise faster than expected.

## Other costs

- **Menu costs:** the cost of changing posted prices.
- **Shoe-leather costs:** time and effort spent minimizing cash holdings.
- **Uncertainty:** harder long-term planning, which can reduce investment.
- **Unit-of-account costs:** money is a less reliable measuring stick.

Deflation has costs too: it raises the real burden of debt and can encourage people to postpone spending.

<Note title="Real versus nominal">
A **nominal** value is in current dollars; a **real** value adjusts for the price level. The percentage change in a real value is approximately the nominal change minus the inflation rate.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A fixed-rate loan">
A bank lends at a 6 percent nominal rate, expecting 2 percent inflation. Inflation turns out to be 5 percent. What real return does the bank receive?

1. Expected real rate: $$6 - 2 = 4\%$$.
2. Actual real rate: $$6 - 5 = 1\%$$.

**Answer:** about 1 percent; the bank (lender) loses and the borrower gains relative to expectations.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Inflation turns out to be much higher than people expected. Which of the following is most likely to be hurt?

**A.** A worker whose nominal wage is fixed by contract. They receive fixed nominal amounts that buy less than expected. Unexpected inflation redistributes purchasing power from those who receive fixed nominal payments (lenders, savers, fixed-income earners) to those who make them (borrowers).

**Q.** Inflation turns out to be much higher than people expected. Which of the following is most likely to be helped?

**A.** A homeowner who took out a fixed-interest loan. They pay fixed nominal amounts with dollars that are worth less than expected. Unexpected inflation redistributes purchasing power from those who receive fixed nominal payments (lenders, savers, fixed-income earners) to those who make them (borrowers).
