Aggregate demand is the economy-wide version of demand: the total spending on domestic output at each **price level**.

## Why AD slopes downward

It is not the law of demand for one good. A lower **overall** price level raises the quantity of real GDP demanded through three effects:

- **Wealth (real balances) effect:** savings buy more, so people spend more.
- **Interest-rate effect:** people need less money for transactions, interest rates fall, and borrowing for investment and big purchases rises.
- **Exchange-rate (foreign purchases) effect:** domestic goods become cheaper relative to foreign goods, so net exports rise.

## Shifting AD

Anything that changes $$C$$, $$I$$, $$G$$ or $$X_n$$ at a given price level shifts AD.

| Component | Shifts AD right when... |
| --- | --- |
| Consumption | consumer wealth or confidence rises, taxes are cut |
| Investment | business expectations improve, interest rates fall |
| Government | government purchases rise |
| Net exports | foreign incomes rise, the domestic currency depreciates |

<Note title="Price level is on the axis">
A change in the price level is a movement along AD. Only the components of spending, changed by something other than the price level, shift the curve.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Shifting AD">
A stock market boom raises household wealth, and at the same time the government cuts purchases of military equipment. Can you tell how AD moves?

1. Higher wealth raises consumption: AD shifts right.
2. Lower government purchases: AD shifts left.
3. The net effect depends on the sizes of the two changes.

**Answer:** the direction of the AD shift is indeterminate without more information.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** As the price level falls, people need less money for transactions, interest rates fall, and investment rises. This is an explanation of the downward slope of aggregate demand known as

**A.** The interest-rate effect. This matches the channel described. AD slopes downward because of the wealth effect (real value of assets), the interest-rate effect (money demand and investment) and the exchange-rate effect (net exports).

**Q.** As the price level falls, the real value of households' savings rises, so they spend more. This is an explanation of the downward slope of aggregate demand known as
