When the government spends one more dollar, that dollar becomes someone's income. They spend part of it, which becomes someone else's income, and so on. Total spending rises by a multiple of the original change.

## The spending multiplier

$$
\text{multiplier} = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS}.
$$

Maximum change in real GDP $$= \text{multiplier} \times \text{initial change in spending}$$. This applies to changes in $$G$$, $$I$$, $$C$$ or $$X_n$$ that are not caused by income.

## The tax multiplier

A tax cut does not enter spending directly: households first save part of it. So

$$
\text{tax multiplier} = -\frac{MPC}{1 - MPC}.
$$

It is negative (tax cuts raise GDP) and smaller in size than the spending multiplier by exactly 1.

## The balanced-budget multiplier

Raising $$G$$ and taxes by the same amount increases real GDP by that same amount: the balanced-budget multiplier is 1.

<Note title="Closing a gap">
To close a recessionary gap of 400 with $$MPC = 0.8$$ (multiplier 5), government purchases must rise by $$\frac{400}{5} = 80$$. Using a tax cut instead requires $$\frac{400}{4} = 100$$, because the tax multiplier is only 4.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Multiplier with a tax change">
$$MPC = 0.9$$. The government cuts taxes by 30 billion. What is the maximum change in real GDP?

1. Tax multiplier: $$-\frac{0.9}{0.1} = -9$$.
2. Change in GDP: $$-9 \times (-30) = 270$$.

**Answer:** real GDP rises by up to 270 billion.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The marginal propensity to consume is 0.6. If the government cuts taxes by 60 billion, what is the maximum increase in real GDP?

**Answer:** 90 billion

**Why:** Tax multiplier $$\frac{0.6}{1 - 0.6} = 1.5$$ in size.

**Working:** Tax multiplier = MPC ÷ (1 − MPC) = 1.5. Maximum change = 1.5 × 60 = 90 billion.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 150 billion: This uses the spending multiplier; part of a tax cut is saved, so the tax multiplier is smaller.
- 36 billion: This is only the first round of extra spending.
- 60 billion: This ignores the multiplier process.

### Worked exam question 2

The marginal propensity to consume is 0.8. If government purchases increase by 80 billion, what is the maximum increase in real GDP?

**Answer:** 400 billion

**Why:** Spending multiplier $$\frac{1}{1 - 0.8} = 5$$.

**Working:** Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − 0.8) = 5. Maximum change = 5 × 80 = 400 billion.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 320 billion: This uses the tax multiplier; government purchases use the spending multiplier.
- 64 billion: This multiplies by the MPC instead of the multiplier.
- 160 billion: This doubles the change; the multiplier depends on the MPC.

### Worked exam question 3

Real GDP is 50 billion below full-employment output, and the MPC is 0.8. Assuming no crowding out, what increase in government purchases would close the gap?

**Answer:** 10 billion

**Why:** Divide the gap by the spending multiplier 5.

**Working:** Spending multiplier = 5. Required increase = 50 ÷ 5 = 10 billion.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 50 billion: The multiplier means government purchases need to rise by less than the gap.
- 40 billion: This multiplies by the MPC; divide by the multiplier instead.
- 12.5 billion: This is the tax cut that would be needed, using the tax multiplier.

**Q.** When disposable income rises by 1000, consumption spending rises by 750. What is the value of the spending multiplier?

**A.** 4. MPC = 750 ÷ 1000 = 0.75, so the multiplier is $$\frac{1}{1 - 0.75}$$. MPC = ΔC ÷ ΔYd = 0.75; MPS = 0.25; spending multiplier = 1 ÷ MPS = 4.

### Free-response practice: Fiscal policy in Istra

Istra is currently producing 140 billion below its full-employment output. The marginal propensity to consume is 0.5. The government plans to close the gap with a change in government purchases, financed by borrowing.

**(a)** Calculate the change in government purchases needed to close the gap, assuming no crowding out. Show your work.

To earn the point: Calculates the spending multiplier. Model answer: 1 ÷ (1 − 0.5) = 2

To earn the point: Gives the change in government purchases. Model answer: 140 ÷ 2 = 70 billion increase.

**(b)** If the government instead used a change in taxes, would the change in taxes need to be larger than, smaller than or equal to your answer in part (a)? Explain.

To earn the point: Larger, because part of a tax cut is saved, so the tax multiplier is smaller. Model answer: The tax multiplier is 1, less than 2, so a larger tax cut (140 billion) is needed.

**(c)** Describe a correctly labeled graph of the loanable funds market and show the effect of the government's borrowing on the real interest rate.

To earn the point: Labels the axes (real interest rate, quantity of loanable funds), with upward supply and downward demand. Model answer: Vertical axis: real interest rate.

To earn the point: Shows demand shifting right (or supply left) and a higher real interest rate. Model answer: D₁ to D₂; r₁ to r₂, higher.

**(d)** Given your answer in part (c), what happens to private investment? Explain how this affects long-run economic growth.

To earn the point: Private investment falls (crowding out). Model answer: A higher real interest rate makes borrowing for investment more expensive.

To earn the point: Links lower investment to slower growth. Model answer: Less investment means slower growth of the capital stock, so LRAS grows more slowly.

**(e)** Assume the policy closes the gap. What happens to the unemployment rate relative to the natural rate?

To earn the point: Unemployment falls to the natural rate. Model answer: Cyclical unemployment disappears; unemployment equals the natural rate.
