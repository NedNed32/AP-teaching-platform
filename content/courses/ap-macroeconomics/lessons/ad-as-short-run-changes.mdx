Shocks to spending or to costs move the short-run equilibrium. The direction of the change in the price level relative to output tells you which curve moved.

## Four shocks

| Shock | Price level | Real GDP | Unemployment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AD increases | up | up | down |
| AD decreases | down | down | up |
| SRAS increases | down | up | down |
| SRAS decreases | up | down | up |

**Demand shocks** move prices and output together. **Supply shocks** move them in opposite directions. A leftward SRAS shift produces **stagflation**: inflation and recession at the same time.

## Two shocks at once

As with supply and demand, combine the effects: one variable will be certain and the other indeterminate. For example, AD increasing and SRAS decreasing both raise the price level, while the effect on real GDP depends on the sizes.

<Note title="Always identify the curve first">
Read the event, decide which component or cost it affects, name the curve and direction, and only then read the new equilibrium. Most errors come from shifting the wrong curve.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A demand shock from abroad">
A recession in a major trading partner reduces its purchases of domestic goods. What happens to the domestic price level, real GDP and unemployment in the short run?

1. Exports fall, so net exports fall: AD shifts left.
2. Along SRAS: lower price level and lower real GDP.
3. Lower output means firms need fewer workers.

**Answer:** price level falls, real GDP falls, unemployment rises.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Assume the economy is initially in long-run equilibrium. If nominal wages fall across the economy, what happens to the price level and real GDP in the short run?

**A.** Price level will decrease; real GDP will increase. SRAS shifts right along AD. The event shifts SRAS right. Along a downward-sloping AD, the price level and real GDP move in opposite directions.

**Q.** Assume the economy is initially in long-run equilibrium. If the country's currency depreciates, what happens to the price level and real GDP in the short run?

### Free-response practice: The Phillips curve

An economy is in long-run equilibrium with an unemployment rate of 5% and inflation of 2%. A sharp, lasting rise in the price of oil then hits the economy.

**(a)** Describe a correctly labeled graph with the short-run and long-run Phillips curves, labeling the current long-run equilibrium as point A.

To earn the point: Labels the axes (inflation rate, unemployment rate), a downward SRPC and a vertical LRPC. Model answer: LRPC vertical at 5%.

To earn the point: Point A where the SRPC crosses the LRPC. Model answer: A at (5%, 2%).

**(b)** Show the short-run effect of the event on the Phillips curve graph, labeling the new point B.

To earn the point: SRPC shifts right; B has higher inflation and higher unemployment. Model answer: B: unemployment above 5%, inflation above 2%.

**(c)** In the long run, what happens to the unemployment rate? Explain.

To earn the point: Returns to the natural rate, with an explanation based on wage or expectation adjustment. Model answer: Unemployment returns to 5%, because nominal wages and inflation expectations adjust, shifting SRAS and the SRPC.
