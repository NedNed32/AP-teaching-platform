The money market is where the nominal interest rate is set: the price of holding money. Its graph is one of the three graphs every AP Macro student must draw from memory.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/money-market.svg" alt="The money market: a vertical money supply curve MS and a downward-sloping money demand curve MD crossing at the equilibrium nominal interest rate." caption="Vertical MS meets downward-sloping MD at the equilibrium nominal interest rate." />

## The curves

- **Money demand (MD)** slopes down: the higher the nominal interest rate, the more people prefer bonds to cash.
- **Money supply (MS)** is vertical, because the central bank sets it regardless of the interest rate.

## Shifts

| Change | Curve | Nominal interest rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price level rises | MD right | rises |
| Real GDP rises | MD right | rises |
| Central bank buys bonds | MS right | falls |
| Central bank sells bonds | MS left | rises |

## Linking to the economy

A lower interest rate encourages investment and interest-sensitive consumption, shifting AD right. This chain is how monetary policy works (topic 4.6).

<Note title="Nominal, not real">
Label the vertical axis **nominal interest rate** on the money market graph. The loanable funds graph uses the **real** interest rate.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A money demand shock">
Online payment apps make it easier to hold less cash. What happens to the money market?

1. People want to hold less money at every interest rate: MD shifts left.
2. With MS unchanged, the equilibrium nominal interest rate falls.

**Answer:** money demand decreases and the nominal interest rate falls.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** The central bank lowers the discount rate. What happens to the nominal interest rate and aggregate demand in the short run?

**A.** Nominal interest rate will decrease; aggregate demand will increase. Expansionary policy lowers interest rates and raises investment. This is expansionary monetary policy: the money supply rises (or banks lend more), the nominal interest rate falls, investment and consumption rise, and AD shifts right.

**Q.** The central bank raises the interest rate paid on reserves. What happens to the nominal interest rate and aggregate demand in the short run?

**A.** Nominal interest rate will increase; aggregate demand will decrease. Contractionary policy raises interest rates and reduces investment. This is contractionary monetary policy: the money supply falls (or banks lend less), the nominal interest rate rises, investment falls, and AD shifts left.

**Q.** The central bank raises the discount rate. What happens to the nominal interest rate and aggregate demand in the short run?

**Q.** In the money market, the central bank sells government bonds. Which of the following is true?

**A.** Money supply decreases, and the nominal interest rate rises.. Open market operations change the money supply. The event shifts money supply left; with the other curve fixed, the equilibrium nominal interest rate rises.

**Q.** In the money market, real GDP falls during a recession. Which of the following is true?

**A.** Money demand decreases, and the nominal interest rate falls.. Changes in the price level or real GDP change how much money people want to hold. The event shifts money demand left; with the other curve fixed, the equilibrium nominal interest rate falls.

**Q.** In the money market, the price level rises. Which of the following is true?

**A.** Money demand increases, and the nominal interest rate rises.. Changes in the price level or real GDP change how much money people want to hold. The event shifts money demand right; with the other curve fixed, the equilibrium nominal interest rate rises.
