Governments cannot command growth, but they can create conditions for it. Growth policies target the supply side: the quantity and quality of resources and the incentives to use them productively.

## Policies that raise potential output

| Policy | Channel |
| --- | --- |
| Public investment in infrastructure | more public capital raises private productivity |
| Education and training subsidies | more human capital |
| Research and development support | faster technological progress |
| Incentives to save and invest | more private capital (lower interest rates, tax credits) |
| Strong property rights and rule of law | secure returns on investment |
| Free trade | specialization by comparative advantage |

## Demand-side versus supply-side

- **Demand-side** policies (most fiscal and monetary policy) shift AD and target the business cycle.
- **Supply-side** policies shift LRAS and target long-run growth.

Some policies do both: infrastructure spending raises AD now and LRAS later.

<Note title="Deficits can work against growth">
Financing growth policies with persistent deficits risks crowding out private investment (topic 5.5). The net effect on growth depends on whether public investment adds more capacity than the private investment it displaces.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying a policy">
A government subsidizes college tuition. Explain its short-run and long-run effects.

1. Short run: higher government spending (or transfers) raises AD.
2. Long run: more educated workers increase human capital and productivity.
3. Higher productivity shifts LRAS right.

**Answer:** AD rises in the short run; LRAS shifts right in the long run, raising potential output.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which of the following policies is most likely to increase long-run economic growth?

**A.** Subsidies for research and development. It increases physical capital, human capital or technology, shifting LRAS right. Long-run growth comes from more and better physical capital, human capital and technology. Policies that only change spending in the short run do not shift LRAS.
