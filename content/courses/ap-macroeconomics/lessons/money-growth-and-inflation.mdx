Why do some countries suffer runaway inflation? In the long run, persistent inflation comes from one source: the money supply growing faster than real output.

## The equation of exchange

$$
M \times V = P \times Y
$$

- $$M$$: money supply
- $$V$$: velocity, the number of times a dollar is spent per year
- $$P$$: price level
- $$Y$$: real output, so $$PY$$ is nominal GDP

## The quantity theory

In the long run, $$V$$ is roughly stable and $$Y$$ is fixed at potential output. So changes in $$M$$ show up in $$P$$. In growth rates:

$$
\%\Delta M + \%\Delta V \approx \%\Delta P + \%\Delta Y.
$$

**Money is neutral in the long run:** it affects nominal variables (prices) but not real ones (output, employment).

<Note title="Hyperinflation">
Governments that finance spending by printing money see extremely high inflation, because money grows far faster than output. That is why independent central banks matter.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Inflation from money growth">
Velocity is constant, real GDP grows 2 percent and the money supply grows 10 percent. Find the approximate inflation rate.

1. $$10 + 0 \approx \%\Delta P + 2$$.

**Answer:** inflation is about 8 percent.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Assume velocity is constant. If the money supply grows by 7% per year and real output grows by 3% per year, what is the approximate inflation rate according to the quantity theory of money?

**Answer:** 4%

**Why:** %ΔM + %ΔV = %ΔP + %ΔY.

**Working:** With constant velocity: 7% ≈ inflation + 3%, so inflation ≈ 4%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 10%: Real output growth absorbs part of the money growth; subtract it.
- 7%: This ignores the growth in real output.
- 3%: This is the growth rate of real output.

### Worked exam question 2

In an economy, the money supply is 300, velocity is 5 and real output is 200. According to the equation of exchange, what is the price level?

**Answer:** 7.5

**Why:** $$P = \frac{MV}{Y}$$.

**Working:** $$MV = PY$$, so $$P = \frac{300 \times 5}{200} = 7.5$$.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 12,000: Rearrange MV = PY for P: divide by Y.
- 1.5: Velocity must be included.
- 5: This is velocity, not the price level.
