Short-run policy smooths the business cycle. Long-run growth decides whether living standards double in a generation or stagnate. Small differences in growth rates compound into enormous differences.

## Showing growth

- PPC shifts **outward**.
- LRAS shifts **right**.
- Real GDP per capita rises over time.

## Sources of productivity growth

| Source | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Physical capital | more and better machines, infrastructure |
| Human capital | education, training, health |
| Technology | new production methods and inventions |
| Natural resources | discovery or better use of resources |

Institutions such as secure **property rights**, rule of law and stable markets make investment in all of these worthwhile.

## The rule of 70

$$
\text{doubling time} \approx \frac{70}{\text{growth rate (percent)}}.
$$

At 1 percent a year, income doubles in 70 years; at 7 percent, in only 10.

<Note title="Growth versus recovery">
A rise in real GDP when an economy recovers from a recession is a movement toward the PPC, not growth of the PPC itself. Growth means potential output increases.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Comparing growth rates">
Country A grows at 1.4 percent a year and country B at 3.5 percent. How long does each take to double its real GDP per capita?

1. A: $$\frac{70}{1.4} = 50$$ years.
2. B: $$\frac{70}{3.5} = 20$$ years.

**Answer:** 50 years for A, 20 years for B.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Real GDP per capita grows at a constant 2.5% per year. Using the rule of 70, about how many years will it take to double?

**A.** 28 years. 70 ÷ 2.5. Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate = 70 ÷ 2.5 ≈ 28 years.

**Q.** Real GDP per capita grows at a constant 1.4% per year. Using the rule of 70, about how many years will it take to double?

**A.** 50 years. 70 ÷ 1.4. Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate = 70 ÷ 1.4 ≈ 50 years.

**Q.** Real GDP per capita grows at a constant 3.5% per year. Using the rule of 70, about how many years will it take to double?

**A.** 20 years. 70 ÷ 3.5. Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate = 70 ÷ 3.5 ≈ 20 years.

**Q.** Real GDP per capita grows at a constant 5% per year. Using the rule of 70, about how many years will it take to double?

**A.** 14 years. 70 ÷ 5. Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate = 70 ÷ 5 ≈ 14 years.

**Q.** Real GDP per capita grows at a constant 2% per year. Using the rule of 70, about how many years will it take to double?

**A.** 35 years. 70 ÷ 2. Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate = 70 ÷ 2 ≈ 35 years.

**Q.** Real GDP per capita grows at a constant 7% per year. Using the rule of 70, about how many years will it take to double?

**A.** 10 years. 70 ÷ 7. Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate = 70 ÷ 7 ≈ 10 years.

**Q.** Which of the following policies is most likely to increase long-run economic growth?

**A.** Subsidies for research and development. It increases physical capital, human capital or technology, shifting LRAS right. Long-run growth comes from more and better physical capital, human capital and technology. Policies that only change spending in the short run do not shift LRAS.
