Financial capital moves around the world looking for the best return. When one country's real interest rate rises, funds flow toward it, and that changes both its loanable funds market and its exchange rate.

## The chain

$$
\text{real interest rate up} \to \text{capital inflow} \to \text{demand for currency up} \to \text{currency appreciates}.
$$

The inflow also **increases the supply of loanable funds** in the receiving country, which moderates the rise in its interest rate.

## In the sending country

Funds leave: the supply of loanable funds falls (its real interest rate rises a little), and its currency depreciates as investors sell it.

## Putting the graphs together

| Graph | Receiving country (higher rates) |
| --- | --- |
| Loanable funds | supply shifts right |
| Foreign exchange (its currency) | demand shifts right; currency appreciates |
| Net exports | fall, because of the appreciation |

<Note title="Real interest rate, not nominal">
International investors care about real returns. A country with high nominal rates but even higher inflation does not attract capital.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A deficit at home">
A large US budget deficit raises the US real interest rate. Trace the international effects.

1. Higher real rates attract foreign capital into the United States.
2. Demand for dollars rises: the dollar appreciates.
3. A stronger dollar reduces US net exports.
4. The capital inflow adds to the US supply of loanable funds.

**Answer:** capital inflow, dollar appreciation, lower net exports, and more loanable funds supply in the US.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The real interest rate in Ferrin rises relative to the real interest rate in Bresk. Which of the following is most likely?

**Answer:** Financial capital flows into Ferrin, and its currency appreciates.

**Why:** Investors seek higher real returns, increasing demand for the currency.

**Working:** Higher real interest rates attract foreign financial investment into Ferrin. Foreigners must buy Ferrin's currency, so it appreciates, and the inflow adds to Ferrin's supply of loanable funds.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Financial capital flows out of Ferrin, and its currency depreciates.: Higher real returns attract capital rather than repelling it.
- Financial capital flows into Ferrin, and its currency depreciates.: Capital inflows increase demand for the currency, so it appreciates.
- The supply of loanable funds in Ferrin decreases.: Capital inflows add to the supply of loanable funds.
