In the long run, all prices, including wages, adjust. Then output depends only on the economy's real resources and technology, not on the price level. That is why the LRAS curve is **vertical**.

## The vertical line

LRAS sits at **potential (full-employment) output**, where unemployment equals its natural rate. Doubling every price and wage leaves real production unchanged.

## LRAS and the PPC

A point on the PPC and the LRAS line describe the same thing: the economy using all its resources efficiently. Anything that shifts the PPC outward shifts LRAS right.

## What shifts LRAS

- Quantity of resources: labor force, capital stock, natural resources.
- Quality of resources: **human capital** (education and training).
- **Technology** and productivity.
- Institutions that support production, such as property rights.

<Note title="Not demand">
Changes in aggregate demand cannot shift LRAS in the long run. A spending boom may push output above potential temporarily, but the economy returns to the LRAS line.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Which curves move?">
A country invests heavily in public education, and years later its workforce is more skilled. How are SRAS and LRAS affected?

1. Higher human capital raises productivity.
2. Higher productivity lowers costs per unit (SRAS right) and raises potential output (LRAS right).

**Answer:** both SRAS and LRAS shift right, and the PPC shifts outward.
</WorkedExample>
