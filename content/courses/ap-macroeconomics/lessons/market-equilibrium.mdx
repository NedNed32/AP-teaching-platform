Put supply and demand on one graph and they cross at the **equilibrium**: the price at which the amount buyers want equals the amount sellers offer.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/supply-demand-shift.svg" alt="A supply curve and a demand curve crossing at E1; a second, dashed demand curve to the right crosses supply at E2, higher and to the right." caption="An increase in demand raises both equilibrium price and quantity." />

## Getting to equilibrium

- **Below** equilibrium: a **shortage**. Buyers compete for scarce goods and the price rises.
- **Above** equilibrium: a **surplus**. Sellers cut prices to clear unsold goods.

## One shift

| Change | Price | Quantity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand increases | up | up |
| Demand decreases | down | down |
| Supply increases | down | up |
| Supply decreases | up | down |

## Two shifts

When both curves shift, one variable is certain and the other is **indeterminate** (it depends on which shift is larger). Work out each shift separately and see where they agree.

| Shifts | Certain | Indeterminate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D up, S up | quantity up | price |
| D down, S down | quantity down | price |
| D up, S down | price up | quantity |
| D down, S up | price down | quantity |

<Note title="Draw before you answer">
Even a rough sketch prevents most errors. Shift one curve, mark the new equilibrium, then shift the other.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Two shifts in the coffee market">
A frost destroys part of the coffee harvest, and a study reports health benefits of coffee. What happens to the equilibrium price and quantity of coffee?

1. Frost: supply decreases, pushing price up and quantity down.
2. Health news: demand increases, pushing price up and quantity up.
3. Both push price up; their effects on quantity conflict.

**Answer:** the price rises; the change in quantity is indeterminate.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** In the market for movie tickets, the price of streaming subscriptions (a substitute) rises. What happens to the equilibrium price and quantity of movie tickets?

**A.** Price will increase; quantity will increase. A pricier substitute increases demand. A pricier substitute increases demand: demand increases. A demand shift moves price and quantity in the same direction.

**Q.** In the market for smartphones, many new firms enter the market. What happens to the equilibrium price and quantity of smartphones?

**A.** Price will decrease; quantity will increase. More sellers increase supply. More sellers increase supply: supply increases. A supply shift moves price and quantity in opposite directions.

**Q.** In the market for sugar, the government places a per-unit tax on sugar producers. What happens to the equilibrium price and quantity of sugar?

**A.** Price will increase; quantity will decrease. A per-unit tax raises costs, decreasing supply. A per-unit tax raises costs, decreasing supply: supply decreases. A supply shift moves price and quantity in opposite directions.

**Q.** In the market for lumber, the wage paid to sawmill workers rises. What happens to the equilibrium price and quantity of lumber?

**A.** Price will increase; quantity will decrease. A higher input price decreases supply. A higher input price decreases supply: supply decreases. A supply shift moves price and quantity in opposite directions.

**Q.** In a competitive market, demand decreases and, at the same time, supply increases. Which of the following describes the change in equilibrium?

**A.** Price will decrease; quantity cannot be determined. Shift each curve separately: one variable moves the same way in both cases, the other depends on the sizes of the shifts. The demand shift alone moves price down and quantity down. The supply shift alone moves price down and quantity up. Where the effects agree the result is certain; where they conflict it is indeterminate.
