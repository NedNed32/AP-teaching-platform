Money is whatever people generally accept in exchange. Its value comes from what it does, not what it is made of.

## Three functions

| Function | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Medium of exchange | used to buy goods and services |
| Unit of account | used to measure and compare value (prices) |
| Store of value | holds purchasing power over time |

Modern money is **fiat money**: valuable because the government declares it legal tender and people trust it, not because it is backed by gold.

## Measuring the money supply

| Measure | Includes |
| --- | --- |
| M1 | currency, checkable deposits, savings deposits and other liquid deposits |
| M2 | M1 plus small-denomination time deposits and retail money market funds |

M1 is the most liquid; M2 is broader. Stocks, bonds and credit cards are **not** money.

<Note title="Moving money between accounts">
Shifting funds between two forms that are both in M1 (cash to checking) changes nothing. Moving from an M1 component to an M2-only component (checking to a CD) lowers M1 but leaves M2 unchanged.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Computing M1 and M2">
Currency 1,500; checkable deposits 2,000; savings deposits 4,000; small time deposits 1,000; retail money market funds 700; stocks 5,000. Find M1 and M2 (using the current definitions).

1. M1: $$1500 + 2000 + 4000 = 7500$$.
2. M2: $$7500 + 1000 + 700 = 9200$$.
3. Stocks are excluded.

**Answer:** M1 = 7,500, M2 = 9,200.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A household moves 1400 from a checking account to a small-denomination certificate of deposit. Which of the following is true?

**Answer:** M1 decreases by 1400, and M2 is unchanged.

**Why:** Check which measures each asset belongs to.

**Working:** M1 includes currency, checkable deposits and savings deposits. M2 adds small time deposits and retail money market funds. Moving funds within M1 changes nothing; moving from M1 to an M2-only asset lowers M1 but not M2.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- M1 is unchanged, and M2 increases by 1400.: Currency, checkable and savings deposits are in M1; small time deposits are in M2 only; stocks are not money.
- Both M1 and M2 are unchanged, because the money goes to the seller of the stock.: Currency, checkable and savings deposits are in M1; small time deposits are in M2 only; stocks are not money.
- Both M1 and M2 are unchanged.: Currency, checkable and savings deposits are in M1; small time deposits are in M2 only; stocks are not money.
