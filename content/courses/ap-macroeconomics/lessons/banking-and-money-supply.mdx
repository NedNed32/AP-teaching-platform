Banks create money. When a bank lends out part of a deposit, the borrower spends it, it is deposited in another bank, and that bank lends part again. Deposits multiply through the system.

## The balance sheet

| Assets | Liabilities |
| --- | --- |
| Required reserves | Demand deposits |
| Excess reserves | Owner's equity |
| Loans, securities | |

$$
\text{required reserves} = \text{reserve ratio} \times \text{deposits}, \qquad \text{excess reserves} = \text{total reserves} - \text{required reserves}.
$$

A single bank can lend up to its **excess reserves**.

## The money multiplier

$$
\text{money multiplier} = \frac{1}{\text{reserve ratio}}.
$$

Maximum change in **demand deposits** $$= \text{excess reserves} \times \text{multiplier}$$.

## Is the deposit new money?

- A **cash deposit** of 1,000 converts currency into deposits: M1 is unchanged at that moment. The money supply can then grow by the lending: $$\text{excess reserves} \times \text{multiplier}$$.
- When the **central bank buys bonds** from a bank, it injects new reserves, and the full injection can be multiplied.

<Note title="Why the real multiplier is smaller">
The formula gives a maximum. Banks may keep extra reserves and people may hold some loans as cash, so the actual expansion is smaller.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A cash deposit">
The reserve requirement is 10 percent. Someone deposits 5,000 in cash. Find the bank's new excess reserves, the maximum new loans by the whole banking system, and the maximum change in the money supply.

1. Required reserves on the deposit: $$0.10 \times 5000 = 500$$; excess reserves: 4,500.
2. Multiplier: 10. Maximum new deposits from lending: $$4500 \times 10 = 45000$$.
3. The cash deposit itself did not change M1, so the maximum change in the money supply is 45,000.

**Answer:** excess reserves 4,500; up to 45,000 increase in the money supply.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The required reserve ratio is 10% and banks hold no excess reserves. The central bank buys 17,000 of government bonds from a commercial bank. What is the maximum possible increase in the money supply?

**Answer:** 170,000

**Why:** The whole purchase adds excess reserves of 17,000, multiplied by 10.

**Working:** Buying bonds from a bank gives it 17,000 of new excess reserves. Maximum change in the money supply = 17,000 × 10 = 170,000.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 153,000: When the central bank buys bonds from a bank, all of the payment is new excess reserves; nothing is required against it.
- 17,000: This ignores the money multiplier.
- 1,700: This is the reserve ratio times the purchase.

### Worked exam question 2

A bank's balance sheet is shown. The required reserve ratio is 5%.

*Balance sheet*

| Assets | Amount | Liabilities | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reserves | 1035 | Demand deposits | 5700 |
| Loans | 3600 | Owner's equity | -1065 |

What is the maximum amount this bank can lend immediately?

**Answer:** 750

**Why:** Its excess reserves: 1,035 − 285.

**Working:** Required reserves = 0.05 × 5,700 = 285. Excess reserves = 1,035 − 285 = 750, which is the most the bank can lend.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1,035: Required reserves cannot be lent.
- 285: This is the required reserve amount.
- 4,665: Lending capacity comes from reserves above the requirement, not deposits minus reserves.

### Free-response practice: Banking and the money supply

The required reserve ratio is 20%. A customer deposits 19,000 of currency into a checking account at First Bank. Assume banks hold no excess reserves before the deposit.

**(a)** By how much do First Bank's required reserves change? By how much do its excess reserves change?

To earn the point: Required reserves. Model answer: 0.2 × 19,000 = 3,800

To earn the point: Excess reserves. Model answer: 19,000 − 3,800 = 15,200

**(b)** What is the maximum amount by which the money supply can increase as a result of this deposit? Show your work.

To earn the point: Multiplies excess reserves by the money multiplier. Model answer: 15,200 × 5 = 76,000

**(c)** Explain why the actual increase in the money supply is likely to be smaller.

To earn the point: Banks may hold excess reserves, or the public may hold some money as cash (leakages). Model answer: If banks keep some excess reserves or borrowers withdraw cash, less is re-deposited and lent.

**(d)** If instead the central bank had bought this same amount of bonds from First Bank, would the maximum increase in the money supply be larger, smaller or the same? Explain.

To earn the point: Larger: the whole purchase is new excess reserves and new money. Model answer: Maximum increase 19,000 × 5 = 95,000.
