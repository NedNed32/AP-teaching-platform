Economics begins with a single fact: there is never enough of everything for everyone. Time, materials and skilled workers are limited, but the things people want are not. That gap is **scarcity**, and every economic question is a version of how to deal with it.

## Scarcity forces choices

Because resources are scarce, using them for one purpose means not using them for another. A city that builds a stadium cannot use the same land and budget for a hospital. The value of the best alternative given up is the **opportunity cost** of the choice.

## Factors of production

Economists group productive resources into four categories:

| Factor | Examples | Payment to owners |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land | soil, water, oil, minerals | rent |
| Labor | human effort and skill | wages |
| Physical capital | machines, tools, buildings | interest |
| Entrepreneurship | organizing the others and taking risks | profit |

**Human capital**, the knowledge and skills workers gain through education and training, makes labor more productive.

## The three basic questions

Every economy must decide **what** to produce, **how** to produce it, and **for whom**. Market economies answer mostly through prices; command economies through central planning; real economies mix the two.

<Note title="Scarcity is not shortage">
A shortage is temporary: at a given price, buyers want more than sellers offer, and the price can adjust. Scarcity is permanent and applies even when markets are in equilibrium.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying resources">
A bakery uses flour, an oven, two bakers, and an owner who decided to open the shop. Classify each.

1. Flour: comes from land (wheat grown on farmland), a natural resource input.
2. Oven: physical capital.
3. Bakers: labor (their training is human capital).
4. Owner: entrepreneurship.

**Answer:** land, capital, labor and entrepreneurship, one of each factor.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** The economy shifts fully employed resources from producing consumer goods to producing capital goods. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The economy moves along its PPC.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.

**Q.** A natural disaster destroys a large share of the country's factories. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The PPC shifts inward.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.

**Q.** A large wave of skilled immigrants joins the labor force. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The PPC shifts outward.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.

**Q.** A new technology makes workers in every industry more productive. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The PPC shifts outward.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.

**Q.** Many workers in the economy become unemployed during a recession. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The economy moves to a point inside its PPC.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.
