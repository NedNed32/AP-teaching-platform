Programs that collect and analyze data can do a lot of good, and a lot of harm. Unit 4 starts with the responsibilities that come with data.

## Privacy

- Collect only the data you need.
- Tell users what you collect and why, and get **consent**.
- Protect **personally identifiable information** from unauthorized access.

## Security

Stored data can be stolen. Encryption, access controls and careful handling of inputs reduce the risk.

## Bias

A data set is a sample of the world. If it leaves out groups, or reflects past unfair decisions, an algorithm trained on it can repeat or amplify that unfairness. Examine where data comes from and who it represents.

<Note title="Data is not neutral">
Choices about what to record, how to label it and which records to include all shape the conclusions. Programmers should question those choices, not just process the data.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Spotting bias">
A hiring program is trained on ten years of a company's past hires, most of whom came from a few universities. What problem might arise?

1. The data reflects past hiring patterns, not applicants' actual ability.
2. The program may rank graduates of other universities lower without good reason.

**Answer:** the program could reproduce historical bias, unfairly filtering out qualified applicants.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A survey app stores users' names together with their answers to sensitive questions. Which practice best reduces harm if the stored data is stolen?

**A.** Store answers without names, or encrypt the data. Removing identifiers or encrypting limits the damage of a breach.

**Q.** A program that predicts loan approvals was built from past decisions in which some groups were rarely approved. What is the most likely problem?

**A.** The program may repeat the unfair patterns in the historical data. Biased training data leads to biased predictions.

**Q.** A school app collects students' locations at all times, although it only needs to know whether a student is on campus. Which change best protects student privacy?

**A.** Record only whether each student is on campus, not exact locations. Collecting only the data needed reduces privacy risk.

**Q.** Researchers analyze a data set of fitness-tracker users to make claims about the health of all adults. What is the main concern with this conclusion?

**A.** Fitness-tracker users may not represent all adults. A non-representative sample limits how far conclusions apply.
