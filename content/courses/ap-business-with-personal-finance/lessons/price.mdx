## Strategies

- **Cost-plus**: cost + markup.
- **Competitive**: set relative to rivals.
- **Value-based**: based on what customers will pay.
- **Penetration** (low to gain share) and **skimming** (high at launch).
- **Psychological** ($9.99).

## Calculations

$$\text{price} = \text{cost} \times (1 + \text{markup \%})$$

$$\text{break-even units} = \frac{\text{fixed costs}}{\text{price} - \text{variable cost per unit}}$$

Profit margin on price: $$\frac{\text{price} - \text{cost}}{\text{price}}$$ (different from markup on cost).

<Note title="Markup versus margin">
A $20 item sold for $30 has a 50% markup on cost but a 33% margin on price.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Break-even">
A food truck has fixed costs of $9,000 per month. Each meal sells for $12 and costs $5 to make. How many meals must it sell to break even?

1. Contribution margin: $12 − $5 = $7.
2. 9,000 ÷ 7 = 1,285.7.

**Answer:** 1,286 meals.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A product costs $10 to make. The business uses cost-plus pricing with a 75% markup on cost. What is the price?

**Answer:** $17.50

**Why:** Price = cost × (1 + markup).

**Working:** $10 × 1.75 = $17.50.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $7.50: That is only the markup amount.
- $40.00: That sets a margin on price, not a markup on cost.
- $85.00: Apply the percentage to the cost.

### Worked exam question 2

An item costs $50 and sells for $62.50. What is the profit margin on the selling price?

**Answer:** 20%

**Why:** Margin = (price − cost) ÷ price.

**Working:** ($62.50 − $50) ÷ $62.50 = 20%; the markup on cost is 25%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 25%: That is the markup on cost.
- 80%: That is cost as a share of price.
- 11.1%: Divide by the price.

### Worked exam question 3

A business has fixed costs of $17,500, sells its product for $32 and has variable costs of $5 per unit. What is its profit if it sells 1,011 units?

**Answer:** $9,797

**Why:** Profit = quantity × (price − variable cost) − fixed costs.

**Working:** 1011 × $27 − $17,500 = $9,797.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $14,852: Subtract variable costs too.
- $27,297: Subtract the fixed costs.
- −$7,703: Fixed costs are subtracted once.

### Worked exam question 4

A business has fixed costs of $7,500 per month, sells its product for $40 and has variable costs of $29 per unit. How many units must it sell each month to break even?

**Answer:** 682

**Why:** Fixed costs ÷ contribution margin (price − variable cost), rounded up.

**Working:** $7,500 ÷ ($40 − $29) = 681.8, so 682 units.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 188: Divide by the contribution margin, not the price.
- 259: Divide by price minus variable cost.
- 109: Subtract variable cost from price.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Sales of a product have leveled off at a high level and many competitors offer similar versions. Which stage of the product life cycle is this?

**A.** Maturity. High, flat sales and intense competition.

**Q.** A startup releases a simple version of its app with only the core feature to learn whether people use it. What is this?

**A.** A minimum viable product (MVP). An MVP tests demand with minimal investment.

**Q.** A company targets college students who live on campus and value convenience. What kind of segmentation combines these?

**A.** Demographic, geographic and psychographic. Students (demographic), on campus (geographic), value convenience (psychographic).

**Q.** A brand sells only through its own website and stores. What type of distribution channel is this?

**A.** Direct. No intermediaries.

**Q.** A business reads a government report on household spending to estimate market size. What kind of research is this?

**A.** Secondary research. It uses data collected by others.

**Q.** Which promotional tool is best suited to explaining an expensive, complex product to a business customer?

**A.** Personal selling. A salesperson can answer specific questions.

**Q.** A website shows 'Only 3 left in stock!' next to an item. Which persuasion principle is this?

**A.** Scarcity. Limited availability creates urgency.

**Q.** A new game console launches at a high price that is lowered over time. What pricing strategy is this?

**A.** Price skimming. High initial price for early adopters, then lower.
