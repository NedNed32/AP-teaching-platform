| Assets | Liabilities and equity |
| --- | --- |
| cash, accounts receivable, inventory (current) | accounts payable, short-term loans (current) |
| equipment, buildings (long-term) | long-term loans |
| | owner's equity |

Both sides must balance: assets = liabilities + equity.

$$\text{current ratio} = \frac{\text{current assets}}{\text{current liabilities}} \qquad \text{debt-to-equity} = \frac{\text{total liabilities}}{\text{equity}}$$

A current ratio above 1 means short-term obligations can be covered.

**Personal net worth** works the same way: what you own minus what you owe.

<Note title="Snapshot">
A balance sheet shows one moment. Comparing balance sheets over time shows whether net worth is growing.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Liquidity">
A shop has $18,000 cash, $7,000 inventory and $20,000 of bills due within the year. What is its current ratio, and is it healthy?

1. (18,000 + 7,000) ÷ 20,000 = 1.25.

**Answer:** 1.25: it can cover its short-term bills, with a modest cushion.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

*Balance sheet items*

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Cash | $22,000 |
| Inventory | $28,000 |
| Equipment | $65,000 |
| Current liabilities | $36,000 |
| Long-term loan | $28,000 |

What is the business's current ratio?

**Answer:** 1.39

**Why:** Current assets (cash + inventory) ÷ current liabilities.

**Working:** ($22,000 + $28,000) ÷ $36,000 = 1.39.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 3.19: Equipment is not a current asset.
- 0.72: The ratio is inverted.
- 0.78: Use only current liabilities.

### Worked exam question 2

*Balance sheet items*

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Cash | $56,000 |
| Inventory | $14,000 |
| Equipment | $73,000 |
| Current liabilities | $23,000 |
| Long-term loan | $7,000 |

What is the owner's equity?

**Answer:** $113,000

**Why:** Equity = assets − liabilities.

**Working:** Assets $143,000 − liabilities $30,000 = $113,000.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $143,000: Subtract the liabilities.
- $120,000: Subtract the long-term loan too.
- $173,000: Liabilities are subtracted.
