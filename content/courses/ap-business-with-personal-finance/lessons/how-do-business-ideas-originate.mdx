Good business ideas usually start with a **problem** experienced by a group of customers.

## Sources of ideas

- Personal experience and frustrations.
- Changes in technology, demographics or regulation (PESTEL).
- Gaps in what competitors offer.
- Improving an existing product's cost, quality or convenience.

## From idea to opportunity

1. Define the problem and the target customer.
2. Write a **value proposition**.
3. Form a **hypothesis** about customer behavior and test it (surveys, interviews, prototypes).
4. Refine the idea based on evidence.

<Note title="Ideas versus opportunities">
An idea becomes an opportunity only if enough customers want it, will pay for it, and the business can deliver it profitably.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Test a hypothesis">
A student believes classmates would pay $3 for healthy breakfast bars sold before school. How could they test this cheaply?

1. Form a testable hypothesis.
2. Run a small pre-sale or survey with a sample of classmates.

**Answer:** run a small trial sale or survey and compare actual purchases with the prediction.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A purchasing manager awards a contract to a company owned by her brother without disclosing the relationship. What is the ethical problem?

**A.** A conflict of interest. Personal interests influenced a business decision.

**Q.** Which organizational structure has few management levels and wide spans of control?

**A.** A flat structure. Flat structures have few layers.

**Q.** A company sells the lowest-priced basic laptops by producing at huge scale. What competitive strategy is this?

**A.** Cost leadership. It competes on low cost and price.

**Q.** Which goal is SMART?

**A.** Increase online sales from $40,000 to $50,000 per month by December 31. It is specific, measurable and time-bound.

**Q.** Two founders want limited personal liability and simple pass-through taxation. Which form of business best fits?

**A.** A limited liability company (LLC). LLCs offer limited liability with flexible, simple taxation.

**Q.** Which department is mainly responsible for recruiting, training and benefits?

**A.** Human resources. HR manages people.

**Q.** Which is a vision statement rather than a mission statement?

**A.** "A world where every family has access to fresh, affordable food.". Vision describes the long-term future the organization wants.

**Q.** A student notices that classmates struggle to find quiet study spaces and starts an app listing open rooms. Where did this business idea come from?

**A.** Identifying a problem experienced by a group of customers. Many ideas start from an unmet need.

### Free-response practice: Business Canvas Project: exam-day validation (practice)

On the real exam, this question asks about **your own** Business Canvas Project. For practice, answer about this sample project.

A student team developed a tutoring service using peer tutors for middle school students and parents. Their hypothesis was that at least 20% of parents would book a trial session. In a survey of 150 people in the target market, 14% said they would.

**(a)** Write a brief pitch for the product idea that identifies the target customer, the problem, and the value proposition.

To earn the point: Identifies the target customer and the problem. Model answer: Middle school students and parents who face a specific, described problem.

To earn the point: States a clear value proposition. Model answer: Explains the benefit to the customer and how it differs from alternatives.

**(b)** Explain how the team used hypothesis testing to inform a decision. State whether the evidence supports the hypothesis.

To earn the point: Describes the test and compares the result with the hypothesis. Model answer: 14% versus the 20% threshold: the hypothesis is not supported.

To earn the point: Connects the result to a decision. Model answer: Revise the product, price or target customer before investing further.

**(c)** Explain why a challenge frequently faced by entrepreneurs (for example, limited funding, competition or uncertain demand) could threaten the viability of this product idea.

To earn the point: Identifies a relevant challenge and explains its impact on viability. Model answer: Limited funding could prevent the team from reaching enough customers to cover fixed costs before cash runs out.
