## SWOT

| | Helpful | Harmful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| internal | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| external | Opportunities | Threats |

## Porter's Five Forces

| Force | High when |
| --- | --- |
| competitive rivalry | many similar competitors |
| threat of new entrants | low startup costs, few barriers |
| threat of substitutes | other products meet the same need |
| buyer power | few large buyers, easy switching |
| supplier power | few suppliers, unique inputs |

Strong forces reduce an industry's profitability. Businesses use the analysis to find positions where the forces are weaker.

<Note title="Internal versus external">
Strengths and weaknesses are things the business controls; opportunities and threats come from its environment (PESTEL, competitors).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classify for SWOT">
A bakery's staff are highly trained (1), a new highway will bring more traffic past the store (2), and a national chain is opening nearby (3). Classify each.

1. (1) internal and helpful.
2. (2) external and helpful.
3. (3) external and harmful.

**Answer:** (1) strength, (2) opportunity, (3) threat.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** In a SWOT analysis, how should this be classified? "A new highway will bring more traffic past the store."

**A.** Opportunity. It comes from the external environment. Opportunity: external and helpful.

**Q.** In a SWOT analysis, how should this be classified? "A large national chain plans to open a store nearby."

**A.** Threat. It comes from the external environment. Threat: external and harmful.

**Q.** In a SWOT analysis, how should this be classified? "The company relies on a single supplier that also serves its competitors."

**A.** Weakness. It is internal to the business. Weakness: internal and harmful.

**Q.** In a SWOT analysis, how should this be classified? "Demand for the company's product category is growing among teenagers."

**A.** Opportunity. It comes from the external environment. Opportunity: external and helpful.

**Q.** In a SWOT analysis, how should this be classified? "New regulations will raise the cost of the company's main ingredient."

**A.** Threat. It comes from the external environment. Threat: external and harmful.

**Q.** In a SWOT analysis, how should this be classified? "The company's website is outdated and hard to use on phones."

**A.** Weakness. It is internal to the business. Weakness: internal and harmful.

**Q.** In a SWOT analysis, how should this be classified? "The company's staff are highly trained and give expert advice."

**A.** Strength. It is internal to the business. Strength: internal and helpful.

**Q.** In a SWOT analysis, how should this be classified? "The company has a loyal customer base and strong brand recognition."

**A.** Strength. It is internal to the business. Strength: internal and helpful.

**Q.** Which of the four functions of management involves comparing results with goals and correcting course?

**A.** Controlling. Controlling measures and adjusts performance.

**Q.** During a fire in a warehouse, a manager gives direct orders without consulting the team. Which leadership style fits this situation?

**A.** Autocratic. Emergencies need fast, clear decisions.

**Q.** A café wants to measure how well it keeps customers coming back. Which KPI fits best?

**A.** Customer retention rate. It measures repeat customers.

**Q.** Before comparing two strategic options, why should a business define its decision criteria?

**A.** So the options are judged consistently against what matters most to the business. Criteria make the comparison objective.

**Q.** Many companies can supply the plain cardboard boxes a business needs, and switching between them is easy. According to Porter's Five Forces, supplier power is:

**A.** Low. Many suppliers and easy switching weaken supplier power.

**Q.** Customers can easily switch from a company's energy drink to coffee or tea. Which of Porter's Five Forces is strong?

**A.** Threat of substitutes. Other products meet the same need.
