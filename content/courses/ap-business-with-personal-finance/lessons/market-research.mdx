## Quantitative and qualitative

**Quantitative** data are numbers (percentages, sales); **qualitative** data are descriptions (opinions, reasons).

## Using findings

Compare groups, find trends, and connect a **finding** (for example, 45% of customers want accessories but only 30% find them) to a **decision** (expand the accessory range).

<Note title="Hypothesis testing">
State what you expect before collecting data, then check whether the evidence supports it.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Interpret data">
In a survey, 60% of business customers found the device they wanted but only 35% found the accessory they wanted. What opportunity does this suggest?

1. Accessory demand is going unmet.

**Answer:** expanding the accessory selection for business customers.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Maple Street Bakery surveyed its customers.

*Customer survey*

| Customer type | Share of customers (%) | Found the product they wanted (%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individuals seeking a device | 44 | 85 |
| Individuals seeking an accessory | 23 | 40 |
| Businesses seeking a device | 21 | 68 |
| Businesses seeking an accessory | 6 | 32 |

Which customer group represents the largest number of customers whose needs went unmet?

**Answer:** Individuals seeking an accessory

**Why:** Unmet customers = share of customers × share who did not find the product.

**Working:** Unmet (share × not found): Individuals seeking a device 6.6%; Individuals seeking an accessory 13.8%; Businesses seeking a device 6.7%; Businesses seeking an accessory 4.1%.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Individuals seeking a device: It is the largest group, but most of its customers were served.
- Businesses seeking a device: Fewer of its customers went without.
- Businesses seeking an accessory: It has the lowest success rate, but it is a smaller group.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Sales of a product have leveled off at a high level and many competitors offer similar versions. Which stage of the product life cycle is this?

**A.** Maturity. High, flat sales and intense competition.

**Q.** A startup releases a simple version of its app with only the core feature to learn whether people use it. What is this?

**A.** A minimum viable product (MVP). An MVP tests demand with minimal investment.

**Q.** A company targets college students who live on campus and value convenience. What kind of segmentation combines these?

**A.** Demographic, geographic and psychographic. Students (demographic), on campus (geographic), value convenience (psychographic).

**Q.** A brand sells only through its own website and stores. What type of distribution channel is this?

**A.** Direct. No intermediaries.

**Q.** A business reads a government report on household spending to estimate market size. What kind of research is this?

**A.** Secondary research. It uses data collected by others.

**Q.** Which promotional tool is best suited to explaining an expensive, complex product to a business customer?

**A.** Personal selling. A salesperson can answer specific questions.

**Q.** A website shows 'Only 3 left in stock!' next to an item. Which persuasion principle is this?

**A.** Scarcity. Limited availability creates urgency.

**Q.** A new game console launches at a high price that is lowered over time. What pricing strategy is this?

**A.** Price skimming. High initial price for early adopters, then lower.

### Free-response practice: Business Canvas Project: exam-day validation (practice)

On the real exam, this question asks about **your own** Business Canvas Project. For practice, answer about this sample project.

A student team developed a tutoring service using peer tutors for middle school students and parents. Their hypothesis was that at least 20% of parents would book a trial session. In a survey of 150 people in the target market, 14% said they would.

**(a)** Write a brief pitch for the product idea that identifies the target customer, the problem, and the value proposition.

To earn the point: Identifies the target customer and the problem. Model answer: Middle school students and parents who face a specific, described problem.

To earn the point: States a clear value proposition. Model answer: Explains the benefit to the customer and how it differs from alternatives.

**(b)** Explain how the team used hypothesis testing to inform a decision. State whether the evidence supports the hypothesis.

To earn the point: Describes the test and compares the result with the hypothesis. Model answer: 14% versus the 20% threshold: the hypothesis is not supported.

To earn the point: Connects the result to a decision. Model answer: Revise the product, price or target customer before investing further.

**(c)** Explain why a challenge frequently faced by entrepreneurs (for example, limited funding, competition or uncertain demand) could threaten the viability of this product idea.

To earn the point: Identifies a relevant challenge and explains its impact on viability. Model answer: Limited funding could prevent the team from reaching enough customers to cover fixed costs before cash runs out.

### Free-response practice: Brightwave enters the market

A media company plans to launch Brightwave, a streaming service, in a market with four established rivals. Its executives reviewed industry publications and the rivals' public financial reports.

*Rival streaming services*

| Service | Market share by revenue (%) | Monthly price ($) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NetCom | 11 | 14.99 |
| OrbioTV | 28 | 14.99 |
| VistaFlix | 30 | 15.99 |
| Kinovo | 31 | 15.99 |

**(a)** Describe the market research method the executives used.

To earn the point: Secondary research. Model answer: They used existing data from publications and published financial statements, not data they collected themselves.

**(b)** Describe one finding revealed by the research.

To earn the point: An accurate finding from the data. Model answer: Kinovo leads with 31% of revenue; NetCom is the cheapest at $14.99.

**(c)** Explain how Brightwave could use a finding to achieve a business goal, such as gaining market share.

To earn the point: Connects a finding to a strategy and goal. Model answer: Because the market is concentrated and prices range from $14.99 to $15.99, Brightwave could use penetration pricing below $14.99 or differentiate with exclusive content to win share.
