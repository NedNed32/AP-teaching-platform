| Investment | Risk | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| savings account, CD | very low | emergency fund, short-term goals |
| bonds | low to moderate | income, stability |
| stocks | higher | long-term growth |
| mutual funds / index funds | depends on holdings | diversified long-term investing |
| real estate | moderate | housing, investment |

**Risk and return** are linked: higher expected returns come with more risk. Time horizon matters: long-term goals like retirement can tolerate more short-term ups and downs.

Tax-advantaged accounts help: retirement accounts (401(k), IRA) and education savings plans (529). Start early, diversify, keep fees low, and avoid trying to time the market.

<Note title="Compounding over decades">
Money invested at 20 has decades more to compound than money invested at 40, which can make a larger difference than the amount invested.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Employer match">
An employer matches 50% of contributions up to 6% of a $50,000 salary. How much does the employer add if the employee contributes 6%?

1. Employee: 0.06 × 50,000 = 3,000.
2. Match: 0.5 × 3,000.

**Answer:** $1,500 per year.
</WorkedExample>

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Using the rule of 72, about how many years will it take an investment earning 3% per year to double?

**A.** About 24 years. 72 ÷ 3 = 24. 72 ÷ 3 ≈ 24 years.

**Q.** Using the rule of 72, about how many years will it take an investment earning 12% per year to double?
