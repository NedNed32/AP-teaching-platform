$$\text{simple interest} = P \times r \times t \qquad A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$$

- $$P$$: principal, $$r$$: annual rate, $$n$$: compounding periods per year, $$t$$: years.
- **Rule of 72**: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ (rate in percent).

Starting early matters: compounding grows money faster the longer it has.

## Where to save

Savings accounts, money market accounts and certificates of deposit (CDs) are low-risk. Deposits at insured banks and credit unions are protected up to legal limits.

Inflation erodes purchasing power, so savings earning less than inflation lose real value.

<Note title="Pay yourself first">
Automatically moving part of each paycheck to savings makes saving a habit rather than an afterthought.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Compound growth">
A student deposits $2,000 at 4% interest compounded annually. What is the balance after 3 years?

1. $$2000(1.04)^3 = 2000 \times 1.124864$$.

**Answer:** $2,249.73.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Gus deposits $2,200 in an account earning 5% interest compounded annually. What is the balance after 4 years, with no other deposits or withdrawals?

**Answer:** $2,674.11

**Why:** A = P(1 + r)^t.

**Working:** $2,200 × 1.05^4 = $2,674.11.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $2,640.00: That is simple interest; compounding earns interest on interest.
- $440.00: That is only the interest, and it ignores compounding.
- $2,546.78: Compound for all 4 years.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Using the rule of 72, about how many years will it take an investment earning 3% per year to double?

**A.** About 24 years. 72 ÷ 3 = 24. 72 ÷ 3 ≈ 24 years.

**Q.** Using the rule of 72, about how many years will it take an investment earning 12% per year to double?

**A.** About 6 years. 72 ÷ 12 = 6. 72 ÷ 12 ≈ 6 years.

**Q.** Using the rule of 72, about how many years will it take an investment earning 8% per year to double?

**A.** About 9 years. 72 ÷ 8 = 9. 72 ÷ 8 ≈ 9 years.

**Q.** Using the rule of 72, about how many years will it take an investment earning 9% per year to double?

**A.** About 8 years. 72 ÷ 9 = 8. 72 ÷ 9 ≈ 8 years.

**Q.** Using the rule of 72, about how many years will it take an investment earning 4% per year to double?

**A.** About 18 years. 72 ÷ 4 = 18. 72 ÷ 4 ≈ 18 years.

### Free-response practice: Maya's financial plan

Maya earns $4,300 per month before taxes. Payroll deductions total $903. Rent is $1,020 per month, and other living expenses average $880. Maya has a $2,800 credit card balance at 24% APR and wants to save $9,500 for a car down payment in 20 months.

**(a)** Calculate Maya's monthly net (take-home) pay and the amount left after rent and other expenses.

To earn the point: Net pay. Model answer: $4,300 − $903 = $3,397

To earn the point: Amount remaining. Model answer: $3,397 − $1,020 − $880 = $1,497

**(b)** Determine how much Maya must save each month to reach the goal, and whether the current budget allows it.

To earn the point: Required monthly savings. Model answer: $9,500 ÷ 20 = $475.00

To earn the point: Correct comparison. Model answer: Yes: $1,497 available is at least $475.00.

**(c)** Rent is 30% of net pay. Recommend one change that would help Maya reach the goal, and explain it using the data, including the credit card balance.

To earn the point: A reasonable recommendation tied to the data. Model answer: Pay off the 24% APR card first: its interest costs more than savings earn. Then cut variable expenses or extend the timeline, or reduce housing costs if above about 30% of net pay.
