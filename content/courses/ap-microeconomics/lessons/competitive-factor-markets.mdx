A firm hiring in a perfectly competitive labor market is a **wage taker**: the market sets the wage, and the firm chooses how many workers to hire.

## Two graphs

- **Market:** labor supply and demand set the wage $$W^*$$.
- **Firm:** a horizontal line at $$W^*$$ is the firm's labor supply and its MFC. The firm hires where its downward-sloping MRP crosses MFC.

## The hiring rule

$$
\text{hire where } MRP = MFC \;(= W).
$$

With a table, hire every worker whose MRP is at least the wage.

## Combining two inputs

To produce at the lowest cost, spend each dollar where it adds the most output:

$$
\frac{MP_L}{P_L} = \frac{MP_K}{P_K}.
$$

To maximize profit as well, each ratio must also equal $$\frac{1}{MR}$$, which is the same as $$MRP = P$$ for every factor.

<Note title="Price taker in two markets">
A firm can be a price taker in its product market (MR = P) and a wage taker in its labor market (MFC = W). The exam may give you either or both conditions.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Hiring from a table">
A firm sells its output for 10 in a competitive market and pays a wage of 60. Workers 1 to 5 have marginal products 12, 10, 8, 6, 4. How many should it hire?

1. MRP: 120, 100, 80, 60, 40.
2. Workers 1 to 4 have MRP at least 60; worker 5 adds only 40.

**Answer:** 4 workers.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A firm sells its output in a perfectly competitive market at a price of 7 and hires workers in a perfectly competitive labor market at a wage of 23.

*Marginal product of labor*

| Worker | Marginal product |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 17 |
| 2 | 12 |
| 3 | 8 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 1 |
| 6 | 1 |

How many workers should the firm hire to maximize profit?

**Answer:** 4

**Why:** Worker 4 is the last whose MRP (42) is at least the wage.

**Working:** MRP = MP × P: 119, 84, 56, 42, 7, 7. Hire while MRP ≥ wage (23): 4 workers.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 3: Another worker would add more revenue than the wage costs.
- 5: This worker's MRP is below the wage.
- 6: This worker's MRP is below the wage.

### Worked exam question 2

A firm uses labor (wage 15) and capital (rental price 16). The marginal product of labor is 105 and the marginal product of capital is 32. To produce its output at the lowest cost, the firm should

**Answer:** use more labor and less of the other input

**Why:** Labor yields 7 units per dollar; capital yields 2.

**Working:** MP per dollar: labor 105 ÷ 15 = 7; capital 32 ÷ 16 = 2. Shift toward labor until the ratios are equal.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- use more capital and less of the other input: Compare marginal product per dollar, not marginal product alone.
- keep its input mix unchanged: The marginal products per dollar are unequal, so costs can be lowered.
- use more of both inputs: To minimize cost for a given output, shift spending between inputs rather than adding both.

### Free-response practice: Hiring workers

A firm sells its product in a perfectly competitive market at 2 per unit and hires labor in a perfectly competitive market at a wage of 28. The marginal products of workers 1 to 5 are 17, 15, 11, 9, 6.

**(a)** Calculate the marginal revenue product of the third worker.

To earn the point: MP × P. Model answer: 11 × 2 = 22

**(b)** How many workers will the firm hire? Explain.

To earn the point: Correct number with the MRP ≥ wage rule. Model answer: 2: MRP is at least the wage of 28 for workers 1 to 2 only.

**(c)** If the price of the product rises, what happens to the firm's demand for labor? Explain.

To earn the point: Increases, because MRP rises. Model answer: Each worker's MRP = MP × P rises, so labor demand shifts right.
