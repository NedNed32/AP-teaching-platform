The law of demand says quantity falls when price rises. Elasticity says **by how much**, and that answer decides whether a price increase raises or lowers a firm's revenue.

## The formula (midpoint method)

$$
E_d = \frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P} = \frac{\frac{Q_2 - Q_1}{(Q_1 + Q_2)/2}}{\frac{P_2 - P_1}{(P_1 + P_2)/2}}
$$

Report the absolute value.

| Coefficient | Demand is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| greater than 1 | elastic | restaurant meals, a specific brand |
| equal to 1 | unit elastic | |
| less than 1 | inelastic | insulin, gasoline in the short run |
| 0 | perfectly inelastic (vertical) | |
| infinite | perfectly elastic (horizontal) | |

## Total revenue test

| Demand | Price up | Price down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| elastic | revenue falls | revenue rises |
| inelastic | revenue rises | revenue falls |
| unit elastic | revenue unchanged | revenue unchanged |

## Determinants

Demand is more elastic when there are **many substitutes**, the good is a **large share of the budget**, it is a **luxury** rather than a necessity, and more **time** has passed to adjust.

<Note title="Elasticity changes along a straight-line demand curve">
A linear demand curve has a constant slope but not a constant elasticity: it is elastic at high prices (upper half) and inelastic at low prices (lower half). Total revenue is maximized at the unit-elastic midpoint.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Midpoint calculation">
The price of a concert ticket rises from 40 to 60, and quantity demanded falls from 500 to 300. Find the elasticity.

1. $$\%\Delta Q = \frac{300 - 500}{400} = -50\%$$.
2. $$\%\Delta P = \frac{60 - 40}{50} = 40\%$$.
3. $$E_d = \frac{50}{40} = 1.25$$.

**Answer:** 1.25, so demand is elastic, and total revenue falls (from 20,000 to 18,000).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

When the price of a good rises from 16 to 18, the quantity demanded falls from 320 to 270. Using the midpoint method, what is the price elasticity of demand, and is demand elastic or inelastic?

**Answer:** 1.44, elastic

**Why:** Percentage changes use the averages of the two values.

**Working:** %ΔQ = 50 ÷ 295 ≈ 16.9%; %ΔP = 2 ÷ 17 ≈ 11.8%. Elasticity ≈ 1.44, so demand is elastic.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 0.69, inelastic: This divides the percentage change in price by the percentage change in quantity: upside down.
- 1.44, inelastic: The value is right but the classification is wrong: elastic means greater than 1.
- 1.25, elastic: This uses the starting values instead of the midpoint method.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A firm raises its price and finds that its total revenue decreases. Over this range, demand for its product is

**A.** price elastic. Price and total revenue moved in opposite directions. Price rose and total revenue fell. Opposite movements mean quantity changed by a larger percentage than price: elastic.

**Q.** A firm raises its price and finds that its total revenue increases. Over this range, demand for its product is

**A.** price inelastic. Price and total revenue moved in the same direction. Price rose and total revenue rose. Same-direction movements mean quantity changed by a smaller percentage than price: inelastic.

**Q.** A firm lowers its price and finds that its total revenue increases. Over this range, demand for its product is

**A.** price elastic. Price and total revenue moved in opposite directions. Price fell and total revenue rose. Opposite movements mean quantity changed by a larger percentage than price: elastic.

**Q.** A firm lowers its price and finds that its total revenue decreases. Over this range, demand for its product is

**A.** price inelastic. Price and total revenue moved in the same direction. Price fell and total revenue fell. Same-direction movements mean quantity changed by a smaller percentage than price: inelastic.

**Q.** Which good is likely to have the most price-inelastic demand?

**A.** Life-saving prescription medicine. Availability of substitutes, necessity, budget share, time and production flexibility drive elasticity. Demand is more elastic with close substitutes, for luxuries, for large budget shares and over longer time periods. Supply is more elastic when production can expand easily.

**Q.** Supply of which good is likely to be the most price-elastic?

**A.** T-shirts made with easily available materials and labor. Availability of substitutes, necessity, budget share, time and production flexibility drive elasticity. Demand is more elastic with close substitutes, for luxuries, for large budget shares and over longer time periods. Supply is more elastic when production can expand easily.

**Q.** Which good is likely to have the most price-elastic demand?

**A.** One particular brand of bottled water. Availability of substitutes, necessity, budget share, time and production flexibility drive elasticity. Demand is more elastic with close substitutes, for luxuries, for large budget shares and over longer time periods. Supply is more elastic when production can expand easily.

### Free-response practice: A per-unit tax

In a competitive market, the equilibrium price is 16 and quantity is 210. The government imposes a per-unit tax of 6 on sellers. Afterwards, buyers pay 20, sellers keep 14, and 150 units are sold.

**(a)** Calculate the government's tax revenue.

To earn the point: Correct value. Model answer: 6 × 150 = 900

**(b)** Calculate the deadweight loss.

To earn the point: Correct value. Model answer: ½ × 60 × 6 = 180

**(c)** Do buyers or sellers bear more of the tax? What does this imply about the relative elasticities of demand and supply?

To earn the point: Correct side and elasticity comparison. Model answer: Buyers (4 of 6); demand is relatively more inelastic than supply.
