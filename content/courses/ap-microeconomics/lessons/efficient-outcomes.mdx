Competitive markets maximize total surplus, but only when prices capture all the benefits and costs. When they do not, the market fails to reach the socially efficient outcome.

## The efficiency condition

$$
MSB = MSC
$$

When private benefits and costs equal social ones ($$MPB = MSB$$ and $$MPC = MSC$$), the market equilibrium is efficient.

## Sources of inefficiency

| Market failure | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Negative externality | overproduction |
| Positive externality | underproduction |
| Public goods | underprovision (free riders) |
| Market power | underproduction, $$P > MC$$ |
| Price controls, taxes | deadweight loss |

## Deadweight loss

Whenever output differs from the efficient quantity, the triangle between MSB and MSC over the gap in output is a **deadweight loss**: surplus lost to society.

<Note title="Efficiency is not fairness">
Efficiency asks whether total surplus is as large as possible. It does not say whether the distribution of that surplus is fair; that is the question of equity (topic 6.5).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Spotting the inefficiency">
In a market, the equilibrium quantity is 500. At that quantity, MSB = 20 and MSC = 28. Is output efficient?

1. MSC exceeds MSB at 500: the last units cost society more than their value.
2. Output should fall until MSB = MSC.

**Answer:** no; the market overproduces, as with a negative externality, creating a deadweight loss.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** Vaccinations protect not only the people vaccinated but also those around them. Which of the following is true?

**A.** The market produces too little; a per-unit subsidy could correct it.. Negative externalities cause overproduction; positive ones cause underproduction. ### Free-response practice: A negative externality

The market for steel is perfectly competitive. Each unit imposes an external cost of 25 on third parties.

**(a)** Describe a correctly labeled graph of this market showing the market quantity (Qm) and the socially optimal quantity (Qs).

To earn the point: MSC drawn above MPC (supply) by the external cost. Model answer: MSC = MPC + 25

To earn the point: Correct relationship between Qm and Qs. Model answer: Qm is greater than Qs.

**(b)** Shade or describe the deadweight loss.

To earn the point: Triangle between the social and private curves between Qs and Qm. Model answer: Between MSC and demand from Qs to Qm.

**(c)** Identify a per-unit government policy that would achieve the socially optimal quantity, and give its size.

To earn the point: Correct policy and size. Model answer: A per-unit tax of 25.
