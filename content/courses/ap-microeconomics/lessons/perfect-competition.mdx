Perfect competition is the benchmark against which other market structures are measured: many small firms, identical products, easy entry and exit, and full information.

## Characteristics

- Many buyers and sellers.
- Identical (standardized) products.
- No barriers to entry or exit.
- Every firm is a **price taker**.

## The firm and the market

Draw two graphs side by side:

- **Market:** supply and demand set the price $$P^*$$.
- **Firm:** a horizontal line at $$P^*$$ is the firm's demand, MR and AR. The firm produces where $$MR = MC$$.

## Long-run equilibrium

Entry and exit drive price to **minimum ATC**:

$$
P = MR = MC = \min ATC.
$$

| Efficiency | Condition | Met in long run? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Allocative | $$P = MC$$ | yes |
| Productive | $$P = \min ATC$$ | yes |

## Adjustment to a demand increase

Market demand rises, price rises, firms earn economic profit; new firms enter, market supply rises, and price returns to minimum ATC (in a constant-cost industry).

<Note title="Two graphs, consistent prices">
On the exam, the price on the firm's graph must match the market price, and the firm's quantity (q) is tiny compared with market quantity (Q). Label them differently.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Profit in the short run">
The market price is 15. A firm's MC equals 15 at 200 units, where its ATC is 12. Find the firm's profit and predict the long run.

1. Profit $$= (15 - 12) \times 200 = 600$$.
2. Positive economic profit attracts entry.
3. Market supply increases and the price falls until it equals minimum ATC.

**Answer:** profit 600 now; in the long run firms enter and economic profit falls to zero.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A perfectly competitive firm sells its output at a market price of 13. Its marginal cost of each unit is shown.

*Marginal cost*

| Unit | Marginal cost |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 11 |
| 2 | 7 |
| 3 | 11 |
| 4 | 18 |
| 5 | 25 |
| 6 | 31 |
| 7 | 35 |

How many units should the firm produce to maximize profit?

**Answer:** 3

**Why:** Unit 3 is the last one whose marginal cost (11) is at most the price (MR) of 13.

**Working:** For a price taker, MR = P = 13. Produce every unit whose MC is at most 13: units 1 to 3.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 2: Producing more adds units whose marginal revenue exceeds marginal cost.
- 4: This unit costs more than the price it sells for.
- 1: Producing more adds units whose marginal revenue exceeds marginal cost.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A perfectly competitive, constant-cost industry is in long-run equilibrium. Market demand then decreases permanently. Which of the following describes the long-run adjustment?

**A.** Firms exit, market supply decreases, and the price rises back to minimum ATC.. Short-run losses cause exit. The lower price creates economic losses. Firms exit, shifting market supply left until price returns to minimum ATC and economic profit is zero.

**Q.** A perfectly competitive, constant-cost industry is in long-run equilibrium. Market demand then increases permanently. Which of the following describes the long-run adjustment?

**A.** Firms enter, market supply increases, and the price falls back to minimum ATC.. Short-run profits attract entry. The higher price creates economic profits. Firms enter, shifting market supply right until price returns to minimum ATC and economic profit is zero.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? In long-run equilibrium, price equals average total cost but exceeds marginal cost, and firms have excess capacity.

**A.** Monopolistic competition. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? A few interdependent firms consider rivals' reactions when setting prices.

**A.** Oligopoly. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? A single firm with high barriers to entry can earn economic profit in the long run.

**A.** Monopoly. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? In long-run equilibrium, price equals marginal cost and minimum average total cost.

**A.** Perfect competition. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

### Free-response practice: A perfectly competitive firm

The market for a product is perfectly competitive, and the industry has constant costs. The market price is 22. A typical firm maximizes profit by producing 90 units, where its average total cost is 24 and its average variable cost is 20.

**(a)** Describe correctly labeled side-by-side graphs for the market and for the typical firm. On the firm's graph show MR, MC, ATC and the profit-maximizing quantity.

To earn the point: Market graph with supply and demand setting the price. Model answer: Market price 22 at the intersection of S and D.

To earn the point: Firm's horizontal demand = MR = P at the market price. Model answer: Horizontal line at 22.

To earn the point: Firm produces where MR = MC, with ATC drawn consistently. Model answer: q = 90 where MC crosses MR; ATC at 24.

**(b)** Calculate the firm's economic profit or loss. Show your work.

To earn the point: Correct value. Model answer: (22 − 24) × 90 = -180 (a loss)

**(c)** Should the firm continue to produce in the short run? Explain using cost curves.

To earn the point: Yes, with the AVC comparison. Model answer: Yes: price 22 is at least AVC 20, so producing covers variable costs and part of the fixed cost of 360.

**(d)** What happens to the number of firms in the industry in the long run? Explain.

To earn the point: Correct direction with reason. Model answer: Firms exit because they earn economic losses.

**(e)** After all long-run adjustments, compare the market price with the typical firm's minimum average total cost.

To earn the point: They are equal. Model answer: In long-run equilibrium, P = minimum ATC, and economic profit is zero.
