A monopsony is the mirror image of a monopoly: one **buyer** instead of one seller. A mining company that is the only employer in a remote town is the classic example.

## Why MFC exceeds the wage

The monopsonist faces the upward-sloping market labor supply curve. To attract one more worker it must raise the wage, and (without wage discrimination) pay that higher wage to everyone. So the cost of the extra worker is the new wage **plus** the raise for all existing workers:

$$
MFC > W.
$$

## Hiring and wage-setting

1. Hire where $$MRP = MFC$$.
2. Pay the lowest wage that attracts that many workers, read down from the **supply** curve.

## Compared with competition

| | Monopsony | Competitive labor market |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employment | lower | higher |
| Wage | lower (and below MRP) | equals MRP |
| Efficiency | deadweight loss | efficient |

<Note title="Minimum wage can raise employment here">
In a monopsony, a minimum wage set between the monopsony wage and the competitive wage makes MFC flat at that wage over a range, which can raise both the wage and employment. This is a classic exam question.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Marginal factor cost">
A firm can hire 4 workers at 15 each or 5 workers at 16 each. What is the MFC of the 5th worker?

1. Total cost with 4 workers: $$4 \times 15 = 60$$.
2. With 5 workers: $$5 \times 16 = 80$$.
3. MFC = 80 − 60 = 20.

**Answer:** 20, which is above the wage of 16.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A monopsonist can hire 7 workers at a wage of 17 each, or 8 workers at a wage of 19 each. What is the marginal factor cost of worker 8?

**Answer:** 33

**Why:** 152 − 119.

**Working:** Total cost rises from 119 to 152, so MFC = 33, greater than the wage of 19.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 19: MFC exceeds the wage because the higher wage is paid to all workers.
- 2: This is only the raise for one worker.
- 152: This is total labor cost.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Compared with a perfectly competitive labor market, a monopsony employer hires

**A.** fewer workers at a lower wage. It hires where MRP = MFC, with MFC above the supply curve, then pays the wage on the supply curve. A monopsonist's MFC lies above labor supply, so MRP = MFC at a lower employment level, and the wage needed to attract those workers is also lower.

**Q.** A town's only large employer, compared with what a competitive labor market would produce, hires

**A.** fewer workers at a lower wage. It hires where MRP = MFC, with MFC above the supply curve, then pays the wage on the supply curve. A monopsonist's MFC lies above labor supply, so MRP = MFC at a lower employment level, and the wage needed to attract those workers is also lower.
