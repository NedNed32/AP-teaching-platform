An externality is a side effect on someone outside the transaction. Because buyers and sellers ignore it, the market produces the wrong quantity.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/externality.svg" alt="A negative externality: demand equals marginal social benefit, supply equals marginal private cost, and a higher dashed marginal social cost curve; the market quantity is larger than the efficient quantity." caption="With a negative externality, MSC is above MPC and the market overproduces." />

## Negative externalities

- $$MSC = MPC + MEC$$ (marginal external cost).
- The market produces where $$MPB = MPC$$, **more** than the efficient quantity where $$MSB = MSC$$.
- Fix: a **per-unit tax equal to the MEC** (a Pigouvian tax), or regulation, or tradable permits.

## Positive externalities

- $$MSB = MPB + MEB$$ (marginal external benefit).
- The market produces **less** than the efficient quantity.
- Fix: a **per-unit subsidy equal to the MEB** to buyers or sellers, or public provision.

## Deadweight loss

In both cases, the triangle between MSB and MSC from the market quantity to the efficient quantity is the deadweight loss.

<Note title="Which curve moves?">
Draw the external cost as a gap between MPC and MSC (negative externality), or the external benefit as a gap between MPB and MSB (positive externality). The market curves are the private ones; the efficient outcome uses the social ones.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Setting a Pigouvian tax">
Producing each ton of a chemical imposes an external cost of 15 on nearby residents. Market supply is $$P = 10 + Q$$ and demand is $$P = 70 - Q$$. Find the market and efficient quantities and the corrective tax.

1. Market: $$10 + Q = 70 - Q$$, so $$Q = 30$$.
2. MSC: $$P = 25 + Q$$. Efficient: $$25 + Q = 70 - Q$$, so $$Q = 22.5$$.
3. A tax of 15 per ton shifts private supply up to MSC.

**Answer:** market quantity 30; efficient quantity 22.5; a per-unit tax of 15 corrects it.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Producing each unit of a good imposes a constant external cost of 24 on third parties. Market demand is $$P = 113 - Q$$ and market supply (marginal private cost) is $$P = 18 + Q$$. What per-unit tax would achieve the socially efficient quantity?

**Answer:** 24

**Why:** A Pigouvian tax equal to the marginal external cost.

**Working:** Setting the tax equal to the marginal external cost (24) makes producers face the full social cost, so output falls from 47.5 to 35.5.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 12: The tax should equal the whole external cost per unit.
- 47.5: This is the market quantity, not a tax.
- 0, because the market is already efficient: With a negative externality, the market overproduces.

### Worked exam question 2

Producing each unit of a good imposes a constant external cost of 22 on third parties. Market demand is $$P = 92 - Q$$ and market supply (marginal private cost) is $$P = 6 + Q$$. What is the socially efficient quantity?

**Answer:** 32

**Why:** $$92 - Q = 28 + Q$$.

**Working:** MSC = 6 + Q + 22. Efficient quantity where MSB = MSC: 92 − Q = 28 + Q, so Q = 32, below the market quantity 43.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 43: This is the market quantity, which ignores the external cost.
- 54: The external cost reduces the efficient quantity.
- 16: Set MSB = MSC: demand equals supply plus the external cost.

### Free-response practice: A negative externality

The market for steel is perfectly competitive. Each unit imposes an external cost of 25 on third parties.

**(a)** Describe a correctly labeled graph of this market showing the market quantity (Qm) and the socially optimal quantity (Qs).

To earn the point: MSC drawn above MPC (supply) by the external cost. Model answer: MSC = MPC + 25

To earn the point: Correct relationship between Qm and Qs. Model answer: Qm is greater than Qs.

**(b)** Shade or describe the deadweight loss.

To earn the point: Triangle between the social and private curves between Qs and Qm. Model answer: Between MSC and demand from Qs to Qm.

**(c)** Identify a per-unit government policy that would achieve the socially optimal quantity, and give its size.

To earn the point: Correct policy and size. Model answer: A per-unit tax of 25.
