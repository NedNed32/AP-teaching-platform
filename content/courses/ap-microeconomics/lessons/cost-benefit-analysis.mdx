Rational decision-makers compare costs and benefits. The key insight of economics is that most choices are about **how much**, so the comparison happens at the margin: one more unit at a time.

## Explicit and implicit costs

The full cost of a choice includes:

- **Explicit costs:** money actually paid (tuition, rent, wages).
- **Implicit costs:** opportunities given up (the salary you could earn instead of going to school).

**Economic cost = explicit + implicit.** Accounting cost includes only the explicit part.

## The marginal rule

Keep doing an activity as long as the **marginal benefit (MB)** is at least the **marginal cost (MC)**. The optimal quantity is where

$$
MB = MC
$$

(or, with whole units, the last unit where MB is still at least MC).

## Sunk costs

A **sunk cost** has already been paid and cannot be recovered. It should not affect decisions about what to do next. Only future, avoidable costs and benefits matter.

<Note title="Total versus marginal">
Maximizing **net benefit** (total benefit minus total cost) is the same as following the MB = MC rule. Total benefit can keep rising after the optimum; what matters is whether the next unit is worth its cost.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="How many hours to tutor?">
A tutor's marginal benefit from each hour of tutoring and the marginal cost (fatigue and lost free time) are:

| Hour | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MB | 30 | 25 | 20 | 15 | 10 |
| MC | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 |

1. Hours 1 to 3: MB exceeds MC, so each adds net benefit.
2. Hour 4: MB 15 is less than MC 20, so it reduces net benefit.

**Answer:** 3 hours.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The table shows the marginal benefit and marginal cost of each unit of an activity.

*Marginal benefit and marginal cost*

| Unit | Marginal benefit | Marginal cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 49 | 10 |
| 2 | 40 | 14 |
| 3 | 31 | 20 |
| 4 | 22 | 26 |
| 5 | 13 | 33 |
| 6 | 4 | 40 |

How many units should a rational decision-maker choose?

**Answer:** 3

**Why:** Unit 3 is the last one whose marginal benefit is at least its marginal cost.

**Working:** Keep going while marginal benefit is at least marginal cost. That holds through unit 3 (MB 31, MC 20), but not for the next unit.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 2: Stopping here leaves units whose benefit exceeds their cost.
- 4: This unit costs more than it is worth.
- 1: Stopping here leaves units whose benefit exceeds their cost.
