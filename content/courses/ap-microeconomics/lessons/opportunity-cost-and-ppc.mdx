The production possibilities curve is the first model in economics. With two goods on the axes, it shows what an economy *can* produce, what it gives up to get more of one good, and how it grows.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/ppc.svg" alt="A bowed-out production possibilities curve with an efficient point on the curve, an inefficient point inside it, and an unattainable point outside it." caption="Points on the curve are efficient; inside is inefficient; outside is unattainable." />

## Reading a PPC

| Point | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| On the curve | efficient: all resources fully used |
| Inside | inefficient: unemployed or misallocated resources |
| Outside | unattainable with current resources and technology |

## Opportunity cost on the curve

Moving along the curve, gaining more of one good means giving up some of the other. The opportunity cost of one more unit of B is the amount of A given up:

$$
\text{opportunity cost of B} = \frac{\text{A given up}}{\text{B gained}}.
$$

- A **straight-line** PPC has **constant** opportunity cost (resources equally suited to both goods).
- A **bowed-out** PPC has **increasing** opportunity cost (resources are specialized).

## Shifts

The whole curve shifts outward with more resources, better technology or more human capital. A technology improvement in only one good pivots the curve outward along that good's axis.

<Note title="Recession versus shrinking capacity">
A recession moves the economy to a point **inside** the PPC; it does not shift the curve. The curve shifts inward only if productive capacity itself falls, for example after a natural disaster destroys capital.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Opportunity cost from a table">
An economy can produce these combinations:

| Option | Wheat (tons) | Cloth (bolts) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 0 | 30 |
| B | 10 | 26 |
| C | 20 | 18 |

What is the opportunity cost of producing the second 10 tons of wheat (from B to C)?

1. Cloth falls from 26 to 18: 8 bolts given up.
2. Per ton: $$\frac{8}{10} = 0.8$$ bolts.

**Answer:** 8 bolts of cloth for those 10 tons, or 0.8 bolts per ton. The first 10 tons cost only 4 bolts, so opportunity cost is increasing.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The table shows combinations of shirts and shoes an economy can produce with its resources fully employed.

*Production possibilities*

| Option | Shirts | Shoes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 19 | 0 |
| B | 16 | 10 |
| C | 11 | 20 |
| D | 4 | 30 |

Moving from option C to option D, what is the opportunity cost of each additional unit of shoes?

**Answer:** $$\frac{7}{10}$$ units of shirts

**Why:** 7 units of shirts are given up for 10 more units of shoes.

**Working:** Opportunity cost per unit of shoes = (shirts given up) ÷ (shoes gained) = 7 ÷ 10 = $$\frac{7}{10}$$. The cost rises along the table, so opportunity cost is increasing.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- $$\frac{10}{7}$$ units of shirts: This inverts the ratio: it is shoes per unit of shirts.
- $$\frac{11}{10}$$ units of shirts: This divides the total shirts at the first option instead of the amount given up.
- $$\frac{2}{15}$$ units of shirts: This divides totals at the new option; opportunity cost uses the changes.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A natural disaster destroys a large share of the country's factories. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The PPC shifts inward.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.

**Q.** A large wave of skilled immigrants joins the labor force. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The PPC shifts outward.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.

**Q.** Many workers in the economy become unemployed during a recession. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The economy moves to a point inside its PPC.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.

**Q.** A new technology makes workers in every industry more productive. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The PPC shifts outward.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.

**Q.** The economy shifts fully employed resources from producing consumer goods to producing capital goods. Which of the following best describes the effect on the economy's production possibilities curve (PPC)?

**A.** The economy moves along its PPC.. This matches whether resources, technology or only their use has changed. Changes in the quantity or quality of resources or in technology shift the PPC. Unemployment moves the economy inside the curve; reallocating fully employed resources moves it along the curve.
