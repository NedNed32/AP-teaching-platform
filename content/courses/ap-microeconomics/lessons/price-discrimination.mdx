Student discounts, early-bird fares and senior prices are all price discrimination: the same product sold at different prices to different buyers.

## Conditions

1. The firm has **market power** (a downward-sloping demand curve).
2. It can **separate** buyers into groups with different willingness to pay (different elasticities).
3. It can **prevent resale** from low-price buyers to high-price buyers.

## Who pays more?

The group with **less elastic** demand pays the higher price. Business travelers, who must fly on short notice, pay more than vacationers who can plan ahead.

## Perfect price discrimination

If the firm charges every buyer their maximum willingness to pay:

- its MR curve becomes the demand curve, so it produces where **demand meets MC**, the efficient quantity;
- **deadweight loss is zero**;
- **consumer surplus is zero**: all surplus goes to the firm.

<Note title="Efficient, but not equitable">
Perfect price discrimination is allocatively efficient, yet consumers gain nothing. Efficiency is about the size of total surplus, not who gets it.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Who gets the discount?">
A theater sells tickets to students and adults. Students' demand is more elastic. Which group should be charged less, and why can the theater do it?

1. Raising the price to the elastic group loses many sales; lowering it gains many.
2. A student ID check separates the groups and prevents resale.

**Answer:** students pay less, because their demand is more elastic; IDs make separation and no-resale possible.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** A monopolist becomes able to perfectly price discriminate. Compared with a single-price monopoly, which is true?

**A.** Output rises to the efficient level, and consumer surplus falls to zero.. Price discrimination charges more to buyers with less elastic demand, requires market power and no resale, and perfect discrimination captures all surplus. Price discrimination needs market power, separable groups and no resale. The less elastic group pays more. Perfect price discrimination produces the efficient quantity with no consumer surplus.
