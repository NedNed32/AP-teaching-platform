Restaurants, hair salons and clothing brands: many sellers, each offering something a little different. That combination is monopolistic competition.

## Features

- Many firms, each with a small share.
- **Differentiated** products, so each firm has a downward-sloping demand curve.
- Easy entry and exit.
- Non-price competition: advertising, branding, location.

## Short run

Like a monopolist, each firm produces where $$MR = MC$$ and prices from its demand curve. It may earn profits or losses.

## Long run

- Profits attract **entry**: each firm's demand shifts left (fewer customers per firm).
- Losses cause **exit**: each remaining firm's demand shifts right.
- Equilibrium: the demand curve is **tangent** to ATC, so $$P = ATC$$ and economic profit is zero.

## Efficiency

| Condition | Met? |
| --- | --- |
| Allocative ($$P = MC$$) | no, $$P > MC$$ |
| Productive ($$P = \min ATC$$) | no: excess capacity |

The inefficiency is the price of variety: consumers get choices that perfect competition would not offer.

<Note title="Zero profit, yet P is above MC">
Do not confuse the two conditions. In long-run monopolistic competition, $$P = ATC$$ (normal profit) but $$P > MC$$ (allocative inefficiency).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Adjusting to profit">
A popular new coffee style earns local cafés economic profits. Describe the long-run adjustment.

1. Profits attract new cafés offering similar drinks.
2. Each existing café loses some customers: its demand shifts left and becomes more elastic.
3. Entry continues until demand is tangent to ATC.

**Answer:** entry reduces each café's demand until economic profit is zero, with price above MC and excess capacity remaining.
</WorkedExample>

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? In long-run equilibrium, price equals average total cost but exceeds marginal cost, and firms have excess capacity.

**A.** Monopolistic competition. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? A few interdependent firms consider rivals' reactions when setting prices.

**A.** Oligopoly. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**A.** Monopoly. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.
