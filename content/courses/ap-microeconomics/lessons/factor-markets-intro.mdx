In product markets, firms sell and households buy. In **factor markets** the roles reverse: households sell labor, land and capital, and firms buy them.

## Derived demand

Firms do not want workers for their own sake; they want the output workers produce. So the demand for a factor is **derived** from the demand for the product.

## Marginal revenue product

The value of hiring one more worker is the extra revenue that worker generates:

$$
MRP = MP \times MR.
$$

For a firm selling in a perfectly competitive product market, $$MR = P$$, so $$MRP = MP \times P$$. Because marginal product eventually diminishes, MRP slopes downward: it **is** the firm's demand curve for labor.

## Marginal factor cost

$$MFC$$ is the cost of one more unit of the factor. In a competitive labor market, the firm can hire as many workers as it wants at the market wage, so $$MFC = \text{wage}$$.

<Note title="Same logic as MR = MC">
Factor markets apply the familiar marginal rule from the hiring side: keep hiring while the next worker adds more to revenue (MRP) than to cost (MFC).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="An MRP table">
A firm sells shirts at 4 each. The marginal products of the 1st, 2nd and 3rd workers are 20, 15 and 10. Find each worker's MRP.

1. $$20 \times 4 = 80$$.
2. $$15 \times 4 = 60$$.
3. $$10 \times 4 = 40$$.

**Answer:** MRP of 80, 60 and 40.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A firm sells its output in a perfectly competitive market at a price of 7 and hires workers in a perfectly competitive labor market at a wage of 23.

*Marginal product of labor*

| Worker | Marginal product |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 17 |
| 2 | 12 |
| 3 | 8 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 1 |
| 6 | 1 |

How many workers should the firm hire to maximize profit?

**Answer:** 4

**Why:** Worker 4 is the last whose MRP (42) is at least the wage.

**Working:** MRP = MP × P: 119, 84, 56, 42, 7, 7. Hire while MRP ≥ wage (23): 4 workers.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 3: Another worker would add more revenue than the wage costs.
- 5: This worker's MRP is below the wage.
- 6: This worker's MRP is below the wage.

### Free-response practice: Hiring workers

A firm sells its product in a perfectly competitive market at 2 per unit and hires labor in a perfectly competitive market at a wage of 28. The marginal products of workers 1 to 5 are 17, 15, 11, 9, 6.

**(a)** Calculate the marginal revenue product of the third worker.

To earn the point: MP × P. Model answer: 11 × 2 = 22

**(b)** How many workers will the firm hire? Explain.

To earn the point: Correct number with the MRP ≥ wage rule. Model answer: 2: MRP is at least the wage of 28 for workers 1 to 2 only.

**(c)** If the price of the product rises, what happens to the firm's demand for labor? Explain.

To earn the point: Increases, because MRP rises. Model answer: Each worker's MRP = MP × P rises, so labor demand shifts right.
