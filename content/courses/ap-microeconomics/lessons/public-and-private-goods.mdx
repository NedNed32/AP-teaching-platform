Markets work well for goods like sandwiches: if you eat one, I cannot, and you cannot eat it without paying. Some goods do not work like that, and markets struggle to provide them.

## Four kinds of goods

| | Excludable | Non-excludable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rival** | private goods (food, clothing) | common resources (ocean fish, clean air) |
| **Non-rival** | club goods (streaming service, toll road) | public goods (national defense, lighthouses) |

## Public goods and free riders

A public good is **non-rival** and **non-excludable**. Because people can benefit without paying, each person has an incentive to **free ride**. Private firms cannot collect enough revenue, so markets provide too little. Governments usually provide public goods, funded by taxes.

## Common resources

Rival but non-excludable goods are overused: the **tragedy of the commons**. Each user ignores the cost their use imposes on others. Solutions include quotas, permits and assigning property rights.

<Note title="Non-rival is not free">
A non-rival good still costs resources to produce. Non-rivalry means an extra user adds no cost once it exists, not that it costs nothing to build.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying goods">
Classify: (I) a city fireworks show; (II) a subscription music service; (III) groundwater pumped by many farmers.

1. (I) Anyone nearby can watch, and viewers do not reduce each other's view: public good.
2. (II) Non-paying users can be excluded, but one listener does not use up the song: club good.
3. (III) Water one farmer pumps is unavailable to others, and it is hard to exclude pumpers: common resource.

**Answer:** public good, club good, common resource.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** How is a toll road that is rarely congested best classified?

**A.** Club good: non-rival and excludable. Check rivalry (does one person's use reduce availability?) and excludability (can non-payers be kept out?). Goods are classified by rivalry and excludability: private (both), public (neither), common resources (rival only), club goods (excludable only).
