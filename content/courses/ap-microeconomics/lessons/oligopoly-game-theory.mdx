In an oligopoly a few large firms dominate, so each firm's best choice depends on what its rivals do. Game theory is the tool for analyzing that interdependence.

## The payoff matrix

Each cell shows the payoffs (Firm A, Firm B) for a pair of strategies:

| | B: High price | B: Low price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A: High price** | (50, 50) | (20, 70) |
| **A: Low price** | (70, 20) | (30, 30) |

## Dominant strategies

For Firm A: if B prices high, A gets 70 from low versus 50 from high; if B prices low, 30 from low versus 20 from high. **Low price is dominant for A.** By symmetry, it is dominant for B too.

## Nash equilibrium

A cell is a Nash equilibrium if neither player wants to switch, given the other's choice. Here (Low, Low) with (30, 30) is the Nash equilibrium, even though both would be better off at (High, High). This is a **prisoner's dilemma**, and it is why collusion is hard to sustain.

## Steps for any matrix

1. For each of B's strategies, find A's best response (circle it).
2. For each of A's strategies, find B's best response.
3. Cells where both payoffs are circled are Nash equilibria.

<Note title="Read the payoffs carefully">
By convention the first number belongs to the row player and the second to the column player. Compare only a player's own payoffs when finding its best response.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A game with no dominant strategy for one player">
Payoffs (A, B):

| | B: Left | B: Right |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A: Up** | (8, 6) | (4, 7) |
| **A: Down** | (5, 3) | (6, 5) |

1. A's best responses: to Left, Up (8 > 5); to Right, Down (6 > 4). A has no dominant strategy.
2. B's best responses: to Up, Right (7 > 6); to Down, Right (5 > 3). Right is dominant for B.
3. Given B plays Right, A plays Down.

**Answer:** the Nash equilibrium is (Down, Right) with payoffs (6, 5).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Two firms choose simultaneously whether to advertise. Each cell shows (Firm A's profit, Firm B's profit).

*Payoffs (Firm A, Firm B)*

| Firm A \ Firm B | Firm B: Advertise | Firm B: Don't advertise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Firm A: Advertise | (11, 10) | (8, 12) |
| Firm A: Don't advertise | (2, 5) | (11, 8) |

Which outcome is the Nash equilibrium?

**Answer:** Firm A does not advertise, Firm B does not advertise

**Why:** Neither firm can raise its own payoff by changing only its own strategy.

**Working:** Check each cell: in (Don't advertise, Don't advertise) with payoffs (11, 8), Firm A would get 8 by switching and Firm B 5, both lower. No other cell has that property.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Firm A advertises, Firm B advertises: At least one firm would switch strategies from this cell to increase its own payoff.
- Firm A advertises, Firm B does not advertise: At least one firm would switch strategies from this cell to increase its own payoff.
- Firm A does not advertise, Firm B advertises: At least one firm would switch strategies from this cell to increase its own payoff.

### Worked exam question 2

Two firms choose simultaneously whether to advertise. Each cell shows (Firm A's profit, Firm B's profit).

*Payoffs (Firm A, Firm B)*

| Firm A \ Firm B | Firm B: Advertise | Firm B: Don't advertise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Firm A: Advertise | (7, 7) | (3, 10) |
| Firm A: Don't advertise | (3, 8) | (5, 2) |

Which of the following is true about Firm A?

**Answer:** Firm A has no dominant strategy.

**Why:** If B advertises, A gets 7 (advertise) or 3 (not); if B does not, A gets 3 or 5.

**Working:** A strategy is dominant if it gives the higher payoff whichever strategy the other firm picks. Compare only Firm A's payoffs within each column.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Firm A's dominant strategy is to advertise.: Compare Firm A's own payoffs (the first number) for each of Firm B's choices.
- Firm A's dominant strategy is not to advertise.: Compare Firm A's own payoffs (the first number) for each of Firm B's choices.
- Firm A's dominant strategy depends on Firm B's dominant strategy.: A dominant strategy, by definition, is best whatever the other firm does.

### Free-response practice: A pricing game

Two firms, Alpha and Beta, choose prices at the same time. Each cell shows (Alpha's profit, Beta's profit) in thousands. Both firms know the payoffs.

*Payoffs (Alpha, Beta)*

| Alpha \ Beta | Beta: High price | Beta: Low price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alpha: High price | (13, 3) | (3, 15) |
| Alpha: Low price | (8, 15) | (2, 6) |

**(a)** Does Alpha have a dominant strategy? Explain using the payoffs.

To earn the point: Correct answer with the payoff comparison. Model answer: Yes, High price: it pays more whether Beta chooses High (13 > 8) or Low (3 > 2).

**(b)** Does Beta have a dominant strategy? Explain.

To earn the point: Correct answer. Model answer: No dominant strategy for Beta.

**(c)** Identify every Nash equilibrium.

To earn the point: Lists the Nash equilibrium cells (or states there is none in pure strategies). Model answer: (High price, Low price)

**(d)** Suppose the government pays Alpha a bonus of 2 whenever Alpha chooses Low price. Recompute Alpha's payoffs. Does Alpha now have a dominant strategy?

To earn the point: Adds the bonus to both Low-price payoffs and evaluates dominance. Model answer: Low-price payoffs become 10 and 4. No.
