A monopolist is the market. It faces the whole downward-sloping market demand curve and chooses both its output and its price.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/monopoly.svg" alt="A monopoly graph with demand, a steeper marginal revenue curve and constant marginal cost; the monopoly price is read from demand above where MR meets MC, and a shaded deadweight-loss triangle lies between the monopoly and competitive quantities." caption="Monopoly output where MR = MC; price from the demand curve; the shaded triangle is deadweight loss." />

## Choosing output and price

1. Find the quantity where $$MR = MC$$.
2. Go up to the **demand** curve to find the price.
3. Profit $$= (P - ATC) \times Q$$.

## Compared with perfect competition

| | Monopoly | Perfect competition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quantity | lower | higher |
| Price | higher ($$P > MC$$) | $$P = MC$$ |
| Allocative efficiency | no: deadweight loss | yes |
| Long-run profit | possible | zero |

The **deadweight loss** is the triangle between demand and MC from the monopoly quantity to the efficient quantity.

## Barriers to entry

Economies of scale (natural monopoly), patents and copyrights, control of a key resource, government licenses, and network effects.

<Note title="Regulating a natural monopoly">
A regulator can force price down to MC (the socially optimal price), but the firm may then lose money because $$P < ATC$$. A **fair-return price** at $$P = ATC$$ lets the firm break even.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Linear demand and constant cost">
Demand is $$P = 100 - 2Q$$, and MC = ATC = 20. Find the monopoly output, price, profit and deadweight loss.

1. $$MR = 100 - 4Q = 20$$ gives $$Q = 20$$, and $$P = 100 - 40 = 60$$.
2. Profit $$= (60 - 20) \times 20 = 800$$.
3. Efficient quantity: $$P = MC$$ gives $$100 - 2Q = 20$$, $$Q = 40$$.
4. DWL $$= \frac{1}{2} \times (40 - 20) \times (60 - 20) = 400$$.

**Answer:** $$Q = 20$$, $$P = 60$$, profit 800, deadweight loss 400.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A monopolist faces demand $$P = 55 - 3Q$$ and has constant marginal cost and average total cost of 13. What is the profit-maximizing quantity?

**Answer:** 7

**Why:** $$MR = 55 - 6Q = 13$$ gives $$Q = 7$$, so $$P = 34$$.

**Working:** MR = 55 − 6Q. Setting MR = MC: Q = 7; price from demand: P = 34. Profit = (34 − 13) × 7 = 147. The efficient quantity is 14, so DWL = ½ × 7 × 21 = 73.5.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 14: This is the efficient quantity where P = MC.
- 5.5: MR = MC sets the quantity; MR has twice the slope of demand.
- 18.33: This is where demand reaches a price of zero.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? In long-run equilibrium, price equals average total cost but exceeds marginal cost, and firms have excess capacity.

**A.** Monopolistic competition. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? A few interdependent firms consider rivals' reactions when setting prices.

**A.** Oligopoly. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? A single firm with high barriers to entry can earn economic profit in the long run.

**A.** Monopoly. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**Q.** Which market structure is described? In long-run equilibrium, price equals marginal cost and minimum average total cost.

**A.** Perfect competition. This matches the structure's long-run conditions. Perfect competition: P = MC = min ATC. Monopolistic competition: P = ATC > MC, excess capacity. Oligopoly: few interdependent firms. Monopoly: one firm, barriers, possible long-run profit.

**Q.** A regulator sets the price a natural monopoly may charge at the level where the demand curve intersects average total cost. Which of the following results?

**A.** The firm earns zero economic profit (a fair return).. At P = ATC, total revenue equals total cost. The socially optimal price (P = MC) gives the efficient quantity but, for a natural monopoly, a loss. The fair-return price (P = ATC) gives zero economic profit with output between the monopoly and efficient levels.

**Q.** A regulator sets the price a natural monopoly may charge at the level where the demand curve intersects marginal cost. Which of the following results?

**A.** The allocatively efficient quantity is produced, but the firm may need a subsidy because price is below average total cost.. At P = MC, output is efficient, but a natural monopoly's ATC exceeds MC. The socially optimal price (P = MC) gives the efficient quantity but, for a natural monopoly, a loss. The fair-return price (P = ATC) gives zero economic profit with output between the monopoly and efficient levels.

### Free-response practice: A monopoly

A profit-maximizing monopolist faces the demand curve $$P = 73 - 2Q$$, so its marginal revenue is $$MR = 73 - 4Q$$. Its marginal cost and average total cost are constant at 17.

**(a)** Calculate the profit-maximizing quantity and price.

To earn the point: Sets MR = MC to find quantity. Model answer: 73 − 4Q = 17, so Q = 14

To earn the point: Reads price from demand. Model answer: P = 73 − 2(14) = 45

**(b)** Calculate the monopolist's profit.

To earn the point: Correct profit. Model answer: (45 − 17) × 14 = 392

**(c)** Calculate the allocatively efficient quantity and the deadweight loss of monopoly.

To earn the point: Efficient quantity where P = MC. Model answer: 73 − 2Q = 17, so Q = 28

To earn the point: Deadweight loss. Model answer: ½ × (28 − 14) × (45 − 17) = 196

**(d)** The government imposes a lump-sum tax on the monopolist. What happens to the profit-maximizing quantity? Explain.

To earn the point: No change, because MC and MR are unchanged. Model answer: A lump-sum tax raises fixed cost only, so MR = MC at the same quantity; profit falls.

**(e)** If the monopolist could perfectly price discriminate, what would happen to consumer surplus and deadweight loss?

To earn the point: Both become zero. Model answer: Output rises to 28; the firm captures all surplus, so consumer surplus and deadweight loss are zero.
