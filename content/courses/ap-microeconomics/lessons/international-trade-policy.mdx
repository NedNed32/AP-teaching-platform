Opening a market to world trade changes the price and redistributes surplus. Trade barriers move it back partway, with a loss of efficiency.

## Free trade

| World price versus domestic equilibrium | Result |
| --- | --- |
| below | the country **imports**; consumers gain, producers lose, total surplus rises |
| above | the country **exports**; producers gain, consumers lose, total surplus rises |

At the world price, the gap between domestic quantity demanded and quantity supplied is the amount of imports (or exports).

## Tariffs

A tariff raises the domestic price from the world price $$P_w$$ to $$P_w + t$$:

- domestic production rises, consumption falls, imports fall;
- government collects $$t \times \text{imports}$$;
- two deadweight-loss triangles appear (overproduction and underconsumption).

## Quotas

A quota has the same price and quantity effects as an equivalent tariff, but the revenue goes to whoever holds the import licenses rather than the government.

<Note title="Winners, losers, net loss">
Trade barriers help domestic producers and hurt domestic consumers. The consumers' loss is larger than the producers' gain plus government revenue: the difference is the deadweight loss.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Imports under a tariff">
At the world price of 4, domestic buyers want 100 units and domestic producers supply 40. A tariff of 1 raises the price to 5, where buyers want 90 and producers supply 50. Find imports before and after, and the tariff revenue.

1. Before: $$100 - 40 = 60$$ imported.
2. After: $$90 - 50 = 40$$ imported.
3. Revenue: $$1 \times 40 = 40$$.

**Answer:** imports fall from 60 to 40; tariff revenue is 40.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

At the world price of 10, domestic consumers buy 131 units and domestic producers supply 52. A tariff of 1 raises the domestic price to 11, where consumers buy 125 and producers supply 60. How much revenue does the tariff raise?

**Answer:** 65

**Why:** 1 × imports of 65.

**Working:** Imports after the tariff = 125 − 60 = 65. Tariff revenue = 1 × 65 = 65.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 79: Use imports after the tariff.
- 125: The tariff applies only to imports, not to domestic production.
- 715: Revenue is the tariff per unit, not the full price.

### Worked exam question 2

At the world price of 8, domestic consumers buy 143 units and domestic producers supply 58. A tariff of 3 raises the domestic price to 11, where consumers buy 134 and producers supply 70. By how much do imports fall?

**Answer:** 21

**Why:** Imports fall from 85 to 64.

**Working:** Imports = domestic quantity demanded − domestic quantity supplied: 85 before, 64 after, a fall of 21.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 64: This is the level of imports after the tariff.
- 85: This is the level of imports before the tariff.
- 9: This is only the fall in consumption; domestic production also rises.
