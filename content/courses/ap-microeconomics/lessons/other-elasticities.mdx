Price elasticity measures response to a good's own price. Two other elasticities measure response to other prices and to income, and their **signs** carry the key information.

## Cross-price elasticity

$$
E_{AB} = \frac{\%\Delta Q_A}{\%\Delta P_B}
$$

| Sign | Relationship | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| positive | substitutes | butter and margarine |
| negative | complements | printers and ink |
| zero | unrelated | shoes and salt |

## Income elasticity

$$
E_I = \frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta \text{Income}}
$$

| Value | Type of good |
| --- | --- |
| greater than 1 | normal, luxury (income-elastic) |
| between 0 and 1 | normal, necessity |
| negative | inferior |

<Note title="Keep the sign">
Unlike price elasticity of demand, do **not** take the absolute value of cross-price or income elasticity. The sign is the whole point.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying with elasticities">
When incomes rise from 40,000 to 44,000 (10 percent), spending on cruises rises 25 percent and bus rides fall 5 percent. Classify each good.

1. Cruises: $$\frac{25}{10} = 2.5$$, positive and greater than 1.
2. Bus rides: $$\frac{-5}{10} = -0.5$$, negative.

**Answer:** cruises are a normal luxury good; bus rides are an inferior good.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

When consumer income rises by 40%, the quantity demanded of a good rises by 25%. The good is

**Answer:** a normal good (necessity)

**Why:** Income elasticity = 25 ÷ 40 = 0.63.

**Working:** Income elasticity 0.63: negative means inferior, between 0 and 1 a necessity, above 1 a luxury.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- a normal good (luxury): Check the sign and size of the income elasticity.
- an inferior good: Check the sign and size of the income elasticity.
- a complement: Complements are identified with cross-price elasticity, not income elasticity.

### Worked exam question 2

When the price of good Y rises by 30%, the quantity demanded of good X falls by 35%. What is the cross-price elasticity, and how are the goods related?

**Answer:** -1.17; complements

**Why:** A negative cross-price elasticity means complements.

**Working:** Cross-price elasticity = %ΔQx ÷ %ΔPy = -35 ÷ 30 = -1.17.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- -1.17; substitutes: The sign tells the relationship: positive for substitutes, negative for complements.
- -0.86; complements: The percentage change in quantity goes on top.
- 1.17; unrelated goods: Unrelated goods have a cross-price elasticity of zero; keep the sign.
