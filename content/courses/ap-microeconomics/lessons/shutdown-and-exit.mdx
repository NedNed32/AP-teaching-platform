A firm losing money faces two different questions: should it stop producing today (the short run), and should it leave the industry altogether (the long run)?

## Short run: shut down?

In the short run, fixed costs must be paid anyway. So compare price with **average variable cost**:

| Price | Short-run decision | Loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $$P \geq$$ min ATC | produce | profit or break even |
| min AVC $$\leq P <$$ min ATC | produce | loss smaller than fixed cost |
| $$P <$$ min AVC | shut down | loss equals fixed cost |

The firm's short-run supply curve is its MC curve above minimum AVC.

## Long run: enter or exit?

In the long run there are no fixed costs, so:

- **Economic profit** ($$P >$$ min ATC) attracts entry: market supply increases, the price falls.
- **Economic loss** ($$P <$$ min ATC) causes exit: market supply decreases, the price rises.

Entry and exit continue until $$P =$$ minimum ATC and economic profit is zero.

<Note title="Losses do not mean shut down">
A firm can keep producing at a loss in the short run, if price covers average variable cost. Shutting down would lose all of the fixed cost, which is worse.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Shut down or not?">
A firm has fixed cost 300. At its best output of 100 units, price is 7, AVC is 6 and ATC is 9. Should it produce in the short run?

1. Producing: loss $$= (7 - 9) \times 100 = -200$$.
2. Shutting down: loss = fixed cost $$= -300$$.

**Answer:** produce; the loss of 200 is smaller than 300, because price exceeds AVC.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A perfectly competitive firm's minimum average variable cost is 23 and its minimum average total cost is 29. The market price is 33. What should the firm do?

**Answer:** Produce in the short run; it earns an economic profit, so firms enter in the long run.

**Why:** Price is above minimum ATC.

**Working:** Short run: produce if P ≥ min AVC (23). Long run: entry if P > min ATC (29), exit if P &lt; min ATC. Here P = 33.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Produce in the short run at a loss; in the long run, firms exit the industry.: Compare price with minimum AVC (short-run shutdown) and minimum ATC (long-run entry or exit).
- Shut down in the short run; in the long run, firms exit the industry.: Compare price with minimum AVC (short-run shutdown) and minimum ATC (long-run entry or exit).
- Shut down in the short run; in the long run, firms enter the industry.: Firms do not enter an industry that is making losses.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A perfectly competitive, constant-cost industry is in long-run equilibrium. Market demand then decreases permanently. Which of the following describes the long-run adjustment?

**A.** Firms exit, market supply decreases, and the price rises back to minimum ATC.. Short-run losses cause exit. The lower price creates economic losses. Firms exit, shifting market supply left until price returns to minimum ATC and economic profit is zero.

**Q.** A perfectly competitive, constant-cost industry is in long-run equilibrium. Market demand then increases permanently. Which of the following describes the long-run adjustment?

**A.** Firms enter, market supply increases, and the price falls back to minimum ATC.. Short-run profits attract entry. The higher price creates economic profits. Firms enter, shifting market supply right until price returns to minimum ATC and economic profit is zero.
