Governments often step into markets: capping rents, setting minimum wages, taxing gasoline or subsidizing electric cars. Each intervention changes prices, quantities and who gains.

## Price controls

| Control | Binding when | Result | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ceiling (maximum) | below equilibrium | shortage, deadweight loss | rent control |
| Floor (minimum) | above equilibrium | surplus, deadweight loss | minimum wage |

A non-binding control (a ceiling above equilibrium, or a floor below it) has no effect.

## Per-unit taxes

A tax of $$t$$ per unit shifts supply up by $$t$$ (or drives a wedge of $$t$$ between the price buyers pay and the price sellers keep).

- Quantity falls.
- **Tax revenue** $$= t \times Q_{\text{after tax}}$$.
- **Deadweight loss** is the triangle between the old and new quantity.
- **Incidence:** the side with **more inelastic** curve bears more of the tax.

## Subsidies

A per-unit subsidy shifts supply down (right). Quantity rises above the efficient level, so the subsidy also creates a deadweight loss, and its cost to government is $$\text{subsidy} \times Q$$.

<Note title="Who writes the check does not matter">
Whether the law collects the tax from buyers or sellers, the economic burden is shared the same way, set by elasticities.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A per-unit tax">
Before a tax, $$P = 10$$ and $$Q = 100$$. After a tax of 4 per unit, buyers pay 13, sellers keep 9, and $$Q = 80$$. Find the tax revenue, the share paid by buyers, and the deadweight loss.

1. Revenue: $$4 \times 80 = 320$$.
2. Buyers pay $$13 - 10 = 3$$ of the 4: three quarters of the tax.
3. Deadweight loss: $$\frac{1}{2} \times (100 - 80) \times 4 = 40$$.

**Answer:** revenue 320; buyers bear 75 percent; deadweight loss 40.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Demand is $$Q_d = 108 - 4P$$ and supply is $$Q_s = 18 + 5P$$. The government sets a price floor of 15. What results?

**Answer:** A surplus of 45 units

**Why:** At 15, $$Q_d = 48$$ and $$Q_s = 93$$.

**Working:** Equilibrium is P = 10, Q = 68. At the floor of 15, quantity demanded is 48 and quantity supplied is 93: a surplus of 45.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- A shortage of 45 units: A binding floor (above equilibrium) creates a surplus.
- No effect, because the control is not binding: Equilibrium price is 10, so a floor of 15 is binding.
- A surplus of 20 units: Compare quantity demanded with quantity supplied at the controlled price, not with the old equilibrium.

### Worked exam question 2

Before a tax, the market price is 29 and quantity is 190. After a per-unit tax of 6, buyers pay 33, sellers keep 27, and quantity falls to 130. How much tax revenue does the government collect?

**Answer:** 780

**Why:** 6 × 130.

**Working:** Tax revenue = tax per unit × quantity sold after the tax = 6 × 130 = 780.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1,140: Revenue uses the quantity after the tax.
- 4,290: This is total spending by buyers.
- 520: This is only the buyers' share of the tax revenue.

### Worked exam question 3

Before a tax, the market price is 24 and quantity is 200. After a per-unit tax of 5, buyers pay 27, sellers keep 22, and quantity falls to 160. What is the deadweight loss?

**Answer:** 100

**Why:** $$\frac{1}{2} \times (200 - 160) \times 5$$.

**Working:** DWL = ½ × (lost quantity) × (tax) = ½ × 40 × 5 = 100.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 200: The deadweight loss is a triangle: multiply by one half.
- 800: This is the tax revenue.
- 60: The height of the triangle is the whole tax, not the buyers' share.

### Worked exam question 4

Before a per-unit tax of 7, the market price is 21. After the tax, buyers pay 26 and sellers keep 19. Which of the following is true?

**Answer:** Buyers bear more of the tax, so demand is relatively more inelastic.

**Why:** Buyers pay 5 more; sellers receive 2 less.

**Working:** Buyers' price rose by 5 and sellers' price fell by 2. The more inelastic side bears more of the burden.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Sellers bear more of the tax, so supply is relatively more inelastic.: Compare how much each side's price changed.
- Buyers bear more of the tax, so demand is relatively more elastic.: The side that cannot easily adjust (more inelastic) bears more of the tax.
- The side the law taxes pays the whole tax.: The burden depends on elasticities, not on who is legally taxed.

### Free-response practice: A per-unit tax

In a competitive market, the equilibrium price is 16 and quantity is 210. The government imposes a per-unit tax of 6 on sellers. Afterwards, buyers pay 20, sellers keep 14, and 150 units are sold.

**(a)** Calculate the government's tax revenue.

To earn the point: Correct value. Model answer: 6 × 150 = 900

**(b)** Calculate the deadweight loss.

To earn the point: Correct value. Model answer: ½ × 60 × 6 = 180

**(c)** Do buyers or sellers bear more of the tax? What does this imply about the relative elasticities of demand and supply?

To earn the point: Correct side and elasticity comparison. Model answer: Buyers (4 of 6); demand is relatively more inelastic than supply.
