In the long run a firm can choose its plant size. The long-run average total cost curve traces the lowest-cost plant for every output level.

## The LRATC curve

LRATC is the "envelope" of many short-run ATC curves, each for a different plant size. Its shape reflects returns to scale.

| Region of LRATC | Returns to scale | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| falling | economies of scale (increasing returns) | specialization, bulk purchasing, spreading large fixed costs |
| flat | constant returns | |
| rising | diseconomies of scale (decreasing returns) | communication and coordination problems |

## Why it matters for market structure

- If economies of scale continue across the whole market's output, one large firm can produce at the lowest cost: a **natural monopoly** (topic 4.2).
- If the **minimum efficient scale** is small relative to the market, many firms can compete at low cost.

<Note title="Scale is not the short run">
Returns to scale ask what happens when **all** inputs grow together. Diminishing marginal returns ask what happens when one input grows while another stays fixed.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Reading returns to scale">
A bakery doubles its ovens, workers and space. Output rises from 1,000 to 2,500 loaves a day. Later, doubling all inputs again raises output only from 2,500 to 4,000. Describe each step.

1. First doubling: output more than doubles, so increasing returns to scale: LRATC falls.
2. Second doubling: output less than doubles, so decreasing returns to scale: LRATC rises.

**Answer:** economies of scale at first, then diseconomies of scale.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A one-time license fee is imposed on the firm. How are the firm's short-run cost curves affected?

**A.** ATC and AFC shift up; MC and AVC do not change.. Fixed costs affect only average fixed and total costs; variable costs affect marginal and average variable costs too. A change in fixed cost shifts AFC and ATC but leaves MC and AVC unchanged. A change in a per-unit (variable) cost shifts MC, AVC and ATC.

**Q.** The price of raw materials used in each unit falls. How are the firm's short-run cost curves affected?

**A.** MC, AVC and ATC all shift down.. Fixed costs affect only average fixed and total costs; variable costs affect marginal and average variable costs too. A change in fixed cost shifts AFC and ATC but leaves MC and AVC unchanged. A change in a per-unit (variable) cost shifts MC, AVC and ATC.
