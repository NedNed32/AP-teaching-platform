How should a consumer split a budget between pizza and movies? Economics answers with marginal utility: spend each dollar where it adds the most satisfaction.

## Diminishing marginal utility

The first slice of pizza is wonderful; the fifth is barely worth eating. Marginal utility usually **falls** as you consume more of a good.

## The rule

Compare goods by **marginal utility per dollar**, $$\frac{MU}{P}$$. If one good gives more satisfaction per dollar, shift spending toward it. The consumer maximizes total utility when

$$
\frac{MU_A}{P_A} = \frac{MU_B}{P_B}
$$

and the whole budget is spent.

## Steps with a table

1. Compute $$\frac{MU}{P}$$ for each unit of each good.
2. Buy units in order of highest $$\frac{MU}{P}$$ until the budget runs out.
3. Check that the last units bought give (about) equal MU per dollar.

<Note title="Compare per dollar, not totals">
A good with higher marginal utility is not necessarily the better buy: a concert ticket may give more satisfaction than a sandwich, but much less per dollar.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Spending a small budget">
A consumer has 10 to spend. Apples cost 1 and sandwiches cost 3. The MU of the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th apple is 6, 5, 4, 3; of the 1st and 2nd sandwich, 15 and 12.

1. MU per dollar: apples 6, 5, 4, 3; sandwiches 5, 4.
2. Buy in order: apple (6), then apple and sandwich (5 each), then apple and sandwich (4 each).
3. Cost so far: 3 apples (3) + 2 sandwiches (6) = 9; the next best is the 4th apple (3 per dollar) for 1 more, using the full 10.

**Answer:** 4 apples and 2 sandwiches.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A consumer spends her whole budget on goods A and B. For the last units bought, the marginal utility of A is 14 with a price of 2, and the marginal utility of B is 40 with a price of 4. To maximize utility, she should

**Answer:** buy more of good B and less of the other good

**Why:** Good A gives 7 utils per dollar; good B gives 10.

**Working:** MU per dollar: A = 14 ÷ 2 = 7; B = 40 ÷ 4 = 10. Shift spending toward B until the ratios are equal.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- buy more of good A and less of the other good: Compare marginal utility per dollar, not marginal utility alone.
- keep her purchases unchanged: The marginal utilities per dollar are not equal, so a better bundle exists.
- buy more of the good with the higher marginal utility, B: A higher marginal utility does not mean a better buy if the price is also higher.
