Every society answers the same three questions: what to produce, how to produce it, and who gets it. Economic systems differ in who answers them.

## Three systems

| System | Who decides | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Market | individuals and firms, guided by prices | efficiency, innovation, choice | inequality, market failures |
| Command | central planners | can direct resources to state priorities | weak incentives, shortages and surpluses |
| Mixed | markets plus government | combines incentives with public goods and safety nets | debates over the right balance |

## Prices as signals

In a market economy, prices carry information and incentives. If people want more of a good, its price rises; producers earn more by making it, so resources flow toward it. This decentralized coordination is often called the **invisible hand**.

## What markets need

- **Property rights**, so people can keep what they earn and invest.
- **Rule of law and enforceable contracts**.
- **Competition**, so no single seller can dictate prices.

<Note title="Traditional economies">
Some textbooks add a fourth type, the traditional economy, where custom decides what is produced. The AP exam focuses on market, command and mixed systems.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Responding to demand">
Demand for bicycles surges in a city. Explain how a market economy responds without anyone ordering more bicycles.

1. Shortages at the old price push the price of bicycles up.
2. Higher prices make bicycle production more profitable.
3. Existing shops produce more and new sellers enter, drawing resources from less valued uses.

**Answer:** rising prices and profits signal producers to supply more, reallocating resources automatically.
</WorkedExample>

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Prices determined by supply and demand guide what is produced. Which economic system does this describe?

**A.** Market economy. This matches who makes the key allocation decisions. Market economies rely on prices and private decisions; command economies on central planning; mixed economies combine markets with government roles.

**Q.** A central planning agency sets production targets for every factory. Which economic system does this describe?

**A.** Command economy. This matches who makes the key allocation decisions. Market economies rely on prices and private decisions; command economies on central planning; mixed economies combine markets with government roles.

**Q.** Most goods are sold in markets, but the government provides schools and regulates pollution. Which economic system does this describe?

**A.** Mixed economy. This matches who makes the key allocation decisions. Market economies rely on prices and private decisions; command economies on central planning; mixed economies combine markets with government roles.
