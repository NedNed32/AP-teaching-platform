A firm turns inputs into output. In the **short run** at least one input (usually capital) is fixed; in the **long run** all inputs can change.

## Product measures

$$
MP = \frac{\Delta TP}{\Delta L}, \qquad AP = \frac{TP}{L}.
$$

| Workers | Total product | Marginal product | Average product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | | |
| 1 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| 2 | 24 | 14 | 12 |
| 3 | 34 | 10 | 11.3 |
| 4 | 40 | 6 | 10 |
| 5 | 42 | 2 | 8.4 |

## Three stages

1. **Increasing marginal returns:** early workers specialize, so MP rises (workers 1 to 2 above).
2. **Diminishing marginal returns:** MP falls but is still positive (from worker 3 on).
3. **Negative marginal returns:** too many workers get in each other's way and TP falls.

When MP is above AP, AP rises; when MP is below AP, AP falls. So MP crosses AP at AP's maximum.

<Note title="Diminishing returns versus diseconomies of scale">
Diminishing marginal returns is a **short-run** idea (one input fixed). Diseconomies of scale (topic 3.3) are a **long-run** idea, when all inputs grow.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Where do diminishing returns begin?">
Using the table above, after which worker do diminishing marginal returns begin?

1. MP: 10, 14, 10, 6, 2.
2. MP rises through the 2nd worker and falls from the 3rd.

**Answer:** diminishing marginal returns begin with the 3rd worker.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The table shows a firm's total product as it hires more workers, with capital fixed.

*Total product*

| Workers | Total product |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 10 |
| 2 | 24 |
| 3 | 33 |
| 4 | 37 |
| 5 | 38 |
| 6 | 38 |

What is the marginal product of worker 6?

**Answer:** 0

**Why:** 38 − 38.

**Working:** Marginal product = change in total product = 38 − 38 = 0. Diminishing marginal returns begin after worker 2, where MP starts falling.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 38: This is total product, not the change in it.
- 6.3: This is average product.
- 1: This is the marginal product of the previous worker.
