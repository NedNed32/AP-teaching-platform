Markets are not always at equilibrium. When the price is wrong, quantities do not match, and pressure builds to move the price.

<Figure src="assets/lessons/supply-demand-shift.svg" alt="Supply and demand with an original equilibrium E1 and a new equilibrium E2 after demand shifts right." caption="An increase in demand raises price and quantity." />

## Disequilibrium

| Price relative to equilibrium | Outcome | Pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| below | shortage ($$Q_d > Q_s$$) | price rises |
| above | surplus ($$Q_s > Q_d$$) | price falls |

The size of a shortage or surplus is the horizontal gap between the curves at that price.

## Shifts and surplus

| Shift | Price | Quantity | Consumer surplus | Producer surplus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Demand up | up | up | usually up | up |
| Supply up | down | up | up | usually up |
| Supply down | up | down | down | depends |

When both curves shift, combine their effects: one of price or quantity is certain, and the other is indeterminate.

<Note title="Shortage is not scarcity">
A shortage is a market condition caused by a price below equilibrium and disappears when the price adjusts. Scarcity never disappears.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Measuring a surplus">
In a market, $$Q_d = 200 - 4P$$ and $$Q_s = 6P$$. What happens at a price of 25?

1. $$Q_d = 200 - 100 = 100$$; $$Q_s = 150$$.
2. Supply exceeds demand by 50.
3. Equilibrium: $$200 - 4P = 6P$$, so $$P = 20$$, below 25.

**Answer:** a surplus of 50 units; the price will tend to fall toward 20.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Demand is $$Q_d = 108 - 4P$$ and supply is $$Q_s = 18 + 5P$$. The government sets a price floor of 15. What results?

**Answer:** A surplus of 45 units

**Why:** At 15, $$Q_d = 48$$ and $$Q_s = 93$$.

**Working:** Equilibrium is P = 10, Q = 68. At the floor of 15, quantity demanded is 48 and quantity supplied is 93: a surplus of 45.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- A shortage of 45 units: A binding floor (above equilibrium) creates a surplus.
- No effect, because the control is not binding: Equilibrium price is 10, so a floor of 15 is binding.
- A surplus of 20 units: Compare quantity demanded with quantity supplied at the controlled price, not with the old equilibrium.
