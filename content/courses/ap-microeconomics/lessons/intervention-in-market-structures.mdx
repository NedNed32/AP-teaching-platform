The same government policy can have very different effects depending on whether it changes a firm's fixed costs or its marginal costs.

## Lump-sum versus per-unit

| Policy | Shifts | Output and price (short run) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lump-sum tax or fee | ATC up (not MC) | unchanged; profit falls |
| Lump-sum subsidy | ATC down (not MC) | unchanged; profit rises |
| Per-unit tax | MC, AVC, ATC up | output falls, price rises |
| Per-unit subsidy | MC, AVC, ATC down | output rises, price falls |

In perfect competition, a lump-sum tax can still matter in the **long run**: it creates losses, so some firms exit and the market price rises.

## Regulating a monopoly

| Regulated price | Where | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Socially optimal | $$P = MC$$ (demand meets MC) | efficient quantity; the firm may need a subsidy if $$P < ATC$$ |
| Fair return | $$P = ATC$$ | zero economic profit; output higher than unregulated, but not fully efficient |

A **price ceiling** set at the socially optimal price makes the monopolist a price taker up to that quantity, raising output.

<Note title="Fixed costs again">
Lump-sum taxes are a favorite exam trap: because they do not change marginal cost, they do not change the profit-maximizing quantity in the short run.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="A license fee">
A city charges every restaurant a flat annual license fee. A monopolistically competitive restaurant was earning zero economic profit. What happens in the short and long run?

1. Short run: MC unchanged, so output and price stay the same; ATC rises, so the restaurant now has a loss.
2. Long run: losses cause some restaurants to exit; each remaining restaurant's demand increases until profit returns to zero.

**Answer:** no short-run change in output or price, a loss, then exit until remaining firms break even.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** The government imposes a per-unit tax on a profit-maximizing monopolist. In the short run, what happens to the monopolist's output and price?

**A.** Output falls and price rises.. A per-unit tax raises marginal cost. A per-unit tax raises MC; MR = MC at a lower quantity, and the price read from demand is higher.

**Q.** The government imposes a lump-sum tax (a fixed amount, regardless of output) on a profit-maximizing monopolist. In the short run, what happens to the monopolist's output and price?

**A.** Output and price do not change; profit falls.. A lump-sum tax raises fixed cost, not marginal cost. A lump-sum tax is a fixed cost: ATC rises but MC is unchanged, so the profit-maximizing output and price stay the same and profit falls.

### Free-response practice: A monopoly

A profit-maximizing monopolist faces the demand curve $$P = 73 - 2Q$$, so its marginal revenue is $$MR = 73 - 4Q$$. Its marginal cost and average total cost are constant at 17.

**(a)** Calculate the profit-maximizing quantity and price.

To earn the point: Sets MR = MC to find quantity. Model answer: 73 − 4Q = 17, so Q = 14

To earn the point: Reads price from demand. Model answer: P = 73 − 2(14) = 45

**(b)** Calculate the monopolist's profit.

To earn the point: Correct profit. Model answer: (45 − 17) × 14 = 392

**(c)** Calculate the allocatively efficient quantity and the deadweight loss of monopoly.

To earn the point: Efficient quantity where P = MC. Model answer: 73 − 2Q = 17, so Q = 28

To earn the point: Deadweight loss. Model answer: ½ × (28 − 14) × (45 − 17) = 196

**(d)** The government imposes a lump-sum tax on the monopolist. What happens to the profit-maximizing quantity? Explain.

To earn the point: No change, because MC and MR are unchanged. Model answer: A lump-sum tax raises fixed cost only, so MR = MC at the same quantity; profit falls.

**(e)** If the monopolist could perfectly price discriminate, what would happen to consumer surplus and deadweight loss?

To earn the point: Both become zero. Model answer: Output rises to 28; the firm captures all surplus, so consumer surplus and deadweight loss are zero.
