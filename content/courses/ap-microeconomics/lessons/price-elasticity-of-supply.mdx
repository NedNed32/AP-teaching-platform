Producers also respond to price, but how quickly depends on how easily they can change output.

## The formula

$$
E_s = \frac{\%\Delta Q_s}{\%\Delta P}
$$

Use the midpoint method for percentage changes. Supply elasticity is positive, because supply curves slope upward.

## What makes supply elastic

- **Time:** in the long run firms can build plants and new firms can enter, so supply becomes more elastic.
- **Spare capacity and easy inputs:** if extra workers and materials are easy to get, output can rise quickly.
- **Storability:** goods that can be stored can be released when prices rise.

| Coefficient | Supply is |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | perfectly inelastic (vertical) |
| between 0 and 1 | inelastic |
| greater than 1 | elastic |
| infinite | perfectly elastic (horizontal) |

<Note title="Why it matters">
When supply is inelastic, an increase in demand mostly raises the price; when it is elastic, it mostly raises quantity. The same logic decides who bears a tax (topic 2.8).
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Short run versus long run">
When the price of strawberries rises from 2 to 3 per box, growers increase the quantity supplied from 1,000 to 1,100 boxes this week. Find the elasticity and interpret it.

1. $$\%\Delta Q = \frac{100}{1050} \approx 9.5\%$$.
2. $$\%\Delta P = \frac{1}{2.5} = 40\%$$.
3. $$E_s \approx \frac{9.5}{40} \approx 0.24$$.

**Answer:** about 0.24: supply is inelastic in the short run, because fields cannot be planted overnight.
</WorkedExample>

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which good is likely to have the most price-inelastic demand?

**A.** Life-saving prescription medicine. Availability of substitutes, necessity, budget share, time and production flexibility drive elasticity. Demand is more elastic with close substitutes, for luxuries, for large budget shares and over longer time periods. Supply is more elastic when production can expand easily.

**Q.** Supply of which good is likely to be the most price-elastic?
