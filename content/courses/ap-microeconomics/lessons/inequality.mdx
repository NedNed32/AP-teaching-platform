Markets reward people according to what they sell, including their labor, so incomes differ, sometimes widely. Economists measure inequality and study policies that change it.

## Measuring inequality

The **Lorenz curve** plots the cumulative percentage of income (vertical) against the cumulative percentage of households (horizontal), from poorest to richest.

- The 45-degree **line of perfect equality**: every 10 percent of households gets 10 percent of income.
- The further the Lorenz curve bows below that line, the greater the inequality.

$$
\text{Gini} = \frac{\text{area between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve}}{\text{total area under the line of equality}}.
$$

## Sources of inequality

Differences in human capital, skills and effort; discrimination; inherited wealth; market power; luck.

## Taxes and transfers

| Tax | Share of income paid as income rises |
| --- | --- |
| Progressive | rises |
| Proportional (flat) | stays the same |
| Regressive | falls |

Progressive taxes and transfer programs (such as cash assistance) reduce inequality, moving the Lorenz curve toward the line of equality.

<Note title="Equity versus efficiency">
Redistribution can reduce incentives to work or invest. Much policy debate is about how to balance a fairer distribution against a smaller total.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classifying a tax">
Household A earns 20,000 and pays 2,000 in tax; household B earns 100,000 and pays 15,000. Is the tax progressive, proportional or regressive?

1. A pays 10 percent; B pays 15 percent.
2. The percentage rises with income.

**Answer:** progressive.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A household earning 50,000 pays 2,500 in tax, and a household earning 200,000 pays 16,000. The tax is

**Answer:** progressive

**Why:** The rates are 5% and 8%.

**Working:** Tax as a share of income: 5% for the lower income and 8% for the higher. Rising share: progressive; falling: regressive; equal: proportional.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- regressive: Compare the tax as a percentage of income, not the amount paid.
- proportional: Compare the tax as a percentage of income, not the amount paid.
- lump-sum: A lump-sum tax is the same amount for everyone.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A country's Lorenz curve moves farther from the line of perfect equality. Which of the following is true?

**A.** Income inequality increases and the Gini coefficient rises.. The Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient measure relative shares, not income levels. The farther the Lorenz curve from the 45-degree line, the higher the Gini coefficient and the greater the inequality. Equal proportional changes in all incomes leave shares unchanged.

**Q.** Every household's income doubles. Which of the following is true?

**A.** The Lorenz curve and the Gini coefficient do not change.. The Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient measure relative shares, not income levels. The farther the Lorenz curve from the 45-degree line, the higher the Gini coefficient and the greater the inequality. Equal proportional changes in all incomes leave shares unchanged.

**Q.** A new program of progressive taxes and cash transfers is introduced. Which of the following is true?

**A.** The Lorenz curve moves toward the line of equality and the Gini coefficient falls.. The Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient measure relative shares, not income levels. The farther the Lorenz curve from the 45-degree line, the higher the Gini coefficient and the greater the inequality. Equal proportional changes in all incomes leave shares unchanged.
