An accountant and an economist can look at the same firm and disagree about whether it is profitable. The difference is implicit costs.

## Two definitions

$$
\text{accounting profit} = TR - \text{explicit costs}
$$

$$
\text{economic profit} = TR - \text{explicit costs} - \text{implicit costs}
$$

Implicit costs include the owner's forgone salary and the return the owner's money could earn elsewhere.

## Normal profit

When economic profit is **zero**, the firm earns a **normal profit**: just enough to keep its resources in their current use. Positive economic profit attracts new firms; economic losses push firms out. This entry and exit drives long-run outcomes in competitive markets (topic 3.6).

<Note title="Economic profit is always smaller">
Because economic profit subtracts an extra cost, it is always less than or equal to accounting profit. A firm can have positive accounting profit and an economic loss at the same time.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Should the owner stay?">
A café earns revenue of 300,000. It pays 220,000 in rent, wages and supplies. The owner could earn 70,000 as a manager elsewhere and earn 5,000 interest on the money invested in the café. Find the accounting and economic profit.

1. Accounting profit: $$300000 - 220000 = 80000$$.
2. Implicit costs: $$70000 + 5000 = 75000$$.
3. Economic profit: $$80000 - 75000 = 5000$$.

**Answer:** accounting profit 80,000; economic profit 5,000, so staying open is the better choice.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A firm maximizes profit by producing 430 units, which it sells at a price of 31. At that output, average total cost is 36. What is the firm's economic profit (or loss)?

**Answer:** A loss of 2,150

**Why:** (P − ATC) × Q.

**Working:** Profit = (P − ATC) × Q = (31 − 36) × 430 = -2150.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 13,330: This is total revenue.
- 15,480: This is total cost.
- 5: This is profit per unit; multiply by quantity.

### Worked exam question 2

A firm earns total revenue of 380,000 and pays explicit costs of 230,000. The owner could earn 20,000 working elsewhere. What is the firm's economic profit?

**Answer:** 130,000

**Why:** Revenue minus explicit and implicit costs.

**Working:** Accounting profit = 380,000 − 230,000 = 150,000. Economic profit = 150,000 − 20,000 = 130,000.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 150,000: This is accounting profit.
- 360,000: This subtracts only the implicit cost.
- 170,000: Implicit costs are subtracted, not added.

### Worked exam question 3

A firm earns total revenue of 310,000 and pays explicit costs of 160,000. The owner could earn 70,000 working elsewhere. What is the firm's accounting profit?

**Answer:** 150,000

**Why:** Revenue minus explicit costs.

**Working:** Accounting profit = 310,000 − 160,000 = 150,000. Economic profit = 150,000 − 70,000 = 80,000.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 80,000: This is economic profit.
- 240,000: This subtracts only the implicit cost.
- 220,000: Implicit costs are subtracted, not added.
