Conditioning does not explain all learning. Thinking and social experience matter too.

## Social learning theory

- **Observational learning**: we learn by watching others. In Bandura's study, children who watched an adult attack an inflatable doll later copied the attack.
- **Modeling**: demonstrating a behavior for others to imitate. We are most likely to copy models who are similar to us, admired or rewarded.
- **Vicarious conditioning**: seeing someone else rewarded or punished changes our own behavior.

Models can teach helpful (prosocial) behavior as well as harmful behavior.

## Cognitive factors

- **Insight learning**: a solution appears suddenly, without trial and error.
- **Latent learning**: learning takes place without reward and shows only when there is an incentive. Rats that explored a maze with no reward ran it quickly once food was added.
- **Cognitive maps**: mental pictures of a layout, which the rats had formed while exploring.

These findings show that learning involves mental processes, not only responses and rewards.

<Note title="Learning without doing">
Observational and latent learning both show that an organism can learn without being reinforced itself. Performance and learning are different things.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Explain the behavior">
A boy watches his sister get dessert for clearing the table. The next night he clears the table. Explain.

1. He was not rewarded himself.
2. He saw her behavior rewarded.

**Answer:** Vicarious conditioning (vicarious reinforcement), a form of observational learning.
</WorkedExample>

### Free-response practice: Article analysis: a study of aggressive behavior

Read the summary of a hypothetical study, then answer parts A to F.

**Introduction.** Researchers investigated whether children imitate aggressive behavior that they observe. They hypothesized that the behavior of an adult model would affect aggressive behavior.

**Participants.** The sample was 72 preschool children, recruited from one city. Parents gave informed consent, each child gave assent, and the plan was approved by an institutional review board. Families could withdraw at any time.

**Method.** The children were randomly assigned to one of two conditions. Children in one condition watched a video of an adult hitting an inflatable doll. Children in the other condition watched a video of an adult playing quietly with other toys. The researchers recorded the number of times that each child hit the doll during ten minutes of free play.

**Results.** The group that watched the aggressive adult: mean number of hits = 14.2. The group that watched the quiet adult: mean number of hits = 3.1. The difference was statistically significant (p = 0.01).

**(a)** Identify the research method used in the study.

To earn the point: Identifies the method as an experiment. Model answer: The study is an experiment: the researchers manipulated a variable and randomly assigned the subjects to conditions.

**(b)** State the operational definition of aggressive behavior.

To earn the point: States how the variable was measured in this study. Model answer: Aggressive behavior was defined as the number of times that each child hit the doll during ten minutes of free play.

**(c)** Describe the meaning of the difference between the two group averages.

To earn the point: Accurately describes which group scored higher or lower and what that means for the variable measured. Model answer: The group that watched the aggressive adult hit the doll more often. On average, the value was 14.2 for the group that watched the aggressive adult and 3.1 for the group that watched the quiet adult, and a p value of 0.01 means a difference this large is unlikely to be due to chance.

**(d)** Identify at least one ethical guideline applied by the researchers.

To earn the point: Identifies an ethical guideline that the summary shows was followed. Model answer: Informed consent and assent: parents consented and each child agreed to take part. (Also acceptable: approval by an institutional review board; the right to withdraw.)

**(e)** Explain the extent to which the research findings may or may not be generalizable, using specific and relevant evidence from the study.

To earn the point: Explains a limit (or strength) of generalizability with reference to the sample. Model answer: The sample was 72 preschool children from one city, so the findings may not apply to people of other ages, cultures or backgrounds. Because the sample was not randomly selected from a wider population, it may not be representative.

**(f)** Explain how at least one of the research findings supports or refutes the researchers' hypothesis.

To earn the point: Uses a specific finding from the study as evidence. Model answer: The group that watched the aggressive adult hit the doll more often. (14.2 compared with 3.1).

To earn the point: Explains how the finding supports the hypothesis, using a psychological concept. Model answer: This supports the hypothesis that the behavior of an adult model affects aggressive behavior. Because subjects were randomly assigned, the difference can be attributed to the behavior of an adult model. The finding illustrates observational learning: people learn behaviors by watching and imitating models.
