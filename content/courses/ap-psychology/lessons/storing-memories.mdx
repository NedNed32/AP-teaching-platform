## Rehearsal

- **Maintenance rehearsal** repeats information to hold it in short-term memory.
- **Elaborative rehearsal** connects it to meaning and to what you already know, which stores it in long-term memory.

Sleep helps **memory consolidation**, the strengthening of stored memories.

## Autobiographical memory

**Autobiographical memory** is the memory of your own life. A few people have **highly superior autobiographical memory** and can recall almost every day.

## When storage fails

| Condition | What is lost |
| --- | --- |
| Retrograde amnesia | memories from before the injury |
| Anterograde amnesia | the ability to form new memories |
| Alzheimer's disease | memory first, then other abilities, progressively |
| Infantile amnesia | events from the first few years of life, in all adults |

The **hippocampus** is needed to form new explicit memories, so damage to it causes anterograde amnesia. Implicit memories, which depend on the cerebellum and other areas, can survive.

<Note title="Retro means backward">
Retrograde amnesia reaches back to before the injury. Anterograde amnesia affects what comes after.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Which amnesia?">
After an accident, Sam remembers his childhood but cannot remember what he ate an hour ago or the nurse he met this morning. Name the condition.

1. Old memories are intact.
2. New memories are not being formed.

**Answer:** Anterograde amnesia, probably from damage to the hippocampus.
</WorkedExample>

### Free-response practice: Evidence-based question: Does sleep improve memory?

Use the three hypothetical sources to answer the question: **Does sleep improve memory?**

**Source A.** In an experiment, 60 students learned a list of word pairs at 9 p.m. Half were randomly assigned to sleep normally and half to stay awake all night. Twelve hours later, the sleep group recalled a mean of 31 pairs out of 40 and the awake group recalled a mean of 22 (p = 0.01).

**Source B.** Researchers recorded the brain activity of 25 adults in a sleep laboratory after they had learned a maze. The more slow-wave (NREM stage 3) sleep a participant had, the faster that participant completed the maze the next morning (r = +0.55).

**Source C.** A survey of 900 high school students found that those who reported sleeping fewer than six hours on school nights had lower average grades than those who slept eight hours or more. The researchers noted that students who slept less also reported more stress and more hours of paid work.

**(a)** Propose a specific and defensible claim, based in psychological science, that responds to the question.

To earn the point: States a claim that answers the question and can be supported by the sources. Model answer: Sleep improves memory because memories are consolidated during sleep.

**(b)** (i) Support your claim using at least one piece of specific and relevant evidence from one of the sources. (ii) Explain how the evidence supports your claim, using a psychological perspective, theory, concept or research finding learned in AP Psychology.

To earn the point: Uses specific, relevant evidence from one source and names the source. Model answer: In Source A, students who slept recalled a mean of 31 of 40 word pairs, compared with 22 for students who stayed awake.

To earn the point: Explains how the evidence supports the claim. Model answer: This supports the claim through memory consolidation: during sleep, the hippocampus and cortex strengthen newly encoded memories, so the sleep group stored the word pairs better. Because the study was an experiment with random assignment, sleep can be said to have caused the difference.

To earn the point: Correctly applies a psychological concept in the explanation. Model answer: The concept must be named and applied correctly, as in the model explanation.

**(c)** (i) Support your claim using an additional piece of specific and relevant evidence from a different source. (ii) Explain how this evidence supports your claim, using a different psychological perspective, theory, concept or research finding.

To earn the point: Uses specific, relevant evidence from a different source and names the source. Model answer: In Source B, participants who had more slow-wave sleep completed the maze faster the next morning (r = +0.55).

To earn the point: Explains how the evidence supports the claim. Model answer: This supports the claim because NREM stage 3 sleep is linked to strengthening the neural connections formed during learning (long-term potentiation), so more deep sleep goes with better retention of the maze.

To earn the point: Correctly applies a different psychological concept from the one used in part B. Model answer: The concept must differ from the one used in part B and be applied correctly.
