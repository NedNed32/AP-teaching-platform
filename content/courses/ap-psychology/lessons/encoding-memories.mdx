How information is encoded decides how well it is remembered.

## Organizing information

- **Mnemonic devices** use vivid imagery and organization. In the **method of loci**, you imagine items placed along a familiar route.
- **Chunking** groups items into meaningful units: 1-9-4-5 becomes 1945.
- **Categories and hierarchies** arrange material into broad groups with narrower groups inside.

## Spacing your practice

- **Massed practice** is cramming in one session.
- **Distributed practice** spreads study over several sessions.
- The **spacing effect**: distributed practice gives much better long-term retention.

## Serial position effect

When recalling a list, people remember:

- the first items (**primacy effect**), because they were rehearsed most and reached long-term memory;
- the last items (**recency effect**), because they are still in working memory;
- the middle items least.

After a delay, the recency effect fades but the primacy effect remains.

<Note title="Study advice from the science">
Short sessions on several days beat one long session. This is one of the most reliable findings in psychology.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Predict the recall">
Students hear 20 words and recall them an hour later. Which words are remembered best?

1. After a delay, working memory no longer holds the last words.
2. The first words were rehearsed into long-term memory.

**Answer:** The first few words (the primacy effect).
</WorkedExample>

### Free-response practice: Article analysis: a study of the remembered speed of the cars

Read the summary of a hypothetical study, then answer parts A to F.

**Introduction.** Researchers investigated whether the wording of a question changes what witnesses remember. They hypothesized that the wording of the question would affect the remembered speed of the cars.

**Participants.** The sample was 100 adult volunteers who watched a film of a car accident, recruited from one city. All participants gave informed consent, were told they could withdraw at any time, and were debriefed afterward. The plan was approved by an institutional review board.

**Method.** The participants were randomly assigned to one of two conditions. Participants in one condition were asked how fast the cars were going when they smashed into each other. Participants in the other condition were asked how fast the cars were going when they contacted each other. The researchers recorded the speed in kilometers per hour that each participant wrote down.

**Results.** The group asked the question with the word 'smashed': mean estimated speed (km/h) = 66. The group asked the question with the word 'contacted': mean estimated speed (km/h) = 51. The difference was statistically significant (p = 0.01).

**(a)** Identify the research method used in the study.

To earn the point: Identifies the method as an experiment. Model answer: The study is an experiment: the researchers manipulated a variable and randomly assigned the subjects to conditions.

**(b)** State the operational definition of the remembered speed of the cars.

To earn the point: States how the variable was measured in this study. Model answer: The remembered speed of the cars was defined as the speed in kilometers per hour that each participant wrote down.

**(c)** Describe the meaning of the difference between the two group averages.

To earn the point: Accurately describes which group scored higher or lower and what that means for the variable measured. Model answer: The group asked about 'smashed' gave higher speed estimates. On average, the value was 66 for the group asked the question with the word 'smashed' and 51 for the group asked the question with the word 'contacted', and a p value of 0.01 means a difference this large is unlikely to be due to chance.

**(d)** Identify at least one ethical guideline applied by the researchers.

To earn the point: Identifies an ethical guideline that the summary shows was followed. Model answer: Informed consent: participants agreed to take part after being told about the study. (Also acceptable: debriefing; the right to withdraw; approval by an institutional review board.)

**(e)** Explain the extent to which the research findings may or may not be generalizable, using specific and relevant evidence from the study.

To earn the point: Explains a limit (or strength) of generalizability with reference to the sample. Model answer: The sample was 100 adult volunteers who watched a film of a car accident from one city, so the findings may not apply to people of other ages, cultures or backgrounds. Because the sample was not randomly selected from a wider population, it may not be representative.

**(f)** Explain how at least one of the research findings supports or refutes the researchers' hypothesis.

To earn the point: Uses a specific finding from the study as evidence. Model answer: The group asked about 'smashed' gave higher speed estimates. (66 compared with 51).

To earn the point: Explains how the finding supports the hypothesis, using a psychological concept. Model answer: This supports the hypothesis that the wording of the question affects the remembered speed of the cars. Because subjects were randomly assigned, the difference can be attributed to the wording of the question. The finding illustrates the misinformation effect: information received after an event can distort the memory of it.

**Source A.** In an experiment, 90 students were randomly assigned to study 40 vocabulary words either in six ten-minute sessions on different days or in one sixty-minute session. One month later, the spaced group recalled a mean of 27 words and the single-session group a mean of 16 (p = 0.01).

**Source B.** In an experiment, 120 students read a passage and were randomly assigned either to take three practice quizzes or to reread the passage three times. On a test one week later, the quiz group scored a mean of 68 percent and the rereading group 49 percent (p = 0.01).

**Source C.** In a survey of 600 students, 71 percent said that rereading notes was their main study method, and most rated it as highly effective. Only 12 percent said that they regularly tested themselves.
