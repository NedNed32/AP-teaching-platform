**Positive psychology** is the scientific study of what helps people and communities thrive. It adds to, and does not replace, the study of disorders.

## Well-being

- **Subjective well-being** is a person's own evaluation of their happiness and satisfaction with life.
- Practicing **gratitude** increases well-being.
- Using one's **signature strengths** raises happiness and engagement.

## Character strengths and virtues

Strengths are grouped under six virtues:

| Virtue | Example strengths |
| --- | --- |
| Wisdom | creativity, curiosity, love of learning |
| Courage | bravery, perseverance, honesty |
| Humanity | love, kindness, social intelligence |
| Justice | teamwork, fairness, leadership |
| Temperance | forgiveness, humility, self-control |
| Transcendence | gratitude, hope, humor, appreciation of beauty |

## After hardship

- **Resilience** is the ability to recover.
- **Posttraumatic growth** goes further: some people report closer relationships, new purpose and greater appreciation of life after trauma.

<Note title="Resilience or growth">
Resilience returns a person to where they were. Posttraumatic growth leaves them changed for the better in some way.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Identify the virtue">
A student leads her team fairly and makes sure everyone contributes. Which virtue do her strengths show?

1. Fairness, teamwork and leadership are the strengths.
2. These are grouped together.

**Answer:** Justice.
</WorkedExample>

### Free-response practice: Evidence-based question: How does long-term stress affect health, and what reduces its effects?

Use the three hypothetical sources to answer the question: **How does long-term stress affect health, and what reduces its effects?**

**Source A.** In a study of 350 adults, the number of stressful life events in the past year correlated with the number of colds in that year (r = +0.33). Adults who reported sleeping less tended to report both more stress and more colds.

**Source B.** In an experiment, 84 office workers were randomly assigned to practice mindfulness meditation or to listen to an audiobook for twenty minutes a day. After eight weeks, the meditation group had a mean cortisol level of 9.8 nmol/L and the audiobook group 13.5 nmol/L (p = 0.02).

**Source C.** Researchers followed 500 people for a year after a major flood. Those who reported strong support from family and friends had fewer symptoms of illness and depression than those who reported little support.

**(a)** Propose a specific and defensible claim, based in psychological science, that responds to the question.

To earn the point: States a claim that answers the question and can be supported by the sources. Model answer: Long-term stress harms health, but coping strategies and social support reduce its effects.

**(b)** (i) Support your claim using at least one piece of specific and relevant evidence from one of the sources. (ii) Explain how the evidence supports your claim, using a psychological perspective, theory, concept or research finding learned in AP Psychology.

To earn the point: Uses specific, relevant evidence from one source and names the source. Model answer: In Source A, people with more stressful life events had more colds in the same year (r = +0.33).

To earn the point: Explains how the evidence supports the claim. Model answer: This supports the claim through the general adaptation syndrome: prolonged stress keeps the body in the resistance phase and eventually exhausts it, which suppresses the immune system and makes illness more likely.

To earn the point: Correctly applies a psychological concept in the explanation. Model answer: The concept must be named and applied correctly, as in the model explanation.

**(c)** (i) Support your claim using an additional piece of specific and relevant evidence from a different source. (ii) Explain how this evidence supports your claim, using a different psychological perspective, theory, concept or research finding.

To earn the point: Uses specific, relevant evidence from a different source and names the source. Model answer: In Source B, workers who practiced mindfulness meditation had lower cortisol levels (9.8) than workers who listened to an audiobook (13.5).

To earn the point: Explains how the evidence supports the claim. Model answer: This supports the claim through emotion-focused coping: meditation manages the emotional and physical reaction to stress, which lowers the body's stress-hormone response.

To earn the point: Correctly applies a different psychological concept from the one used in part B. Model answer: The concept must differ from the one used in part B and be applied correctly.
