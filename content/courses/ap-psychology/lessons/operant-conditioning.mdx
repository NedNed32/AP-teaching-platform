In **operant conditioning**, behavior is shaped by its consequences. Thorndike's **law of effect** says that behaviors followed by good outcomes become more likely.

## Four consequences

Positive means adding a stimulus; negative means removing one. Reinforcement increases behavior; punishment decreases it.

| Effect on behavior | Add a stimulus | Remove a stimulus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Behavior increases | positive reinforcement (a treat) | negative reinforcement (the alarm stops) |
| Behavior decreases | positive punishment (extra chores) | negative punishment (phone taken away) |

- **Primary reinforcers** satisfy biological needs (food). **Secondary reinforcers** are learned (money, praise).
- **Shaping** rewards successive approximations.
- **Instinctive drift**: trained animals revert to inborn behaviors.
- **Superstitious behavior** arises when a reward follows a behavior by chance.
- **Learned helplessness**: after uncontrollable bad events, an organism stops trying.

## Schedules of reinforcement

**Continuous reinforcement** rewards every response: learning is fast, but so is extinction. **Partial reinforcement** rewards only some responses and resists extinction.

| Schedule | Reinforcement comes after | Example | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed ratio | a set number of responses | a free drink after ten purchases | fast, with pauses after reward |
| Variable ratio | an unpredictable number of responses | slot machines | fast and steady |
| Fixed interval | a set time | a paycheck every two weeks | speeds up as the time nears |
| Variable interval | an unpredictable time | checking for messages | slow and steady |

<Note title="Negative reinforcement is not punishment">
Negative reinforcement removes something unpleasant, so the behavior increases. Ask: did the behavior go up or down?
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Name the consequence">
A teenager's curfew is extended after a month of coming home on time. What kind of consequence is this, assuming he keeps coming home on time?

1. The behavior continues or increases: reinforcement.
2. A restriction is removed: negative.

**Answer:** Negative reinforcement.
</WorkedExample>

**Method.** The children were randomly assigned to one of two conditions. Children in one condition watched a video of an adult hitting an inflatable doll. Children in the other condition watched a video of an adult playing quietly with other toys. The researchers recorded the number of times that each child hit the doll during ten minutes of free play.

**Results.** The group that watched the aggressive adult: mean number of hits = 14.2. The group that watched the quiet adult: mean number of hits = 3.1. The difference was statistically significant (p = 0.01).

To earn the point: States how the variable was measured in this study. Model answer: Aggressive behavior was defined as the number of times that each child hit the doll during ten minutes of free play.

**(c)** Describe the meaning of the difference between the two group averages.

To earn the point: Accurately describes which group scored higher or lower and what that means for the variable measured. Model answer: The group that watched the aggressive adult hit the doll more often. On average, the value was 14.2 for the group that watched the aggressive adult and 3.1 for the group that watched the quiet adult, and a p value of 0.01 means a difference this large is unlikely to be due to chance.

### Free-response practice: Evidence-based question: Do rewards increase motivation?

Use the three hypothetical sources to answer the question: **Do rewards increase motivation?**

**Source A.** In an experiment, 60 preschool children who liked drawing were randomly assigned to draw for a promised certificate or to draw with no reward. A week later, during free play, the rewarded children chose to draw for a mean of 6.5 minutes and the unrewarded children for 13.8 minutes (p = 0.01).

**Source B.** In a study of 200 call-center workers, half were randomly assigned to receive a bonus for each sale. Over a month, the bonus group made a mean of 41 sales and the comparison group a mean of 33 (p = 0.02). When the bonus ended, the bonus group's sales fell to a mean of 30.

**Source C.** A survey of 500 university students found that students who said they studied mainly because they found their subject interesting had higher grades and reported less burnout than students who said they studied mainly for grades or money (r = +0.40 between interest and grades).

**(a)** Propose a specific and defensible claim, based in psychological science, that responds to the question.

To earn the point: States a claim that answers the question and can be supported by the sources. Model answer: Rewards increase a behavior while they last, but they can reduce intrinsic motivation, so the behavior falls when the rewards stop.

**(b)** (i) Support your claim using at least one piece of specific and relevant evidence from one of the sources. (ii) Explain how the evidence supports your claim, using a psychological perspective, theory, concept or research finding learned in AP Psychology.

To earn the point: Uses specific, relevant evidence from one source and names the source. Model answer: In Source A, children promised a certificate later chose to draw for 6.5 minutes, compared with 13.8 minutes for children who were not rewarded.

To earn the point: Explains how the evidence supports the claim. Model answer: This supports the claim through intrinsic and extrinsic motivation: the reward gave the children an external reason for drawing, which weakened their desire to draw for its own sake.

To earn the point: Correctly applies a psychological concept in the explanation. Model answer: The concept must be named and applied correctly, as in the model explanation.

**(c)** (i) Support your claim using an additional piece of specific and relevant evidence from a different source. (ii) Explain how this evidence supports your claim, using a different psychological perspective, theory, concept or research finding.

To earn the point: Uses specific, relevant evidence from a different source and names the source. Model answer: In Source B, workers paid a bonus made more sales (41 compared with 33), but their sales fell to 30 when the bonus ended.

To earn the point: Explains how the evidence supports the claim. Model answer: This supports the claim through operant conditioning: the bonus acted as positive reinforcement, which increased the behavior, and when reinforcement stopped the behavior declined (extinction).

To earn the point: Correctly applies a different psychological concept from the one used in part B. Model answer: The concept must differ from the one used in part B and be applied correctly.
