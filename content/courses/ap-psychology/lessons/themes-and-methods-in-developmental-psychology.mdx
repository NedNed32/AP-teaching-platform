Developmental psychology studies how people change, physically, cognitively and socially, from conception to death.

## Themes

- **Nature and nurture**: how heredity and experience interact.
- **Stability and change**: which traits last and which alter.
- **Continuous or discontinuous**: gradual change (a ramp) or distinct stages (a staircase).

## Designs

| Design | Method | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-sectional | different ages tested once | quick | groups differ in more than age |
| Longitudinal | same people retested over years | shows real change | slow; people drop out |

## Sampling

- **Random sampling** gives every member of the population an equal chance, which produces a **representative sample** and allows **generalization**.
- A **convenience sample** uses whoever is available and risks **sampling bias**.
- **Random assignment** is different: it places participants in conditions by chance, which allows conclusions about cause in an experiment.

## Correlation

A **correlation coefficient** (r) runs from −1.00 to +1.00.

- The sign gives the direction: **positive** (both rise together) or **negative** (one rises as the other falls).
- The distance from zero gives the strength.
- A **scatterplot** shows the pattern.

Correlation does not show causation, because of the **directionality problem** (which variable comes first?) and the **third variable problem** (something else may cause both).

<Note title="Sign and size">
The sign of r tells direction only. To compare strength, ignore the sign and compare the size.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Interpreting r">
Hours of television watched and school grades correlate at r = −0.40. What does this mean, and what can we not conclude?

1. Negative: more television goes with lower grades.
2. 0.40 is a moderate relationship.
3. It is a correlation, so no cause is shown.

**Answer:** A moderate negative relationship. We cannot conclude that television lowers grades; a third variable, such as parental supervision, may explain both.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Four studies of child development report these correlation coefficients: +0.28, −0.78, +0.70, −0.52. Which coefficient shows the strongest relationship?

**Answer:** −0.78

**Why:** Strength is the distance from zero, whatever the sign; −0.78 is farthest from zero.

**Working:** The sign of a correlation shows its direction; its distance from zero shows its strength. −0.78 is farthest from zero.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- +0.70: +0.70 is closer to zero.
- −0.52: −0.52 is closer to zero.
- +0.28: +0.28 is closer to zero.

### Worked exam question 2

Four studies of child development report these correlation coefficients: −0.20, +0.05, −0.61, −0.70. Which coefficient shows the weakest relationship?

**Answer:** +0.05

**Why:** Strength is the distance from zero, whatever the sign; +0.05 is closest to zero.

**Working:** The sign of a correlation shows its direction; its distance from zero shows its strength. +0.05 is closest to zero.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- −0.70: −0.70 is farther from zero.
- −0.61: −0.61 is farther from zero.
- −0.20: −0.20 is farther from zero.
