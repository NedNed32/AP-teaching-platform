Random values make programs unpredictable: games, simulations, shuffled quizzes.

## RANDOM

`RANDOM(a, b)` returns an integer from a to b, **inclusive**, with each value equally likely. Each call can give a different result, so running the same program twice may give different output.

## Probability with RANDOM

```text
IF (RANDOM(1, 10) ≤ 3)
{
   DISPLAY ("rain")
}
```

The condition is true for 1, 2 or 3: a 3 in 10 (30 percent) chance.

<Note title="Two calls, two values">
`RANDOM(1, 6) + RANDOM(1, 6)` makes two separate calls, so totals from 2 to 12 are possible and 7 is most likely. `2 * RANDOM(1, 6)` makes one call and gives only even numbers.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Simulating a 25 percent event">
Which condition is true about 25 percent of the time: `RANDOM(1, 4) = 4` or `RANDOM(0, 4) = 4`?

1. RANDOM(1, 4) has 4 equally likely values: 1 in 4.
2. RANDOM(0, 4) has 5 values: 1 in 5.

**Answer:** `RANDOM(1, 4) = 4`.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

Which of the following describes all possible values of `RANDOM(1, 7) * 2 + 1`?

**Answer:** The 7 numbers 3, 5, …, 15

**Why:** RANDOM gives 1 to 7; doubling and adding 1 gives every second number from 3 to 15.

**Working:** The random value r is 1 to 7, so 2r + 1 ranges over 3, 5, …, 15.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Every integer from 3 to 15: Doubling skips every other number.
- The 7 numbers 1, 3, …, 13: RANDOM(1, n) starts at 1, not 0.
- Every integer from 2 to 8: The result is doubled before adding.

### Worked exam question 2

What is the probability that the expression `RANDOM(2, 10) ≥ 8` evaluates to true?

**Answer:** 3 out of 9

**Why:** The values 8 through 10 (3 of them) out of 9 equally likely values from 2 to 10.

**Working:** RANDOM(2, 10) has 9 equally likely results; 3 of them are at least 8.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 2 out of 9: 8 itself is included by ≥.
- 3 out of 8: RANDOM includes both 2 and 10, so there are 9 values.
- 6 out of 9: That is the probability of the expression being false.
