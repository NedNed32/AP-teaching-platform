Algorithms can reflect and amplify human biases. A program is only as fair as its data and design.

## Where bias comes from

- **Data**: if training data under-represents a group or reflects past discrimination, the algorithm learns those patterns.
- **Design**: choices about what to measure and optimize can disadvantage some users.
- **People**: developers' blind spots shape what gets tested.

## Reducing bias

- Collect representative data and examine it for gaps.
- Test results across different groups of users.
- Include diverse perspectives in design and review.

<Note title="More data is not automatically better">
Adding more data collected the same biased way does not fix the bias; the new data has the same gaps.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Spot the bias">
A voice assistant was trained mostly on recordings of speakers from one region. What problem is likely?

1. Accents and speech patterns from other regions are under-represented.
2. The assistant will understand those users less accurately.

**Answer:** biased training data leads to worse performance for some groups.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A navigation app improves its traffic estimates using location reports from millions of drivers. This is an example of what?

**A.** Crowdsourcing. Many people contribute data through the Internet.

**Q.** Which practice best reduces bias in a computing innovation?

**A.** Testing the innovation with a diverse range of users and data. Diverse testing reveals unequal performance.

**Q.** A face recognition system works well for some groups but poorly for others. What is the most likely cause?

**A.** The training data did not include enough images of some groups. Unrepresentative data creates biased results.

**Q.** A hiring algorithm learns from ten years of past hiring decisions in which some groups were rarely hired. What is the risk?

**A.** The algorithm may repeat the bias in those past decisions. Algorithms reflect biases in their data.

**Q.** Volunteers around the world classify millions of telescope images for an astronomy project. What is this called?

**A.** Citizen science. Public participation in scientific research is citizen science.

**Q.** Which is a concern with information gathered by crowdsourcing?

**A.** Some contributions may be inaccurate or deliberately false. Crowdsourced data needs quality checks.
