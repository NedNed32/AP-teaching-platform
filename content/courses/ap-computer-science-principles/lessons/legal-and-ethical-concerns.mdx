Digital content is easy to copy, which makes questions of ownership and ethics important.

## Ownership and licensing

- Creators own their work through **copyright**, even when it is posted online.
- **Creative Commons** licenses let creators grant permission in advance, with conditions such as attribution or non-commercial use.
- **Open source** software shares source code so others can use and improve it.
- **Open access** research is freely available to read.

## Ethical use

Using material without permission or credit can be illegal and is unethical. Always check the license and cite your sources. The Create performance task requires you to acknowledge any code or media you did not create.

<Note title="Legal is not the same as ethical">
Some uses of data may be legal but still raise ethical concerns, such as collecting more personal data than a service needs.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Using an image">
A student finds a photo with a Creative Commons Attribution license. May they use it in an app?

1. The license allows use if the creator is credited.

**Answer:** yes, provided the app gives attribution as the license requires.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A student copies a paragraph from a website into a report without giving credit. What is this?

**A.** Plagiarism. Using others' work without credit is plagiarism.

**Q.** What is open source software?

**A.** Software whose source code is freely available for others to use and modify. That is the definition of open source.

**Q.** Which statement about digital content is true?

**A.** Content found online is usually protected by copyright unless its license allows use. Being online does not make content free to use.

**Q.** A company collects far more personal data than its app needs, which is legal in its country. Why might this still be a concern?

**A.** Collecting unnecessary data can be unethical and increases privacy risks. Legal and ethical are not the same.

**Q.** A student wants to use a song in an app. The song is published with a Creative Commons license that allows reuse with attribution. What should the student do?

**A.** Use the song and credit the creator as the license requires. The license grants permission with attribution.
