| Pluralist | Corporatist |
| --- | --- |
| UK: many independent groups | China: state-sanctioned unions and associations |
| Nigeria: many competing groups | Russia: state-linked organizations |
| Mexico today (more pluralist than under the PRI) | Mexico under the PRI: unions incorporated into the party |

Corporatism can provide stability and order; pluralism provides more independent voice.

<Note title="Mexico's change">
Under the PRI, labor and peasant organizations were incorporated into the ruling party; democratization brought more pluralism.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Classify">
A country has one official labor federation, created by the ruling party, that negotiates wages with the state. Pluralist or corporatist?

1. One recognized group, linked to the state.

**Answer:** corporatist (state corporatism).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries uses a nomenklatura system in which the ruling party controls key appointments?

**A.** China. The CCP controls appointments through the nomenklatura system.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries elects its lower house entirely by single-member-district plurality?

**A.** United Kingdom. The UK Commons and Nigeria's House of Representatives use first past the post.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries uses a mixed electoral system for its lower house, combining district seats and proportional seats?

**A.** Mexico. Mexico (300 SMD + 200 PR) and Russia (225 + 225) use mixed systems.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries has no formal party system, with politics organized around factions such as reformists and principlists?

**A.** Iran. Iran's politics are factional rather than party-based.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries has a dominant ruling party alongside 'systemic' opposition parties that rarely challenge the regime?

**A.** Russia. United Russia dominates; other Duma parties generally support the regime.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries requires a winning presidential candidate to receive at least 25% of the vote in two-thirds of its states?

**A.** Nigeria. Nigeria's rule encourages broad, cross-regional support.

**Q.** How do proportional representation systems typically affect government formation?

**A.** They often produce multiparty legislatures and coalition governments. Seats are spread across more parties.

**Q.** In a corporatist system, interest groups are typically:

**A.** A limited number of officially recognized groups that work with or are controlled by the state. That defines corporatism.

**Q.** Why might an authoritarian government raise the vote threshold for parties to enter the legislature?

**A.** To keep small opposition parties from winning seats. Thresholds can exclude rivals.

**Q.** According to Duverger's law, single-member-district plurality systems tend to produce:

**A.** Two-party systems. Small parties struggle to win seats.

**Q.** What is patronage?

**A.** Providing jobs, contracts or benefits to supporters in exchange for loyalty. A tool for maintaining support.
