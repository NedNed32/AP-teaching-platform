Beliefs about the role of the state, religion and individual rights vary across countries and groups.

- **Religion**: central to Iran's system; a major cleavage in Nigeria.
- **Generational change**: younger, urban citizens often favor more openness.
- **Economic development**: can shift values toward post-materialism.

Surveys (such as attitudes toward democracy) help measure values, but answers can be shaped by fear in authoritarian states.

<Note title="Survey reliability">
In authoritarian states, respondents may not answer honestly, so survey data must be read with caution.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Interpret">
A survey finds younger urban citizens are more likely than older rural citizens to support expanded civil liberties. What explains this?

1. Generational and urban–rural differences in socialization and development.

**Answer:** different political socialization and economic experiences.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which ideology emphasizes free markets, privatization and reduced state intervention?

**A.** Neoliberalism. That defines neoliberalism.

**Q.** Freedom of the press is best classified as:

**A.** A civil liberty. A protection from government interference.

**Q.** Why are coinciding cleavages more likely to cause conflict than cross-cutting cleavages?

**A.** Divisions reinforce each other, so groups share fewer identities. Overlapping divisions deepen conflict.

**Q.** Which is an example of civil society?

**A.** An independent environmental organization. Voluntary groups outside the state.

**Q.** Which agent of political socialization do authoritarian states most often control directly?

**A.** State media and the education system. These shape beliefs at scale.
