Participation can be **voluntary** or **state-controlled**, **conventional** (voting, campaigning) or **unconventional** (protests, strikes).

- Democracies use participation to choose leaders and influence policy.
- Authoritarian regimes may mobilize participation to show support or to channel discontent safely.

Examples: protest movements in Iran; election-related protests in Russia; village-level elections in China.

<Note title="Participation and legitimacy">
High voluntary participation can strengthen legitimacy; forced participation does not.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Explain">
Why might a regime allow local elections but not national ones?

1. Local elections give limited voice and feedback without threatening central control.

**Answer:** to gain some legitimacy and information while keeping national power.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The table shows voter turnout in national elections in Country D, a hypothetical country.

*Turnout in Country D*

| Election year | Turnout (%) |
| --- | --- |
| 2008 | 80 |
| 2012 | 72 |
| 2016 | 67 |
| 2020 | 60 |
| 2024 | 55 |

Which conclusion is best supported by the data?

**Answer:** Turnout declined steadily, which may indicate falling trust or interest in elections in Country D

**Why:** Turnout went from 80% to 55%.

**Working:** From 80% (2008) to 55% (2024): a downward trend.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Turnout rose steadily, showing growing confidence in elections: Read the direction of the trend.
- Turnout proves that Country D's elections are free and fair: Turnout alone cannot show whether elections are free and fair.
- Turnout was stable across all elections: It changed by 25 points.

### Worked exam question 5

The table shows hypothetical data for four countries.

*Adults who joined a protest in the past year (%)*

| Country | 2004 | 2009 | 2014 | 2019 | 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Country A | 15 | 13 | 11 | 9 | 6 |
| Country B | 2 | 5 | 7 | 10 | 12 |
| Country C | 20 | 20 | 21 | 21 | 20 |
| Country D | 18 | 16 | 14 | 11 | 10 |

Which conclusion about Country A is best supported by the data?

**Answer:** The change from 15 to 6 is consistent with falling unconventional participation

**Why:** The value fell over the period.

**Working:** Country A: 15 → 13 → 11 → 9 → 6.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- The change from 15 to 6 is consistent with rising unconventional participation: Read the direction of the change.
- Country A had the highest value of the four countries in every year: Check the other countries' values.
- The data prove that Country A is a liberal democracy: One indicator cannot establish a regime type.

### Worked exam question 6

| Country C | 7 | 9 | 11 | 12 | 14 |
| Country D | 7 | 9 | 11 | 12 | 14 |

Which country had the highest value in 2024?

**Answer:** Country A

**Why:** Its 2024 value (20) is the highest.

**Working:** 2024 values: Country A 20, Country B 14, Country C 14, Country D 14.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Country B: It was highest in 2004, not 2024.
- Country C: Its 2024 value is lower.
- Country D: Its 2024 value is lower.
