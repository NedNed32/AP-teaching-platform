Social movements raise issues, mobilize citizens and pressure governments: Nigeria's #EndSARS protests, the Zapatista movement in Mexico, environmental movements in the UK, and protest waves in Iran.

Governments respond by **accommodation** (reforms), **co-optation**, or **repression**.

Interest groups range from business associations to labor unions; their access depends on whether the system is pluralist or corporatist.

<Note title="Response tells you about the regime">
Democracies usually tolerate protest; authoritarian regimes more often restrict or repress it.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Evaluate">
Nigeria's government disbanded a police unit after mass protests. What does this show?

1. Protest pressure produced a policy response.

**Answer:** social movements can influence policy, at least partially.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** How do proportional representation systems typically affect government formation?

**A.** They often produce multiparty legislatures and coalition governments. Seats are spread across more parties.

**Q.** In a corporatist system, interest groups are typically:

**A.** A limited number of officially recognized groups that work with or are controlled by the state. That defines corporatism.

**Q.** Why might an authoritarian government raise the vote threshold for parties to enter the legislature?

**A.** To keep small opposition parties from winning seats. Thresholds can exclude rivals.

**Q.** According to Duverger's law, single-member-district plurality systems tend to produce:

**A.** Two-party systems. Small parties struggle to win seats.

**Q.** What is patronage?

**A.** Providing jobs, contracts or benefits to supporters in exchange for loyalty. A tool for maintaining support.
