States adapt social policy in response to economic, demographic and political pressures:

- **Mexico**: conditional cash transfers to reduce poverty; later shifts to universal programs.
- **China**: from the one-child policy to encouraging more births.
- **UK**: the National Health Service and welfare reforms.
- **Iran**: subsidy reform, replacing fuel subsidies with cash payments.
- **Nigeria**: debates over removing fuel subsidies.
- **Russia**: pension reform raising the retirement age.

<Note title="Policy and legitimacy">
Unpopular reforms (raising pension ages, removing subsidies) can trigger protests and hurt legitimacy.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Explain">
Why did China move away from the one-child policy?

1. An aging population and shrinking workforce.

**Answer:** to address demographic decline and its economic effects.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** Which of the following course countries began gradual market reforms in 1978 while keeping one-party rule?

**A.** China. China's reform era began in 1978.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries is a member of a free trade agreement with the United States and Canada (USMCA)?

**A.** Mexico. Mexico joined NAFTA in 1994, replaced by USMCA in 2020.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries replaced a one-child policy with policies encouraging more births?

**A.** China. China moved to two-child (2016) and three-child (2021) policies.

**A.** Russia. Russia's 1990s privatization concentrated wealth among insiders.
