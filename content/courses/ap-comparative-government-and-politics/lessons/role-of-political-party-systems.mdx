Parties recruit leaders, mobilize voters, link citizens and government, and organize legislatures.

In authoritarian systems, ruling parties **co-opt** elites, distribute **patronage** and control appointments (China's nomenklatura system). Party membership can be a path to career advancement.

<Note title="Parties and accountability">
When parties alternate in power, voters can hold governments accountable by voting them out.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Explain">
How does party control of appointments help a one-party state?

1. Loyal members fill key posts in government, business and society.

**Answer:** it ensures loyalty and keeps elites invested in the regime.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The table shows hypothetical data for four countries.

*Seat share of the largest party (%)*

| Country | 2004 | 2009 | 2014 | 2019 | 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Country A | 29 | 25 | 25 | 25 | 25 |
| Country B | 31 | 34 | 39 | 43 | 49 |
| Country C | 66 | 70 | 74 | 77 | 80 |
| Country D | 38 | 41 | 44 | 47 | 50 |

Which country had the highest value in 2024?

**Answer:** Country C

**Why:** Its 2024 value (80) is the highest.

**Working:** 2024 values: Country A 25, Country B 49, Country C 80, Country D 50.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Country A: Its 2024 value is lower.
- Country B: Its 2024 value is lower.
- Country D: Its 2024 value is lower.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** How do proportional representation systems typically affect government formation?

**A.** They often produce multiparty legislatures and coalition governments. Seats are spread across more parties.

**Q.** In a corporatist system, interest groups are typically:

**A.** A limited number of officially recognized groups that work with or are controlled by the state. That defines corporatism.

**Q.** Why might an authoritarian government raise the vote threshold for parties to enter the legislature?

**A.** To keep small opposition parties from winning seats. Thresholds can exclude rivals.

**Q.** According to Duverger's law, single-member-district plurality systems tend to produce:

**A.** Two-party systems. Small parties struggle to win seats.

**Q.** What is patronage?

**A.** Providing jobs, contracts or benefits to supporters in exchange for loyalty. A tool for maintaining support.
