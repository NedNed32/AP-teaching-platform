<Note title="Thresholds">
PR systems often require a minimum vote share (such as Russia's 5%) to win seats, which limits small parties.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Compute">
A party wins 30% of votes. Under pure PR in a 450-seat chamber, about how many seats would it get?

1. $$0.30 \times 450 = 135$$.

**Answer:** about 135 seats.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The table shows hypothetical national election results in a legislature of 100 seats.

*Vote share and seat share (%)*

| Party | Votes (%) | Seats (%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Party W | 37 | 37 |
| Party X | 37 | 37 |
| Party Y | 18 | 18 |
| Party Z | 8 | 8 |

Based on the data, which party benefited most from the electoral system?

**Answer:** Party W

**Why:** It has the largest positive gap between seat share and vote share (0 points).

**Working:** Seat minus vote share: Party W 0, Party X 0, Party Y 0, Party Z 0.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Party X: Compare seats with votes for each party.
- Party Y: Compare seats with votes for each party.
- Party Z: Compare seats with votes for each party.

### Worked exam question 2

The table shows hypothetical national election results in a legislature of 100 seats.

*Vote share and seat share (%)*

| Party | Votes (%) | Seats (%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Party W | 51 | 51 |
| Party X | 31 | 31 |
| Party Y | 12 | 12 |
| Party Z | 6 | 6 |

Which electoral system most likely produced these results?

**Answer:** Proportional representation

**Why:** Each party's seat share closely matches its vote share.

**Working:** Seat shares mirror vote shares, as in PR.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Single-member-district plurality: SMD usually gives the largest party a seat bonus.
- A system with no elections: These are election results.
- A system in which seats are distributed equally among parties: Seat shares are not equal.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries uses a nomenklatura system in which the ruling party controls key appointments?

**A.** China. The CCP controls appointments through the nomenklatura system.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries elects its lower house entirely by single-member-district plurality?

**A.** United Kingdom. The UK Commons and Nigeria's House of Representatives use first past the post.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries uses a mixed electoral system for its lower house, combining district seats and proportional seats?

**A.** Mexico. Mexico (300 SMD + 200 PR) and Russia (225 + 225) use mixed systems.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries has no formal party system, with politics organized around factions such as reformists and principlists?

**A.** Iran. Iran's politics are factional rather than party-based.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries has a dominant ruling party alongside 'systemic' opposition parties that rarely challenge the regime?

**A.** Russia. United Russia dominates; other Duma parties generally support the regime.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries requires a winning presidential candidate to receive at least 25% of the vote in two-thirds of its states?

**A.** Nigeria. Nigeria's rule encourages broad, cross-regional support.

### Free-response practice: Voter turnout by region

The table shows hypothetical turnout in national elections in a developing democracy, by urban and rural voters.

*Turnout (%)*

| Year | Urban voters | Rural voters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2007 | 69 | 62 |
| 2011 | 73 | 59 |
| 2015 | 78 | 53 |
| 2019 | 83 | 51 |
| 2023 | 84 | 45 |

**(a)** Using the data, identify the group with the higher turnout in 2023.

To earn the point: Correct identification with data. Model answer: Urban voters: 84% vs 45%.

**(b)** Describe the trend in rural turnout from 2007 to 2023.

To earn the point: Accurate trend with values. Model answer: Rural turnout fell from 62% to 45%.

**(c)** Define political participation and describe one factor that could explain the trend in rural turnout.

To earn the point: Definition. Model answer: Actions citizens take to influence government, such as voting.

To earn the point: A plausible factor. Model answer: Insecurity, distance to polling places, distrust in the fairness of elections, or migration of young people to cities.

**(d)** Draw a conclusion about how the trends could affect the legitimacy of elected governments, and explain your reasoning.

To earn the point: A conclusion supported by the data and reasoning. Model answer: Falling participation among a large group can weaken the perceived legitimacy of winners, who are chosen by a smaller share of citizens.
