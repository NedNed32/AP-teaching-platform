Comparative politics asks why political systems differ and what effects those differences have. It studies six course countries: **China, Iran, Mexico, Nigeria, Russia and the United Kingdom**.

## Tools

- **Empirical** data: election results, turnout, GDP, human development and corruption indexes.
- **Normative** judgments: claims about what is better or fair.
- **Quantitative** data (numbers) and **qualitative** evidence (text, interviews, case studies).
- The **comparative method**: compare cases that are similar in most ways but differ in one key variable.

When reading data, identify the trend, then draw a conclusion that the data actually supports. Correlation does not prove causation.

<Note title="Read the axes">
On the exam's data questions, read the title, axes and units first. Many wrong answers misread what is measured.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Empirical or normative?">
Classify: (1) Mexico's voter turnout was about 61% in 2024. (2) Mexico's turnout is too low.

1. (1) can be checked against data.
2. (2) is a value judgment.

**Answer:** (1) empirical, (2) normative.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which is an example of a normative statement?

**A.** The government should reduce income inequality. It expresses a value judgment.

**Q.** Which best describes a state?

**A.** A political organization with sovereignty over a territory and population. That is the definition of a state.

**Q.** A government relies mainly on rapid economic growth to justify its rule. What source of legitimacy is this?

**A.** Performance legitimacy. Legitimacy based on results.

**Q.** What distinguishes a regime from a government?

**A.** A regime is the set of fundamental rules and institutions; a government is the people currently in power. Regimes outlast governments.

**Q.** Which is most characteristic of an illiberal democracy?

**A.** Elections are held, but civil liberties and independent institutions are weak. Elections without full liberal protections.

**Q.** Which indicator would most strongly suggest declining regime legitimacy?

**A.** Sustained mass protests and falling trust in institutions. These signal citizens no longer accept the government's right to rule.
