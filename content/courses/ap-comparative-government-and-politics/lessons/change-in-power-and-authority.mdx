Governments redistribute power to respond to pressure, improve effectiveness or strengthen legitimacy.

- **United Kingdom**: devolution to Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland; a Supreme Court separate from the House of Lords (2009).
- **China**: economic decentralization to provinces since the reform era, while the CCP keeps political control.
- **Russia**: recentralization since 2000, including appointed or tightly managed regional leaders.
- **Mexico**: federal reforms shifting power among levels; constitutional changes to the judiciary in 2024.
- **Nigeria**: debates over "true federalism" and control of oil revenue.

<Note title="Why governments change power">
Ask what problem the change was meant to solve, and whether it increased or decreased central control.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Explain a change">
Why did the UK devolve power to Scotland?

1. Demand for self-government grew among Scots.
2. Devolution was meant to address it while keeping the union.

**Answer:** to respond to Scottish demands for self-rule and preserve the United Kingdom.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries has a federal system?

**A.** Russia. Mexico, Nigeria and Russia are federal; the UK, China and Iran are unitary.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries returned to civilian rule in 1999 after decades of military governments?

**A.** Nigeria. Nigeria's Fourth Republic began in 1999.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries is a one-party state in which the ruling party's leadership is written into the constitution?

**A.** China. China's constitution affirms the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries saw a dominant party lose the presidency in 2000 after more than 70 years in power?

**A.** Mexico. The PRI lost the presidency in 2000.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries has an uncodified constitution?

**A.** United Kingdom. The UK constitution is not contained in a single document.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries is a unitary state that has devolved power to regional legislatures?

**A.** United Kingdom. The UK devolved power to Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland but remains unitary.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries is a theocratic republic in which a cleric holds the highest authority?

**A.** Iran. Iran's Supreme Leader, a senior cleric, is the highest authority.

**Q.** Which of the following course countries is generally classified as a consolidated liberal democracy?

**A.** United Kingdom. Among the course countries, only the UK is a consolidated liberal democracy.

**Q.** Which is an example of a normative statement?

**A.** The government should reduce income inequality. It expresses a value judgment.

**Q.** Which best describes a state?

**A.** A political organization with sovereignty over a territory and population. That is the definition of a state.

**Q.** A government relies mainly on rapid economic growth to justify its rule. What source of legitimacy is this?

**A.** Performance legitimacy. Legitimacy based on results.

**Q.** What distinguishes a regime from a government?

**A.** A regime is the set of fundamental rules and institutions; a government is the people currently in power. Regimes outlast governments.

**Q.** Which is most characteristic of an illiberal democracy?

**A.** Elections are held, but civil liberties and independent institutions are weak. Elections without full liberal protections.

**Q.** Which indicator would most strongly suggest declining regime legitimacy?

**A.** Sustained mass protests and falling trust in institutions. These signal citizens no longer accept the government's right to rule.
