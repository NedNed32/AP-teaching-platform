Most successful attacks begin with a person, not a computer. **Social engineering** exploits trust and emotion to get around technical controls.

| Attack | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| phishing | mass emails or texts that impersonate a trusted brand |
| spear phishing | a targeted message using personal details |
| whaling | spear phishing aimed at executives |
| vishing / smishing | phishing by voice call / SMS |
| pretexting | a made-up story to justify a request |
| baiting | leaving infected USB drives or offering free downloads |
| tailgating | following an authorized person through a secure door |

## Warning signs

Urgency ("your account will close today"), unusual requests, mismatched sender addresses or links, and requests for credentials or payment.

## Defenses

Security awareness training, verifying requests through a second channel, reporting procedures, email filtering and multifactor authentication.

<Note title="People are a layer">
In defense in depth, trained users are one of the layers. Technical controls catch some attacks, and alert people catch others.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Identify the attack">
A caller claims to be from the help desk, says there is a problem with an employee's account, and asks for the employee's password to fix it. What is this?

1. The caller invents a scenario to justify the request.
2. It happens by phone.

**Answer:** pretexting by voice (vishing). Help desks never need a user's password.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** An email addressed to a company's CFO by name references a real upcoming acquisition and asks for an urgent wire transfer. What is this attack?

**A.** Whaling (spear phishing aimed at an executive). It is targeted, personalized and aimed at a senior executive.

**Q.** An attacker leaves USB drives labeled 'Salary Info 2027' in a company parking lot, hoping employees will plug them in. What is this attack?

**A.** Baiting. It lures victims with something they want.

**Q.** A text message says a package could not be delivered and asks the recipient to pay a small fee through a link. What is this?

**A.** Smishing. Phishing by SMS text message.

**Q.** Which control most directly reduces the success of phishing attacks against employees?

**A.** Regular security awareness training with simulated phishing exercises. It teaches people to recognize and report phishing.

**Q.** Which tactic is most characteristic of phishing messages?

**A.** Creating a sense of urgency so the victim acts without thinking. Urgency short-circuits careful checking.
