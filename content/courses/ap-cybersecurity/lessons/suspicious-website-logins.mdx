A phishing link usually leads to a login page that looks real. Anything typed there goes to the attacker.

## Reading a URL

The **registered domain** is the part just before the top-level domain (.com, .net, .org), read from the right:

| URL | Real domain |
| --- | --- |
| https://accounts.example.com/login | example.com |
| https://example.com.secure-login.io/ | secure-login.io |
| https://examp1e.com/ | examp1e.com (typosquat) |

## Red flags

- A domain that does not match the organization.
- Lookalike characters (rn for m, 1 for l).
- Unexpected login prompts after clicking a link in a message.
- A site asking for more information than usual.

Password managers help: they fill in credentials only on the exact domain they were saved for.

<Note title="HTTPS is not trust">
Attackers can get valid certificates for their own domains. The padlock means encrypted, not legitimate.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Spot the fake">
Which URL is most likely legitimate for a bank at bankofexample.com: (a) https://bankofexample.com.verify-id.co/, (b) https://secure.bankofexample.com/login, (c) https://bankofexarnple.com/?

1. (a) belongs to verify-id.co.
2. (c) uses rn in place of m.
3. (b) is a subdomain of bankofexample.com.

**Answer:** (b).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A user receives an email that appears to come from Brightmail, whose real website is `brightmail.org`. Which link most likely leads to a legitimate Brightmail login page?

**A.** `https://login.brightmail.org/account`. Its registered domain is brightmail.org; "login" is a subdomain. The registered domain is the part immediately before the first single slash after the host, read from the right. Only the first link is hosted on brightmail.org.
