A flat network lets an attacker who gets in reach everything. **Segmentation** limits movement.

- **VLANs** separate traffic logically on the same switches (staff, students, cameras, guests).
- A **DMZ** holds servers the internet must reach (web, email), with firewalls on both sides.
- **Air gaps** physically isolate critical systems.

## Subnet sizes

A prefix of /n leaves $$32 - n$$ host bits:

$$\text{usable hosts} = 2^{32-n} - 2$$

| Prefix | Addresses | Usable hosts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| /24 | 256 | 254 |
| /26 | 64 | 62 |
| /28 | 16 | 14 |
| /30 | 4 | 2 |

<Note title="IoT on its own segment">
Smart cameras and thermostats are often poorly secured. Putting them on a separate VLAN keeps them from being a path to sensitive systems.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Size a subnet">
A lab needs addresses for 50 devices. What is the smallest prefix that works?

1. /27 gives 30 usable hosts: too few.
2. /26 gives 62 usable hosts.

**Answer:** /26.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A network segment must support 450 devices. What is the smallest subnet (largest prefix) that provides enough usable addresses?

**Answer:** `/23`

**Why:** /23 gives 510 usable hosts; /24 gives only 254.

**Working:** Usable hosts = 2^(32 − prefix) − 2; the smallest that reaches 450 is /23.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- `/24`: That gives only 254 usable hosts.
- `/22`: That works but is larger than necessary.
- `/25`: Too few addresses.

### Worked exam question 2

How many usable host addresses are in a `192.168.37.0/30` subnet?

**Answer:** 2

**Why:** 2^(32 − 30) − 2: subtract the network and broadcast addresses.

**Working:** 2 host bits give 4 addresses, 2 usable.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 4: The network and broadcast addresses cannot be assigned.
- 1073741824: Use the host bits, 32 − prefix.
- 3: Two addresses are reserved.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Why is a single shared Wi-Fi password a weakness for a large organization?

**A.** Access cannot be revoked for one person without changing it for everyone. Individual credentials (enterprise authentication) solve this.

**Q.** Where should a company place its public web server?

**A.** In a DMZ separated from the internal network by firewalls. It isolates internet-facing systems.

**Q.** Why should smart cameras and other IoT devices be placed on their own VLAN?

**A.** If an IoT device is compromised, segmentation limits the attacker's access to other systems. Segmentation contains breaches.

**Q.** Which policy is a managerial control for network security?

**A.** An acceptable use policy that users must agree to. Policies are managerial controls.

**Q.** An attacker sends forged ARP replies so that traffic on a local network passes through the attacker's computer. What is this?

**A.** ARP spoofing, an on-path (man-in-the-middle) attack. It redirects local traffic through the attacker.

**Q.** A school's Wi-Fi still uses WEP encryption. What should replace it?

**A.** WPA3 (or WPA2 with AES). WEP can be cracked quickly.
