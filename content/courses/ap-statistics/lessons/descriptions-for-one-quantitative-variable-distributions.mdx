Describe a distribution with **SOCS**, in context:

- **Shape**: symmetric, skewed left or right; unimodal, bimodal, uniform.
- **Outliers** and other unusual features: gaps, clusters.
- **Center**: a typical value (median or mean).
- **Spread** (variability): range, IQR or standard deviation.

Skew is named for the tail: a long right tail means **skewed right**.

<Note title="Compare, do not just list">
When comparing distributions, use comparison words: greater than, similar to, less variable.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Describe">
Commute times for 40 workers have a long tail to the right, a median of 18 minutes, an IQR of 12 minutes and one value of 95 minutes. Describe the distribution.

1. Shape: skewed right.
2. Center and spread: median 18 min, IQR 12 min.
3. Unusual: possible outlier at 95 min.

**Answer:** commute times are skewed right with a median of 18 minutes and IQR of 12 minutes, with a possible outlier at 95 minutes.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

For a distribution of home prices in a county, the mean is 45 and the median is 54. Which is the most likely shape?

**Answer:** Skewed left

**Why:** A long left tail pulls the mean below the median.

**Working:** Mean 45 &lt; median 54: the mean is pulled toward the left tail.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Skewed right: The mean is pulled toward the tail.
- Symmetric: In a symmetric distribution, the mean and median are close.
- Uniform: A uniform distribution is symmetric, with mean near the median.
