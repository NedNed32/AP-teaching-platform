| Type | Denominator |
| --- | --- |
| joint | table total |
| marginal | table total (using row or column totals) |
| conditional | a row or column total |

Two variables are **associated** if the conditional distributions differ across categories. In the sports table, 45% of 9th graders and 30% of 12th graders play sports, so grade and sports participation are associated in these data.

<Note title="Association is not causation">
Even a strong association in a two-way table does not prove one variable causes the other.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Conditional proportions">
Of 120 dog owners, 84 walk daily; of 80 cat owners, 20 walk daily. Is pet type associated with walking daily?

1. Dog owners: 84/120 = 0.70.
2. Cat owners: 20/80 = 0.25.

**Answer:** yes; the proportions differ greatly (0.70 vs 0.25).
</WorkedExample>

### Worked exam question 1

*Survey of students*

| Grade | Drives to school | Does not drive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Juniors | 23 | 77 |
| Seniors | 65 | 52 |

A student is chosen at random from those who drive to school. What is the probability the student is a junior?

**Answer:** 0.261

**Why:** P(junior | drives) = juniors who drive ÷ all who drive.

**Working:** 23 ÷ (23 + 65) = 0.261.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 0.106: That is P(junior and drives); divide by drivers only.
- 0.23: That is P(drives | junior).
- 0.461: That is P(junior) overall.
