$$H_0: p_1 - p_2 = 0 \qquad H_a: p_1 - p_2 > 0,\ < 0,\ \text{or} \ne 0$$

$$\hat p_c = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{n_1 + n_2}$$

**Conditions**: random (two samples or random assignment), 10% for samples, and large counts using $$\hat p_c$$: $$n_1\hat p_c$$, $$n_1(1-\hat p_c)$$, $$n_2\hat p_c$$, $$n_2(1-\hat p_c)$$ all at least 10.

<Note title="Experiments">
In a randomized experiment, the 10% condition is not needed because units were assigned, not sampled.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Pooled proportion">
Group 1: 45 of 150 succeed. Group 2: 30 of 150 succeed. Find p̂c.

1. $$(45 + 30)/(150 + 150)$$.

**Answer:** 0.25.
</WorkedExample>

### Worked exam question 1

In a randomized experiment, 80 of 210 subjects improved with treatment 1 and 128 of 250 improved with treatment 2. For a test of H₀: p₁ − p₂ = 0, what is the test statistic?

**Answer:** -2.81

**Why:** Pooled p̂c = 0.4522; z = (p̂₁ − p̂₂)/√(p̂c(1 − p̂c)(1/n₁ + 1/n₂)).

**Working:** p̂₁ = 0.381, p̂₂ = 0.512, SE = 0.0466, z = -2.81.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- -2.84: The test uses the pooled proportion, because H₀ assumes p₁ = p₂.
- -5.65: Use (1/n₁ + 1/n₂).
- -0.13: Divide the difference by its standard error.
