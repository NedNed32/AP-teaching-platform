**Interpretation template**: "Assuming [H₀ in context], there is a [p-value] probability of getting a sample proportion of [observed] or [more extreme direction] by chance alone."

- p-value ≤ α: **reject** H₀; convincing evidence for Hₐ.
- p-value greater than α: **fail to reject** H₀; not convincing evidence for Hₐ.

We never "accept" H₀.

<Note title="Two-sided tests">
For Hₐ: p ≠ p₀, the p-value counts extreme results in both tails, so it is twice the one-tail area.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Interpret">
Testing H₀: p = 0.30 vs Hₐ: p > 0.30 gives p̂ = 0.36 and a p-value of 0.04. Interpret the p-value.

1. Assume p = 0.30.

**Answer:** if 30% is the true proportion, there is a 0.04 probability of a sample proportion of 0.36 or higher by chance alone.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

To test H₀: p = 0.3 versus Hₐ: p > 0.3, a random sample of 150 gives p̂ = 0.38. What are the test statistic and p-value?

**Answer:** z = 2.14, p-value = 0.0163

**Why:** z = (p̂ − p₀)/√(p₀(1 − p₀)/n); one-sided p-value.

**Working:** SD = √(0.3·0.7/150) = 0.0374; z = 2.14; p-value = 0.0163.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- z = 2.02, p-value = 0.0218: The test's standard deviation uses p₀, not p̂.
- z = 2.14, p-value = 0.0325: The alternative is one-sided.
- z = 2.14, p-value = 0.9837: Find the area in the direction of Hₐ.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which change increases the power of a significance test, with everything else unchanged?

**A.** Increasing the sample size. Larger samples detect real effects more often.

**Q.** A factory tests H₀: the defect rate is 2% versus Hₐ: it is greater than 2%. What is a Type I error in this context?

**A.** Concluding the defect rate is greater than 2% when it actually is 2%. Rejecting a true H₀.

**Q.** A 95% confidence interval for p is (0.42, 0.51). Which claim is supported?

**A.** It is plausible that fewer than half the population has the trait. Values below 0.5 lie in the interval.

**Q.** A test of H₀: p = 0.4 versus Hₐ: p > 0.4 gives a p-value of 0.03. Which interpretation is correct?

**A.** If p = 0.4, there is a 0.03 probability of a sample proportion at least as large as the one observed, by chance alone. That is the definition of the p-value.

**Q.** Which statistic is an unbiased estimator of the population proportion?

**A.** The sample proportion p̂ from a random sample. Its sampling distribution is centered at p.

**Q.** What does '95% confident' mean for a confidence interval?

**A.** In many repeated samples, about 95% of intervals built this way would capture the true parameter. Confidence describes the method's long-run success rate.
