$$\bar x \pm t^*\frac{s}{\sqrt n} \qquad df = n - 1$$

**Conditions**: random, 10%, and normal/large sample ($$n \ge 30$$, or the population is normal, or the sample data show no strong skew or outliers).

**Paired data**: compute differences $$d$$, then use $$\bar x_d \pm t^*\frac{s_d}{\sqrt n}$$.

t* is larger than z* for the same confidence level, especially for small samples.

<Note title="Check the graph">
With small samples, sketch or describe a dotplot or boxplot of the data to justify the normality condition.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Construct">
A random sample of 25 students sleeps x̄ = 6.8 h with s = 1.2 h. Find a 95% interval for μ (t* = 2.064).

1. SE = $$1.2/5 = 0.24$$.
2. $$6.8 \pm 2.064(0.24) = 6.8 \pm 0.495$$.

**Answer:** (6.31, 7.30) hours.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A random sample of 18 has mean 72.9 and standard deviation 8. Which is a 90% confidence interval for the population mean?

**Answer:** (69.62, 76.18)

**Why:** x̄ ± t*·s/√n with t* = 1.74 (df = 17).

**Working:** SE = 1.886; margin = 3.28.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- (69.8, 76): With s estimating σ, use t*, not z*.
- (58.98, 86.82): Divide s by √n.
- (72.13, 73.67): Divide by √n, not n.

### Free-response practice: A test for a proportion

A school district claims that 40% of its students take public transit to school. A reporter suspects the true proportion is lower. In a random sample of 130 students, 44 take public transit.

**(a)** State the hypotheses, defining the parameter.

To earn the point: Correct hypotheses with the parameter defined. Model answer: H₀: p = 0.4; Hₐ: p &lt; 0.4, where p is the proportion of all district students who take public transit.

**(b)** Identify the appropriate procedure and check its conditions.

To earn the point: One-sample z test for a proportion. Model answer: Random sample stated.

To earn the point: 10% and large counts checked. Model answer: 130 is less than 10% of all students (assumed); np₀ = 52 and n(1 − p₀) = 78 are both at least 10.

**(c)** Calculate the test statistic and p-value.

To earn the point: Test statistic. Model answer: p̂ = 0.3385; z = (0.3385 − 0.4)/0.043 = -1.43

To earn the point: p-value. Model answer: 0.076

**(d)** State a conclusion in context at α = 0.05.

To earn the point: Correct decision linked to the p-value, in context. Model answer: Because 0.076 is greater than 0.05, fail to reject H₀: not convincing evidence that the proportion is less than 0.4.
