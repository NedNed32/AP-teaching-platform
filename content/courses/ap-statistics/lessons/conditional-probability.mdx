$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} \qquad P(A \cap B) = P(A)\,P(B \mid A)$$

**Tree diagrams** organize multistage processes: multiply along branches, add across branches that lead to the same event.

A classic application: a medical test's chance of a positive result given disease differs from the chance of disease given a positive result.

<Note title="Given means restrict">
'Given B' means look only at the B row or column of the table.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Tree diagram">
1% of people have a condition. A test is positive for 95% of those with it and 8% of those without. Find P(condition | positive).

1. P(positive and condition) = 0.01 × 0.95 = 0.0095.
2. P(positive) = 0.0095 + 0.99 × 0.08 = 0.0887.
3. 0.0095 ÷ 0.0887.

**Answer:** about 0.107.
</WorkedExample>

### Worked exam question 1

*Survey of students*

| Grade | Drives to school | Does not drive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Juniors | 23 | 77 |
| Seniors | 65 | 52 |

A student is chosen at random from those who drive to school. What is the probability the student is a junior?

**Answer:** 0.261

**Why:** P(junior | drives) = juniors who drive ÷ all who drive.

**Working:** 23 ÷ (23 + 65) = 0.261.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 0.106: That is P(junior and drives); divide by drivers only.
- 0.23: That is P(drives | junior).
- 0.461: That is P(junior) overall.
