$$\mu_{\bar x_1 - \bar x_2} = \mu_1 - \mu_2 \qquad \sigma_{\bar x_1 - \bar x_2} = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}$$

Approximately normal when both sampling distributions are approximately normal (normal populations or large samples). The two samples must be **independent**.

<Note title="Paired or two-sample?">
If each value in one group is naturally linked to one value in the other (same person, twins, before and after), the data are paired.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Standard deviation">
σ₁ = 10, n₁ = 25; σ₂ = 12, n₂ = 36. Find the SD of x̄₁ − x̄₂.

1. $$\sqrt{100/25 + 144/36} = \sqrt{4 + 4}$$.

**Answer:** about 2.83.
</WorkedExample>

### Worked exam question 1

Two independent random samples have n₁ = 18, s₁ = 12 and n₂ = 24, s₂ = 10. What is the standard error of x̄₁ − x̄₂?

**Answer:** 3.488

**Why:** √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂).

**Working:** √(12²/18 + 10²/24) = 3.488.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 1.958: Variances add, even for a difference.
- 4.87: Add variances, then take the square root; standard errors do not add.
- 3.395: Each sample contributes s²/n.
