A **frequency table** lists the count in each category; a **relative frequency table** lists proportions.

| Favorite sport | Frequency | Relative frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| soccer | 30 | 0.375 |
| basketball | 24 | 0.300 |
| other | 26 | 0.325 |
| total | 80 | 1.000 |

The proportion in a category, $$\hat p$$, is a summary statistic for a categorical variable. The **mode** is the most common category.

<Note title="Compare with proportions">
When groups have different sizes, compare relative frequencies, not counts.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Relative frequency">
Of 250 surveyed voters, 95 support a measure. Find the proportion.

1. $$95/250 = 0.38$$.

**Answer:** 0.38 (38%).
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

The practice exams do not ask about this topic on its own, but it supports these questions from the same unit. Work through them to see how the ideas are used.

### Worked exam question 1

The data are: 10, 15, 15, 15, 15, 18, 19, 24, 37, 77. Using the 1.5 × IQR rule, which statement is correct?

**Answer:** Q1 = 15, Q3 = 24; values above 37.5 are outliers, so 77 is an outlier

**Why:** IQR = 9; upper fence = Q3 + 1.5·IQR = 37.5.

**Working:** Five-number summary: 10, 15, 16.5, 24, 77. Upper fence 24 + 1.5(9) = 37.5.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Q1 = 15, Q3 = 24; values above 33 are outliers, so 77 is an outlier: The fence uses 1.5 × IQR, not 1 × IQR.
- Q1 = 15, Q3 = 24; values above 30 are outliers: Add 1.5·IQR to Q3, not to the median.
- There are no outliers because every value is within 2 standard deviations of the mean: The 1.5 × IQR rule is the method asked for.

### Worked exam question 2

For a distribution of home prices in a county, the mean is 45 and the median is 54. Which is the most likely shape?

**Answer:** Skewed left

**Why:** A long left tail pulls the mean below the median.

**Working:** Mean 45 &lt; median 54: the mean is pulled toward the left tail.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Skewed right: The mean is pulled toward the tail.
- Symmetric: In a symmetric distribution, the mean and median are close.
- Uniform: A uniform distribution is symmetric, with mean near the median.

**Q.** Researchers recruited 200 volunteers among customers of the store and asked which of two study apps they already use, then compared test scores. Can the researchers conclude that the app causes higher scores, and to whom do the results apply?

**A.** No to cause and effect, and no generalization beyond the volunteers. No random assignment decides causation; no random selection decides generalization. Random assignment: no. Random selection: no.

**Q.** Researchers randomly selected 200 students at the school and randomly assigned them to use one of two study apps, then compared test scores. Can the researchers conclude that the app causes higher scores, and to whom do the results apply?

**A.** Yes to cause and effect, and yes to generalizing to all students at the school. Random assignment decides causation; random selection decides generalization. Random assignment: yes. Random selection: yes.
