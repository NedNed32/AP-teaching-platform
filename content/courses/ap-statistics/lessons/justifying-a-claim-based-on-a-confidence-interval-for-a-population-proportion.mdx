**Interpret the interval**: "We are 95% confident that the interval from 0.41 to 0.49 captures the true proportion of [context]."

**Interpret the level**: "If we took many random samples and built a 95% interval from each, about 95% of the intervals would capture the true proportion."

**Claims**: values inside the interval are plausible; values outside are not.

<Note title="Plausible is not proven">
A value inside the interval is plausible, but the interval does not prove the parameter equals it.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Evaluate a claim">
A company claims 60% of customers are satisfied. A 95% interval from a random sample is (0.52, 0.58). Evaluate the claim.

1. 0.60 is above the interval.

**Answer:** the claim is not plausible; the true proportion appears lower than 60%.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** Which change increases the power of a significance test, with everything else unchanged?

**A.** Increasing the sample size. Larger samples detect real effects more often.

**Q.** A factory tests H₀: the defect rate is 2% versus Hₐ: it is greater than 2%. What is a Type I error in this context?

**A.** Concluding the defect rate is greater than 2% when it actually is 2%. Rejecting a true H₀.

**Q.** A 95% confidence interval for p is (0.42, 0.51). Which claim is supported?

**A.** It is plausible that fewer than half the population has the trait. Values below 0.5 lie in the interval.

**Q.** A test of H₀: p = 0.4 versus Hₐ: p > 0.4 gives a p-value of 0.03. Which interpretation is correct?

**A.** If p = 0.4, there is a 0.03 probability of a sample proportion at least as large as the one observed, by chance alone. That is the definition of the p-value.

**Q.** Which statistic is an unbiased estimator of the population proportion?

**A.** The sample proportion p̂ from a random sample. Its sampling distribution is centered at p.

**Q.** What does '95% confident' mean for a confidence interval?

**A.** In many repeated samples, about 95% of intervals built this way would capture the true parameter. Confidence describes the method's long-run success rate.
