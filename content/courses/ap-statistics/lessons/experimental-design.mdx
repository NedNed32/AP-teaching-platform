## Principles

1. **Comparison** of two or more treatments (including a control or placebo).
2. **Random assignment** to create roughly equivalent groups.
3. **Replication**: enough units in each treatment.
4. **Control** of other variables.

## Designs

- **Completely randomized**: all units randomly assigned to treatments.
- **Randomized block**: randomize within blocks of similar units (for example, by age group).
- **Matched pairs**: pairs of similar units, or each unit gets both treatments in random order.

A difference is **statistically significant** if it is too large to plausibly be explained by the chance variation of random assignment.

<Note title="Describe the random assignment">
Say how: for example, put 40 names on slips, shuffle, draw 20 for treatment A; the rest get treatment B.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Block or not?">
A fertilizer experiment uses plots on both sunny and shady sides of a field. How should treatments be assigned?

1. Sunlight affects growth.
2. Block by sunlight, then randomly assign fertilizers within each block.

**Answer:** a randomized block design, blocking by sun or shade.
</WorkedExample>

**Q.** Why does an experiment use random assignment?

**A.** To create treatment groups that are roughly equivalent, so differences in response can be attributed to the treatments. Randomization balances other variables.

**Q.** Which graph is appropriate for displaying the distribution of favorite music genres among students?

**A.** A bar chart. Genre is categorical.

**Q.** Which variable is quantitative?

**A.** The number of text messages a student sends per day. Counts are quantitative.

**Q.** A principal divides students by grade level and takes a simple random sample of 25 students from each grade. What sampling method is this?

**A.** Stratified random sampling. SRSs within groups (strata).

**Q.** Does increasing the sample size reduce bias in a survey?

**A.** No; a larger sample reduces variability but not bias from the sampling method. Bias is systematic.

**Q.** A radio station asks listeners to call in with their opinions about a new law. Which type of bias is most likely?

**A.** Voluntary response bias. People with strong opinions are more likely to call.

**Q.** In an experiment on a new reading program, students are first grouped by reading level, then randomly assigned to programs within each level. What is the purpose of grouping by level?

**A.** Blocking, to reduce variability caused by reading level. Blocking controls a known source of variation.

### Free-response practice: Planning a study

Researchers want to investigate whether a brief mindfulness exercise before tests improves scores. They have 80 students in a statistics class available.

**(a)** Write an investigative question for this study, and identify the explanatory and response variables.

To earn the point: A clear investigative question that names the population or units and the variables. Model answer: "Does the mindfulness exercise lead to a better score on the next unit test than a reading break for units like these?"

To earn the point: Correct variables. Model answer: Explanatory: treatment (the mindfulness exercise vs a reading break). Response: score on the next unit test.

**(b)** Explain why an experiment, rather than an observational study, should be used to answer the question.

To earn the point: Only random assignment allows a cause-and-effect conclusion; an observational study could be confounded. Model answer: Units that choose a treatment may differ in other ways that affect the response.

**(c)** Describe how to randomly assign the 80 units to the two treatments in a completely randomized design.

To earn the point: A valid random mechanism. Model answer: Number units 1–80; use a random number generator to select 40 different numbers.

To earn the point: Clear assignment. Model answer: Those 40 receive the mindfulness exercise; the remaining 40 receive a reading break.

**(d)** The researchers consider blocking by previous test average (high or low). Explain how blocking would be carried out and why it could improve the experiment.

To earn the point: Form blocks and randomize within each block. Model answer: Separate units into the blocks, then randomly assign half of each block to each treatment.

To earn the point: Benefit: reduces variability in the response due to the blocking variable, making a treatment effect easier to detect. Model answer: Accounts for differences caused by previous test average.
