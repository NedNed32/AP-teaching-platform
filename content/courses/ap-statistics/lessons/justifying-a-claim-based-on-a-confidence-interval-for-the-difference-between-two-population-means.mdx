- Interval entirely above 0: evidence that $$\mu_1 > \mu_2$$.
- Entirely below 0: evidence that $$\mu_1 < \mu_2$$.
- Contains 0: no convincing evidence of a difference.

With random assignment in an experiment, a difference can be attributed to the treatment.

<Note title="State both the direction and the size">
A good conclusion says which group is larger and by how much, plausibly.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Evaluate">
A 95% interval for (new method − old method) mean scores is (2.1, 9.7) in a randomized experiment. Conclusion?

1. All plausible differences are positive; random assignment.

**Answer:** convincing evidence the new method causes higher mean scores, by about 2 to 10 points.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the facts and ideas the practice exams ask about this topic, each with the answer and the reason. If you can answer all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Facts and ideas the exams test

Cover the answers and test yourself.

**Q.** A sample of 12 values is used for a one-sample t interval. Which condition must be checked because the sample is small?

**A.** The sample data show no strong skewness or outliers (population approximately normal). Small samples need approximate normality.

**Q.** As degrees of freedom increase, what happens to the t distribution?

**A.** It approaches the standard normal distribution. Tails become lighter.

**Q.** A 95% confidence interval for μ₁ − μ₂ is (−2.4, 5.1). What can be concluded?

**A.** There is no convincing evidence of a difference in population means, because 0 is in the interval. Zero is a plausible difference.

**Q.** Why is the t distribution used instead of the normal distribution for inference about a mean?

**A.** Because σ is estimated by s, which adds variability. t has heavier tails to account for it.

**Q.** Each of 20 students takes a reading test before and after a summer program. Which procedure compares the scores?

**A.** A paired t procedure on the differences (after − before). Two measurements on the same students are paired.

**Q.** If the sample size quadruples, what happens to the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of x̄?

**A.** It is cut in half. σ/√n: √4 = 2.
