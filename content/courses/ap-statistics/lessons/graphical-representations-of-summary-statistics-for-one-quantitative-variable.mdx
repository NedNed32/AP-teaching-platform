A **boxplot** draws the five-number summary: a box from Q1 to Q3 with a line at the median, and whiskers to the most extreme values that are not outliers. Outliers are plotted individually.

- Each section (whisker to Q1, Q1 to median, median to Q3, Q3 to whisker) contains about 25% of the data.
- A longer right whisker and box section suggest right skew.
- Boxplots are excellent for **comparing** groups side by side, but they hide details such as clusters and gaps.

<Note title="Whiskers stop at non-outliers">
With an outlier, the whisker ends at the largest value that is not an outlier, not at the fence.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Read a boxplot">
A boxplot shows min 3, Q1 8, median 11, Q3 20, max 29. Estimate the percentage of values above 11 and describe the shape.

1. Half the values are above the median.
2. The upper half is more spread out.

**Answer:** about 50%; the distribution is skewed right.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

The data are: 10, 15, 15, 15, 15, 18, 19, 24, 37, 77. Using the 1.5 × IQR rule, which statement is correct?

**Answer:** Q1 = 15, Q3 = 24; values above 37.5 are outliers, so 77 is an outlier

**Why:** IQR = 9; upper fence = Q3 + 1.5·IQR = 37.5.

**Working:** Five-number summary: 10, 15, 16.5, 24, 77. Upper fence 24 + 1.5(9) = 37.5.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- Q1 = 15, Q3 = 24; values above 33 are outliers, so 77 is an outlier: The fence uses 1.5 × IQR, not 1 × IQR.
- Q1 = 15, Q3 = 24; values above 30 are outliers: Add 1.5·IQR to Q3, not to the median.
- There are no outliers because every value is within 2 standard deviations of the mean: The 1.5 × IQR rule is the method asked for.
