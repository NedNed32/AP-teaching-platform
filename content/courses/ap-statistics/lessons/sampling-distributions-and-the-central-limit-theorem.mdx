A **statistic** varies from sample to sample. Its **sampling distribution** describes that variation.

## Sample means

$$\mu_{\bar x} = \mu \qquad \sigma_{\bar x} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}$$

The shape is approximately normal if the population is normal or, by the **central limit theorem**, if $$n \ge 30$$.

Quadrupling the sample size halves the standard deviation of the sampling distribution.

<Note title="Three distributions">
Keep them apart: the population distribution, the distribution of one sample's data, and the sampling distribution of a statistic.
</Note>

<WorkedExample title="Using the CLT">
Bags of chips have mean weight 28.5 g with SD 1.2 g (shape unknown). For random samples of 36 bags, find the probability that the sample mean is under 28.2 g.

1. $$\sigma_{\bar x} = 1.2/6 = 0.2$$; normal by the CLT (n = 36).
2. $$z = (28.2 - 28.5)/0.2 = -1.5$$.

**Answer:** about 0.067.
</WorkedExample>

## On the practice exams

These are the kinds of questions the practice exams ask about this topic. Each has a full answer, the reasoning, and the reason each wrong option is wrong. If you can do all of them without looking, you are ready for this topic on the exams.

### Worked exam question 1

A population has mean 80 and standard deviation 9 (shape unknown). For random samples of 64, what is the approximate probability that the sample mean exceeds 82.1?

**Answer:** 0.031

**Why:** By the CLT, x̄ is approximately normal with SD 9/√64 = 1.125.

**Working:** z = (82.1 − 80)/1.125 = 1.87; P = 0.031.

**Mistakes to avoid**

- 0.4078: Use σ/√n for a sample mean.
- 0.969: That is the area below.
- 0: Divide σ by √n, not n.
